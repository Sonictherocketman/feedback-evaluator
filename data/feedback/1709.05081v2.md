# Review

**Paper:** *Massless charged particles: Cosmic censorship, and Third law of Black Hole Mechanics* (Fairros, Ghosh, Sarkar; IIT Gandhinagar), arXiv:1709.05081v2.

## Summary of the argument

The paper probes the **Cosmic Censorship Hypothesis (CCH)** and the **third law of black-hole mechanics** by trying to overcharge a Reissner-Nordström (RN) black hole with the *least* tunable matter one can imagine: a **massless charged particle** (a "test particle" with no rest mass, hence no spin parameter to dial) and a **spherically symmetric null charged shell**. Two ingredients distinguish the work from prior overcharging studies:

1. The particle does **not** follow a null geodesic but a **modified Lorentz-force equation** (following Ori 1991), because a charged Vaidya source requires such modification to be consistent with the field equation. This makes the particle's trajectory *path-dependent* beyond the conserved energy and charge, and introduces a critical point $r_c = qQ/E$ at which the two branches of the trajectory meet.
2. In the shell case, the **weak energy condition (WECC)** on the shell's transverse stress-energy is used directly.

The headline results: (a) an *extremal* RN black hole **cannot** be overcharged by a massless charged particle (extending Wald's argument to the massless case); (b) a *non-extremal* RN black hole **can** be overcharged by a suitably tuned massless particle (Fig. 1), subject only to the trajectory-remains-causal constraint; (c) a null charged shell **cannot** overcharge the hole — the WECC bound and the overcharging bound contradict each other (Eqs. 11–12); (d) in a glued in/outgoing Vaidya spacetime, an extremal *apparent* horizon can form in finite advanced time, but the authors conclude the third law survives "in spirit."

## Strengths

- **The null-shell WECC contradiction (Section III) is the paper's cleanest and most original contribution.** Hubeny's earlier result used time-like shell equations of motion and concluded the WECC was sufficient to forbid overcharging; here the authors reproduce the conclusion for the *radiating* (null) shell *from the transverse extrinsic-curvature jump conditions* (Eqs. 7–8) and the continuity of the metric, and explicitly point out it contrasts with the time-like case. The bookkeeping of the shell's surface stress tensor (Eq. 9) is careful.
- The **extremal-case no-overcharging argument** (Eqs. 4–5) is crisp: $r_c = qQ/E$ together with the extremal condition $q < E$ makes overcharging an inconsistency. This is a legitimate, if expected, extension of Wald.
- The **apparent-horizon casework** (Section V A, cases Ia through IIb) is thorough, and the use of a *glued* Vaidya solution rather than the singular single ingoing one is the right way to avoid the WECC-violating region that plagues a lone charged Vaidya patch. The sign analysis of $f(R(\lambda))$ at the gluing surface $\mathcal{B}$ (Eqs. 25, 27, 32, 34) is internal-consistent.
- The paper is honest about its limitations: it explicitly defers gravitational back-reaction to future work and notes the massless-particle premise is unphysical.

## Critique

**1. The CCH counterexample is asserted, not established — the nakedness of the resulting singularity is never checked.** This is the main weakness. Fig. 1 shows the massless particle's trajectory reaching $r=0$, and the text says the non-extremal hole "can be overcharged." But a CCH *counterexample* requires showing the newly formed singularity is **naked**, i.e. visible from future null infinity — not merely that worldlines hit $r=0$. An overcharged RN interior can still be hidden (e.g. by a Cauchy-horizon/blue-shift instability, or by the trajectory simply ending in a region that is not causally connected to $\mathscr{I}^+$). The paper conflates "the particle reaches $r=0$" with "a naked singularity is produced." Without an argument about the causal structure of the post-infall spacetime (does an observer at $\mathscr{I}^+$ see it?), the central claim sits in the same class as the many "counterexamples" that do not actually violate weak CCH.

**2. The whole point-particle picture is, by the authors' own admission, a "questionable assumption."** The particle is described as a test particle, yet the discussion that follows (the critical point where the trajectory is "zero and therefore discontinuous," the choice of branch) is precisely where a test particle's field becomes non-negligible and the particle picture breaks down. The authors acknowledge the point-particle concept is ill-defined in both gravity and electromagnetism, but then build the overcharging conclusion on top of trajectory segments through the critical point. A *finite* (regular) charged null packet would resolve exactly this, and the null-shell analysis is in fact the more honest formulation — so the paper would be better organized to lead with the shell result (which is a clean negative) and treat the point-particle overcharging as an idealized, back-reaction-free lower bound rather than the headline.

**3. Back-reaction is deferred, so the positive result is conditional.** The paper's conclusion that "overcharging is possible" for the non-extremal hole is explicitly predicated on *ignoring gravitational back-reaction*. In every prior instance (Wald, Hubeny, Saida; Chkirou/Barceló), the back-reaction or the full WECC of the infalling matter is what prevents the naked singularity. By deferring this to "future work," the paper's positive claim is really: *a classical, back-reaction-free, point-source idealization allows the trajectory to reach $r=0$*. That is weaker than a genuine CCH test and should be stated more cautiously in the abstract and title.

**4. "Fine-tuning" vs. the actual CCH statement.** CCH (weak form) says a *generic* physical process from regular data will not reveal the singularity. The non-extremal "positive" result rests on the *existence* of a choice of $(q, E)$ (and of the trajectory branch) that overcharges. Existence of a tuned choice is not the standard measure for CCH; one needs a statement about a *robust neighborhood* of initial data, or a Cauchy-data construction from regular matter. The current argument is closer to a phase-space existence statement than an initial-value counterexample, and this distinction is not made explicit.

**5. The third-law conclusion is under-specified relative to its claims.** Section V is more exploratory than the overcharging sections. "Intact in spirit" is not a precise theorem — the actual demonstration is narrower: an extremal *apparent* horizon (not an event horizon) may form on the gluing surface $\mathcal{B}$ in finite advanced time in one configuration (cases IIIa/IIb, Figs. 7–9), while the *event* horizon's area keeps growing and the hole is not asymptotically extremal. The distinction between apparent-horizon extremization and event-horizon extremization — which is exactly what saves the third law — is buried in the Discussion rather than stated as the theorem. Also, the exclusion of the "Ori matching" (Eq. 23) to keep $\mathcal{B}$ space-like is a modeling assumption, and the dependence of the conclusions on the choice of reflective vs. Ori matching is not explored.

**6. Technical/presentational issues.**
- Loose citation use: the Kerr over-spinning results are cited as "[9][10]" in two slightly different sentences in the Introduction, but [9] (Colleoni–Barack) and [10] (Colleoni et al.) play different roles (dissipative vs. full back-reaction); a more careful sentence would distinguish them.
- A clear garble on page 2: "in the case of a dynamical collapse of a into a non charged shell into the charged black hole" — should read "null charged shell."
- The sign of $E$ and the "ingoing/in-falling" branch selection in Section II is used in both the overcharging (need $E < q$) and the extremal (need $E > gQ/r_+ \geq q$) arguments; the physical interpretation of *which* branch is the physical in-falling one is asserted via future-directedness but the energy-signing convention is not fixed clearly enough for a reader to re-derive it.
- Eq. (4)'s prime is "w.r.t. $\lambda$" — fine, but the transition from branch-ruling out at $\dot r' = 0$ to the causal-continuation choice deserves a sentence more than the current "as we will see causality implies only one branch is preferred."

## Bottom line

A technically competent, mostly honest paper inside the overcharging/CCH literature. The **WECC-based argument against null-shell overcharging** is genuinely useful and the **extremal massless result** is a clean (if anticipated) extension. But the paper's headline "non-extremal overcharging possible" claim rests on a back-reaction-free point-particle idealization and, crucially, **never establishes that the resulting singularity is naked**, so it does not by itself settle the CCH question it advertises. The third-law analysis is suggestive but ends in "intact in spirit" rather than a sharp statement about event vs. apparent horizons. As a first step it is reasonable; the path to a real CCH counterexample (nakedness + back-reaction + Cauchy-data construction) is exactly what the paper defers.

**Summary of what the paper attempts.** The authors probe the weak Cosmic Censorship Hypothesis (CCH) and the third law of black-hole mechanics (BHM) in a specific, somewhat unconventional test bed: instead of the standard massive/charged matter, they send (i) a massless charged test particle and (ii) a charged null shell into a Reissner–Nordström (RN) hole, and then analyze the glued ingoing/outgoing null charged Vaidya solution. The headline claims are: (a) no overcharging from an initially *extremal* RN hole by a massless charged particle (a clean extension of Wald) and only a caveated "yes" for the non‑extremal hole; (b) no overcharging by a null charged shell while the stress–energy satisfies the weak energy condition (WEC); (c) in the glued Vaidya model an extremal apparent horizon can form in finite time, but it is not the outermost marginally trapped surface / is unobservable, so the third law "remains intact in spirit."

### What works well

1. **The massless‑particle observation is clean and correctly argued.** The single most useful physical point in the paper is that the $m^2 f(r)$ term that appears in $r'^2$ for a massive particle drops out for a massless one, which invalidates the direct transposition of the Hubeny‑type window analysis. The resulting no‑go for the *extremal* starting hole (Sec. II) is short, correct, and a legitimate extension of Wald's argument. Good result.

2. **The null‑shell WEC argument is a genuinely nicer formulation.** Using the transverse extrinsic‑curvature jump to extract the surface stress tensor (Eqs. 8–10) and deriving the WEC condition (11) that then *independently* contradicts the overcharging condition (12) is a solid way to do it, and the contrast with [5] (time‑like shell, equations of motion) is stated clearly. I think this is the most defensible technical contribution in the paper.

3. **Honesty about limitations.** The authors openly flag that massless charged particles are hypothetical, that the point‑particle notion is ill‑defined, and that their "positive" overcharging conclusion for the non‑extremal hole is expected to be altered by back‑reaction. I appreciate not overselling.

### Specific technical points / concerns

1. **Notational clash between $q$ and $g$ (Sec. II).** The particle's charge is written as $q$ in the Lorentz force (2) and in the overcharging condition $q+Q>E+M$, but the trajectory equations use $g$ in $(E-gQ/r)$, the critical radius $r_c=gQ/E$, and the future‑directedness bound (5) $E>gQ/r_+$. If $g=q$, this must be made uniform; if they are distinct, the relation between them must be stated. As written this is confusing and worth correcting.

2. **Back‑reaction is the load‑bearing wall and it is not built.** The whole "overcharging a *non‑extremal* RN hole may be possible" claim lives or dies on back‑reaction, which is exactly the mechanism that rescues CCH in the massive‑particle literature ([8],[9],[10] by Barausse–Cardoso–Khanna, etc.). The paper defers this to "future work." I would want at least a dimensional/parametric estimate of the gravitational *and* electromagnetic self‑force / radiated energy on the massless particle's trajectory before drawing the "seems possible" conclusion. Without it, that half of the result stays provisional.

3. **The gluing prescription in Sec. IV is asserted rather than derived.** Gluing ingoing to outgoing Vaidya exactly at $r=r_c$ (where the *single‑particle* trajectory turns around) via the "reflective" (first) matching condition is a modelling choice, not a consequence of the field equations. This is the crux of the third‑law analysis and the thinnest part of the construction.

Both are reasonable, but the second is doing real philosophical work and deserves a sharper statement about what the third law is *actually* claiming (finite steps vs finite advanced time; a local vs. globally‑observable horizon). The "in spirit" hedge should be replaced by a precise statement of which formulation of the third law survives.

5. **Case numbering (Sec. Given that the CCH/overspinning literature is dominated by the Kerr case, and given that the authors' motivating question is "does CCH hold," the restriction to RN is a real narrowing. This is a competent, honest, and technically careful paper on a genuinely important open problem. # Critique: "Massless charged particles: Cosmic censorship, and Third law of Black Hole Mechanics" (Fairos, Ghosh, Sarkar; arXiv:1709.05081v2)

## What the paper does

The paper tests the cosmic censorship hypothesis (CCH) and related aspects of black hole mechanics by considering three things: (i) a massless test particle falling into a Reissner–Nordström (RN) black hole of charge Q, where overcharging is investigated using a *modified* Lorentz-force equation of motion (following Booth [20] and the "bouncing surface" r_c = qQ/E); (ii) a charged null shell collapsing onto the black hole, where overcharging is ruled out from the shell's surface stress-tensor and the weak energy condition (WEC); and (iii) a glued charged Vaidya solution built from ingoing and outgoing Vaidya pieces (with the modified equation of motion) matched along a space-like surface B, analyzed for the possibility of extremizing the black hole in finite advance time (third-law issue).

The headline conclusions: overcharging from an exactly extremal state is impossible even for massless particles (an extension of Wald's argument); overcharging of a non-extremal black hole is impossible for massless particles (via the bouncing surface) and for null shells (via WEC); and extremization in finite time is possible in the glued-Vaidya setting only in fine-tuned circumstances, after which "the third law seems to remain intact in spirit."

## Strengths

1. **Clean extremal result (Sec. II).** The argument is correct and more compact than necessary to state: for an extremal hole (r_+ = Q = M), future-directedness of the trajectory just outside the horizon requires E > qQ/r_+ = q (eq. 5), whereas overcharging requires q + Q > M + E, i.e. q > E — a contradiction. The extension of Wald's massive-particle result to the massless case is rigorous and self-contained.

2. **The null shell result (Sec. III) is fully sound.** I checked the algebra: the WEC for the shell gives (2qQ + q²) ≥ 2ER, evaluated at the surface, and requiring the shell to reach the outer horizon gives 2qQ + q² − 2EM ≤ 2E√(M²−Q²) (eq. 11); overcharging Q + q > M + E implies 2qQ + q² − 2EM > E² + M² − Q² (eq. 12); together these force (√(M²−Q²) − E)² < 0. This is a standard Hubeny-type argument, but it is executed correctly, and doing it from the surface stress-energy tensor (rather than test-particle equations of motion) is the right method, as the authors note. This is the strongest part of the paper because it concerns the full matched solution, not test-particle bookkeeping.

3. **The glued charged Vaidya construction (Secs. IV–V) is a nontrivial piece of work.** Gluing ingoing/outgoing charged Vaidya with a space-like junction and tracking the two families of apparent horizons across cases (Ia/Ib/IIa/IIb, then extremization cases 1–3) is a genuinely original setup built on Booth [20]'s bouncing-surface prescription, and the case-by-case sign analysis, while long, is broadly the right way to address the question.

4. **Intellectual honesty.** The authors repeatedly flag caveats: back-reaction is left out, the massless charged particle does not exist in nature, the extremization in Case 3 is tied to m̄̇|_{v_0} > 0, and the "unphysical (excised) region" language is flagged as depending on [20]. This keeps the claims mostly within what the analysis supports — with one notable exception discussed below.

## Weaknesses

### 1. The non-extremal massless-particle result conflates test-particle termination with a CCH statement

This is the paper's main new particle result, and it is the weakest link. The argument in Sec. II (and Fig. 1) runs: for overcharging one needs (at minimum) E < q, which puts the bouncing surface r_c = qQ/E at r_c > Q; choosing the particle so that r_c < r_+ (i.e. keeping the overcharging-inequality window E > qQ/r_+ as well), the particle crosses the outer horizon and then, per the modified equation, "bounces" at r_c. The paper then concludes overcharging is not possible.

The problem: overcharging is a statement about the **final horizon parameters** (M + E, Q + q), not about the particle reaching the singularity. A particle that crosses r_+ and is then asymptotically trapped at r_c between r_c and r_+ has nonetheless been captured *inside the event horizon*; in a back-reacting spacetime its charge and energy must ultimately modify the outer horizon. What the fixed-background analysis actually shows is that the test-particle picture **breaks down** at a new, non-horizon surface where its own approximation is least trustworthy — it is a failure of the model, not a resolution of the problem. (Concretely, integrating dr/dλ = −(E − qQ/r) near r_c gives r − r_c ∝ e^{−(E/r_c)λ}: the particle hovers at r_c at finite advanced time but infinite affine parameter, a configuration that only survives because the background is not allowed to respond.) By contrast, the extremal case is robust precisely because the barrier sits at the horizon. The abstract's flat "overcharging is not possible [with massless particles]" oversells this: for the non-extremal setup the honest statement is "the fixed-background analysis terminates before it can decide; back-reaction is required." The body of the paper hedges ("further constraints ... are expected"), but the title's framing does not.

Related sloppiness: the overcharging condition is q + Q > M + E, and the paper repeatedly substitutes the one-directional implication "E < q" ("From the overcharging condition it follows that E < q ..."). The actual window is qQ/r_+ < E < q − (M − Q), which exists only for q < (M−Q)/(1 − Q/r_+). The sentence "we estimate r_+ > r_c" (p. 3) is not an estimate but a *choice* of the particle (equivalent to E > qQ/r_+), and the full window is never written down.

Also, Fig. 1 is misleading as drawn: the dotted trajectory is shown crossing back *out* through r_+ and escaping to infinity. In the maximally extended RN, once the turn-around point lies inside the outer horizon (r_- < r_c < r_+), r is timelike and no future-directed curve can exit the diamond through r_+; an outward exit from this surface is not causal. If the point is only that the particle never reaches the singularity, the figure should not depict it leaving the black hole.

### 2. The physical object is inconsistent, which should be stated more forcefully

Massless *charged* particles do not admit a consistent S-matrix description (soft/collinear infrared problems; the authors' own refs [16,18] point this way). The paper mentions this once, in passing. Since Sec. II is entirely about an object that cannot exist in any consistent quantum theory, its value is as an internal consistency check of the CCH argument, and the paper would benefit from saying so explicitly at the outset and scoping its claims accordingly.

### 3. The third-law section addresses the wrong criterion and then dissolves the tension rhetorically

- The paper proves/disproves extremization in **finite advance time** in a classical glued-Vaidya model, but the third law (in the Sullivan–Israel [21] formulation the paper itself adopts) is about reaching extremality in a **finite number of steps** in a dynamical process. The paper never actually formulates "steps" for its setup (it notes the lack of surface-gravity definition during the dynamical stage, then never returns to it properly). A continuous classical accretion process can in general make m(v) touch q(v) at finite time (this is essentially a measure-zero fine tuning of smooth m, q functions, and is possible in the *unmodified* Vaidya solution too) without any violation of the finite-steps version. So the "surprising" finding — that extremization in finite time is possible in special circumstances (Case 3) — is not much of a finding once one sees the criterion mismatch.
- The resolution, "the third law remains intact in spirit because the extremal horizon on B is never the outermost marginal trapped surface," is not established as a theorem; it is asserted in the discussion from the fact that the extremal horizon is transient (m̄̇|_{v_0} > 0 pushes it back to non-extremal). Transience is a reasonable physical argument, but the paper should say *why* this matters for the step-counting formulation rather than "in spirit."
- The entire Case I–III bookkeeping rests on accepting Booth [20]'s excision of the negative-energy-density region as "unphysical spacetime." That prescription is itself a modeling choice (a property of the charged null-fluid interpretation, not of the metric); a reader who rejects the excision gets a different classification of which cases are physical. The paper leans on this premise in all three extremization cases but never examines the dependence.

### 4. Overlap with concurrent/earlier work is under-acknowledged

Ref [29] (Jordá & Wald, arXiv:1707.05862, "Third law and cosmic censorship") addresses essentially the same third-law/CCH question from a general-theorem perspective and appears to have been concurrent; the paper mentions it in a sentence but does not position its glued-Vaidya results against it (e.g., how its "special circumstances" relate to the Jordá–Wald theorem linking the third law and weak CCH for charged matter). Similarly, the extremal massless test-particle result is close to what Kommemi [14] already obtained for massless test particles; the claim "This extends the result of [4] for the massless case" (where [4] is Wald, massive particles) undersells the prior art. The genuinely new ingredient of Sec. II — the modified equation of motion and the bouncing surface — should be what the paper foregrounds as its difference from both.

### 5. Presentation and derivational hygiene (Section V especially)

Section V has real notational and typographical problems that make several derivations hard to verify:

- Bars (outgoing quantities m̄, q̄), dots (derivatives ṁ, q̇) and tildes are overloaded and sometimes appear garbled. Eqs. (30)–(31): "x + 2q̄²/2m̄ = R > m̄ ⟹ x > 2m̄² − 2q̄² > 0" needs the definition of x stated cleanly (it is only implied from the RHS of (29)), and the final ">" chain silently assumes R > m̄ on B.
- Eq. (48): "∂²f/∂λ²|_{λ=λ₀} = (R̄/R̄³)(m̄q̄ − m̄q̇)/m̄̇ (dV/dλ)|..." — the term "(m̄q̄ − m̄ q̄)" with mixed bars/dots is not parseable as printed, and the sign conclusion "α > 0 for ṁ < 0" depends on precisely this term.
- In Case Ia the contradiction step says "B ceases to be space-like ... which contradicts our initial assumption" where the actual contradiction is that B would become *time*-like; the sentence as written ("B ceases to be space-like ... B ceases to be space-like, which contradicts...") appears mangled in the typeset text.
- Eq. (26)/(25) sign bookkeeping in Case Ia and Case IIa is plausible but written tersely enough that an independent check requires re-deriving half of Section VA; a referee or reader cannot easily audit which of the four cases each sign choice covers.
- Minor: "r_+ > r_+ > r_c" (typo, p. 3), "Case 1" vs "Case-IIa" numbering inconsistency in Sec. VB.

None of these is individually fatal, but they cumulatively undermine confidence in the case analysis, which is the paper's most novel technical content.

## What would strengthen the paper

1. State the massless non-extremal result as: "the fixed-background modified-Lorentz trajectory is incomplete inside the horizon; a full back-reaction treatment is required to decide overcharging," and do not present it as a resolution in the abstract.
2. Write down the explicit overcharging window for (q, E) and verify that Fig. 1's parameters lie in it.
3. Redraw Fig. 1 so the trapped/bouncing trajectory does not exit the black hole through r_+ from the timelike-r region.
4. Re-express the third-law discussion explicitly in step-counting language, or explicitly justify why finite-advance-time extremization in the glued model is the relevant classical surrogate (and compare to Jordá–Wald [29]).
5. A notation pass over Section V with one consistent convention (m vs ṁ vs m_out), plus a statement of the dependence of the Case I–III conclusions on accepting [20]'s excision prescription.

## Verdict

The paper is a competent, well-motivated piece of classical CCH analysis: the extremal massless-particle proof and the null-shell proof are correct, and the glued charged Vaidya third-law analysis is a real (if incremental) technical contribution. However, its headline massless-particle conclusion for non-extremal black holes rests on a fixed-background breakdown rather than a genuine dynamical obstruction, the third-law conclusion targets a surrogate criterion and is wrapped up rhetorically ("intact in spirit"), the physical premise (massless charged particles) is inconsistent and under-emphasized, and the most novel section is marred by derivational and notational sloppiness. A solid but uneven paper: publishable after substantive revision of the framing and of Section V's presentation.

## [1709.05081v2-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## The paper's stated contribution (in my own words)

Fairos, Ghosh and Sarkar attack the **cosmic censorship hypothesis (CCH)** and the **third law of black hole mechanics** by studying the infall of *massless* charged matter into a Reissner–Nordström (RN) black hole. Three parts:

1. **Massless single particle.** They compute the trajectory of a massless charged test particle in an RN background, including the electromagnetic back-reaction on the path (the modified Lorentz-force equation of Ori, ref. [20], which removes the regions where the charged Vaidya fluid would violate the weak energy condition). The key structural difference from the massive case (Wald [4], Hubeny [5]) is that the absence of the $m^2 f(r)$ term in $\dot r^2$ produces a turning point $r_c=qQ/E$ where the radial branch $\dot r=\pm(E-gQ/r)$ can be smoothly joined. They show: an *extremal* RN cannot be overcharged (a clean contradiction $q<E$ vs. $q+E>E+M$), but a *non-extremal* one **can** be, provided the particle's trajectory is kept causal (choosing $q$ and $E$ so $r_c<r_+$ and $E<q$).

2. **Null charged shell.** They show a spherically symmetric null charged shell cannot overcharge an RN hole, deriving this directly from the weak energy condition on the shell's transverse stress tensor (eqs. 11–12), contrasted with the equations-of-motion route used in [5]. Result: no overcharging.

3. **Third law in the Ori glued Vaidya.** In the modified charged Vaidya solution they do a case-by-case analysis (Cases 1/2/3, Ia/Ib) of apparent-horizon evolution and show that extremality *can* be reached in finite advanced time (seemingly violating the third law), but that at extremality $\ddot\alpha=\hat R\,\hat m\,(qq'-\dot m\dot q'>0$ — strictly positive — so the extremal horizon on the gluing surface $\mathcal B$ can never be an outermost marginally trapped surface. Hence the third law holds "in spirit."

## Is it novel? What is and isn't new

The paper sits squarely inside the well-worn **BH overcharging / CCH-counterexample program** that began with Wald (1974) and Hubeny (1999) and was later enriched by the self-force work of Barausse–Cardoso–Khanna [8], Colleoni–Barak [9,10], etc. Judging novelty against the cited prior work:

**What is genuinely new:**
- **The massless limit itself.** No cited work treats *massless* charged test particles. The trajectory has a branch-switching structure (critical point $r_c$) absent in the massive case, and this yields a result that *contradicts* the massive-case consensus: a non-extremal RN **can** be overcharged if back-reaction is neglected. That is a real, if narrow, new finding.
- **The third-law analysis on the Ori glued Vaidya with the $\ddot\alpha>0$ argument.** Tying the apparent-horizon evolution across the "Ori matching" hypersurface to a positivity statement on $\alpha$ at extremality is a specific, self-contained contribution not in [20], [21], or [27].

**What is not new:**
- **The overcharging program, method, and setup** are entirely the standard template: take Wald's [4] or Hubeny's [5] contradiction, perturb it. The extremal case is a verbatim extension of the standard $q<E$ vs. $q+E> M+E+Q$ contradiction — pure increment over [4]/[5].
- **The shell result (no overcharging)** is a *restatement* of the negative results already obtained in Hubeny [5], Tóth [18], and Natarjo–Quemada–Vicente [15]; the only novelty is the cleaner WEC-only derivation, not the outcome.
- **The massless overcharging "success" is self-disclaimed.** The authors concede (Discussion, Conclusions) it survives only by dropping back-reaction; once the self-force/stress-energy is included the conclusion is expected to revert. So it does not actually change the verdict on CCH.
- The **third-law discussion** explicitly borrows its machinery from Israel [27] and from the prior extremal-Vaidya work of Booth [26] and Creelman–Booth [25]; it is a careful application rather than a conceptual advance, and the authors note a highly relevant concurrent/follow-up paper [25] came out during review.

So the novelty is real but **incremental**: it adds one unphysical (massless) corner to an established impossibility program and one case-analysis of an existing third-law result, without overturning any cited conclusion. The one point it does not share with the literature (massless non-extremal overcharging) is the piece most likely to be closed off by the self-force analysis the authors themselves leave as future work, and the CCH/third-law conclusions they draw were independently and more fully settled by the 2017–2019 self-force literature (Barak, Dützert, Joracek–Wald [28,29]).

Overall: a sound, carefully argued, but narrow technical contribution to a niche problem; its "positive" result is provisional and its "negative" results largely replicate prior work.

## [1709.05081v2-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


# Review of "Massless charged particles: Cosmic censorship, and Third law of Black Hole Mechanics" (Fairos, Ghosh, Sarkar; arXiv:1709.05081v2)

## Part 1 — Methodology, at a reproducible level

**Setting and matter model.** The paper works in 4D Einstein-Maxwell theory with spherical symmetry only. Two matter models are studied: (i) a single massless charged test particle, and (ii) a spherical null charged shell (later a charged null Vaidya fluid). The central question throughout is whether a black hole with ADM mass M and charge Q can be driven to |Q| > M (naked singularity) or driven to extremality (Q = M) in finite advanced time.

**Experiment 1: test particle into a Reissner–Nordström (RN) hole (Sec. II).**
1. Background metric is exact RN, f(r) = 1 − 2M/r + Q²/r², electromagnetic potential A = −(Q/r)dt.
2. The particle has no rest mass but carries charge q, so its motion is *not* geodesic: the modified Lorentz force equation k^a ∇_a k^b = q E^b{}_c k^c (ref. [20], Ori's prescription) is integrated instead of the null-geodesic equation. This is the single most important modeling choice — without it the particle follows a null geodesic and the overcharging window identified in [5] for the massive case reopens.
3. Because ∂_t is a Killing vector, E = −(k_a + q A_a) ξ^a_∂t is conserved along the trajectory, giving k^t = (E − qQ/r)/f(r). The massless condition k^a k_a = 0 gives r'² = (E − qQ/r)², prime = d/dλ.
4. Causality (future direction) at the outer horizon r_+ requires E > gQ/r_+ (Eq. 5).
- **Extremal case:** overcharging needs q + Q > E + M (with Q = M). Combined with Eq. (5) this forces q < E, a contradiction — extremal RN cannot be overcharged. This is the main theorem, extending Poisson-Israel to the massless case.
- **Non-extremal case:** overcharging window is E < qQ/r_c with r_c = qQ/E; causality plus the requirement that the critical point r_c lie between r_− and r_+ and that the ingoing branch r' = −(E − qQ/r) be selected give the bound E < q. So: pick non-extremal (M, Q), pick q, then choose E satisfying q > E > qQ/r_+ with E < qQ/r_c. The trajectory crosses r_+, switches branch at r_c smoothly (k^a goes to zero there), and continues in-falling toward the region Q + q > M + E.

**Experiment 2: null charged shell (Sec. III).**
1. A spherically symmetric, non-radiating, future-directed null shell of energy E, charge q, collapsing into an RN exterior (mass M, charge Q). Shell at r = R(λ) with four-velocity U^a = (V', R', 0, 0), V' = 0, R = R⁺.
2. Interior and exterior are flat/RN matched at the shell (Israel junction). Transverse extrinsic curvatures K̂^a{}_b = g_ab n^a(∂_A∂_b l)^A are computed; the surface stress-energy t^ab = μ l^a l^b + P q^ab with P = 0 and μ = (l_− R̈ − f̄)/(4π R⁻f̄) is obtained from the discontinuity of transverse curvatures.
3. The weak energy condition (WEC) at the shell, evaluated on the timelike vector ∂_v, gives the surface energy (Eq. 10): E(r) = (R'/R)[(2qQ + q²)/R² − 2E/R] ≥ 0, which with R' < 0 (infall) yields 2E ≥ (2qQ + q²)/(M + √(M² − Q²)) — Eq. (11).
4. The overcharging condition is Q + q > M + E — Eq. (12). Using √(M²−Q²) ≥ 0, the required E ≤ M² − Q² − E² is incompatible with Eq. (11); the two inequalities contradict each other, so a WEC-obeying null shell cannot overcharge. (Contrast: [5] used timelike-shell equations of motion and missed this closure.)

**Experiment 3: glued Vaidya + third law (Secs. IV–V).**
1. The charged Vaidya fluid is taken to obey the same Ori-style modified Lorentz force equation. This is deliberate: with only geodesic motion, E<0 regions inside r_c = q̄(v)/m̄(v) make a timelike observer measure negative energy density (WEC violation) in the incoming Vaidya solution. Enforcing the Lorentz-force equation removes those regions, and the gluing hypersurface ℬ at r = r_c is then forced to be spacelike.
2. The glued solution: incoming Vaidya (M_, ds² = −ḟ(u)du² + 2dudr + r²dΩ²) and outgoing Vaidya (M̂) glued at ℬ: r = R(λ), v = V(λ), r = R̂(λ), u = U(λ). Metric continuity gives ĈR̂V² − 2R̂R̂'Ū² = R̂²Ū² (Eq. 21). The two matching branches are the "Reflective matching" (V' + Û' + f Ù' − Ù' = 0) and "Ori matching"; only reflective matching is analyzed, with the second ruled out by the spacelike-ℬ requirement and apparent-horizon coincidence arguments.
3. Extremal state is defined covariantly via expansions: θ_l = 0 and θ_n = 0, equivalent for the symmetric charged Vaidya to m(v) = |q(v)| [Booth, eq. 26]. The outer horizon is θ_l = 0, θ_n < 0; the inner horizon θ_l > 0, θ_n = 0.
4. Apparent-horizon tracking: the outer/inner apparent horizons of the incoming solution, r_±(λ), satisfy f(R(λ)) = 0; their evolution is controlled by dR/dλ|_{λ_0} = (R'/R₃)[2m̄(V)R̂ − 2|q(V)|V'(λ)] (Eq. 24). Five cases (Ia–IIIb) are enumerated by the relative locations of the apparent horizons, ℬ, and r_± at the initial point; each case is ruled possible or unphysical by the sign of f(R), the sign of dR/dλ, and the auxiliary relation dα/dλ = 2(m̈m − q̇q̄)(dV/dλ) with α = m̄² − q̄² (Eqs. 36–37).
5. Conclusion of Sec. V: extremization in the past of ℬ (Case 1, Fig. 6) is impossible; extremization to the future of ℬ produces a non-outermost extremal horizon in the excised region (unphysical); extremization **on ℬ itself** is possible provided ã|_{λ_0} ≥ 0, with apparent horizons either bouncing back to the physical region or evolving into it, depending on the sign of ∂²f/∂λ²|_{λ_0}. The third law — "zero surface gravity is approached only in infinitely many steps" — is thus argued to hold because the extremal horizon on ℬ is not the outermost marginally trapped surface; the authors note the tension that this leans on Israel's PRL 57, 397 (1986) proof, which already presumes some form of cosmic censorship.

## Part 2 — Assumptions the methodology papers over

1. **Spherical symmetry is global and never broken.** Charged null shell and Vaidya fluid are strictly spherically symmetric and non-radiating. No perturbative check (e.g., superradiance, ergoregion instabilities) is performed; CCH is a claim about *all* perturbations, and the paper tests only the perfectly symmetric sector.
2. **Test-particle decoupling (experiment 1).** The RN background is held fixed while a charged massless particle is dropped in. The particle's own stress-energy (which is generically non-spherical and non-stationary once the Lorentz force acts) is ignored, as are gravitational and electromagnetic self-force back reaction — the authors explicitly defer this to future work. The overcharging conclusion for non-extremal holes therefore rests on idealized point-particle kinematics.
3. **Massless charged particles exist.** As the authors themselves note, no such particles are known in nature; their existence is tied to unresolved infrared divergence issues in QFT (ref. [19], Lechner). The entire "experiment" is built on hypothetical matter.
4. **The Ori (2091) modified Lorentz-force prescription (ref. [20]) is the correct model** for charged null matter. There is no derivation, no comparison with alternative prescriptions, and no discussion of how sensitive the results are to it. The WEC-restoring argument for the Vaidya fluid depends entirely on this single modeling choice.
5. **Charge and mass add linearly after absorption (Q + q, M + E).** The paper quotes the overcharging bounds as if ADM parameters update by simple addition, but never verifies this from the stress-energy budget of the particle or shell (back reaction is explicitly excluded). It also assumes the charge of the hole and the charge of the test particle combine with no radiative loss of charge.
6. **WEC is the only energy condition used, and only along one timelike vector.** The shell and fluid analyses test the WEC at r = R using ∂_v only; stronger/other conditions that might close the window differently are never considered, and the WEC itself is assumed to hold for matter the EM field actually produces.
7. **The gluing hypersurface ℬ is spacelike, by fiat.** This choice drives the elimination of the "Ori matching" branch and the apparent-horizon bookkeeping. It is motivated (both Vaidya pieces must coexist in the same region — a claim borrowed from [24]) but not proven for the full two-sided solution.
8. **Reflective matching is assumed without a derivation of its physical necessity** in this setting; only its existence is shown by reference to [25].
9. **The "unphysical region" is excised a priori.** Much of the third-law argument works by showing the extremal horizon lands in, or its evolution exits, the unphysical region of the glued spacetime. Defining which slices are "unphysical," the excision procedure, and why this invalidates a candidate third-law violation are asserted, not derived.
13. In Case 3 (extremization on ℬ), the subcase m̄ < 0 (negative mass influx) is dismissed and the other subcase is dismissed on the grounds that "the instantaneously extremal horizon is never observable by an asymptotic observer" — i.e., observational observability is silently imported as a mathematical criterion for physicality.
11. **Third law is read as a statement about the outermost marginally trapped surface**, and the authors flag (fairly late, in the Discussion) that their rescue relies on Israel [27]'s original proof, which "already assumes some form of cosmic censorship" — a circularity the paper does not resolve.
12. **No role for radiative degrees of freedom.** In 4D, spherical symmetry forbids gravitational radiation, so the entire analysis excludes the dynamical channels (gravitational wave extraction, quasinormal ringing) that would be present for any genuinely non-spherical infall. The "finite time" question is answered only in the symmetry sector.
13. **No quantification of stability.** Nothing is shown about whether the near-extremal configurations or the extremal horizon on ℬ are stable; the third-law statement is qualitative ("in certain special circumstances … the third law seems to remain intact in spirit").
14. **The Vaidya mass/charge functions m(v), q(v) are never tied to an explicit energy flux.** They enter as arbitrary monotone functions satisfying only m(v_0) = q(v_0); the extremization analysis therefore shows existence for *some* flux rather than a robust property, and the condition ã|_{λ_0} ≥ 0 is a condition on the flux choice, not on dynamics.

## Assessment

The work is an exercise in checking one highly idealized matter sector (spherically symmetric charged null matter with a specific Lorentz-force prescription) against two cornerstones of black-hole mechanics. The main theorem (extremal RN cannot be overcharged by a massless charged particle once the Lorentz force is included) is clean and correctly extends the massive-case result; the shell contradiction (Eqs. 11–12) is a genuinely useful tightening of [5]. But the methodology is assumption-heavy in precisely the places that matter: the existence of the matter itself, the specific equation of motion, the absence of back reaction, global spherical symmetry, and the excision/observability conventions that rescue the third law. The third-law section is the weakest: it is more a careful case taxonomy of a toy glued Vaidya model (with arbitrary flux functions and an unphysical-region excision that is asserted) than a theorem about black holes, and the authors themselves note its dependence on a proof that already assumes censorship. As a probe it is sound; as evidence about CCH or the third law in general it is narrow, and the paper does not clearly separate what is proven within the model from what is claimed about physics generally.

Reproducibility, to close: all experiments are analytic. To reproduce Eq. (5)'s bound, integrate the two first-order ODEs for k^t and r along the two branches of Eq. (4) with constants (M, Q, q, E) in the stated inequality window; to reproduce the shell result, compute the transverse-extrinsic-curvature jump for the metric of Eqs. (6)/(13) and impose Eqs. (10)–(12); to reproduce the third-law case analysis, use Eqs. (21)–(24) and (36)–(37) with monotone m(v), q(v) and the flux-derivative sign conditions of Cases 1–3.

I've read the full paper (Fairozs, Ghosh & Sarkar, "Massless charged particles: Cosmic censorship, and Third law of Black Hole Mechanics," arXiv:1709.05081v2). Here is my evaluation, focused on the logical argument.

## My understanding of the paper's logical argument

**Motivation.** The weak cosmic censorship hypothesis (WCC/CCH) says that singularities formed in gravitational collapse remain hidden behind event horizons. Both the *second law* of black hole mechanics (area theorem) and the *third law* (no finite process reaches extremality) rest on an assumption that regular initial data cannot be turned into a naked singularity or an exactly zero-surface-gravity horizon by a physical process. The standard way to probe these claims is to try to *overcharge* (or overspin) a Reissner–Nordström (RN) black hole by throwing in matter. Earlier work (Wald; Poisson–Israel; Hubeny; Saha/Ilicz, etc.) uses *massive* test particles or *timelike* shells.

**The paper's move.** Replace that matter with *massless charged* objects — a single charged null particle, and a spherically symmetric charged null shell — because a massless particle has no tunable rest mass, so the constraints found in the massive case should tighten and the conclusions differ. It insists (correctly, following Ori) that a charged massless particle does *not* follow a null geodesic but a modified Lorentz-force trajectory, precisely so that the associated charged Vaidya solution satisfies the weak energy condition (WEC) where a timelike observer could otherwise live.

**Claim A — single massless particle, extremal seed.** In the RN background the conserved quantity is $E=(k_a+qA_a)\xi^a$, giving $k^t=(E-gQ/r)/f(r)$ and the null condition $r'^2=(E-gQ/r)^2$ with two branches. Future-directedness across the outer horizon requires $E>gQ/r_+$. For an *extremal* seed ($|Q|=M$, $r_+=Q$) the overcharging condition $q+Q>E+M$ forces $q<E$, contradicting $E>gQ/r_+$, so **you cannot overcharge an initially extremal RN hole** with a null charged particle (this is the massless analogue of Wald). For a *non-extremal* seed, the critical point $r_c=qQ/E$ must lie inside the outer horizon ($r_c<Q$); choosing $q$ so that $E>gQ/r_c$ and $E<q$ makes *extremization reachable* (Fig. 1), but jumping all the way to an overcharged state would be left to back-reaction, which the authors defer.

**Claim B — null charged shell.** Rather than using a timelike shell's equations of motion (as in [8]), the paper uses Israel junction conditions on a non-radiating null shell. The shell's surface stress tensor (transverse extrinsic curvatures) yields $\\mathcal E(r)$. Demanding the WEC at $R=r_+$ gives $2qQ+q^2-2EM\le 2E\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}$, while overcharging $Q+q>M+E$ implies $2qQ+q^2-2EM>E^2+M^2-Q^2$. Since $(\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}-E)^2\ge0\Rightarrow M^2-Q^2+E^2\ge2E\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}$, the two inequalities are mutually exclusive, so **a WEC-respecting null shell cannot overcharge.** The result is cleaner than the timelike case because it never invokes a timelike EOM.

**Claim C — third law in the modified charged Vaidya.** Gluing an ingoing and an outgoing charged Vaidya (reflective matching on the space-like surface $\mathcal B$ at $r=r_c$) gives the full spacetime. The authors define an extremal apparent horizon by $\theta_l=\theta_n=0$ ($m(v)=|q(v)|$), track the two apparent horizons $r_\pm$ of the ingoing piece across $\mathcal B$, and do an exhaustive case split (Ia/Ib/IIa/IIB) on whether they lie in the future or past of $\mathcal B$ and which can cross it while $\mathcal B$ stays space-like. The derivative test $\partial_\lambda f|_{\mathcal B}\propto \dot R(2m\dot R\dot V-2|q|\dot V^2)$ shows that an apparent horizon in the *unphysical* (excised) region can never evolve into the physical region during outward evolution (the reverse is possible). With $\alpha=\dot m^2-\hat q^2$, the extremization analysis gives: extremization in the past of $\mathcal B$ impossible; in the future possible but unphysical; *on* $\mathcal B$ possible under $\ddot\alpha_{v_0}\ge0$. The bottom line: a momentarily extremal horizon can form on $\mathcal B$, but because it sits on the artificial matching surface it is not the outermost marginally trapped surface, so "the third law seems to remain intact in spirit."

## Feedback on the logical argument

**What is done well.**

- The framing is honest. The authors openly concede that massless charged particles are not known in nature and that their existence is tied to the Coleman–Glashow divergence in QFT; they do not overclaim physical reality. That candour keeps the claims calibrated.
- Forcing the *modified* Lorentz-force trajectory rather than a null geodesic is the right call and correctly motivates the WEC-consistent charged Vaidya of Ori. This is the piece many earlier overcharging studies skip, and treating it here is a genuine improvement.
- The claim B proof is the logically cleanest in the paper: a straightforward sign contradiction between the WEC inequality and the overcharging inequality, with the $(\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}-E)^2\ge0$ step doing the work. No hidden assumptions.
- The apparent-horizon bookkeeping in §V A is patient and the case split is exhaustive relative to the stated assumptions. The "dR/dλ at the crossing + space-likeness of B" test is a legitimate local argument.

**Where the logic is weaker or needs strengthening.**

1. **The third-law conclusion is hedged rather than proven.** "The third law seems to remain intact in spirit" is a verdict, not a proof of no-extremization. The paper shows an extremal apparent horizon *can* form on $\mathcal B$ in finite time, and saves the law by saying that horizon is not the *outermost* marginally trapped surface. But it never cleanly identifies what the outermost marginally trapped surface is in those same configurations and shows its surface gravity stays strictly positive. As it stands, the reader is asked to accept "in spirit." A closing argument that the outermost MTS carries $\kappa>0$ throughout would upgrade this from assertion to result.

2. **Excising the region where the (unmodified) Vaidya violates WEC is a modeling choice doing a lot of work.** The "Case 2 is unphysical" and several of the §V A conclusions all lean on discarding the unphysical region of the glued Vaidya. This is defensible (no timelike observer can sit there), but it is precisely the region where an extremal horizon forms in some cases, so the move can look as though the conclusion is protected by construction rather than by dynamics. The paper should state more sharply *why* excision is the physical prescription rather than merely the convenient one.

3. **Back-reaction is deferred throughout, which caps the "non-extremal extremization possible" result.** For the single-particle case the authors argue (via [19]) that there are no EM radiative or conservative self-force terms, so only gravitational back-reaction acts as a censor — but they do not compute it. So "extremization possible" for a non-extremal seed is a test-matter result that could be overturned by the very effect (gravitational self-reaction/cosmic-censor) that they invoke to keep it CCH-safe. The logical status of that headline should be flagged more explicitly as conditional.

4. **The reflective-vs-Ori matching is selected for convenience and could be questioned.** They reject Ori matching because it would make $\mathcal B$ null, contradicting the space-like assumption. This is fine, but it means the whole §V conclusion is relative to one matching prescription; a sentence on whether the "extremal horizon on B" artefact survives the other matching (they say they won't consider it, since it forces B null) would strengthen the claim that the artefact is robust and not a byproduct of the reflective choice.

5. **Notation/typographical roughness (minor).** A lowercase symbol that reads as both "g" and "q" appears in the $r'$ formulas and $r_c$ (e.g., $E>gQ/r_+$ vs $r_c=qQ/E$); whether this is a font/OCR artefact or an actual $q/g$ inconsistency it should be checked and kept uniform, since the extremization thresholds hinge on exactly these quantities.

**Overall assessment of the argument.** The logical spine — "use massless charged matter with a WEC-consistent modified trajectory, then show overcharging fails for extremal seeds and shell, and that the third law survives in the glued Vaidya" — is coherent, internally consistent, and well-motivated, and it extends a real (if crowded) research line in a non-trivial way (massless particle/shell; third law in the Ori Vaidya). The main reservations are that two of its positive-sounding results ("extremization possible"; "third law intact") rest on (i) explicitly deferred back-reaction and (ii) a "in spirit / excised region" caveat rather than a direct $\kappa>0$ proof, and that the physical relevance is inherently limited by the hypothetical matter.

## [1709.05081v2-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Review of "Massless charged particles: Cosmic censorship, and Third law of Black Hole Mechanics" (arXiv:1709.05081v2)

## Summary

The paper investigates two questions with (massless) charged null test matter: (i) whether a Reissner–Nordström (RN) black hole can be overcharged — testing the weak cosmic censorship hypothesis (CCH) — using first a pointlike null particle on the modified Lorentz-force trajectory of eq. (2) (Sec. II) and then a null charged shell (Sec. III); and (ii) whether the third law of black hole mechanics survives in a charged Vaidya spacetime in which an ingoing and an outgoing piece are glued along a space-like hypersurface 𝓑 to excise the region where the charged-Vaidya fluid violates the weak energy condition (Secs. IV–V). The main results are: an extremal hole cannot be overcharged by a null particle; a non-extremal hole apparently can; a WEC-respecting shell cannot overcharge; and, in the glued Vaidya model, the black hole "can become extremal in a finite time, in certain special circumstances," while "the third law seems to remain intact in spirit."

The paper is technically careful — the shell contradiction (eqs. 11–12 and the $(\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}-E)^2\ge 0$ termination) is clean algebra, and the apparent-horizon case analysis of Sec. V.A is systematic. It is also honest about its test-particle limitations ("expected to change" once back reaction is included). However, the load-bearing step of the paper's second half does not survive scrutiny, and that determines the assessment below.

## The single weakest link

**The step from the result of Sec. V to its conclusion — the claim that "the third law seems to remain intact in spirit" (Sec. V end / Sec. VI / Sec. VII) — is not entailed by the paper's own data; the construction demonstrated in Case 3 is, within the paper's own assumptions, a counterexample to the law as the authors themselves formulate it.**

Exactly where the entailment breaks:

1. The third law the authors adopt (the formulation of ref. [1], restated on p. 5) says that the state of zero surface gravity *cannot be attained in a finite number of steps*. The derivation of Sec. V does not defend this statement; it shows the opposite holds *in the model under construction*. Case 3 finds a glued charged-Vaidya solution for which $m(v_0)=|q(v_0)|$ at a finite advanced time $v=v_0$, i.e. $\kappa(v_0)=0$ attained at one finite step. The only reason the authors classify this as compatible with the law is that the extremization occurs *exactly on the gluing hypersurface 𝓑* — the "certain special circumstances" of the abstract.

2. Reaching the extremal point on 𝓑 requires nothing more than (a) $\alpha\equiv m^2-q^2>0$ for $v<v_0$, with an exactly tuned profile approaching $\alpha(v_0)=0$ (eqs. 44–45), and (b) the outgoing piece being an extremal outgoing Vaidya ($m(U)=q(U)$ for $U\ge v_0$), which makes $\dot m,\dot q$ discontinuous — a kink — precisely at $v_0$. No condition the paper checks rules this out:
   - The matching condition (23) degrades at the double root, where $f|_{\mathcal B}=0$, to $2\dot R(\dot V+\dot U)=0$, satisfied automatically by the reflective matching $\dot V+\dot U=0$; it constrains the slope of 𝓑, not $\dot m(v_0)$ or $\dot q(v_0)$. The condition (47) relating $\dot R$ to the flux derivatives and $\dot V$ is a single algebraic constraint on an otherwise free profile choice — it restricts, but does not exclude, kinked fluxes.
   - The WEC is enforced only on the ingoing flux for $v<v_0$; at $v_0$ it is never tested, because that is where the flux is deliberately re-routed into the outgoing extremal piece.
   - The original blocking mechanism of the [21] proof — an inequality from the energy condition at the would-be extremal instant — is *absent by construction*, because the region where the flux violates the WEC (the interior $r<r_c$ where a time-like observer measures negative energy, as the authors themselves derive in Sec. IV) has been excised. The very excision that makes the model exist is what removes the only ingredient that obstructs finite-step extremization. The paper then declares the law "intact in spirit," but offers no argument — no energy-condition exclusion of kinked profiles, no smoothness/step-counting argument, no measure-zero claim — for why extremization at the junction should not count as a legitimate finite step. The word "seems" marks precisely where the derivation ends.

3. This defect is compounded by the gluing itself, which is the physical grounding of the entire Sec. IV–V analysis. 𝓑 is a space-like junction across which the flux profile kinks; generically the second fundamental form of 𝓑 jumps, and by the junction conditions the glued spacetime is sourced by the Vaidya flux **plus an unanalyzed surface stress layer on 𝓑**. The paper does not compute this surface stress, nor check it against any energy condition, yet presents the glued region as "the full physical spacetime" and derives the third-law verdict from it. The third law is a statement about solutions of the field equations with physical stress-energy; the object it is evaluated on here is a patching of two exact solutions with a free, unconstrained, energy-conditions-unchecked surface matter component. Properties of that patch (extremization at finite $v$) are therefore not the property of any solution the model claims to represent.

In short: the data (the case analysis) entail "*the glued model admits extremization at one finite step*". The conclusion states "*the third law holds*". The bridge between them — that extremization at the artificial seam is not a genuine violation — is asserted, not derived, and is in direct tension with the derivation. This is the step where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data. (For completeness: if kinked profiles and the surface layer are admitted, the model violates the law; if they are to be excluded, that exclusion principle must be stated and justified, and it is absent.)

## Secondary defect (noted for context; not load-bearing)

In Sec. II the sentence "At this point [$r_c=gQ/E$, where $k^a=0$], it is possible to extend the curve into the future and smoothly join to either of the two branches. However... causality implies that only one branch is preferred" is mathematically empty as written. At $r_c$ the four-momentum $k^a=\dot x^a$ vanishes: the vector is neither future- nor past-directed, so "causality prefers one branch" has no content, and a massless particle with zero four-momentum is not a particle. More concretely, eq. (4) gives the ordinary differential equation $dr/d\lambda=\pm(E-gQ/r)$, whose right-hand side is $C^1$ for $r>0$ with a rest point at $r_c$; by uniqueness, no trajectory reaches $r_c$ at finite affine parameter (approach is exponential in $\lambda\to+\infty$), and the only solution occupying $r_c$ is the constant one. The dotted trajectory of Fig. 1, which passes through $r_c$ and re-emerges on the other branch, is thus not a solution of the equation of motion (2). Crucially, this step is *not* what carries the overcharging conclusion — the future-directed crossing of $r_+$ on the infalling branch (guaranteed by $E>gQ/r_+$, eq. (5)) already transfers $(E,q)$ through the horizon, so the non-extremal CCH result survives without it — but it is the upstream source of the model's pathology: the negative-energy region that forces the excision in Sec. IV is precisely the interior region where the null-particle dynamics degenerates in this way. The authors' own hedge that the curve is "discontinuous" understates the point; the trajectory is not a curve of the dynamics at all.

## Verdict

The paper delivers one robust result — the shell argument (Sec. III) and the extremal-hole overcharging impossibility (Sec. II) are sound extensions of Poisson–Israel-type reasoning to null charged matter — but its headline second-half claim, that the third law survives the glued charged-Vaidya model, fails at the exact step where the model has *demonstrated* a finite-step extremization and the authors simply re-label the result as "intact in spirit." The conclusion is unsupported by the derivation and rests on a patching procedure (excision of the WEC-violating interior + space-like gluing with an uncomputed surface stress) whose freedom is silently exploited by the very counterexample it produces. The argument should either (i) exhibit the condition that excludes junction-kink extremalization (an energy condition on the surface layer, or a $C^1$ flux regularity requirement shown to be incompatible with (44)–(47)), or (ii) concede that, within the stated assumptions, the third law *fails* in this model — in which case the paper's third-law conclusion must be inverted. Neither is done.

**Recommendation:** major revision; the Sec. V–VI claim is currently overclaimed relative to the analysis.

## [1709.05081v2-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


I've read the full paper (Fairoos, Ghosh, Sarkar, "Massless charged particles: Cosmic censorship, and Third law of Black Hole Mechanics", IIT Gandhinagar, 12 pp). ## 1. The central framing problem

The paper's headline — testing cosmic censorship (CCH) and the third law with massless charged matter — is far bigger than its method supports. The overcharging calculation (§II) is a **zeroth-order test-particle computation on a fixed RN background**, with back reaction explicitly *not* computed. # Peer Review: "Massless charged particles: Cosmic censorship, and Third law of Black Hole Mechanics" (Fairos, Ghosh, Sarkar; arXiv:1709.05081v2)

## Summary

The paper investigates two questions using the Reissner-Nordström (RN) black hole background. First, whether an infalling **massless** charged particle (or a charged null shell) can over-charge a near-extreme RN black hole, by solving the modified Lorentz-force equation of motion and using weak energy condition (WEC) arguments for the shell. Second, whether the **third law of black-hole mechanics** is violated in the glued Vaidya construction of Booth [20] when one uses the null geodesic / Lorentz-force trajectory rather than the geodesic.

The main claims are: (i) an initially extremal RN cannot be over-charged by a single massless charged particle (Section II); (ii) an initially non-extremal RN *can* be brought arbitrarily close to extremal via a massless charged particle when back-reaction is ignored, with the particle remaining causal up to a critical radius $r_c$; (iii) a spherically symmetric null charged shell satisfying the WEC cannot over-charge a black hole (Section III); (iv) when the third law is examined in the glued charged-Vaidya construction with the proper equation of motion, the law appears to be "violated in letter" (an extremal apparent horizon can appear in finite proper time of the gluing parameter) but only at an unphysical, non-outmost apparent horizon, so the law is "intact in spirit" (Sections IV–V).

## Major Strengths

1. **Physically motivated choice of massless particles.** The authors correctly observe that the rest-mass term $m^2f(r)$ is the one that Hubeny's no-overcharging proof for massive particles depends upon, so it is a natural question to remove it. Massless charged particles (e.g., neutrinos in the massless limit, photons in the standard Maxwell–Einstein theory) are the only fundamental charged particles for which such a limit is consistent. The resulting change in the radial equation (Eq. 4) is real, not a mere specialisation: both branches $r'=\pm(E-qQ/r)/f(r)$ now have to be considered, and the "causal branch" selection at $r=r_c$ is a genuinely new analysis relative to the massive case.

2. **Equation of motion is treated consistently.** Rather than treating the charged massless test particle as following a null geodesic (which, as the authors rightly point out, would over-charge any non-extremal RN with $E>q$), they include the Lorentz-force deviation and use the resulting modified trajectory. This is the minimal physically consistent treatment and is the correct starting point for the CCH discussion.

3. **The null-shell proof is clean.** Section III derives the shell's surface energy (Eq. 10) from the transverse extrinsic curvatures, and shows that WEC + the over-charging inequality give the contradiction (Eqs. 11–12). This is genuinely stronger than the massive-particle counter-argument in [5], which the authors correctly identify as relying on time-like-motion equations; here the WEC alone suffices.

4. **The third-law section is the most substantive contribution.** The treatment of the glued Vaidya construction with the *modified* equation of motion (not the geodesic one used in [21]) and the careful case analysis (IA, IB, IIA, IIB) to track the apparent horizons across the gluing hypersurface $\mathcal B$ is detailed and, as far as I can follow, correct. The final conclusion — that an extremal apparent horizon can form in finite "steps" but is unphysical because it is not the outermost marginally trapped surface / is hidden by an asymptotic observer — is the right conclusion, and the argument for it (Figures 6–9) is laid out in enough detail to be verifiable.

5. **Clear, well-structured presentation.** The paper is short (12 pp.), the figures are informative, and each section is self-contained. The discussion of reflectively vs. Ori matching condition is appropriately flagged.

## Major Weaknesses

1. **The physical relevance of the model is under-examined.** A massless charged particle is not a known fundamental particle (the photon is charged under $U(1)$ gauge symmetry but is uncharged in the standard sense, and a charged massless boson would need the Higgs mass term to vanish, which is not the case for any Standard Model particle). The authors acknowledge this only briefly in the introduction. For a prestigious-journal submission, a paragraph on how these results translate to *nearby* massive cases (i.e., take $m\to 0$ limit and ask whether $m/\sqrt{E^2-m^2}$ corrections can change the bound) would strengthen the paper and remove the "unphysical model" criticism.

2. **The "over-charging" of a non-extremal RN by a single massless particle (Section II, last paragraph of p. 3) is a real gap.** The authors show that the particle's trajectory crosses $r=r_c< r_-$ and that at $r=r_c$, $k^a$ is zero — so the particle "stops" at a finite radius outside the horizon. But they then write that the original non-extremal black hole "jumps to an overcharged one while avoiding the extremal stage." This jump is not physically well-defined without back-reaction, and the authors defer back-reaction to future work. The statement as written (without back-reaction) is a statement about a *test particle near a fixed background*, so the conclusion "the BH jumps to an overcharged state" overstates what is shown — really only the test-particle trajectory is computed, and the background is assumed static throughout. This deserves a more explicit disclaimer.

3. **The third-law analysis is hard to follow and the final verdict is qualitative.** The case-by-case analysis in Section V.A is a long chain of sign arguments that, while I find correct on first reading, would benefit from a summary table at the end of the section. The final verdict — "third law intact in spirit" — leans on the remark (p. 11) that Israel's proof [27] "already assumes some form of cosmic censorship." This is a well-known observation but deserves more development: if one is trying to *test* CCH and the third law, relying on a theorem whose proof already uses a form of censorship is a somewhat circular starting point. The authors should be more explicit about which *independent* ingredient (WEC on the shell? the modified trajectory?) is doing the work.

4. **Comparison with subsequent and near-contemporaneous work is incomplete.** The discussion cites [11] (Zimmerman et al. 2013) on back-reaction for massive particles and [9,10] on dissipative/conservative effects for Kerr, but the paper does not address what happens when one *includes* the electromagnetic back-reaction of a massless charged particle. As the authors themselves note in the discussion, for massless charged particles the "disipative part of the gravitational back-reaction" [11] does not apply, and neither do radiative effects [19]. So the back-reaction analysis for massless particles is qualitatively different from the massive case, and the paper would be much stronger with even a brief sketch of what *would* apply.

5. **No numerical support for the claims in Section II or V.** All conclusions are analytic, which is appropriate, but a single numerical illustration of the particle trajectory for a benchmark set of (M, Q, E, q) would make the "particle stops at $r_c$" statement more concrete and would help readers verify the sign arguments.

## Minor Points

- Eq. (14) calls $M^{ab}=\frac1{16\pi r^2}(m'-q\tilde q/r)\delta_r^a\delta_r^b$ the "matter" stress tensor, but it really is the fluid stress energy; the electromagnetic part $E^{ab}=\rho\, k^a k^b$ is separate. The notation is fine but the sentence "This is the electromagnetic part..." in Section IV (p. 4) attaches $E^{ab}$ to the Lorentz-force interpretation without clearly separating the two contributions to $T^{ab}$; a reader may confuse the "E in $E^{ab}$" (energy density) with the particle energy $E$.

- Equation (16) writes the modified equation of motion in the same form as Eq. (2), but (2) is for the trajectory and (16) is being invoked *after* the assumption that the particle is on a null trajectory. The logical order — "the particle must follow the modified eq. of motion, and this implies a deviation from an affinely-parameterized null geodesic" — is correct but the transition in the text is compressed.

- Figure 1 (p. 3) uses both dotted and dashed lines with no in-figure legend distinguishing them; the caption refers to "the dotted line" without defining what the dashed line represents.

- The acknowledgements (Section VIII) list grant numbers, which is appropriate for a journal version.

- The reference list is thorough but does not include the 2010s literature on CCH using the first law with back-reaction (e.g., the recent lines of work by Chatterjee, Ganguli, Virmani [24] is cited in reference but only for the gluing condition; their CCH analysis is adjacent).

## Verdict for a Prestigious Journal

The paper is **solid, correct, and complete** for the questions it sets out to answer. The massless-particle result and the null-shell WEC proof are clean. The third-law section is the most novel contribution but is qualitative in its final conclusion and would benefit from a stronger discussion of circularity in its assumptions.

The paper would be **acceptable for CQG, PRD, or J. Phys. G** as is. For a truly prestigious venue (e.g., *Communications in Mathematical Physics*, *Classical and Quantum Gravity*'s top tier, or an analogous high-impact outlet), the paper as written is **borderline**: the physical model is somewhat contrived (massless charged particles are not in nature), the "over-charging of a non-extremal BH" step relies on ignoring back-reaction while simultaneously asserting a state-jump, and the third-law conclusion is stated in "in spirit" language rather than as a theorem. With the two major fixes — (a) an explicit back-reaction discussion, and (b) a sharper statement distinguishing the *independent* CCH assumptions from the circularity in Israel's proof — the paper could clear the bar for a top-tier outlet.
