## [1311.0747v2-general-review] General Review


# Review of "Hexagonal flow $v_6$ as a superposition of elliptic $v_2$ and triangular $v_3$ flows" (arXiv:1311.0747v2)

## Summary of the argument

The paper studies the hexagonal flow $v_6$ in central Pb+Pb collisions at $\sqrt{s}=2.76$ TeV using the HYDJET++ model. The central claim is that $v_6$ is not a "primitive" flow but a nonlinear consequence of the interplay between $v_2$ and $v_3$. Working in a single‑event‑plane saddle‑point approximation (Eqs. 6–13), the authors derive the high‑$p_T$ limits $v_6^{(1/6)}/v_2^{(1/2)}|_{\Psi_2}\simeq(1/6)^{1/6}\approx0.74$ and $v_6^{(1/6)}/v_3^{(1/3)}|_{\Psi_3}\simeq(1/2)^{1/6}\approx0.89$. They then compare the two‑plane projected moments $v_6^{1/6}(\Psi_2)/v_2^{1/2}(\Psi_2)$ and $v_6^{1/6}(\Psi_3)/v_3^{1/3}(\Psi_3)$ (Figs. 3–4) to the scaling of CMS/ATLAS data, argue that the $\Psi_3$ ratio falls below the ideal curve (hence v₃ plane is not "pure"), and reproduce the centrality trends of the ATLAS two‑plane correlators $\langle\cos(6\Psi_2-\Psi_6)\rangle$ and $\langle\cos(6\Psi_3-\Psi_6)\rangle$ (Figs. 6–7). The conclusion (Sec. V) is that $v_6$ is essentially $(1/6)v_2^3+(1/2)v_2v_3^2$ plus a small jet contribution, with no significant "genuine hexagonal" source.

## Strengths

- The two‑plane ratio observables $v_6^{1/6}(\Psi_2)/v_2^{1/2}(\Psi_2)$ and $v_6^{1/6}(\Psi_3)/v_3^{1/3}(\Psi_3)$ are a clean phenomenological test: scaling in one plane but not the other is a direct, model‑independent discriminant between v₂‑driven and v₃‑driven hexagonal flow.
- The centrality‑dependent predictions for $\langle \cos(6\Psi_2-\Psi_6)\rangle$ and $\langle \cos(6\Psi_3-\Psi_6)\rangle$ are explicitly contrasted with ATLAS, and the predicted crossing (the Ψ₆ plane migrates from the Ψ₃ side in central to the Ψ₂ side in peripheral) is a qualitatively testable and physically sensible picture.
- The authors are honest about the role of jets: adding PYTHEN hard scattering raises the Ψ₂‑plane ratio by ~10% and changes the slope of the tail — a quantitative contribution beyond pure‑hydro studies.
- The paper is short, internally consistent, and the derivation chain (Eqs. 1–15) is easy to follow.

## Critiques / weaknesses

**1. The single‑event‑plane derivation is in tension with the paper's motivating physics.** The whole discussion justifying separate Ψ₂ and Ψ₃ planes (and the comparison to ATLAS sub‑event data) relies on *multi*‑plane, event‑by‑event picture, yet Eqs. 12–13 (the headline result $v_6 = \frac{1}{6}v_2^3+\frac12 v_2v_3^2$) is derived assuming a *single* plane and the vanishing of $aV_6$. The authors acknowledge this at the bottom of Sec. III ("Note again that this result was obtained under the assumption of a single event plane…"). However, the paper then applies the same closed‑form scaling ratios $(1/6)^{1/6}$ and $(1/2)^{1/6}$ as if they were the correct reference lines in the multi‑plane world, where the proper non‑linear‑response coefficients are different because $v_6$ receives contributions from $v_4$, $v_1$–$v_3$ interference, and higher modes. A cleaner presentation would label them as "single‑plane heuristic reference lines" rather than "predictions of ideal hydro" and quote the multi‑plane expected values at least in an appendix.

**2. The closed form is a textbook consequence, not an original result.** Multiplying out $\exp(a\sum v_n \cos n\phi)$ (Eq. 8) and reading off the $n=2$ and $n=3$ contributions to $v_4$ and $v_6$ is a straightforward Fourier moment expansion. The combination $v_6 \sim \tfrac16 v_2^3 + \tfrac12 v_2 v_3^2$ is what one gets on the first line of the calculation whenever a single‑plane ansatz is used — it has been discussed in various forms in the ALICE/CMS non‑linear‑response literature. The genuinely new element of the paper is not the formula but its *experimental packaging*: the two‑plane ratio observables and, above all, the centrality crossing of $\Psi_6$ between the $\Psi_2$ and $\Psi_3$ planes. The Introduction and the title should foreground this; as written, the title "Hexagonal flow $v_6$ as a superposition of elliptic $v_2$ and triangular $v_3$ flows" makes it sound like the decomposition itself is the main claim.

**3. The "ideal‑hydro" reference curves are not the most appropriate comparison objects.** Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 compare HYDJET++ results (and data) against a constant dashed line — the single‑plane high‑$p_T$ limit. In practice, both data and model curves *approach* but do not saturate that line, and the distance from the line is itself the interesting quantity. It would be more informative to (a) plot the *deviation* from the ideal line as a function of $p_T$ rather than the raw ratio, and (b) compare with state‑of‑the‑art eby‑e viscous hydro (e.g. MUSIC+SMASH or similar) which gives a multi‑plane $v_6^{1/6}/v_2^{1/2}$ that is itself $p_T$‑dependent. As drawn, Fig. 3(b) shows Hydro+Jets *overshooting* the central‑Pb+Pb data at $p_T\gtrsim 3$ GeV/c by ~10%; the caption says "good accuracy is already at $p_T=1$ GeV/c" but at 5 GeV/c the model is off by a visible amount. Honest quantification of agreement (e.g. per‑point $\chi^2$ or mean absolute deviation) would strengthen the paper.

**4. The centrality argument rests on a one‑parameter model of v₃.** In HYDJET++ the triangular flow is a free, smoothly varying parameter $\epsilon_3(b)$ tied to freeze‑out radius (Eq. 5 and the paragraph following it). The prediction that $v_6(\Psi_2)$ is "weak in semiperipheral and gradually increases for more peripherality" (Sec. IV, Fig. 5) is largely an input about the $b$‑dependence of $\epsilon_3(b)/\epsilon_2(b)$. The paper's statement that "the model artificially increased the triangularity of the freeze‑out hypersurface" is a red flag: it is not clear how much of the central $v_6$ result is *predicted* versus *tuned*. A sensitivity study — varying $\epsilon_3(b)$ by ±30% and showing the band on the v₆ curves and on the two correlators — would make the claim of robustness quantitative rather than narrative.

**5. v₃ itself is not a purely initial‑state quantity at LHC.** The paper treats $v_3$ as "the triangular flow" without distinguishing the genuinely initial‑state part (from $\epsilon_3$) from the nonlinear, $v_2^3$-induced part of the final‑state $v_3$. Experiments (e.g. ALICE three‑sub‑event studies, CMS cumulant ratio $v_4\{2\}/v_2\{4\}$ and its $n=3$ analog) have separated these two. The mixing formula $v_6\sim v_2 v_3^2$ is then ambiguous: does the $v_3$ in the second factor mean the measured final‑state $v_3$ or the initial‑state one? A brief paragraph clarifying this would help readers place the result in the modern multi‑sub‑event landscape.

**6. No error analysis or uncertainties on model comparisons.** Figs. 1–4 put MC histograms on top of data points; no systematic bands, no model parameter uncertainty, no statement about how much the jet contribution at 10% level is robust to PYTHEN reweighting choices. For a paper whose headline conclusions rest on a 10–15% jet effect (Conclusion (2)), an error estimate is essential.

**7. Minor issues.**
- The arXiv stamp says $v_2$, March 2014, yet the in‑text "Date: June 17, 2021" — confusing provenance.
- Mixed notation in Eq. 13 caption: $v_6^{1/6}$ is occasionally written $v_6^{\frac{1}{6}}$ or $v_6^{(1/6)}$; should be unified.
- The statement "no scaling" for $v_6^{1/6}(\Psi_3)/v_3^{1/3}(\Psi_3)$ is a bit strong: Fig. 4(a) shows the curves staying within ~0.2 of each other; this is more "partial scaling with residual centrality dependence" than "no scaling."
- The two‑plane correlator method, while standard, depends on a pseudo‑rapidity gap to avoid resonance correlations in the *backwards* direction. With HYDJET++'s soft+hard superposition, whether such correlations are even generated is unclear — the authors would benefit from stating explicitly that PYTHEN's underlying event model is consistent with or inconsistent with this gap.

## Suggested concrete additions (highest‑leverage, in priority order)

1. Relabel the $(1/6)^{1/6}$ and $(1/2)^{1/6}$ lines as "single‑plane heuristic limits" and, if possible, quote a multi‑plane reference value (e.g. from a published eby‑e hydro calculation) alongside.
2. Add an uncertainty band to the two correlator figures by propagating a ±30% variation of the $\epsilon_3(b)$ parameter.
3. Add a short subsection or paragraph distinguishing initial‑state vs. nonlinear $v_3$, clarifying which is used in Eq. 13.
4. Report per‑point or integrated agreement statistics between model and CMS/ATLAS in Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4.
5. Revise the title / abstract to put the *two‑plane ratio discriminant* and the *centrality crossing of Ψ₆* — which are the actually new and useful results — front and center, instead of the (standard) superposition formula.
6. Add a brief note on how this framework fares against modern multi‑sub‑event cumulant measurements at LHC (ALICE 5.02 / 5.36 TeV, CMS 2.76 TeV $n=3,4,5$ sub‑event cumulants), even qualitatively, to anchor the 2013‑era result in the current experimental landscape.

## Overall verdict

A competent, well‑written short study that turns a standard Fourier‑mixing formula into a useful experimental discriminator (the two‑plane $v_6^{1/6}$ ratios and the predicted Ψ₆ centrality crossing). The derivation is correct within its stated (single‑plane) assumption, the comparison to CMS/ATLAS data is fair, and the role of jets is quantified. The main limitations are (i) the tension between the single‑plane ansatz used for the main equation and the multi‑plane picture motivated elsewhere; (ii) the lack of a multi‑plane, event‑by‑event, viscous‑hydro benchmark; and (iii) limited quantification of uncertainties, especially on the 10–15% jet effect that drives one of the three conclusions. As written, the paper is best read as a first‑principles phenomenological template for $v_6$ rather than as a model that quantitatively reproduces LHC data. With the additions above it would stand as a genuinely useful reference; as published it supports the broader (and by now well‑established) understanding that $v_6$ at LHC is dominated by nonlinear $v_2$–$v_3$ coupling rather than a primitive hexagonal source.

The paper's core message is sound and, in hindsight, consistent with subsequent LHC measurements; however, the result $v_6\sim v_2v_3^2$ has been superseded in both formalism (multi‑sub‑event cumulants, eby‑e hydro, event‑plane vs. cumulative methods) and precision (LHC Run 2 and Run 3 data) since the paper was submitted, so its direct impact on the current state of the field is modest.

## [1311.0747v2-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


# Review — "Hexagonal flow v$_6$ as a superposition of elliptic v$_2$ and triangular v$_3$ flows"

Bravina, Brusheim Johansson, Eyyubova, Korotkikh, Malinina, Petrushanko, Snigirev, Zabrrodin

## Summary of the work

The authors study the hexagonal flow $v_6$ in $\sqrt{s}=2.76$ TeV Pb+Pb with the hybrid (hydrodynamics + jets) generator HYDJET++, and its projections onto two different event planes, $\Psi_2$ (from elliptic $v_2$) and $\Psi_3$ (from triangular $v_3$). The central analytic result, obtained by a saddle‑point/free‑out expansion, is

$$v_6 \simeq \tfrac{1}{6}v_2^3 + \tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2$$

i.e. $v_6$ is a *nonlinear* superposition of $v_2$ and $v_3$, with no genuine gemine hexagonal source in the model. They then track the $v_6^{1/6}/v_2^{1/2}$ (in $\Psi_2$) and $v_6^{1/6}/v_3^{1/3}$ (in $\Psi_3$) ratios, the jet contribution to both, and two‑plane correlators $\langle\cos(6(\Psi_2-\Psi_6))\rangle$ and $\langle\cos(6(\Psi_3-\Psi_6))\rangle$, and use all of these to argue that the experimentally observed drop of the 3–6 harmonic correlator is driven by (i) $v_2$ dominating $v_3$ in semiperipheral collisions and (ii) the lack of correlation between $\Psi_2$ and $\Psi_3$, rather than by a fall in $v_3$ magnitude alone.

## Overall assessment

This is a competent, well‑motivated, and — importantly — largely *correct* early statement of a picture that the field subsequently adopted and validated: that $v_6$ is predominantly a nonlinear response of the lower‑order harmonics, and that separating $v_6$ onto the elliptic and triangular event planes is the right diagnostic. As a 2013/2014 piece it anticipated the independent six‑plane $v_6$ result and the broader nonlinear‑response/cumulant framework. For that reason I treat it as a solid contribution that pointed early in the right direction, rather than merely a model exercise.

The main reservations are about *how the claim is supported*:

**1. Single‑event‑plane derivation, then a loose relaxation.** Equation (13) is derived assuming a *single* fluid plane (a single $u_{\max}$ expanded in harmonics, Eqs. 6–8). Only afterward do the authors project $v_6$ onto $\Psi_2$ and $\Psi_3$ separately and point out that in reality $\Psi_n$ need not coincide. There is a real tension: the clean $v_6=\frac16v_2^3+\frac12v_3^2$ relation and the ideal limits $v_6^{1/6}/v_2^{1/2}\to(1/6)^{1/6}\approx0.74$ in $\Psi_2$ and $\to(1/2)^{1/6}\approx0.89$ in $\Psi_3$ are really the single‑plane result dressed up for two planes. The paper should state more explicitly which parts of Eq. (13) survive once the planes decorrelate, and give even a rough estimate of the decorrelation correction. As written it reads as if the exact coefficients carry over to the two‑plane situation, which is the part I would question first.

**2. Sole reliance on HYDJET++.** All phenomenology — in particular the *high‑$p_T$ drop of $v_2$ at $p_T\gtrsim3$ GeV/c* that the authors lean on to explain the flat/rising behavior — is a feature of this one model (hydro + quenched jets). The conclusion that "basic features of $v_6$ are understood in terms of $v_2$/$v_3$ interplay" is a general hydrodynamic claim, so it is somewhat thin to rest it entirely on one hybrid. At least one cross‑check, e.g. a full viscous‑hydrodynamics calculation or a second transport implementation, or even a toy nonlinear‑response calculation with the same initial conditions, would make the interpretation much more robust and decouple the physics conclusion from one code's peculiarities.

**3. The $v_6$ rise appears too early.** In the model the (hydrodynamically generated) $v_6(p_T)$ in the $\Psi_2$ plane "starts to rise at $p_T \ge 1.5$ GeV/c," while the plotted CMS data sit flatter and the abstract assigns the $\ge 3$ GeV/c rise to the jet component. There is thus a visible discrepancy between the shape of the model $v_6$ at low/intermediate $p_T$ and the data (Figs. 1d, 2d) that is mentioned but not really resolved. A short quantitative sentence on whether the model's *magnitude and slope* of the $v_6$ rise are acceptable, not just its onset, would be valuable. This is the figure I would most challenge under review.

**4. The correlator interpretation is post‑dictive.** The argument that the 3–6 correlator drop follows from $v_2/v_3$ plus $\Psi_2$–$\Psi_3$ decorrelation is coherent and is a legitimate *alternative* to the competing "it is just $v_3$ becoming smaller" explanation (the authors rightly challenge that reading of the AMPT‑based study, ref. [36]). But it is essentially a narrative fit: the model reproduces the trend, and the trend is consistent with the hypothesis. To make it more convincing the authors could show the two ingredients independently (e.g. vary the $\Psi_2$–$\Psi_3$ correlation in the model and show the correlator responds as claimed), which would separate the two proposed mechanisms.

**5. Presentation.** The paper is a little verbose and re‑states the same point (model reproduces data, jets add ~10%, interplay explains things) in the abstract, discussion, and conclusions. A tighter framing of exactly what is new versus the standard $v_n=\sum v_m v_{k}$ response would help.

## What is done well

- The motivation is clear and the connection to the experimentally observed 3–6 correlator behavior is a genuine value‑add, not just a $v_6$ calculation.
- The analytic saddle‑point argument (Sec. III) is a nice, self‑contained demonstration of the nonlinear superposition, and the limiting ratios are a good sanity check.
- Separating $v_6$ into $\Psi_2$‑ and $\Psi_3$‑relative projections and correlating with the (non‑)scaling of the two ratios is a physically sensible diagnostic that later proved to be the right tool.
- Quantifying the jet contribution (≈10–15% increase, steepening of high‑$p_T$ tails) is useful in its own right.
- The pushback against the "smaller $v_3$" interpretation of the correlator drop is a fair and important scientific point.

## Recommendation

Acceptible as an early, correct‑direction contribution, but I would want revisions to (a) make explicit the single‑plane vs. two‑plane status of Eq. (13), (b) add at least one independent/second‑model cross‑check of the nonlinear $v_6$ result, and (c) directly address the low/intermediate‑$p_T$ $v_6$ shape discrepancy with the data. These would move it from "a model that is consistent with the picture" toward "an argument for the picture that is not tied to one code."

## [1311.0747v2-critique] Critique


# Critique of "Hexagonal flow v6 as a superposition of elliptic v2 and triangular v3 flows" (arXiv:1311.0747v2)

## Summary

This paper by Bravina et al. (Oslo/Moscow, 2013/2014) studies the hexagonal flow harmonic v6 in Pb+Pb collisions at √s = 2.76 TeV using the HYDJET++ generator (parametrized "blast-like" hydrodynamics plus PYQUEN jets). The central claim is that v6 is entirely generated by nonlinear mixing of the elliptic and triangular flows, v6 ≃ (1/6)v2³ + (1/2)v3², so that the hexagonal event plane Ψ6 tracks Ψ3 in semicentral collisions and Ψ2 in peripheral ones, explaining the experimentally observed centrality dependence of the two-plane correlators cos 6(Ψ₂−Ψ₆) and cos 6(Ψ₃−Ψ₆).

## Strengths

1. **Timely and physically sensible.** At the time, v6 data from ATLAS/CMS were just appearing, and mode-mixing (crosstalk) between harmonics was an actively debated topic. Translating the mode-mixing language of the hydrodynamics community (refs. 15–18, which they cite) into predictions for the specific v6–Ψ2–Ψ3 plane structure is a useful framing, and the centroidal picture (Ψ₆ rotating between the Ψ₃ direction centrality-depending on the v2³/v3² balance) is intuitive and became part of the standard vocabulary.

2. **Clean theoretical anchor.** The Borghini–Ollitrault saddle-point derivation gives the coefficients (1/6)v₂³ and (1/2)v₃², with correctly identified subleading terms (the linear V₆ response and the V₂V₄ cross term), and the high-pT limits v₆/v₂³ → 1/6 and v₆/v₃² → 1/2 are the right benchmarks against which to measure the model curves (Figs. 3–4).

3. **Jet (nonflow) contribution quantified.** Showing that quenching jets raise the v₆/v₂³ ratio by ~10% and harden its high-pT tail is a concrete, testable correction — relevant because pQCD-dominated v6 at high pT is exactly where the "pure hydrodynamic scaling" language breaks down.

4. **Reasonable statistics and honest caveats.** ~10⁶ events per centrality, and the authors explicitly flag the model's lack of event-by-event fluctuations and its consequences for comparing ratios with data (the extra multiplier, ref. 28).

## Weaknesses

1. **The headline conclusion is circular.** The model *by construction* generates only v2 and v3 at freeze-out; there is no genuine ε₆ → v₆ channel, no linear-response stage, and no event-by-event fluctuations. So the statement "basic features of v6 can be understood in terms of the interplay of v2 and v3" and the softer but related "original hexagonal initial fluctuations seem to play a minor role" are *assumptions baked into the model*, not findings. A model that cannot produce a competing contribution can only reproduce the one it contains. The centrality dependence of the Ψ₆ plane is therefore largely a consequence of the chosen parametrization of ǫ₃(b) (a free parameter) versus ǫ(b) ∝ b/2R_A, not a prediction. The authors need a genuine v₆ mode (or a comparison model with one) to support the "minor role" claim — later event-by-event viscous hydrodynamics showed that the linear ε₆-driven response is not negligible, which cuts at this weakness. Note too that they "artificially increased the triangularity" to get the correlators out of the noise — a tuning that further weakens the inference.

2. **Model limitations are more damaging than acknowledged.** Since HYDJET++ has no evolution stage, it cannot capture: (i) hot-spot dilution of v3 that drives mode mixing in real hydrodynamics; (ii) the difference between the flow-plane v₆{Ψₖ} and the cumulant v₆{n} measured by the experiments; (iii) event-plane resolution effects, which for v6 are severe (the paper's own event-plane correlators rely on a forward–backward split with a large η gap, i.e., low resolution). The comparison of v2 and v3 with ATLAS/CMS is fair, but the actual predictions (ratios, correlator trends) are compared to data only qualitatively: the ATLAS correlators (ref. 38) are *cited as showing similar centrality trends* without a numbers-on-numbers check, and the CMS v6 reference is a 2013 preprint. The paper would be considerably stronger with a direct overlay of the simulated cos 6(Ψ₂−Ψ₆) and cos 6(Ψ₃−Ψ₆) values on the ATLAS points.

3. **Inconsistencies between abstract, text, and figures.**
 - The abstract predicts scaling of v₆^{1/6}{Ψ₂}/v₂^{1/2}{Ψ₂} "in the range 1 ≤ pT ≤ 4 GeV/c," but the authors themselves immediately add that jets make the ratio *rise* at pT ≳ 3.5 GeV/c, so the flat scaling region ends near 3 GeV/c. The conclusion's "10%–15%" jet enhancement also disagrees with the abstract's "about 10%."
 - The Fig. 3 caption mislabels a centrality: "20–30% (triangles pointing up), 20–30% (triangles pointing down)" — the second is evidently 30–40%.
 - Eq. (12)'s coefficient of the (aV₂)(aV₄) term, 3, does not follow from the stated single-plane expansion (a straightforward Taylor expansion of Eq. 8 gives a coefficient of 1); the term is dropped later as subleading, so this is cosmetic, but the derivation should be checked since the paper leans on it.

4. **Figures are of poor quality for publication.** Figure 5 in particular is nearly illegible (overlapping legend, cramped symbols, axis labels colliding with data), and in Fig. 1 the v₆{Ψ₂} curves are rescaled by ×10 with a marker that is easy to miss. Since centrality dependence is a key claim, the figure presenting it should be exemplary.

5. **Missing comparisons.** No identified-particle decomposition, no comparison with the simultaneous v₄/v₂² result from the same group (their refs. 29–30) to show consistency of the nonlinear expansion at n=4 and n=6, and no attempt to confront the model's v₆ magnitude with the absolute LHC measurements rather than only the scaling shape.

## Assessment

This is a competently executed, well-motivated model study that puts a clear and (on the plane-coupling point) largely correct physical picture into the literature at a useful time, and quantifies a jet correction to v₆ scaling that remains relevant. However, its most prominent conclusion — that v₆ is essentially v₂³+v₃² with genuine v₆ a minor effect — is guaranteed by construction rather than demonstrated, its quantitative confrontation with data is thin, and the presentation has real sloppiness (caption errors, an illegible key figure, an unsupported coefficient). It is solid incremental work within a fast-moving subfield, not a decisive advance.

## [1311.0747v2-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Paper: "Hexagonal flow v6 as a superposition of elliptic v2 and triangular v3 flows" (arXiv:1311.0747)

### Stated contribution, in my own words

The authors set out to explain what builds the hexagonal (sixth-harmonic) anisotropic flow $v_6$ seen in LHC heavy-ion collisions, using the HYDJET++ hybrid model (parametrized "soft" hydrodynamics + PYQUEN jets) for Pb+Pb at 2.76 TeV. Their argument has three steps:

1. **A formula, not a measurement.** In a single-event-plane saddle-point (Borghini–Ollitrault-type) expansion, $v_6$ decomposes into $v_6 \approx \tfrac{1}{6}v_2^3 + \tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2$ (their Eqs. 12–13) — i.e., hexagonal flow is mostly a nonlinear imprint of elliptic and triangular flow, not a genuine $n=6$ response.
2. **Plane-resolved model study.** They compute $v_6$ referenced to the $\Psi_2$ and $\Psi_3$ planes separately, showing $v_6^{\{1/6\}}\{\Psi_2\}/v_2^{\{1/2\}}\{\Psi_2\}$ scales in $1\le p_T \le 4$ GeV/c for semicentral/semiperipheral events, and that jets lift this ratio by ~10% and bend it upward above $p_T \approx 3.5$ GeV/c.
3. **A "dominance switch" with centrality.** In semicentral collisions $v_3 > v_2$, so $\Psi_6$ tracks $\Psi_3$; in peripheral collisions $v_2$ dominates and $\Psi_6$ tracks $\Psi_2$. This is tested with two-plane correlators $\langle\cos 6(\Psi_2-\Psi_6)\rangle$ (rising with $b$) and $\langle\cos 6(\Psi_3-\Psi_6)\rangle$ (falling), which they present as an explanation of the ATLAS observations — and as an explicit rebuttal of Bhalerao–Ollitrault–Pal [39], who attributed the $v_3$–$v_6$ decorrelation to a decrease of the triangular flow itself.

### What is and isn't new, against their own citation list

**Not new:**
- **The nonlinear-response skeleton is inherited, not derived.** Eq. (13) is a textbook extension of the $v_4 \approx \tfrac12 v_2^2$ result of Borghini–Ollitrault (which they cite as [31]) obtained by the same saddle-point expansion — it is a line of algebra, not a new mechanism. The single-event-plane caveat the authors themselves attach ("Eq. (13) as a first-order approximation") concedes how little is in it.
- **Mode mixing / crosstalk between harmonics was already established** by the hydrodynamic works they cite: Qin et al. [15], Gardim et al. [16], Qiu–Heinz [17], Teaney–Yan [18]. Their own introduction says "the characteristic mode mixing between the different order flow coefficients has been revealed." So the *qualitative* claim that $v_2$ and $v_3$ feed higher harmonics is not their discovery; the question "which harmonics dominate" they answer, but the phenomenon was theirs to analyze, not to introduce.
- **The ingredients are all pre-existing:** participant triangularity (Alver–Roland [13]; Alver et al. [14]), the event-plane correlator machinery (refs. [37–39]), the HYDJET++ model and its known jet-induced $v_4$ behavior (their own earlier papers [29, 30], extended from $v_4$ to $v_6$ here), and the ATLAS correlator data ([38]) they merely reproduce and reinterpret.

**Genuinely new (within the cited literature):**
- **The plane-resolved decomposition** — separating $v_6\{\Psi_2\}$ from $v_6\{\Psi_3\}$ and showing the two have *opposite* centrality trends — is a concrete analytical handle not present in the hydro papers they cite, which typically work with the full $v_6$ in the participant plane.
- **The centrality-driven dominance-switch scenario** ($\Psi_6$-to-$\Psi_3$ in semicentral, $\Psi_6$-to-$\Psi_2$ in peripheral collisions) as the mechanism behind the rising/falling two-plane correlators, and the accompanying quantitative claim that genuine initial $\varepsilon_6$ fluctuations are minor. The explicit rejection of [39]'s "v3 falls" explanation in favor of "v2/v3 ratio changes + uncorrelated planes of v2 and v3" is a new interpretation.
- **A first quantitative estimate of jet effects on $v_6$** specifically: the ~10–15% ratio enhancement and the $p_T \ge 3$ GeV/c upturn. This is the most original quantitative content, and it is the one place the hybrid model offers information the pure-hydro references cannot.

### Verdict on novelty

The contribution is real but incremental-to-moderate: it is an *interpretive* model study rather than a methodological or conceptual advance. The headline formula extends known results by one line; the headline physics (mixing of $v_2$/$v_3$ into $v_6$) was known; the paper's distinct value is the $\Psi_2/\Psi_3$-resolved picture, the dominance-switch explanation of the plane correlators, and the jet estimates. One caveat that limits the claim (though it is partly acknowledged): the "genuine $v_6$ is minor" conclusion is an artifact of their specific model, which contains no initial $n=6$ deformation and no event-by-event evolution — so it is a statement about what HYDJET++ produces, and is only as strong as the model's fidelity to reality.

**Scores** (novelty: solid analysis on well-trodden ground; overall: soundly executed but model-dependent and thin on new formalism; impact: useful to the heavy-ion community but superseded in its central "no genuine v6" claim, no field-shaping effect):

## [1311.0747v2-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


# Evaluation of arXiv:1311.0747v2 — "Hexagonal flow v₆ as a superposition of elliptic v₂ and triangular v₃ flows" (Bravina et al.)

## 1. Methodology (enough to reproduce the central computation)

**Framework.** The study is a Monte Carlo calculation in the HYDJET++ model [refs. 20, 26, 27] for non‑identical (Pb+Pb) collisions, √s = 2.76 TeV. HYDJET++ is an incoherent superposition of two independent generators: **FASTMC** (soft, "fireball" contribution — an initial almond‑shaped freeze‑out cell expanded by parameterized hydrodynamics followed by a hadronic cascade) and **PYQUEN** (hard processes — 2→2 parton scattering, radiative + collisional energy loss, shadowing effects, jet fragmentation into hadrons).

**Initial anisotropy prescription (the key modification this paper adds).**
- Fireball transverse radius: R_cell(b,φ) = R₀ε₀(b)·√(1−ε²(b))/(1+ε(b)cos2φ), with initial eccentricity ε₀(b) = b/(2R_A) [Eq. (3)].
- The triangular component is imprinted directly on the **freeze‑out radius**, not on the initial density: R(b,φ) = R_cell(b,φ){1 + ε₃(b)cos[3(φ − Ψ₃)]} [Eq. (5)], where the phase Ψ₃ is drawn **randomly and independently of the reaction plane Ψ₂** (this makes the event‑integrated v₃ in the Ψ₂ plane vanish, mimicking data), and ε₃(b) is an additional parameter controlling v₃ strength.

**Analytical saddle‑point relations.** Fast particles are assumed to be emitted from the "u_max hypersurface" where the flow rapidity u_∥ = u^μ∂_μx^μ is maximal. The freeze‑out distribution ∝ exp(−m_T^u u^0_max/T) [Eq. (6)] and the Fourier expansion u_max(φ) = u_max[1 + 2Σₙ Vₙcos(nφ)] [Eq. (7)] are combined to map flow harmonics onto the Fourier coefficients of u_max through a = (p_T − m_T^u_max)·u_max/T: v₂ = aV₂, v₃ = aV₃ [Eqs. (9)–(10)], v₄ = ½a²V₂² + aV₄ [Eq. (11)]. Carrying the expansion one order further gives the central result

v₆ = ⅙(aV₂)³ + ½(aV₃)² + aV₆ + 3(aV₂)(aV₄) [Eq. (12)] → at high p_T (a ≪ 1): **v₆ ≃ ⅙v₂³ + ½v₃²** [Eq. (13)],

i.e. v₆ with no genuine ("genuine" V₆ = 0) hexagonal initial mode. This yields the high‑p_T asymptotes v₆^(1/6)/v₂^(1/2) → 6^(−1/6) ≈ 0.74 and v₆^(1/6)/v₃^(1/3) → 2^(−1/6) ≈ 0.89 (the paper's Fig. 3 caption writes the former as "(1/6)^{1/2}", which is a typo — (1/6)^½ = 0.41, not 0.74).

**Central numerical experiment.**
1. Generate **2 × 10⁶ Pb+Pb events per centrality class**, four classes defined as fractions of the *geometric parton cross section* σ/σ_geo: 10–20%, 20–30%, 30–40%, 40–50%.
2. For each event compute event planes Ψ₂, Ψ₃, Ψ₆ from the Q‑vectors of Eq. (14) [tan(nΨₙ) = Q_{n,y}/Q_{n,x}] — the "pseudo‑rapidity gap" method of Blalerau–Ollitrault [refs. 37–39], with resolution corrections.
3. Observables:
   - v₂(p_T) and v₃(p_T) in the **Ψ₂ plane** (v₆ also in both planes), with data from ATLAS (v₂) and ALICE (v₃), |η| < 2.5;
   - the scaling ratios v₆^(1/6)(Ψ₂)/v₂^(1/2)(Ψ₂) and v₆^(1/6)(Ψ₃)/v₃^(1/3)(Ψ₃) vs p_T, for the **hydro‑only** particle sample and for the **hydro + jets** full sample (Fig. 3, 4), tested over 1 ≤ p_T ≤ 4 GeV/c;
   - centrality dependence in p_T bins 0–1, 1–2.5, 2.5–10 GeV/c (Fig. 5);
   - two‑plane correlators ⟨cos(6(Ψ₂ − Ψ₆))⟩ and ⟨cos(6(Ψ₃ − Ψ₆))⟩ vs centrality, compared with CMS [33].

**Claimed reproductions.** The flat ratio in the Ψ₂ plane (≈0.7–0.8) vs rising ratio in the Ψ₃ plane; v₆(Ψ₂) rising and v₆(Ψ₃) weakening for more peripheral collisions; the increase of v₂–v₃ correlations and decrease of v₃–v₆ correlations with b (attributed to dominance exchange: v₃ → v₂ as the driver of v₆ from central to semiperipheral).

**Not reproducible from the paper alone:** the functional form/values of ε₃(b), T, u_max, R₀, jet‑loss and fragmentation parameters (deferred to the earlier HYDJET++ tuning [26]), the particle weight w_i in the Q‑vectors, and the detailed forward–backward split for the correlators (deferred to [38, 39]).

## 2. Assumptions the paper papers over

1. **ε₃(b) is a free, undetermined parameter.** It is stated to be either "linked to initial eccentricity ε₀(b) or treated as a free parameter" — no relation, value, data fit, or systematic variation is given. The entire triangular‑flow content (and hence about half of v₆ in Eq. 13) rests on it.
2. **ε₀(b) = b/(2R_A), event‑averaged, with no eccentricity or participant‑number fluctuations.** v₃ is built from a *geometric, per‑b* triangular anisotropy with a random phase, not from event‑by‑event initial‑state (CGC/Glauber) fluctuations. Consequently the conclusion that "initial fluctuations play a minor role in v₆ formation" is a consequence of the construction, not a tested finding — the model simply cannot generate fluctuation‑driven v₆ (no n ≥ 4 initial modes, no mixing).
3. **No directed flow / no e‑by‑e anisotropic flow.** HYDJET++ has v₁ ≈ 0 and no EBE hydrodynamic fluctuation stage, so "two‑plane correlators" computed from it are not genuinely e‑by‑e measurements; the method is applied to the mean‑flow model.
4. **Single‑u_max (single‑plane) saddle‑point approximation.** All Fourier components share one expansion centered on the single maximum of u; the paper itself flags Eq. (13) as a "first‑order approximation" while knowing the event planes Ψₙ "do not necessarily coincide," which is precisely what their own correlator section measures.
5. **The a ≪ 1 truncation and V₆ = 0.** Dropping aV₆ and the cross term 3(aV₂)(aV₄) assumes no genuine hexagonal mode and that V₄ is sub‑leading; neither term is estimated or bounded numerically. The 1/6 and 1/2 limits rest entirely on this.
6. **The p_T window 1–4 GeV/c is asserted, not justified.** The condition a ≪ 1 (a ∝ p_T − m_T^max) has to hold at the *low* edge of the window; 1 GeV/c is where the relation is least clean.
7. **Jet modeling is taken on faith.** Jet fragmentation, energy‑loss magnitudes, and the crucial fact that jet‑induced v₂ (and v₃) is *much smaller* than the hydro value are properties of the earlier PYQUEN tuning, quoted but not re‑derived or varied; the "10–15 % rise" of the ratios from jets has no error band.
8. **Non‑standard centrality, no mapping to data.** Model classes are σ/σ_geo bins (parton‑parton based), never translated to N_part or to the ATLAS/ALICE/CMS binning actually plotted; all centrality comparisons are qualitative "trends."
9. **Analysis details deferred to references:** the weight function w_i in the event‑plane Q‑vectors, the forward–backward subevent definition/pseudo‑rapidity gap, and the resolution corrections ([38, 39]) are not stated; so the correlator numbers cannot be independently reproduced from this paper.
10. **No statistical (or any) error bars on model curves**, and model results at midrapidity are compared with detector data at their actual acceptance/rapidity without comment.
11. **Independent random Ψ₃ by construction** forbids the Ψ₂–Ψ₃ correlations known to exist in data; the model's success with v₂–v₆/v₃–v₆ correlators therefore does not test the physical mechanism for those correlations.
12. **Minor:** the typo "(1/6)^{1/2} ≈ 0.74" (should be (1/6)^{1/6}) in the Fig. 3 legend, and the identification of velocity‑plane coefficients Vₙ with measured vₙ (Eq. 9–10) is asserted rather than derived.

## 3. Overall assessment

This is a competent, transparent model study that gives a useful phenomenological picture — v₆ ≈ v₂³/6 + v₃²/2, with the interplay that central v₆ is v₃‑driven and peripheral v₆ is v₂‑driven, reproducing several LHC scaling and correlator trends qualitatively. But the core claim is partly circular (v₆ is *built* as a superposition of seeded v₂, v₃ and then shown to be a superposition), the one genuinely new ingredient (ε₃(b)) is left unspecified, the "fluctuations are minor" conclusion is a built‑in consequence, and several numbers needed for real reproduction are deferred to other papers.

## [1311.0747v2-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


# Evaluation of "Hexagonal flow $v_6$ as a superposition of elliptic $v_2$ and triangular $v_3$ flows" (Bravina et al., 2013)

## My understanding of the logical argument

The paper's argument runs through four connected steps:

**1. Setup and premise.** In HYDJET++ a non-central Pb+Pb collision has a nearly almond-shaped freeze-out surface. The leading anisotropies are the elliptic (eccentricity $\epsilon_2$) driving $v_2$ in event plane $\Psi_2$, and the triangular-flow anisotropy (eccentricity $\epsilon_3$, the "genie" triangularity from initial-state fluctuations) driving $v_3$ in plane $\Psi_3$. Critically, the model constructs **no genuine sixfold spatial anisotropy**, so it cannot generate a "real" $v_6$ by design — any $v_6$ that appears must come from the nonlinear response of the fluid/fragmentation.

**2. The algebraic/mixing result (the core theorem).** Performing a saddle-point (Gaussian) integral over the freeze-out and expanding the fluid velocity $u_{\max}(\phi)$ in a Fourier series (Eq. 7) with two inputs $V_2, V_3$, cosine-product counting gives the mode superposition:

$$v_4 = \tfrac{1}{2}a^2 V_2^2 + aV_4,\qquad v_6 = \tfrac{1}{6}a^3 V_2^3 + \tfrac{1}{2}aV_3^2 + aV_6 + 3aV_2\langle aV_4\rangle.$$

At high $p_t$ the response coefficients drop out and one obtains the clean limit

$$v_6 \approx \tfrac{1}{6}v_2^3 + \tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2\qquad(\text{Eq. 13}).$$

So $v_6$ is a sum of an **elliptic-driven term** $\sim \tfrac{1}{6}v_2^3$ and a **triangular-driven term** $\sim \tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2$.

**3. Two-plane discrimination.** Because the two terms carry different event-plane structure, projecting $v_6$ onto different planes separates them. In the $\Psi_2$ plane, $v_6\sim\tfrac{1}{6}v_2^3$ predicts a scaling of $v_6^{1/6}/v_2^{1/2}\to(1/6)^{1/6}\approx0.74$ at high $p_t$; in the $\Psi_3$ plane, $v_6\sim\tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2$ predicts $v_6^{1/6}/v_3^{1/3}\to(1/2)^{1/6}\approx0.89$. The paper claims the first scaling holds ($p_t\gtrsim1$ GeV/c, centralities $\ge30\%$), whereas the second does **not** collapse as cleanly because of centrality-dependent jet effects. Jets raise both ratios by ~10–15% and produce the observed rise of the high-$p_t$ tails at $p_t\ge3$ GeV/c (in contrast to the hydrodynamic plateau).

**4. Event-plane correlators.** Using the forward–backward two-plane method, they compute $\langle\cos(6(\Psi_2-\Psi_6))\rangle$ and $\langle\cos(6(\Psi_3-\Psi_6))\rangle$. Because $v_6$ becomes stronger relatively as collisions go peripheral, the dominant driver shifts from $v_3$ (central) to $v_2$ (peripheral). Hence $\Psi_6$ should track $\Psi_3$ in central and $\Psi_2$ in peripheral collisions → the $(\Psi_2,\Psi_6)$ correlator rises while the $(\Psi_3,\Psi_6)$ correlator falls toward peripheral. They argue this matches ATLAS, and attribute the fall to (i) $v_2$ being dominantly diluted peripherally faster than $v_3$ and (ii) the lack of a $\Psi_2$–$\Psi_3$ correlation — explicitly *not* the "v3 falls" explanation of the AMPT study.

**Overall thesis:** the genuine/hexagonal initial fluctuation ($\epsilon_6$) is a minor player, and the observable $v_6$ (and its $p_t$- and centrality-dependences) is essentially the interplay of the $v_2^3$ and $v_3^2$ nonlinear channels.

## Feedback

**What the paper gets right and does well.**

- The superposition formula (Eq. 13) and the associated high-$p_t$ scaling limits are a clean, memorable, and (for 2013, when $v_6$ had only just been measured) timely articulation of how $v_6$ inherits structure from $v_2$ and $v_3$. The $1/6$-root bookkeeping ($v_6^{1/6}/v_k^{k/6} \to$ constants) is neat and gives directly testable, parameter-free targets.
- The central conceptual contribution is the **two-plane projection** — using the fact that the $v_2^3$ and $v_3^2$ pieces live in different planes to pull them apart experimentally. That is a genuinely useful diagnostic, not just a rewriting of the response.
- The paper is honest about the model's status: it is not a full EoS hydro code, does not generate a genuine $v_6$, and requires a phenomenological normalization factor $N^{1/n}/n$ before comparison to data. It also acknowledges the limitations (single-event-plane approximation, neglect of genuine $\epsilon_6$).

**Weaknesses.**

1. **Mild circularity of the central claim.** The headline conclusion — "$v_6$ is essentially the interplay of $v_2$ and $v_3$, and genuine $v_6$ is minor" — is *built into the model's construction*: HYDJET++ has no sixfold spatial source, so $v_6$ must come out nonlinear by fiat. The paper therefore does not *derive* the smallness of the genuine harmonic from dynamics; it assumes it and then shows the assumption is self-consistent. This is fine as a phenomenological consistency check, but the framing ("our study favors the idea that basic features… can be understood in terms of the interplay") reads as stronger evidence than it is. A competing model that includes $\epsilon_6$ fluctuations is not quantitatively compared, so the "minor role" claim is untested within the paper.

2. **The mechanism is not new in general.** Nonlinear/mode-mixing generation of higher harmonics (the $v_2^3$-type term feeding $v_6$) was already a known feature of the viscous/ideal hydro response. The real contribution here is the *combination* — the $+ \tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2$ piece, the clean limits, and the two-plane discrimination — but the deeper "why $v_6$ looks nonlinear" is already in the literature, so novelty is limited to the specific synthesis and to the microscopic (hard+soft) setting.

3. **Qualitative data comparisons, limited ranges.** The $p_t$ window where the scaling "collapsed" is narrow ($1$–$3$ GeV/c) and the centrality coverage is four bins. The agreement with ATLAS/CMS curves is repeatedly called "fair" rather than tight, and the normalization fudge factor ($N^{1/n}/n$) signals the model is not quantitatively calibrated to these observables. The $v_6$ data are pulled from a separate extraction (CMS four- and two-party methods), adding a layer of model-independent-systematics that is not propagated.

4. **The correlator section is the weakest link, partly by the authors' own admission.** To make the $(\Psi_2,\Psi_6)$ vs. $(\Psi_3,\Psi_6)$ crossover appear, they state they "artificially increased the triangularity" of the freeze-out. If the qualitative centrality trend tracks a tuned input, the match to ATLAS is much less compelling than it is presented. Also the explanation for the fall of the 3–6 correlator (two competing reasons: faster $v_2$ dilution plus no $\Psi_2$–$\Psi_3$ correlation) is not isolated by a control calculation, so it remains a verbal interpretation.

5. **A framing caveat the authors under-emphasize.** Comparing the model's $v_6$ "measured in the $\Psi_2$ plane" directly to a single-number $v_6$ is subtle: in experiments $v_6$ refers to the harmonic in its own plane (with multi-particle subtractions removing the nonlinear pieces), precisely to *isolate* the genuine $\epsilon_6$-driven signal. The paper's most striking projections are exactly the nonlinear ones; a reader could over-read "we reproduce $v_6$" as "we reproduce the genuine sixfold response," which the model structurally cannot. This distinction should have been stressed far more.

6. **Post-hoc character of parts of Sec. IV.** The centrality-dependent crossover ($\Psi_6$ closer to $\Psi_3$ → $\Psi_2$ as $\sigma_{\text{geo}}$ grows) and the explanation of the correlator trend are developed after the model's $p_t$ results are presented, and read as an account fitted to already-known ATLAS behavior rather than a prior prediction.

**Bottom line.** A competent, clearly written, and useful early-interpretation paper that gives a clean, testable two-plane handle on $v_6$ and an early demonstration (in a microscopic model) that $v_6$ is largely a $v_2$/ $v_3$ nonlinear artifact. Its main limitation is structural rather than computational: it cannot independently establish that the *genuine* $v_6$ is small because that is assumed away, it relies on a normalization fudge and a tuned triangularity, and much of the core physics is a specific restatement of the already-known nonlinear-response picture. The "genuine $v_6$ is minor" conclusion in particular has been qualified by later work, which found non-negligible $\epsilon_6$ fluctuations.

## [1311.0747v2-quantitative-claims] Audit of quantitative claims


The paper is **"Hexagonal flow v6 as a superposition of elliptic v2 and triangular v3 flows"** (Bravina, Brusheim Johansson, Eyyubova, et al., *hep-ph* 1311.0747v2, dated 2014). It is a model-only study using the HYDJET++ hybrid generator (parametrized hydrodynamics + pyquen jets) for Pb+Pb at √s = 2.76 TeV. I read all 7 pages; here is the audit.

## The strongest quantitative claim

**"The ratio v6{Ψ2}/v2^{3/2} scales (is centrality-independent) in the range 1 ≤ pT ≤ 4 GeV/c, tending to the hydrodynamic limit (1/6)^{1/6} ≈ 0.74, and jets increase this ratio by ~10–15% while making it rise above pT ≥ 3 GeV/c."** (Abstract; Fig. 3; Conclusion 1–2). ## Audit

**Is the comparison fair?** Only partially, and the paper is mostly self-aware but does not close the gaps:

1. **The headline ratio itself is never compared to experiment.** Figs. 1–2 check only the inputs (v2{Ψ2} vs. ATLAS/CMS, v3{Ψ3} vs. ATLAS) and show "fair" agreement at 50–100% of experimental values. The model ratios (which are intrinsic, per plane) are therefore not strictly comparable to raw experimental ratios without that multiplier — yet the paper presents the 0.74 limit as the scaling value.
3. **A model-specific assumption is baked into the high-pT tail.** HYDJET++ is distinctive for predicting a *drop* of v2 at pT ≳ 3 GeV/c (jet dominance), which was controversial in 2013 and not shared by standard (viscous) hydrodynamics. The claimed high-pT rise of v6/v2^{3/2} depends directly on this assumption; the claim inherits that unvalidated model behavior.

**Are baselines current?** Yes — appropriately for 2013. ATLAS v2 [PRL/PRC 2012–13], ATLAS v3, CMS v2 (PRL 87, 014902 (2013)), CMS v6 correlator arXiv:1310.8651, and the AMultiP (AMPT) correlator benchmark [39] are all cited as contemporaneous inputs. No stale or strawman baseline. (By today's standards the whole "no genuine v6" picture was subsequently challenged — ALICE v6 measurements and EB-hydro event-mixing studies [e.g., Shen et al., Longland et al.] showed genuine ε6 contributions matter — but the audit must be against the state of the field as of publication.)

**Error bars / significance?** **None reported.** Model results are single HYDJET++ runs (2×10⁶ events/centrality, so statistical errors are negligible), but:
- Fig. 3's "±10–15%" jet effect is read off point-to-point differences between two curves with no uncertainty bands; statistical significance of the differences is not assessed.
- No error bars on any model point, no systematic-error discussion, no comparison tolerance on the v2/v3 agreement ("fair" is stated, not quantified). Standard for 2013 model papers, but it means the most quantitative claims (10% jet enhancement; equality of scaling curves across four centrality bins; approach to 0.74) have no stated confidence.

The paper is internally consistent, transparent about its model's limitations (no v1, no evolution/hot-spot propagation, EBE multiplier issue), and uses current 2013 baselines. Its central picture — v6 dominated by the interplay v2³ + v3², with Ψ6 correlating with Ψ3 semicentrally and Ψ2 peripherally — is physically reasonable and the correlator trends do match experiment. But the strongest *quantitative* claim (the 0.74 scaling ratio, 10% jet enhancement) is a model-internal prediction that was not directly, and is not statistically, validated: the experimental checks in the paper are qualitative (trend agreement of correlators), inputs agree only to within an unquantified margin, and no error bars or significance tests appear anywhere.

## [1311.0747v2-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


The paper (Bravina et al., "Hexagonal flow v6 as a superposition of elliptic v2 and triangular v3 flows," arXiv:1311.0747v2) argues, within the HYDJET++ model, that: (i) in ideal hydrodynamics v6 ≈ ⅙v2³ + ½v3² (Eq. 13), so v6 is a nonlinear response of the fluid to the initial ε2 and ε3 anisotropies; (ii) the model reproduces the measured v2(pT), v3(pT) and v6(pT) in Pb+Pb at 2.76 TeV (Figs. 1–2); (iii) in the Ψ2 plane the ratio v6^(1/6)/v2^(1/2) scales across centralities while it does not scale in the Ψ3 plane (Figs. 3–4); and (iv) the two‑plane correlators cos 6(Ψ2−Ψ6) (rising with centrality) and cos 6(Ψ3−Ψ6) (falling) (Figs. 6–7) are "in line with the experimental observations." The conclusion drawn: basic features of v6 follow from the interplay of v2 and v3, and "original hexagonal initial fluctuations seem to play a minor role in the formation of v6."

## The weakest link: Conclusion (3) — the inference from the event-plane correlators to "ε6 fluctuations play a minor role"

This is the one step where the claim is not entailed by the data. Exactly why:

1. **The only experimental input is one monotonic centrality trend, and the model shows that trend by construction.** The model turns off all event-by-event (EeE) fluctuations, so Ψ6 is not a random observable: it is a smooth, deterministic function of (ε2(b), ε3(b)), the two inputs that drive the flow (Ψ3 being fixed relative to Ψ2). Consequently cos 6(Ψ2−Ψ6) increases toward peripheral collisions and cos 6(Ψ3−Ψ6) decreases largely because the *ratio* v2/v3 changes with b and the model contains nothing else for Ψ6 to correlate with. In a realistic system with EeE fluctuations, Ψ6 in any given event is a mixture of the mean-geometric plane and the noise of the initial ε6 (and higher) fluctuation, so non-zero, centrality-dependent cos 6(Ψ2−Ψ6) appears *even if* ε6 is a major generator of v6. A model in which ε6 plays a major role can therefore produce the same qualitative centrality pattern — the "linearity" with experiment cannot bound the ε6 contribution. Qualitative agreement of one monotonic trend of one or two correlators is not a discriminating test of the minor/major claim.

2. **The model cannot falsify its own conclusion.** ε6 is zero *by construction* (HYDJET++ has no mechanism to generate it), so the agreement of the v6(pT) magnitudes in Figs. 1–2 is a consistency check, not a confirmation. In the measured laboratory frame v6, an ε6-driven component would simply add coherently/incoherently under the root-mean-square; the data precision and the model's own stated need for extra multiplicative normalization factors (Sec. II) do not rule out a sizeable hidden ε6 contribution up to the size of the total signal. The authors never vary a free ε6 parameter to show that adding it deteriorates the fit, which is exactly what the "minor role" claim would require.

3. **The model's decomposition has no experimental counterpart to be tested against.** All the sharp statements of the "superposition" story — v6(vT)(Ψ2), v6(vT)(Ψ3), scaling vs. non-scaling of v6^(1/6)(Ψ2)/v2^(1/2)(Ψ2) and v6^(1/6)(Ψ3)/v3^(1/3)(Ψ3) (Figs. 3, 4) — are predictions of a model in which the event planes are known analytically. Experiments provide only the laboratory-frame v6(pT), centrality dependence, and the cos 6(Ψ2−Ψ6) correlator; they do not provide v6 decomposed relative to Ψ2 or Ψ3, so the data are simply silent on whether the Ψ2-plane projection dominates (the "interplay" picture). The paper presents conclusions (1) and (2) as observational when they are model-internal — this conflation is what carries the unsupported weight into the final takeaway.

4. **Even the one correlator comparison is only qualitative, and the paper itself concedes the underlying expansion is first order.** The model's cos 6(Ψ2−Ψ6) climbs from ~0.3 to ~0.85 across 0–50% centrality, whereas the ATLAS values (the experiment cited as support) are flatter and lower-magnitude; no quantitative comparison of the curves is made. And the single-event-plane expansion that produces v6 ≈ ⅙v2³ + ½v3² (Eq. 13) is explicitly flagged by the authors as "a first order approximation" in a regime where "the interplay between different event planes can be very important" — precisely the regime in which an ε6 noise component would show up as plane-incoherent v6, a quantity the model can neither generate nor bound.

So the chain up to "HYDJET++ without any initial ε6 can reproduce v2, v3 and v6 shapes" stands, but the final sentence — that the *data* show ε6 is a minor source — is a consequence of the model's *input* (ε6 = 0), not of its agreement with the measurements.

## Context for scoring

The underlying idea (v6 mode mixing, v6 ≈ ⅙v2³ + ½v3²) predates this paper (Alver–McLeod; the authors' own v4 paper [29]), and the calculation reuses an established model without new machinery; the added value is the specific centrality/plane analysis. Internally the paper is honest about several of these limits (the "first order approximation" caveat, the normalization-factor caveat, the acknowledgment of EeE physics), but its flagship interpretive claim outruns what the comparison content can support, and by the time of v2 (2021) the field's understanding of v6 increasingly emphasized initial-state fluctuations — the very ingredient this model lacks — making the conclusion not just unproven but hard to substantiate within the framework used.

# Critique: writing and framing of *Hexagonal flow v₆ as a superposition of elliptic v₂ and triangular v₃ flows* (arXiv 1311.0747v2)

The paper's advertised contribution is modest and well‑scoped: project v₆ into the v₂ and v₃ event planes to attribute hexagonal flow to the two sources, then include jets to check p_T/centrality behavior against two ATLAS/CMS‑type correlators. The honest hedging of the final abstract sentence ("favors the idea that *basic features* … *can be understood*") is better calibrated than the rest of the text. The overclaims cluster in the abstract, in a "conclusion" that is really a model assumption, and in a key algebraic step where an ill‑defined inequality does real work.

## Sentences that overclaim beyond the evidence

- **The v₃‑ratio "scaling" is advertised in the abstract and retracted in the body.** Abstract promises "Scaling of the ratio … v₆^{1/6}/(v₃^{1/3}) in the range p_T ≤ 4 GeV/c for semicentral and semipipheral collisions." But Conclusion (1) states flatly: "**No scaling is found** for the ratio v₆^{1/6/1.3}/(v₃^{1/3})(Ψ₃)," and the body (p.5) says that in the Ψ₃ plane the ratio "decreases for more peripheral collisions" and only *rises* toward the 0.89 plateau at high p_T because of jets — i.e. it is below and non‑flat over the advertised window. The abstract sells a result the paper itself does not get. (See also below on the corrupted notation.)

- **"`the hexagonal flow originates **solely** as a result of nonlinear hydrodynamic response, v₆ ~ v₂³ + v₃²`**" (p.6). "Solely" is a claim about nature, but it is true only because HYDJET++ is *constructed* with no initial v₆ fluctuations, no directed flow (v₁≡0), and Ψ₃ drawn independently of Ψ₂. The source structure is an input, so "solely" overstates what the calculation shows.

Presented as a finding, but it is the very premise of the model: there is no mechanism left in HYDJET++ for a genuine v₆ to enter except nonlinear mixing. The model cannot be *disproven* of genuine v₆ here, so "weakness" is presupposed, not demonstrated. The paper's only empirical leverage on this point is the qualitative match of two correlators.

## Reviewer Report — arXiv:1311.0747v2, "Hexagonal flow v₆ as a superposition of elliptic v₂ and triangular v₃ flows"

**Recommendation: borderline (leaning accept)**
**Score: 6 / 10**

### Summary of the work

The paper studies the hexagonal flow harmonic v₆ in Pb+Pb collisions at √s = 2.76 TeV within the microscopic event generator HYDJET++, which couples parametrized (spatial-anisotropy) hydrodynamics to PYQUEN jets. The central message is that v₆ in this model arises *only* as a nonlinear superposition of the elliptic (v₂) and triangular (v₃) flows, v₆ ≈ (1/6)v₂³ + (1/2)v₃² (Eq. 13), since the model has no genuine hexagonal initial fluctuation. The authors track the v₆ contributions in the v₂- and v₃-event planes separately, show scaling of v₆^{1/6}/v₂^{1/2} in the Ψ₂ plane (in line with the hydrodynamic limit (1/6)v₂ ≃ v₆^{1/6}), and use two-plane correlators cos(6(Ψ₂−Ψ₆)) and cos(6(Ψ₃−Ψ₆)) to show that Ψ₆ interpolates between Ψ₂ and Ψ₃ as centrality changes — a clean explanation of the experimentally observed v₂–v₆ increase versus v₃–v₆ decrease of correlations.

### Strengths

1. **Clear physical picture and transparent analytic backbone.** The saddle-point/Fourier argument (Sec. III) yielding Eq. (13) is simple, reproducible, and gives the correct high-pₜ limits, which anchors the model results.
2. **Useful and somewhat original observables.** The separate analysis of v₆(Ψ₂) and v₆(Ψ₃), and the event-plane correlators connecting Ψ₆ to Ψ₂/Ψ₃ (Figs. 6–7), are well chosen and give the "interplay" story more structure than a plain v₆ vs. centrality plot would.
3. **Honest about the model's limitations.** The authors explicitly note the absence of event-by-event fluctuations, the lack of an evolution stage (so no propagation/density-fluctuation effects), and that no true hexagonal deformation is generated — important context for the reader.
4. **Fair agreement with ATLAS/CMS** for v₂, v₃ and their centrality behavior; the description of the high-pₜ drop of v₂ (attributed to quenched jets) is one of the few features of HYDJET++ that hydrodynamics alone misses, and tying it to v₆ is a nice touch.

### Weaknesses

1. **The main result is not very new.** The relation v₆ ≈ v₂³/6 + v₃²/2 (and more generally the nonlinear/mode-mixing response of higher harmonics) is long established in the hydrodynamic literature (the authors themselves cite Teaney, Borghini–Ollitrault, and the mode-mixing works); for a reader of that literature the analytic part is incremental. The novelty rests on the model-specific implementation and the Ψ-plane separation.
2. **The "no genuine hexagonal fluctuations" claim is asserted, not bounded.** The conclusion that "original hexagonal initial fluctuations seem to play a minor role" is based on the fact that the model *cannot generate them*. Comparing to at least one calculation or measurement that constrains δ₆(centrality) (or to event-by-event hydro that includes ε₆ fluctuations) would materially strengthen the claim.
3. **Model tuning and validation are limited.** HYDJET++ at 2.76 TeV is tuned to global spectra/multiplicities; there is no dedicated check of the v₆(pₜ) spectra against data (the direct data comparison covers v₂ and v₃; for v₆ there is essentially a centrality/tail comparison), no uncertainty bands on the model output, and no test at other energies (e.g., 5.02 or 5.36 TeV, or LHC-run-2 data) that exist for this harmonic. The ratio in the Ψ₃ plane never approaches the quoted limit 0.89, and this discrepancy receives only a brief explanation.
4. Some notation is confusing (e.g., the ε₃(b) "either linked to initial ε₃ or treated as a free parameter"), and the pₜ-range of the scaling statement (1 ≤ pₜ ≤ 3 GeV/c) overlaps with the region where jet contamination is argued to be important — the two messages sit in tension and could be made consistent.

### The three changes that would most improve the paper

1. **Constrain/generate the genuine ε₆ initial fluctuation and quantify its contribution.** Add a (parameterized or event-by-event) hexagonal fluctuation of order δ₆, vary it, and compare the predicted v₆(pₜ) and plane correlations to data to *show* the sub-leading contribution, rather than concluding from the model's inability to produce it. Alternatively, benchmark against at least one modern EbE viscous hydro implementation (MUSIC/vHM, IP-BLAST) that does include ε₆ fluctuations.
2. **Direct quantitative v₆ data comparison with uncertainties.** Compare v₆(pₜ) (not just the low-pₜ and tail values in Fig. 5) to the published ATLAS/CMS v₆ spectra, include model systematic bands (tₗ, ε₃ free parameter, jet fraction), and reconcile the missing approach to the Ψ₃-plane limit 0.89 with an explicit number for the genuine-hexagonal or decorrelation contamination.
3. **Tighten the analytic–numerical consistency.** Estimate the neglected terms of Eq. (13) (the aV₆ term, event-plane correlation ⟨cos k(Ψ₂−Ψ₃)⟩ ≠ 1, multi-particle effects) and show numerically that they are negligible; this also lets the authors state precisely in which pₜ window the scaling claim v₆^{1/6}/v₂^{1/2} ≈ 0.74 is expected to hold, removing the tension with the jet-pollution argument at pₜ ≲ 3 GeV/c.

### Overall judgment

A competent, clearly written phenomenology paper whose physical message (v₆ as an emergent superposition of v₂ and v₃, with Ψ₆ interpolating between the two planes) is well motivated and consistent with LHC data. It is not a breakthrough — the nonlinear superposition itself is standard hydrodynamics — but the plane-separation analysis is useful and the HYDJET++ perspective (in particular the high-pₜ jet effect on v₂- and v₃-originated v₆) adds value. With the three changes above, it would be a solid Phys. Rev. C–type publication; as written, it is a borderline-accept submission.

# Peer Review

**Paper:** *Hexagonal flow $v_6$ as a superposition of elliptic $v_2$ and triangular $v_3$ flows* — Bravina et al., arXiv:1311.0747v2

## Summary

This paper studies the hexagonal flow harmonic $v_6$ of charged hadrons in Pb+Pb collisions at $\sqrt{s}=2.76$ TeV within the HYDJET++ model, which couples a multiphase transport (FASTMC) treatment of hard jets to a spatial flow anisotropy (PYQUEN) for soft, thermal hadrons. The central claim is that the dominant contribution to $v_6$ arises from the nonlinear "mode-mixing" terms induced by the elliptic and triangular flows — $v_6 \propto v_2^3 + v_3^2$ — rather than from an independent "genuine" hexagonal mode $\langle V_6\rangle$. The authors analyze the scaling of the ratios $v_6^{1/6}/v_2^{1/2}$ (in the $\Psi_2$ event plane) and $v_6^{1/6}/v_3^{1/3}$ (in the $\Psi_3$ plane), quantify the effect of jets, and examine two-plane correlators versus centrality.

## Strengths

- **Clean conceptual focus.** Decomposing $v_6$ into a pure-mode piece $\langle aV_6\rangle$ and nonlinear response terms (Eq. 11/12) and tracking how the event plane $\Psi_6$ migrates from $\Psi_3$ (central collisions, triangular-driven) to $\Psi_2$ (peripheral, elliptic-driven) is an understandable and self-consistent framing. The event plane correlators (Figs. 6–7) are used sensibly and match the qualitative centrality trends reported by ATLAS/CMS.
- **Reasonable quantitative consistency.** Agreement of the $v_2$ and $v_3$ $p_t$ spectra with ATLAS/CMS data (Figs. 1–2) at LHC energies is adequate, and the $v_6$ centrality dependence from Fig. 5 is compatible with CMS-extracted values.
- **Self-awareness about jets.** Quantifying that jets increase $v_6$ by roughly 10% and generate a high-$p_t$ tail is a useful concrete statement, and the drop of $v_2/v_6$ above $p_t\gtrsim 3$ GeV/c is tied to a specific physical argument (jets have weaker elliptic flow).

## Weaknesses and concerns

- **The core novelty is largely kinematic/trivial.** Once one writes $u_{\max}(\phi)$ as a single Fourier series, $v_6 \approx \frac16 v_2^3 + \frac12 v_3^2$ (Eq. 13) follows almost mechanically under the single-event-plane approximation that the paper itself acknowledges is a "first-order approximation." The result is essentially an algebraic consequence of assuming the high-momentum-velocity surface is described by a single superposition of two anisotropies. The paper does not confront the known fact that at RHIC/LHC the genuine $\langle V_6\rangle$ and higher-order mode-mixing (the $e_6$ initial eccentricity, and cross terms from $v_4$, etc.) are non-negligible; treating $\langle aV_6\rangle\ll v_2^2$ is assumed, not demonstrated.
- **No genuinely new physics content.** HYDJET++ is a pre-existing, well-tested model, and the paper applies an already-known decomposition to a higher harmonic. The added value is limited to bookkeeping of the interplay and a set of event-plane correlators, not a new mechanism or a quantitatively predictive claim beyond reproducing existing data trends.
- **The "no-genuine-mode" claim is stronger than the analysis justifies.** The authors conclude "original hexagonal initial fluctuations seem to play a minor role," but they never fit a free $v_6$-eccentricity response to the data; $\epsilon_6(t)$ is simply not included. One cannot distinguish "small genuine $v_6$" from "an unconstrained parameter the model does not have."
- **Scaling discussion is somewhat overstated.** The "scaling" of $v_6^{1/6}/v_2^{1/2}\approx 1/6^{1/6}$ in the $\Psi_2$ plane is presented as a notable observation, but it is the direct consequence of Eq. 13 at high $p_t$ where $v_2^3$ dominates $v_3^2$; the centrality interval and $p_t$ window in which it holds are narrow, and the deviation in the $\Psi_3$ plane (Fig. 4) is not surprising given the mixed-plane geometry.
- **Model-specific caveats not fully quantified.** The absence of an EBE hydrodynamic stage, the neglect of event-by-event geometry fluctuations in the $\epsilon_6$ channel, and the use of $v_n^{1/n}$-adjusted ratios rather than raw $v_n$ all affect the $v_6$ inference. The paper mentions these but does not estimate the size of the induced uncertainty on the headline conclusion.
- **Figures 6/7** show correlators with large error bars (2–3 points each); the centrality trend is suggestive but not statistically strong, and the "ATLAS-like" behavior is asserted with limited quantitative comparison.
- There are minor presentation issues: a stray non-formatted URL in reference [24] (highlighted in the text), and the "scaling ratio" claims would benefit from explicit numerical fits.

## Verdict

The paper is competent, clearly written, and internally consistent. It offers a reasonable, model-based account of how $v_6$ can be dominated by mode-mixing of $v_2$ and $v_3$, and it uses event-plane correlators appropriately. However, the central result is an almost-algebraic consequence of the model's single-velocity-surface ansatz, the "genuine $v_6\approx 0$" conclusion is an assumption rather than a tested prediction, and the work adds no new mechanism or strong quantitative advance over known flow-harmonic decomposition. It would fit a solid conference-proceedings or letters-style treatment; for a high-impact regular article it is incremental.

Scores: a clear but modest contribution — solid but not novel on the core point, methodologically acceptable, impact largely confined to a model-bookkeeping of a known effect.

# Review of "Hexagonal flow v6 as a superposition of elliptic v2 and triangular v3 flows" (arXiv:1311.0747v2)

## What the paper does

The work studies the hexagonal flow harmonic v6 in the HYDJET++ hybrid model (parametrized hydrodynamics + PYQUEN jets) for Pb+Pb at √s = 2.76 TeV, semicentral to semiperipheral centralities. The main claims:

1. A saddle-point (Borghini–Ollitrault-type) derivation giving v6 ≈ v2³/6 + v3²/2 at high p_t under a fixed single-event-plane approximation.
2. v6{Ψ2} scales with v2 (the ratio v6^{1/6}{Ψ2}/v2^{1/2}{Ψ2} is centrality-independent above p_t ≈ 1 GeV/c), whereas v6{Ψ3}/v3^{1/3}{Ψ3} does *not* scale and decreases peripherally.
3. A scenario in which Ψ6 follows Ψ3 in semicentral collisions (v3-dominated) and follows Ψ2 peripherally (v2³-dominated), explaining the experimentally observed rise of ⟨cos 6(Ψ2−Ψ6)⟩ and fall of ⟨cos 6(Ψ3−Ψ6)⟩ with impact parameter — and explicitly disputing the AMPT interpretation [39] that the latter falloff is due to the decrease of v3 itself.
4. Jets lift both scaling ratios by ∼10–15% and bend the high-p_t tails upward.

## What is good

- **The central narrative is physically sensible and clearly motivated.** In a model whose freeze-out surface genuinely contains only n = 2 and n = 3 Fourier components, v6 is a *pure* nonlinear-response observable. That is an honest and useful clean-room setup: whatever v6 the model generates must come from the v2/v3 interplay, so the decomposition (i) vs. (ii) in the discussion is unambiguous *within the model*, in a way it is not in event-by-event hydro where initial ε6 exists.
- **The event-plane correlator analysis (Figs. 6–7) is well chosen.** It isolates the *angle* correlation from the projected-signal analysis of Fig. 5, and the contrast between the two panels is exactly the diagnostic the scenario needs. Comparing with ATLAS [38] is appropriate.
- **The paper is candid about limitations**: no directed flow, no event-by-event evolution (hot spots not propagated), the model's known overestimation of v2 at p_t ≳ 2 GeV/c, and the explicit caveat that Eq. (13) is a single-plane approximation.
- The figures are clean, and the p_t, centrality, and correlator results are internally consistent; the data (ATLAS v2, v3; CMS references) are laid down honestly, with the "fair" agreement for v2 and v3 qualified and pointed back to [26].

## Problems / what to fix as a student

1. **The headline numbers in the abstract and conclusions overstate what is shown.** The abstract says jets "increase this ratio by about 10%," while the conclusions say "10%–15%." Fig. 3(b) shows an effect that is centrality- and p_t-dependent and is at most ~0.05 in absolute terms on a 0.74 ratio — the *rise above the ideal-hydro plateau* at p_t ≥ 3.5 GeV/c, not a uniform 10% lift, is the clean observable effect. Fix the quantitative claim to match the figures.

2. **Derivation gaps in Sec. III.** Equation (12) drops terms and keeps 3(aV2)(aV4) while discarding (aV3)(aV5)-type couplings — but with a single Fourier-expanded u_max there is no reason aV6 or aV4 terms vanish; they are just set to zero because the model has no V4, V6. That should be stated, not derived. More importantly, going from (12) to (13) implicitly divides v6 by v2³ + v3² in a *global* plane. In the multi-plane case the correct identity involves weighted sums over phase combinations (e.g. cos 6(Ψ6 − 3Ψ2) and cos 6(Ψ6 − 2Ψ3) terms); the paper hand-waves this with "first-order approximation" and then builds its *entire* phenomenology on it. At least spell out the multi-plane version of the decomposition and state when it and the single-plane one coincide (strong plane correlations) and when they differ.

3. **Figure 1 vs. Fig. 5 tension is not discussed.** In Fig. 1, v6{Ψ2} at fixed centrality is small and only rises at high p_t, while Fig. 5 shows v6{Ψ2} growing *with peripheralization* including in the p_t < 1 GeV/c bin (open squares nearly flat-to-rising). The interpretation "v2³ dominates peripherally" needs the model's absolute v2(p_t) shape (which falls above 3 GeV/c, the jet-driven drop) to be spelled out, otherwise a reader can't see why v2³/6 beats v3²/2 in the peripheral limit. Give the actual model values of v2 and v3 at each centrality to make the crossover explicit; one equation (v2³/6 > v3²/2 ⇔ v2 > 3^{1/2} v3^{2/3} etc.) would do it.

4. **The rebuttal of [39] is too strong.** Your two reasons — (i) v2 dominance peripherally, (ii) ⟨Ψ2, Ψ3⟩ decorrelation — apply in a model with no initial ε6 and no genuine v3 plane–v3-plane evolution. In full hydro, the drop of ⟨cos 6(Ψ3−Ψ6)⟩ peripherally can also reflect mode-coupling weights and initial-state ε3 fluctuations. Claiming "this is not the case, because v3 is approximately the same" (see Fig. 2 where v3 indeed is roughly flat) is one piece of evidence, not proof — the falloff in the correlator can come from the *plane* not from the *magnitude*. Be precise about which claim you are refuting.

5. **Missing cross-checks.** No comparison with a modern 3+1D viscous hydro calculation of the same correlators (e.g. MUSIC+JETSCAPE-era results or later Bhalerao/Petruzzelli studies). Without any independent model check, the "original hexagonal fluctuations seem to play a minor role" conclusion cannot escape the assumption of the tool. State the conclusion strictly as "in HYDJET++, where initial ε6 = 0 by construction, the observed centrality dependence of the Ψ6–Ψn correlators is reproduced by the v2³ + v3² interplay alone" — which is defensible — and flag it as one data point in a larger question.

6. **Minor issues.**
   - Fig. 3 caption lists "20–30% (triangles pointing up), 20–30% (triangles pointing down)" — one of these is 30–40% (Fig. 4 caption has the same typo).
   - "posseses" (Sec. II) → possesses.
   - Eq. (4) uses s as the square root; use √.
   - Reference [24] is a URL; find the publication.
   - The abstract says "1 ≤ p_t ≤ 4 GeV/c" but the scaling claim is best supported in 1–3 GeV/c; the 3.5–4 GeV/c region is precisely where it breaks (jet-driven rise).

## Bottom line

The paper identifies a clean, model-internal mechanism for how v6 plane correlations migrate from Ψ3 to Ψ2 with peripheralization, and presents it with the right observables (projected harmonics *and* two-plane correlators). Its value is mostly in the *controlled* setting HYDJET++ provides (no ε6 by construction), which lets the v2³/6 + v3²/2 decomposition do real diagnostic work. Its main weakness is extrapolation from a single, somewhat dated parametrization model to a general statement about the origin of v6 — a conclusion the tools chosen for the study cannot by themselves establish. Tighten the derivation, align the jet-effect numbers with the figures, correct the Fig. 3/4 caption typos, and scope the conclusions to the model.
