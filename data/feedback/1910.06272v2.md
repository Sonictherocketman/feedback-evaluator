## Summary of the argument

The paper's central thesis is that high-energy (>keV) visible-particle energy injection does **not** deposit its energy instantaneously — a cascade of ICS, pair production, and re-scattering spreads the deposition over many Hubble times. Because the deposition is delayed, the CMB anisotropies at recombination are sensitive to energy that was actually injected far earlier (up to z ~ 10000). The authors build a *unified* code that evolves the full electron+positron+photon cascade in the expanding universe (no arbitrary low/high-energy split, no static-universe Monte Carlo for sub-keV deposition), feed modified ionization fractions into `Refast++`/`CosmoRec`, and use `CosmoMC` + Planck 2015 to derive CMB-anisotropy limits on the decaying fraction vs. lifetime. They also re-derive BBN and CMB-spectral-distortion constraints with the same delayed-deposition calculation.

The conclusion is that CMB anisotropies are the strongest probe up to z_X ~ 10000 (i.e. new lower-lifetime limits), and that relaxing the quasi-static/on-the-spot assumption weakens the BBN limits by a factor of a few to nearly an order of magnitude.

## Strengths worth affirming

- **The unified-cascade tool is a genuine technical contribution.** Prior work (Slatyer 2016, ref 38) had to split the physics at an arbitrary ~3 keV interface, a seam whose placement was flagged as causing percent-to-few-percent changes. Removing that seam and the static-universe Monte Carlo is exactly the right thing to do, and the low/high-energy physics is then handled self-consistently.
- **The "delay" physics is the real physical insight.** Framing the whole problem around the injection-to-deposition timescale is a clean unifying thread and correctly motivates why the CMB, not BBN or distortions, is most sensitive in a specific redshift window.
- **Sanity checks are present and appropriate:** the excitation-to-higher-levels comparison (CosmoRec vs Refast++ in Fig. 4) is shown to sit below the cosmic variance; the 6-ACDM degeneracy scan (Table 3, Fig. 14) demonstrates there is no meaningful leak into standard parameters.
- **The BBN comparison is energy-aware.** The authors correctly note (via ref 43) that the older "universal photon spectrum" assumption is inaccurate and that the ~30 MeV channel is special (pair-production threshold on the CMB), so they show the limits as a function of injected photon energy rather than assuming a single number. That is more honest than many papers in this subfield.

## Helpful critique — where the argument is weaker

1. **The analysis still rides on Planck 2015, and the "2018 won't change it" move is asserted, not demonstrated.** The whole selling point of the paper is that *precision* makes the CMB a sensitive probe of early injection. Appendix D argues that 2018 mainly shifts τ and that "no degeneracy" means the limits are unaffected. That is plausible but the argument would be far stronger if the authors had actually re-run the MCMC with the 2018 likelihood. As it stands, the paper's own central claim rests on the stale likelihood, and the update is left to faith.

2. **BBN statistics are handled coarsely.** The "maximum allowed production/destruction" is obtained by subtracting the *theoretical 2σ lower limit* from the *observed 2σ upper limit* (or, for ³He, from the one-sided observed upper bound 1.5×10⁻⁵, which is not even a 2σ central value). Subtracting σ-boundaries as if they were central values is a loose error-propagation that systematically *inflates* the allowed margin — the very thing the paper then uses to claim the limits relax. A joint likelihood over B(6), n_D, Y_p (and n_³He) with proper correlated PDFs would be more defensible and could change the "weaker by a few to an order of magnitude" headline.

3. **The "CMB is strongest up to z_X ~ 10000" claim is stated more broadly than the evidence supports.** The comparison against BBN and distortions is energy- and channel-dependent (the authors themselves single out the ~26 MeV photon channel as BBN-dominated). In several corners of the (energy, lifetime) plane the picture is a narrow crossover, not a clean regime; the sweeping statement "CMB anisotropy constraints are stronger compared to BBN and CMB spectral distortions for z_X ≤ 10000 for all initial energies except…" deserves a figure-by-figure justification in the body, not just an aggregate.

4. **The monochromatic-only, band-envelope interpretation is an untested upper bound.** Fig. 8 presents a band (10 keV–1 TeV) and claims any general channel with most energy above 10 keV is bounded by it. But the *optimal* (tightest) energy differs by channel and by lifetime; a realistic decay with a broad multi-channel spectrum can be dominated by an energy *outside* the sampled set, or lie on the envelope in a way that isn't simply "bounded by the band." Presenting the result as a constraint *surface* rather than per-mass curves would overstate its generality.

5. **Apparent internal tension in the BBN messaging.** The paper's flagship message is that *delay* matters, yet it also states the BBN limits are lifetime-independent for τ_X ≫ 10⁶ s because the (1+z)³ rate scaling matches the (1+z)³ energy dilution. That lifetime-independence derivation assumes on-the-spot absorption; the subsequent "weakening by a few to an order of magnitude from relaxing quasi-static" is a partially separate effect. The two statements should be reconciled more explicitly so a reader doesn't think both the on-the-spot scaling and the delayed-deposition relaxation are being claimed simultaneously as the BBN behavior.

6. **The positron = electron + two 511 keV photons shortcut is a minor but real approximation.** The annihilation photons themselves re-enter the cascade (pair production, ICS); folding them in as a fixed 2×511 keV floor ignores their subsequent history. This is standard in the field, but in a unified-code paper it is worth a sentence quantifying when it breaks down.

7. **Stated-but-deferred excitation treatment for the recombination line spectrum** is the right thing to punt, but the conclusion would read better if it flagged that the *most distinctive* CMB probe of this energy injection (the recombination spectrum, unique to non-equilibrium) is deliberately left out — as it stands, the recombination-spectrum motivation (Sec. 8) is not backed by any calculation.

## Bottom line

A competent, technically careful paper that extends decaying-DM limits into a genuinely new lower-lifetime regime with a cleaner cascade tool, and whose "delayed deposition" framing is sound and useful. The core physics argument holds. The main soft spots are (a) the use of 2015 data for a precision argument, (b) the coarse σ-subtraction statistics in the BBN section that underpin the "limits relax" headline, and (c) somewhat over-broad regime claims plus a constraint-surface that is only as general as its monochromatic, finite-band sampling. None are fatal; together they keep it a solid refinement of an established program rather than a decisive advance.

## [1910.06272v2-critique] Critique


# Critique: Acharya & Khatri, "CMB anisotropy and BBN constraints on pre-recombination decay of dark matter to visible particles" (arXiv:1910.06272v2)

## Summary
The paper studies electromagnetic energy injection into the primordial plasma from dark matter decay (or, more generally, long-lived unstable particles), and derives constraints from (i) CMB anisotropies (Planck 2015) and (ii) primordial-element abundances (BBN). The central physical point is that high-energy (>keV) electromagnetic particles do **not** deposit their energy instantaneously: the cascade runs for many Hubble times, so energy injected well *before* recombination is still deposited around recombination. The authors build a unified code that evolves the full cascade (eV–TeV, low and high energy together, sub-keV particles evolving in the expanding Universe rather than tabulated from a static-UN Monte Carlo) and show CMB anisotropies are sensitive to injection as early as z_X ≈ 10 000, giving their strongest constraints there. They further show that relaxing the quasi-static/on-the-spot assumption weakens BBN constraints by a factor of a few to ~an order of magnitude.

## Strengths
- **Genuine technical advance.** The unified cascade treatment (no low/high-energy split, no reliance on static-UN sub-keV deposition tables, no arbitrary ~keV interface) directly addresses a known brittleness of prior work ([30], [38]). The Fig. 1 footnote and sensitivity discussion (interface at 1–10 keV changing results by "few percent" vs. "much bigger" when using [38]'s tables) make a concrete case for why the old factorization was fragile. This is the most defensible contribution.
- **Careful physics validation.** The claim that excited states are negligible for the anisotropies is properly checked (Fig. 3 de-excitation/photo-ionization ratio, Fig. 4 direct comparison of Refcast++ vs CosmoRec against the cosmic variance), and they cross-check against Slatyer [30] and Poulin [31] in the τ_X > 10^13 s regime where agreement is expected.
- **The high-z_X result is a real extension.** Demonstrating the (a) survival of high-energy photons and (b) "messengers carrying information from z ~10 000" that shift the strongest constraint from the recombination peak (z~1000–1200) to z_X~10 000 is the cleanest new piece of information, and is stated honestly (small surviving fraction, but large enough that CMB remains competitive).
- **Honest treatment of the BBN result.** Rather than hiding that delayed deposition makes their BBN bound weaker than the literature's, they explain the mechanism (more heating dilutes the destructive fraction) and quantify it.
- **Parameter degeneracy check is thorough** (Appendix D, Table 3, Fig. 14) — no significant migration of the 6 ΛCDM parameters.

## Weaknesses
- **Monochromatic injection only, and the "band" is only an approximate generalization.** The constraints are derived for single-energy e+e− and γγ channels; the Fig. 8 band (10 keV–1 TeV) is argued to cover "any general decay channel" provided most energy lands in particles ≥10 keV, but a realistic decay produces broad spectra and the constraints are genuinely energy-dependent (Fig. 9 shows a factor-of-several spread with mass). Leaving arbitrary spectra to "future work" while claiming broad interpretability overstates the generality.
- **Numerical resolution is only argued, not demonstrated.** The cascade is binned into 200 logarithmic cells over more than a decade in energy (eV→TeV) and the timestep is checked for independence of deposition, but there is no explicit resolution/timestep convergence test shown for the *final* constraints. Given that cascade calculations are notoriously grid-sensitive and the deposition fractions near the interface are shown to be nontrivial, this is a notable gap.
- **Uses Planck 2015 likelihoods.** Justified (no degeneracy with τ via Appendix D), but for a precision paper that is now ~a decade old, 2018 likelihoods with CMB-S4/PRISM forecasts of the type they use for PIXIE would be stronger.
- **The recombination-spectrum argument is asserted, not done.** They argue the excitation signature in the primordial recombination line spectrum will matter and defer it to future work; this is the physically interesting "unique probe" they raise, and its absence means the most novel observational lever they identify is unquantified.
- **The BBN section is comparatively thin** and leans entirely on the [78] photodissociation cross-sections; the payoff (weaker constraints than [41–45]) is a cautionary rather than constructive result.
- **Limited standalone scientific impact.** The "stronger" region (very short τ_X) is where COBE-FIRAS/PIXIE spectral-distortion constraints already dominate (Figs. 8, 9), so the CMB anisotropy constraint they add covers a modest slice of genuinely new parameter space.

## Verdict
A solid, technically careful paper. The unified cascade code and the extension of CMB constraints to z_X ~ 10 000 are legitimate, well-motivated advances over prior factorized treatments, and the physics is validated against multiple independent codes. The main limitations are the monochromatic-injection scope, the lack of an explicit convergence study, and the modest net impact — much of the new constraint region is either already covered by spectral-distortion bounds or an extension of the authors' own recent line of work ([46], [35,36]). A good but incremental contribution to the decaying-DM phenomenology literature.

Originality: builds closely on the authors' prior cascade and spectral-distortion work; the unified code and the z_X → 10 000 result are the genuinely new elements. Overall: methodologically sound and well-validated, with real (if modest) gaps. Impact: useful constraint updates and a cautionary BBN result, but not field-shaping.

# Evaluation

## Stated contribution, in my own words

Acharya and Khatri build one unified numerical code that evolves the full electromagnetic cascade — e⁺/e⁻ and photons from eV up to TeV — in an expanding Universe, with the sub-keV atomic physics (ionization, excitation, heating) solved at every time-step rather than factored out as tabulated static-geometry fractions. Then they feed that time-resolved energy-deposition history into Recfast++/CosmoMC and into a BBN network, and use Planck 2015 likelihoods to put 2-σ upper limits on the fraction f_X·f_EM of total dark matter decaying to electromagnetic channels, as a function of lifetime τ_χ and initial injected particle energy (10 keV–1 TeV). The paper has three specific quantitative results: (i) CMB anisotropies constrain energy injected as late as z ≃ 10 000 (τ_χ ≲ 10¹¹·³ s), a region of parameter space previous CMB-anisotropy analyses did not cover; (ii) for generic monochromatic decay energies above ~10 keV, the CMB-anisotropy line is the tightest of all probes (CMB anisotropy, BBN, CMB spectral distortion) up to z_X ≲ 10 000; and (iii) re-derived BBN constraints — once the cascade is actually evolved rather than assumed to deposit on the spot — are weaker than the values in Kawasaki et al., Kawasaki et al. 2018, POUlin & Serpico, Hafnigal et al., Forestell et al. by a factor of a few up to almost an order of magnitude.

## What is genuinely new, given the prior work they cite

1. **A single code bridging eV and TeV** that avoids the 3 keV interface used in Slatyer 2016 (cited as [38]) and its static-sensitivity (Fig. 1). This is a technical, not conceptual, improvement; the high-energy half of it is the authors' own prior paper (Acharya & Khatri PRD 99, 2018 [46]), the sub-keV half is standard Bethe/ICSDb work.
2. **The new parameter range τ_χ < 10¹³ s (z_X > 4500)** for CMB anisotropy constraints. Earlier CMB analyses (Slatyer & Wu 2017 [30], Poulin 2017 [31]) either used the long-lifetime scaling f_X·EM/τ ∝ const (Slatyer) or the on-spot ansatz with a z-dependent effective f_EI(z) (Poulin), and Poulin 2017 explicitly noted the factorization into f(ionization fraction, z, E) degrades at high ionization. Working in that new regime is the substantive physical addition, and the "CMB anisotropy is the strongest probe for z_X ≲ 10 000" claim is a genuine (if modest) refinement of the relative-ranking picture in Fig. 8.
3. **The BBN weakening.** All five referenced BBN references [41,42,43,44,45] used quasi-static or on-spot deposition. The authors' re-derivation that those limits were too aggressive by a factor of ~3 for ³He overproduction and up to ~10× for ²H destruction is a concrete, falsifiable correction; the mechanism (energy lost to heating/escaping photons before it can photodissociate ²H/⁴He) is physically sensible.
4. **PIXIE forecast line** in Fig. 8 and the ⁴He vs ³He energy-threshold structure as a function of injected photon energy (Fig. 10) — useful bookkeeping, not new physics.

## What is NOT new

- The overarching framework (CMB anisotropy + BBN + CMB-SR constrain decaying DM) is already established in [10,12,28,30,31,41–43,46,63].
- The fact that on-spot deposition is wrong at high redshift due to the cooling-time/Hubble-time competition was already understood (Kanazaki 2010 [36], POUlin 2017 [31], Slatyer 2016 [38]). This paper's contribution is implementing it more consistently, not discovering it.
- The role of surviving high-energy photons from early injection (z ≫ 1000) in modifying the recombination history, and the resulting damping-tail and polarization effects, are already in the literature the authors cite ([35,36,46,57]).
- The specific DM decay phenomenology (monochromatic e⁺e⁻/γγ, two-body cascade physics, ICS dominance above 10 keV, atomic vs ICS crossover redshift) is taken wholesale from Slatyer 2009/2013/2016, Kanazaki-Moroi, POUlin 2017.
- The use of Recfast++ for DM-decay CMB anisotropies was already done by Slatyer & Wu 2017 [30].
- The claim that CMB anisotropies are stronger than BBN for z_X ≲ 10 000 is largely a restatement at the edge of an existing fact already visible in Slatyer & Wu 2017 (which found the CMB peak at τ ≈ 10¹⁴·³ s ≈ z_X ≈ 1200) and Poulin 2017.

## Bottom line

The paper is a careful, well-executed engineering extension of the authors' existing cascade code [46] to the lower-lifetime end of the decaying-DM constraint landscape, with a secondary (and more interesting) correction to the BBN literature. The originality is in the unified implementation and in two specific quantitative refinements; it is not in the physics, the method, or the phenomenology, all of which are borrowed from the cited literature. The "new region of parameter space" (z_X ~ 1000, τ_χ ~ 10¹¹ s) does extend the curve a step, but the extension is short and the constraints there are not dramatically tighter than the BBN/SR line in the same region (Fig. 8). The BBN weakening result has the most independent scientific value because it contradicts and improves on five previous papers, but its size (factor of a few to an order of magnitude) is at the tail of what an audience can expect from correcting a known, if approximate, prior assumption.

## [1910.06272v2-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


# Methodology & Assumptions — *CMB anisotropy and BBN constraints on pre-recombination decay of dark matter to visible particles* (Acharya & Khatri, JCAP 2020)

## What needs to be reproduced

The "central experiment" is a 2-σ upper limit on the product `f_X · f_EM` (decaying fraction of DM × fraction of decay energy going to visible EM particles) as a function of the DM lifetime `τ_X` (i.e. decay redshift `z_X ≥ 10000` down to post-recombination), from two independent probes: **CMB anisotropy** and **BBN**, both fed by a *unified full electromagnetic-cascade code* that explicitly evolves the cascade against background expansion rather than using the quasi-static / on-the-spot / static-MC approximations used in earlier work.

To reproduce it you must stack four pieces: (i) the cascade code, (ii) a recombination code with extra source terms, (iii) an MCMC over CMB likelihoods, and (iv) a BBN book-keeping layer.

---

## 1. Energy-injection parameterisation

- Decay of a fraction `f_X` of DM with lifetime `τ_X`, injecting energy density at rate
  `dE_inj/dt = (f_X/τ_X) · ρ_c · c² · (1+z)³ · e^{−t/τ_X}` (Eq. 4.1; Eq. 4.2 for a finite `Δt`).
- Monochromatic decay to one of: photon pair `γγ` (E_γ = m_X/2) or `e⁺e⁻` (each with kinetic energy E₀ = (m_X − m_e)/2).
- Only the product `f_X · f_EM` is fitted; `f_EM` = fraction of decay energy in visible EM (rest may go to neutrinos, dark radiation, etc.).
- Energy injection is treated as a source / sink on the recombination Boltzmann equations; the constraint is reported as a 2-σ upper limit `f_X f_EM` as a function of `τ_X`.

## 2. Unified electromagnetic-cascade code (Section 2)

This is the core new numerical piece.

- **Energy partition**: divide the eV–TeV range into **200 logarithmic bins**, per particle species (e⁻, e⁺, γ).
- **Equations of motion**: coupled set of ODEs for the spectrum of each species in each bin, including:
  - collisional / radiative energy losses to background H, He, baryons and the CMB (e.g. ionization, excitation, Coulomb drag, IC, double-Compton, pair annihilation, pair production, photon recoil, Thomson);
  - **background expansion** explicitly (a photon bin's central redshift changes as the Universe expands — the "redshifted energy loss" channel in Fig. 2).
- **Time-stepping**: at each cosmic timestep, high-energy particles are treated as a dilute test population on top of the background (interaction rates between two injected high-energy particles are neglected because n_inj << n_bg). The cascade is marched high-energy → low-energy first (triangular linear system, back-substitution), so the contribution of sub-keV products generated at one timestep are reused at every higher-energy timestep ("recursion").
- **Low-energy (sub-keV) physics** is now evolved in the *same* code as the high-energy cascade (no static Monte Carlo tables, unlike Kankazaki–Kawasaki–Nakayama), removing the 3-keV interface of Valdes–Evoli–Ferrara [38]. Cross-sections come from the Appendix:
  - tabulated CCC data below ~keV, Bethe approximations above (Eqs. A.1–A.7),
  - photo-ionization cross sections (Appendix B, Eqs. B.1–B.3): H (Kramers-style with bound-free absorption), neutral He (piecewise power-law fit to the cross section around 50–250 eV).
- **Positrons**: approximated as an electron of same kinetic energy plus two 511 keV photons from annihilation.
- **Output**: energy-deposition fraction per cosmic time `f_total(z) = ΔE_dep/Δt ÷ ΔE_inj/Δt` (Eq. 4.3), channel-resolved, where channels = heating, excitation (H/He levels), ionization (H/He ground, He +), and surviving sub-10.2 eV + higher-energy photons (the sub-10.2 eV part feeds the CMB spectral distortion; high-energy photons continue to the next timestep).
- **Consistency checks performed in the paper** (so you should reproduce them when re-implementing): t_ion vs. cosmic variance, comparison to [30] for τ_X ≳ 10¹³ s, comparison to [31] for τ_X ≲ 10¹² s, dependence on the 3-keV interface (few-% for 1–100 keV), timestep independence.

## 3. Recombination / baryon-coupling (Section 3)

## Understanding of the paper's logical argument

**Physical premise.** When dark matter decays (or long-lived particles decay) into visible high-energy (>keV) electrons, positrons, and photons in the early Universe, that energy is *not* deposited on the spot. Instead the particles cascade — bremsstrahlung, γγ pair production, inverse Compton scattering, photo/collisional ionization and excitation, plus low-energy (sub-10.2 eV) photon emission — down to the eV scale, where the energy is absorbed as heat or escapes as a CMB spectral-distortion line. Crucially, high-energy *photons* interact at rates comparable to the Hubble rate for z ≲ 10⁴, so they survive and redshift, acting as "messengers" that carry the memory of energy injected at redshifts far above recombination down to the recombination epoch.

**The gap in prior work.** Earlier constraints focused on energy injection *around* recombination (z ≲ 4500) and assumed "on-the-spot" / quasi-static deposition — energy deposited at the same redshift it was injected, or a factorized treatment using static, redshift-independent Monte-Carlo low-energy deposition fractions (e.g. Slatyer et al., Poulin et al.). The authors argue this on-the-spot assumption is precisely what fails for short lifetimes (short τ_χ), and that the *delay* between injection and deposition materially changes the bounds.

**Method.** They build a unified code that co-evolves the full cascade from eV to TeV in 200 log-spaced bins *in an expanding Universe*, as a system of coupled ODEs / triangular linear algebraic equations solved by back-substitution. They deliberately do *not* factor into a separate Monte-Carlo sub-keV piece and a relativistic cascade piece; both are evolved consistently, with sub-keV particles also tracked in the expanding (rather than static) background. The resulting redshift-dependent energy-deposition fractions f_i(z) (ionization, heating, excitation, escaping photons) are injected as extra source/sink terms into the multi-level recombination equations of Recfast++ (validated against CosmoRec, including higher atomic levels), and the resulting anisotropy spectra are fit to Planck 2015 data with CosmoMC. They separately revisit the BBN constraints by evolving the photo-dissociation network (²H, ³He, ⁴He) with the same cascade engine.

1. CMB anisotropies are sensitive to energy injected as late as z ≈ 10⁻⁴… no — injected at *redshifts as high as* z ≈ 10⁰⁰⁰⁰ (z = 10000), i.e. τ_χ ≲ 10^13 s, a regime where BBN and spectral distortions are weaker. CMB is the strongest probe for z_X ≲ 10000.
2. Their results agree with Slatyer & Wu / Poulin et al. for τ_χ ≳ 10^13 s (f_χ ∝ τ_χ scaling) and extend cleanly into the shorter-lifetime regime.
3. Relaxing the on-the-spot / quasi-static assumption **weakens** the BBN (and, more modestly, CMB) bounds by a factor of a few to nearly an order of magnitude, because the delayed deposit gives energy a higher probability of being thermalized (heating) rather than driving destructive nuclear photo-dissociation.

The weakest link is **the step where the central claim "CMB anisotropies are sensitive to energy injection even at z = 10 000" is taken to be established by a single, unvalidated MCMC run** (Fig. 8, Section 5). The conclusion is *presented* as a qualitative physical insight — surviving high-energy photons acting as messengers from very early times — but is *delivered* as a number from one Planck-2015 MCMC with their homemade cascade code, with no independent validation, no multipole breakdown, and no negative control.

Concretely, the gap has three interlocking parts:

- **No validation of the mechanism, only of the number.** The paper does not run a "disable surviving high-energy photons" variant of the code to show that the CMB constraint at z_X ≲ 10⁴ disappears without it, nor a multipole-ℓ breakdown identifying which (C_ℓ^T/E) modes carry the constraint. Figure 7 shows the *integrated* deposition fraction f_total(z) but never directly quantifies the surviving high-energy photon fraction for the shortest lifetimes. So the causal mechanism (surviving photons → residual free electrons at recombination → E-mode/temperature anomaly) is asserted, not demonstrated, while the abstract's "CMB anisotropies are sensitive to energy injection even at z = 10 000" reads as if it were.

- **No negative control against the on-the-spot limit.** The whole point of the paper is that the on-the-spot approximation is invalid at τ_X ≲ 10¹³ s. The paper claims "broad agreement for τ_X > 10¹³ s with the work of [30]", but Figure 8 does not contain a curve that *reproduces* Poulhin et al. or Slater & Wu in the regime where those should agree. Without this, the new parameter region (τ_X < 10¹³ s) cannot be cross-anchored: the entire new constraint curve hangs from the code's own extrapolation.

- **The "weakening" trend is attributed to a mechanism that the numbers do not isolate.** The paper states (around Eq. 4.4) that the constraint weakens with decreasing τ_X because "a tiny fraction of energy survives until recombination… still [is] large enough for CMB anisotropies to provide constraints". But by τ ~ 10¹¹ s the ionization-fraction channel alone (injection while the universe is still fully ionized → modified x_e, x_He at recombination) can already do significant work — energy deposited in a fully ionized plasma changes the subsequent recombination history regardless of photon survival. The paper never disentangles these two channels, so the "surviving photon" mechanism the paper advertises as its central physical insight is not the quantity that the data actually isolates; the data just shows that the MCMC constraint curve bends.

In short: the data (a single Planck 2015 MCMC with one code) does not entail the stronger, more specific claim in the abstract that surviving high-energy photons are the channel that makes z ~ 10⁴ sensitive to the CMB. The numbers are consistent with that mechanism, but nothing in the paper shows that removing it would change the constraint. A one-line robustness check — running the same MCMC with high-energy photon survival switched off, or with a sub-set of Planck multipoles — would close the gap, and its absence is the single step where the conclusion exceeds the data.

(Minor corroborating issues, not the weakest link themselves: no cross-check against independent cascade codes such as the DarkHistory implementation; no Planck 2018 update despite the paper citing it; the "10 keV–1 TeV band" generalization to arbitrary decay channels is justified by the *claim* that GeV-scale cascades converge to an e⁺e⁻/γ mixture, which Fig. 9 only partially supports — photon-injection constraints for short lifetimes do visibly shift with initial energy.)

This is a careful, computational cosmology paper (Acharya & Khatri, TIFR) on constraints from CMB anisotropies and BBN on pre-recombination electromagnetic energy injection from decaying dark matter / long-lived particles. Let me give you a structured review.

## What the paper does and does well

**Methodology (the core contribution).** The main technical advance is a unified code that evolves the sub-keV *and* high-energy parts of the electromagnetic cascade simultaneously in the expanding Universe, rather than the two-part factorization of Valdes et al. (2010)/Kanzaki & Kawasaki that underlies most prior work. The motivation for unifying is well laid out: previous codes assumed a ~3 keV interface and tabulated static-universe sub-keV deposition, which the authors show is sensitive to that choice. They also drop the quasi-static / on-the-spot (instantaneous-deposition) approximation by explicitly tracking how long photons survive the cascade — this is the conceptual thread of the whole paper.

**New results.** (i) CMB-anisotropy constraints extended to τχ ≲ 10¹³ s (zχ up to ~10⁴), a parameter region previously unconstrained by anisotropies in detail. (ii) BBN constraints redone with full cascade evolution, shown to be weaker by a factor of a few to ~10× than the on-the-spot estimates. (iii) A clean demonstration that CMB anisotropies remain a strong probe up to z~10⁴ because the highest-energy photons redshift over Hubble times and retain memory of the injection.

**Rigor and validation — genuinely good.** Fig. 4 (CosmoRec vs Recfast++, showing extra excitations are below the cosmic variance) is exactly the right kind of cross-check. Fig. 5/§3.1 correctly argue that extra excitations go to recombination-line (spectral-distortion) physics rather than anisotropies. Broad agreement with Slatyer & Wu (2017) and Poulin et al. (2016) for τ>10¹³ s validates the long-lifetime regime. Appendix D shows no significant degeneracy with the 6 ΛCDM parameters. Tables in Appendix A/B for cross-sections make it reproducible. This is work in the "right" style for the subfield.

## Weaknesses / things I'd push a student on

1. **The headline is largely a consequence of assumptions already in the literature.** "CMB anisotropies are sensitive to injection at z~10⁴" is a *plausible and expected* consequence of long Hubble-time photon survival once you've set up the machinery; the paper establishes it quantitatively but doesn't really surprise a reader of Slatyer & Wu or Poulin et al. The BBN weakening from delayed deposition is likewise intuitive once you've relaxed the on-spot assumption. The novelty is in the *tool*, not in the finding, and that tool is a refinement of existing approaches rather than a new paradigm.

2. **Computational cost limits the physical reach.** The paper itself notes the factorization was what made previous work fast. By removing it, they can only afford a handful of *monochromatic* injections (eV–TeV bands, a few seed energies, a few lifetimes). The extension to arbitrary spectra / general decay channels (needed for real models) is left as future work, and only 4He+3He+2H destruction is modeled, not 2H over-production. So the method is powerful but the demonstration is narrow. A student should make explicit that a model-building program still needs a separate (approximate) pipeline for the full (mX, spectrum, branching) space.

3. **Data staleness and the 2015-vs-2018 inconsistency.** The MCMC uses **Planck 2015** likelihoods even though the v2 paper (Dec 2019) has Planck 2018 in the references and Appendix D explicitly compares to Planck 2018. The authors hand-wave that the difference won't matter (it doesn't, for τ; it could for ns and the CMB-decay coupling), but running the anisotropy analysis on 2015 data in 2019 is a real, easily-fixable weakness. The fX·fEM normalization in Fig. 8 (10⁻¹⁰ scaling, ε0 f_d) is also confusing relative to the bare fEM in Fig. 9 and needs a line of clarification.

4. **The "strongest CMB constraints up to z≤10⁴" claim needs its caveat in the abstract.** §5 explicitly notes an exception: for photon injection just above the 4He photodissociation threshold (~26 MeV), BBN is actually stronger until much lower redshift. The abstract presents the CMB-dominance claim too cleanly; the exception should be flagged.

5. **No new observable physics.** This is a precision-constraint paper. The constraints are useful and will be cited by the DM-decay / electromagnetic-injection community (gravitinos, WIMP annihilation, evaporating primordial BHs), but it doesn't open new measurement directions. The one genuinely forward-looking note (recombination-line spectral distortions from excitations, §7) is deferred to future work.

## Net verdict

A technically sound, well-executed, and useful precision-cosmology paper with a real (if refinement-scale) methodological advance and a genuinely new corner of parameter space quantified for the first time. It is not a field-shaping contribution: the ideas are conservative, the data are a vintage old, and the results are the expected payoff of better numerics. For a student, it's a solid A-minus-style paper — the kind you want to have done by the end of a Ph.D. if your aim is precision cosmology, but it should not be oversold as conceptually novel. I'd recommend: rerun the anisotropy constraints on Planck 2018, state the 26 MeV BBN exception in the abstract, and at least sketch (even qualitatively) how the unified code scales to a non-monochromatic spectrum.
