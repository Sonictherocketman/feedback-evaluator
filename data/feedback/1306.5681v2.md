## [1306.5681v2-general-review] General Review


I'll read the paper to evaluate it.



I've read the full paper (Baardsen, Ekström, Hagen, Hjorth-Jensen, "Coupled-cluster studies of infinite nuclear matter," arXiv:1306.5681v2). Below is my critical review of its argument.

## Summary of the argument

The paper develops the formalism for applying coupled-cluster (CC) theory to infinite (symmetric and pure-neutron) nuclear matter, and demonstrates it numerically. The central technical move is deriving a **coupled-ladder approximation** (PPHH-LAD and its particle-particle-only restriction PP-LAD) from the CC doubles (CCD) amplitude equation by retaining only the one-particle-one-hole linear terms (PPHH) or only the particle-particle linear terms (PP), in a plane-wave → coupled-angular-momentum (RCM) basis. A second technical contribution is the treatment of the **Pauli exclusion operator** both exactly (via Suzuki et al.'s partial-wave expansion) and via the standard angular-average approximation, plus an angular-averaged single-particle energy. Results are compared against BHF for two modern chiral two-body interactions (NN³LO and the optimized NNLOᵤₚₜ). The headline findings: (i) PPHH-LAD gives less binding than BHF by ~0.5 MeV at saturation; (ii) exact vs. angular-averaged Pauli operators differ by ~0.2–0.5 MeV/part; (iii) adding hole-hole ladders vs. PP-only changes the result by only ~0.1–0.2 MeV; (iv) the two chiral interactions give similar symmetry energies but diverge in the symmetry energy at high density (driven by the ³P₀/³P₁ partial waves).

## What is done well

- **Careful, self-contained formalism.** The transition from a plane-wave CCD equation to an RCM/coupled-partial-wave basis, with the Wigner-D rotation trick (Eq. 19, App. A) that reduces the amplitude matrix to one CM direction, is presented clearly and is the kind of bookkeeping that makes the calculation tractable.
- **Cleaner treatment of the Pauli operator than prior nuclear-matter ladder work.** The contrast between the exact (non-diagonal in total J) and angular-averaged (diagonal) operators, and the explicit statement that the exact operator makes the t-amplitude non-diagonal, is a genuinely useful clarification and motivates the use of averaging.
- **Consistency checks.** Verifying that the PP-LAD/BHF equivalence reduces to the G-matrix replacement, and cross-checking against SCGF and other BHF studies (Li et al., Song et al., Carbone et al.), shows restraint and awareness of the existing literature.

## Critical weaknesses

1. **The "coupled-cluster" label is generous.** The authors themselves concede the method is "not a strictly coupled-cluster approximation" (p.2). Dropping all non-linear terms in the T₂ amplitude equation means this is a *ladder resummation* written in CC notation, not a proper T₂/CCD calculation. The non-linear particle-hole terms that distinguish CC from ladder theory are precisely what set aside, so the paper's central result — that PPHH-LAD ≈ BHF — is almost tautological, since the ladder and BHF G-matrix are closely related objects. This is the paper's most serious conceptual limitation: the claim of a "first CC calculation for nuclear matter" is overstated relative to what is actually computed.

2. **Two-body interactions only; no three-nucleon forces.** This is the dominant physical weakness. The paper is "a first step toward CC calculations," but all quantitative conclusions (saturation, the ~0.5 MeV offset vs BHF, the symmetry-energy behavior, the overbinding of NN³LO) are computed with two-body forces. As the authors must acknowledge, two-body-only chiral potentials overbind and saturate at the wrong density, so the numerically interesting statements are not physically definitive. A modern assessment would require 3NF to make any conclusion about saturation or the symmetry energy meaningful, and that is entirely absent.

3. **No systematic uncertainty analysis.** By even the paper's own era, there is no quantified error budget: no chiral-EFT truncation error, no combined estimate of the Pauli-angular-averaging error, the J_max truncation error (which they admit is slow and memory-limited for the "exact" case), or the ladder-truncation error. The 0.4 MeV exact-vs-average difference is quoted as a number, not as a defensible error contribution. The memory limit that caps the exact calculation at J_max = 8 is a practical weakness that quietly constrains the accuracy claim.

4. **The derivation of the exact-operator terms is opaque.** Eq. (22), the closed-form Q_pp for the exact Pauli operator, is asserted without a worked derivation, and the surrounding discussion (why the hole-hole expression cannot use the integral-operator form, Eq. 20–21 vs. 24–25) is dense and hard to follow. A reader reproducing the exact-operator case would struggle.

5. **Limited novelty in the comparisons to SCGF / BHF.** The conclusion that a particle-particle+hole-hole ladder sits slightly below BHF and slightly above two-body SCGF is consistent with published observations (cited as Ref. [16]) rather than a discovery. The added value is really methodological (CC-consistent treatment + exact Pauli operator), and that is a modest increment.

## Net assessment

The paper is competent, internally consistent, and a legitimate technical first step in bringing CC machinery (with a consistent chiral-EFT interaction and a defensible Pauli-operator treatment) to infinite nuclear matter. But as a contribution it is incremental rather than transformative: it computes a ladder in CC notation rather than a true CC calculation, it omits the three-body physics that would make the physics claims robust, and it lacks the error analysis that a modern evaluation of the equations of state requires. Its historical value is primarily as a stepping stone to later full CCSD nuclear-matter calculations.

## [1306.5681v2-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


I've read the full paper (14 pages). This is Baardsen, Ekström, Hagen & Hjorth-Jensen (Phys. Rev. / ArXiv 1306.5681v2, "Coupled-cluster studies of infinite nuclear matter"). Here is my honest expert assessment.

## Overall assessment

This is a solid, self-contained formalism-and-implementations paper. Its contribution is clear and legitimate: deriving the coupled-cluster (CC) ladder-approximation equations for infinite nuclear matter in a coupled-partial-wave basis, treating both an *exact* and an *angular-averaged* Pauli operator, and benchmarking the method against conventional Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (BHF) for symmetric nuclear matter (SNM) and pure neutron matter (PNM) using chiral $\overline{\rm N}^3$LO and NNLO$_{\rm opt}$ interactions. The core message — that the CC ladder approximation and BHF differ by at most ~6%, with the hole-hole and exact-Pauli terms adding 0.7–2% each — is a useful and reproducible sanity check on the machinery.

## What is good

- **Rigorous and transparent derivation.** The ladder equations are derived cleanly as an approximation to the CCD $\hat T_2$ amplitude equation (Eq. 7 → 8), and the diagrammatic bookkeeping (Figs. 1–4) is correct and pedagogical. The equivalence of PP-LAD and BHF equations when $g$ is replaced by $G$ is stated and explicitly verified numerically, which is exactly the right consistency check.
- **Careful treatment of the Pauli operator.** The distinction between exact and angular-averaged exclusion operators, and the observation that the exact operator is not diagonal in total angular momentum $\bar{\mathcal J}$ (forcing a larger matrix), is handled correctly and quantitatively.
- **Good convergence study.** Fig. 6 (slow, oscillatory convergence in $J_{\rm max}$) is honest and important — it explains why "exact" could not be run beyond $J_{\rm max}=8$. The physical insight that this is one-pion-dominated convergence is well-motivated.
- **Reproducible, well-referenced.** Connections to SCGF (Schiller, Carbone, Song), the Suzuki BHF partial-wave work, and the BHF/Bochm truncation context are all handled accurately.

## Concerns / weaknesses

1. **The "first step" framing is honest but the physical payoff is limited.** No three-body forces are included. Both chiral interactions overbind and saturate at too high a density (the authors state this explicitly, Figs. 10–11). So the comparison-to-BHF exercise, however valuable for validation, does not advance the actual state of nuclear-matter predictions. The physically interesting questions (T$_3$ ring diagrams, $T_2$ full CCD, MBPT(2)) are deferred. As a methods paper this is acceptable; as a nuclear-structure result it is preliminary.

2. **Missing three-body forces is arguably the central physical limitation.** For PNM in particular, the $\overline{\rm N}^3$LO two-body-only result is not yet close to the saturation point of the equation of state. Without at least density-dependent or full 3N forces, the PNM equations of state (Fig. 11) are of limited use, and the paper's PNM conclusions rest mostly on *distinguishing the two NN models* rather than predicting.

3. **The $J_{\rm max}$ truncation is asymmetric and understated.** "Exact" PPHH-LAD caps at $J_{\rm max}=8$, while BHF runs to 24, and "average" to 16. The 0.7–2% "exact vs. average" and "PP-HH vs. PP" differences are therefore entangled with different convergence levels. The convergence in Fig. 6 is still oscillatory at $J_{\rm max}=8$ (not fully settled), so the "exact" numbers likely carry a non-negligible $J$-truncation error that is not quantified by an error bar. This weakens the claim about the *size* of the exact-Pauli contribution: it may be commensurate with the truncation uncertainty itself.

4. **No uncertainty quantification on the final energies.** The experimental saturation box in the plots is a useful anchor, but the method's own error (truncation in $J$, fixed-point tolerance, angular-averaging approximation, $2M$ effective mass, no explicit treatment of intermediate-state/continuum-shell-space poles, and the absence of $T_1$) is never folded into a single number or band. Given that several distinct approximations are stacked, a more careful error analysis would substantially strengthen the paper.

5. **The $2M$ non-relativistic kinetic term** is used throughout (Eq. 1), which is standard at NNLO but is a known source of a few-MeV systematic offset for the chiral interactions that were parameterized partly including relativistic effects; this is not discussed in the context of the SNM binding-energy discrepancy vs. Li et al.

6. **Some redundancy/verbosity in Sec. II–III.** The formalism section re-derives much of what is in Suzuki et al. and Ref. [33]; acceptable for self-containment but it delays the results and would benefit from tighter writing and a clearer statement of what is genuinely new (the coupled LAD + BHF ladder equations in the coupled-angular-momentum basis with the exact PPHH treatment) versus what is standard.

7. **The "6% difference" headline (abstract) conflates several effects.** The largest CC-vs-BHF gap in Table I is ~0.5 MeV at saturation out of ~24.4 MeV (~2%); ~6% appears only at the most attractive / softest part of the curve where the correlation is smallest relative to the total. As phrased in the abstract, "at most almost a 6% difference" is defensible but easy to read as the typical size; the *typical* difference at saturation is ~2% absolute, with ~0.5 MeV absolute.

## Minor points

- Fig. 5 (HF energy) and Fig. 9 (single-particle potential) are useful but the caption for Fig. 9 comparing G-matrix vs. bare potential is the single clearest illustration of the BHF/PP-LAD difference — it would deserve promotion in the text.
- The fixed-point iteration (Algorithm 1) is described but no statement of convergence rate/iterations-to-convergence is given, which a reader re-implementing would want.
- Charge-symmetry-breaking and charge-independence-breaking are mentioned as included but never quantified or shown to be negligible.

## Verdict

A competent, well-executed implementation-and-validation paper. It does the bookkeeping correctly, is internally consistent, and provides a genuine bridge between the CC-ladder and BHF communities with a clear, reproducible recipe. Its limitation is that, without three-body forces and with an unquantified angular-momentum truncation error, it validates the *method* far better than it predicts *physics*. I would consider this appropriate for a solid but not high-impact journal: original and useful, but incremental relative to the existing BHF/SCGF nuclear-matter literature, and preliminary as a physical result.

## [1306.5681v2-critique] Critique


# Critique: "Coupled-cluster studies of infinite nuclear matter" (Baardsen, Ekström, Hagen, Hjorth-Jensen; arXiv:1306.5681v2)

## What the paper does

The paper develops coupled-ladder approximations (CPHH-LAD, and the reduced PP-LAD / PP2-LAD schemes) derived from the CCD (T₂-truncated) amplitude equations and applies them to symmetric nuclear matter and pure neutron matter with the chiral N³LO and NNLO_opt two-body interactions. Its technical core is the derivation of ladder equations in a coupled partial-wave basis with (i) *exact* Pauli exclusion operators (expanded in a partial-wave series following Suzuki et al., so that Q_pp and Q_hh act as integral operators) and (ii) angular-averaged Pauli operators, combined with angular-averaged inputs to the single-particle energies. Results are benchmarked against conventional BHF theory and cross-checked between the two interactions, with a partial-wave analysis (¹S₀, ³P₀, ³P₁) of why NNLO and N³LO diverge for neutron matter, plus a symmetry-energy study.

## Strengths

1. **Clear, timely motivation.** Before this work, the only CC-type calculations of nuclear matter were Day and Zabolitzky's early-1980s efforts, which included diagrams only through part of the four-body subsystem amplitude and relied on crude Taylor expansions and estimated terms. Since the entire modern EFT program for nuclear matter (BG expansion, hole-line convergence, BHF with chiral forces) begs the question "does a physically truncated many-body expansion converge as fast as perturbation theory suggests?", a genuine CC calculation is a genuinely missing piece. The paper fills it cleanly.
2. **Careful formalism.** The partial-wave treatment, the isospin/antisyinmmetrization bookkeeping (Eq. 14-16, App. A), and the demonstration that T₁ ≡ 0 by total-momentum symmetry for the plane-wave reference are done correctly and are worked out in detail. The memory-saving trick — computing the t-matrix for one angular direction of the CM momentum and rotating it (Eq. A18, following Suzuki et al.) — is a real computational contribution that made "exact" Pauli calculations feasible at all.
3. **Honest benchmarking.** The paper quantifies the differences instead of hiding them: PP-LAD vs PPHH-LAD differs by ~0.2 MeV/nucleon at saturation (~1%); the hole-hole ladder correction is 0.7–2%; the exact-Pauli-operator correction is comparable. The conclusion that hole-hole ladders in nuclear matter are far smaller than in the electron gas / finite systems is a physically informative, well-supported finding, and it justifies the ladder-summed approximations used elsewhere (SCGF, BHF) in nuclear matter.
4. **Interaction independence check for SNM.** Showing N³LO and NNLO_opt give nearly identical SNM equations of state — and then locating the NNLO_opt excess repulsion in neutron matter specifically to the ³P₀/³P₁ channels — is a nice piece of physics that connects the many-body calculation to the known two-body phenomenology.
5. **Readability.** Diagrammatic presentations (Figs. 1–4), the explicit algorithm box, and a substantial appendix make the formalism reproducible even though it is dense.

## Weaknesses

1. **The "coupled-cluster" label is overstated, and the paper acknowledges it.** The scheme drops specific diagrams from the CCD T₂ equation (the sixth diagram in PPHH-LAD, the fifth in PP-LAD) and, more consequentially, *replaces the CCD energy expression with only its first-order-in-t part* (Eq. 10: one 2p2h bubble). The standard CCD energy includes a ring and double-bubble diagram; dropping them means E_CCD is a perturbation-style energy, not the expectation value of the correlated state, without any discussion of how large the discarded terms are. For a paper whose stated purpose is to understand *the convergence of the many-body expansion*, the lack of a variational/consistent energy for the truncated theory is a real gap. At least a single quantification (e.g., evaluate the dropped energy diagrams at the converged t's) would have been cheap and informative.
2. **The ~6% maximum CC-ladder vs BHF difference is never resolved.** The headline result is that CC (ladder) and BHF agree "at most almost 6%" at high k_F, which is ~1.2–1.5 MeV/nucleon at k_F = 2 fm⁻¹. The paper attributes the gap to the single-particle energy expression, the exact-vs-averaged Pauli operators, and hole-hole ladders, but provides no *decomposition* showing whose contribution is which. Since BHF itself is a (BG) perturbation series in the G-matrix through fourth order, "agreement within 6%" is not an independent benchmark — both methods are ladder-dominated with different denominator definitions. The hole-line-expansion estimate of 1.7–2% (cited from Song et al.) is therefore *not* reproduced as an upper bound on the CC ladder error, and the paper leaves the reader without a controlled error bar on its own numbers.
3. **Uncontrolled angular-momentum truncation in the "exact" case.** Memory forces J_max = 8 in the exact-Pauli calculations, and Fig. 6 shows the correlation energy has no sign of leveling off at J = 8 (it oscillates: −17.2, −19.1, −18.3, −18.9, −18.8…). The paper's own analysis explains *why* convergence is slow (strong-coupling collisions at high density keep angular momenta close together), which is valuable, but it means the "exact" results carry an unquantified error that is not negligible next to the 1 MeV effects being studied.
4. **Two-body interactions only, for a conclusion that leans on interactions.** The central physics claim — that NNLO_opt is softer/less repulsive in P-waves and hence the SNM vs neutron-matter behavior differs from N³LO — is made at the two-body level. The authors concede three-body forces change the picture (their PRL [63] shows 3NF matters) yet the paper still draws conclusions about which interaction is a "better perturbation-theory starting point." Given the field's subsequent experience, 2-body-only saturation physics is close to the limit of reliability for EFT nuclear matter.
5. **Signs the 2021 v2 is a stale revision.** Ref. [73] is still cited as "in preparation (2013)" — eight years before the v2 date — and the references to the follow-up NNLO_opt neutron-matter work point to the same unreleased notes. No comparison was added to the meantime literature on full CC in a box or to modern convergence estimates.
6. **Minor omissions:** no treatment/discussion of the pair-instability (gap-equation) channel beyond one sentence, even though neutron matter below ~1.8 fm⁻¹ is a natural pairing region; no error analysis from numerical parameters beyond J_max; and no mention of how the fixed-point iteration (Alg. 1) behaves for hard-core forces, where SCGF/BHF experience suggests stiffness (the Argone v18 aside is the only hint, and it says the CC–BHF difference *grows* for a hard core — counterintuitive and unexplained).

## Overall assessment

This is a careful, competently written first step that did what it set out to do: derive and implement CC ladder equations for infinite matter with exact Pauli operators, and confirm that the ladder-summed CC framework and BHF tell broadly the same qualitative story (correct saturation region, symmetry energy, interaction dependence), with quantified 1–6% differences. Its lasting technical contribution (exact Pauli operators in a coupled-partial-wave CC framework, the rotation-based memory reduction) is real, and the finding that hole-hole ladders are ~1% for nuclear matter is useful for the whole BHF/SCGF/CC community. But the ad-hoc diagram truncation, the perturbation-style energy without validation, the unresolved 6% gap, the slow and uncontrolled J-convergence, and the two-body-only physics keep it from being a definitive convergence study — which was arguably its most valuable intended use.

## [1306.5681v2-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


# Evaluation of arXiv:1306.5681v2 — "Coupled-cluster studies of infinite nuclear matter"

## Stated contribution (in my own words)

The authors derive the **coupled particle-hole and particle-particle/hole-hole ladder approximation (PPHH-LAD)** from the CCD *T₂* amplitude equation of coupled-cluster theory, adapted to infinite nuclear matter in the Subₙ truncation scheme. They work out the full formalism in a coupled partial-wave basis with **both** (a) the exact Pauli exclusion operator (expanded in partial waves following Suzuki et al.) and (b) the standard angular-averaged Pauli operator, and with the angular-averaged single-particle energy denominator. They then apply this to compute the energy-per-nucleon equation of state for symmetric nuclear matter and pure neutron matter, using two chiral interactions (N³LO and the concurrently published optimized NNLO_opt two-body interaction), and benchmark against: standard BHF (with a numerically iterated *G*-matrix, computed in the same partial-wave framework for apples-to-apples comparison), the particle-particle-only ladder (PP-LAD), second-order perturbation theory, and Hartree-Fock. They quantify the size of the ladder correction relative to the Hartree-Fock energy (~0.7–2% of total energy) and the difference from standard BHF (at most ~6% in binding energy per particle, ~1 MeV at saturation).

## What is genuinely new

1. **The CC-specific ladder formulation.** The authors are careful to note that the PP-LAD equations reduce exactly to the BHF *G*-matrix equations *only* if the single-particle spectrum is also computed self-consistently with a *G*-matrix. In their Subₙ-CC framework, the ladders retain a bare *t*-matrix in the energy denominator, so the PPHH-LAD theory is a physically distinct (if closely-related) object from BHF. This specific distinction — a ladder resummation inside the CC ansatz with the CC-specific energy denominator — is new, and the authors make the comparison to BHF a controlled, same-framework calculation (not a comparison to literature BHF numbers), which is methodologically clean.

2. **Exact vs. angular-averaged Pauli in the ladder approximation.** Most ladder-type matter calculations use angular-averaged operators. Deriving the exact-Pauli ladder equations in the coupled partial-wave basis and showing quantitatively that the exact Pauli operator adds ≈0.2–0.5 MeV of additional binding in symmetric nuclear matter, while having a smaller effect in pure neutron matter, is a concrete result not previously reported in this specific formalism (Suzuki et al. had done the analogous BHF comparison, but the ladder/CC context is new).

3. **Application to the NNLO_opt interaction.** Applying the CC ladder to the newly optimized NNLO_opt two-body interaction and identifying the excess repulsion of N³LO relative to NNLO_opt as originating in the ³P₀ and ³P₃ neutron-neutron partial waves is a new result specific to this interaction.

## What is not new

- **The core physical idea** — that particle-particle (and hole-hole) ladder resummations in nuclear matter are equivalent to the BHF *G*-matrix resummation — is standard RBHF physics, well known since the 1950s. The paper makes this clear itself (Section II-B, Fig. 3: "the PP-LAD and BHF equations become equal provided that the *G*-matrix in the single-particle energy expression is replaced by the interaction matrix").
- **All the formal tools** — RCM coordinates, coupled angular-momentum basis, angular-averaged single-particle energies, partial-wave Pauli operators — are explicitly borrowed from the BHF and finite-nucleus CC literature (Suzuki et al. 2002, Schiller et al. 1999, Ramos 1980, Heberle et al. 2009 — all cited).
- **The chiral interactions** (N³LO: Entem & Machleidt et al.; NNLO_opt: Ekström et al. 2013) are from companion/pre-existing work; no new interaction is derived here.
- **The fact that the ladder correction is of order 1–2% of the total energy** is consistent with what the hole-line expansion literature (Song et al. 2009, Ref [13]) already indicated; this is a confirmation, not a new finding.
- **The overall goal** — full CC for nuclear matter in the Subₙ scheme — had already been conceptually proposed (Freeman 1973, cited as Ref. [50], [51]); this paper is a necessary implementation step, not the first proposal.
- **The use of SCGF for matter** (Carbone et al. 2013, Ref. [8]) in the comparison is a pre-existing result, not something done here.

## Summary judgment

The paper is a **solid technical/implementation paper** that does something the field needed: putting the CC ladder approximation on rigorous footing for infinite matter, with a controlled same-framework BHF benchmark, and with the exact-Pauli variant as a controlled diagnostic. # Methodology Summary and Assumption Audit — Baardsen et al., "Coupled-cluster studies of infinite nuclear matter"

## Central experiment (what is being reproduced)

The central calculation is the **equation of state of symmetric nuclear matter (SNM) and pure neutron matter (PNM)** — the binding energy per nucleon as a function of Fermi momentum $k_F$ — at NNLO (chiral) using the N³LO two-body interaction (Entem & Machleidt [64]) and the optimized chiral NNLO_opt interaction (Ekström et al. [63]), compared against Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (BHF) with the same interaction.

### Step-by-step recipe

1. **System / basis.** Isotropic box of volume $\Omega\to\infty$, $A$ nucleons, free Fermi gas reference state. Plane-wave basis with single-particle energies $\epsilon_\mathbf{k}=k^2/2M$. All sums over momenta are replaced by integrals (Eq. A1).

2. **Single-particle energies (angular-average approximation, used in *all* calculations including the "exact" ones).** In the coupled partial-wave basis $|k(ls)Jm_J M_T\rangle$, the single-particle input momentum is replaced by
$$\bar{k}_p=\sqrt{k_p^2+K^2/4+kK^2\langle\cos^2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{K}}\rangle}\qquad\text{(Eq. 17)}$$
with the piecewise angular average $\langle\cos^2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{K}}\rangle$ defined using the step functions $x_{hh}$ and $x_{pp}$ (Eqs. 15–16). This prescription (from Suzuki et al. [15] / Ramos [74,70]) makes the reference energy equal to the Hartree-Fock energy of a free Fermi gas with the bare two-body interaction.

3. **Reference energy.** The Hartree-Fock energy per particle is
$$E_{REF}/A=\frac{3k_F^5}{10m}+\frac{3C}{4k_F^3}\sum_J\sum_{M_T}(2J+1)\int_0^{2k_F}K^2dK\int_0^{\sqrt{k_F^2-K^2/4}}\!dk^2\int_0^1\!d\cos\theta_{\mathbf{kK}}\; x_{hh}(k,K,k_F)\;\langle kl{}^{1S}Jm_J|v|kl{}^{1S}Jm_J\rangle\qquad\text{(Eq. 14)}$$
with $C=1$ for SNM, $C=2$ for PNM. Evaluated with $J_{max}\le 24$.

4. **Coupled ladder amplitude equations (SubTn derivation from CCD).** Start from the full CCD $\tilde T_2$ amplitude equation (Eq. 7), **drop all four-line (non-linear) terms**, obtaining the linear ladder equations:
   - **PPHH-LAD** (Eq. 8): keep the first four linear single-particle-sum terms (particle-particle ladder, PP-LAD) *and* the fifth term (hole-hole ladder, HH-LAD), with the two types coupled together.
   - **PP-LAD**: keep only the first four particle-particle terms.
   
   These give $t$-amplitude matrix elements $\langle k'(l'S)Jm_J M_T|t|\mathbf{kKk}\rangle$ in the coupled partial-wave basis. The energy denominator uses angular-averaged input momenta $\bar{k}_p$.

5. **Correlation energy.**
$$\Delta E_{CCD}/A = \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{k}^2\sum_{k_i k_j k_k k_l}\langle \mathbf{k}_i\mathbf{k}_j|v|\mathbf{k}_i\mathbf{k}_j\rangle\langle \mathbf{k}_i\mathbf{k}_j|t|\mathbf{k}_k\mathbf{k}_l\rangle\qquad\text{(Eq. 10)}$$
which in the partial-wave basis (with angular-averaged Pauli operators) gives Eq. 27, and with exact Pauli operators gives the integral-operator form Eq. A17.

6. **Pauli exclusion operator — two variants.**
   - **Exact:** $Q_{hh}$, $Q_{pp}$ are integral operators (Eqs. 24–25, 20–21) involving the full step-function factors; **not** diagonal in total angular momentum $J$ or $m_J$. The $t$-amplitude matrix is non-diagonal in $J$, requiring storage of the full matrix at all CM angles and greatly increasing memory.
   - **Angular-averaged:** $Q_{hh}\to\bar Q_{hh}$, $Q_{pp}\to\bar Q_{pp}$ (Eqs. 24, 25) become diagonal in $J$ and independent of $m_J$. The $t$-amplitude matrix can be evaluated at a **single CM angle** and rotated to all others (Eq. A18), drastically reducing memory.
   
   The difference between the two is $\sim$0.2–0.5 MeV/particle (more binding for the exact operator in SNM).

7. **Numerical solver — fixed-point iteration (Algorithm 1).** Initialize $E_{old}$ large, $T_{old}=0$; loop until $|E_{new}-E_{old}|$ is below an (unstated) tolerance: set $T_{new}=z(T_{old})$, $E_{new}=\Delta E_{CCD}(T_{new})+E_{REF}$.

8. **Numerical truncation.** Convergence studied with respect to the total angular-momentum cutoff $J_{max}$ in the partial-wave basis. Values used: $J_{max}=24$ for $E_{REF}$ (HF / BHF) and BHF; $J_{max}=16$ for the "average" PPHH-LAD correlation; $J_{max}=8$ for the "exact" PPHH-LAD correlation (limited by memory). Figure 6 shows the slow convergence; the saturation point (smallest-energy $k_F$) is robust, but the **absolute** energy is not fully converged.

9. **BHF comparison.** Solve the BHF $G$-matrix equation (Eq. 12) with the **continuous single-particle energy prescription** (same mean-field $U$ for particles and holes, Eq. 13) using the matrix-inversion method of Haftel & Tabakin [12]. The $G$-matrix is evaluated on a Coulomb-like $v=G-v$ basis in the coupled partial-wave basis. The singularity in the $G$-matrix integral is handled by taking the principal value. BHF uses $J_{max}\le 24$. Energy per particle: $E_{BHF}=\sum_\mathbf{k}\langle\mathbf{k}|h_0|\mathbf{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum\langle\mathbf{k}_i\mathbf{k}_j|g|\mathbf{k}_i\mathbf{k}_j\rangle$ where $g$ is the $T$-matrix (i.e., the $G$-matrix with $v$ replaced by $g$).

10. **Outputs.** Energy per particle vs. $k_F$ for $k_F=1.2$–$2.0$ fm$^{-1}$, saturation values, partial-wave contributions (Figs. 12–13), symmetry energy $S=[E_{pn}/A-E_{nn}/A]/A$ (Eq. 28), and Table I.

### What is *not* stated (numerical details a reproducer must fill in)

- Convergence tolerance for the fixed-point iteration.
- Number of integration grid points for $k$ and $K$.
- Number of angular-momentum channel values $J$ per $ls$ combination.
- The chiral EFT cutoff (or regulator mass) — though this is intrinsic to the N³LO / NNLO_opt tabulated interactions.
- Which specific partial waves $(i,ls)$ are retained for each $J$.

- Number of fixed-point iterations to convergence.

## [1306.5681v2-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies

# Understanding of the paper's logical argument

**Purpose and lineage (Sec. I).** The infinite-νucleon system has been treated for decades by diagrammatic partial summations: the Goldstone expansion (slowly convergent at high density), the hole-line expansion of BHF (convergent in A), and the SCGF/Brueckner-Goldstone ladder. The coupled-cluster (CC) equations — non-perturbative, physical $T_n$ truncation, exponential wave operator — are well understood for finite nuclei, and earlier work (Day & Zabolitzky '81, Manzeke) applied a truncated, approximated CCD scheme to nuclear matter. The gap the paper addresses: nobody has built a *modern* CC ladder formalism for infinite matter with the current chiral N²LO/NNLLO$_{\rm opt}$ potentials and modern treatment of the Pauli operator. The goal is to fill that gap and to use it as a benchmark against BHF, in the hope of understanding the hole-line expansion better and validating three-body-force calculations.

**Formalism (Sec. II). Core claim: BHF and the CC particle-particle ladder (PP-LAD) are two views of the same physics.**

1. Start from the $T$-amplitude equation for the CC doubles. Neglect all nonlinear terms in the CCD $T_2$ amplitude equation → the particle-hole and hole-hole ladder (PPHH-LAD) equations. Neglect the hole-hole (last) line → the PP-LAD equations (Eqs. 7–8, Figs. 1, 2).
2. Internal-verification claim: with the *continuous* single-particle energy $\varepsilon(\mathbf{k}_p) = \mathbf{k}_p^2/2M + U(|\mathbf{k}_p|)$ (Eq. 13, A6–A7) and replacing $v \to G$ in the PP-LAD equations, the PP-LAD becomes *identical* to the BHF equations. This is checked numerically against a reference implementation.
3. Coordinate treatment: from plane waves to RCM to a coupled partial-wave basis $|k(ls)\mathcal{J}m_\mathcal{J}M_T\rangle$, with the standard $A^{l'SM_TS}$ antisymmetrization factor (Eq. A9).

**Key technical choice: exact vs. angular-averaged Pauli exclusion operators.** The exact $Q_{pp},Q_{hh}$, when expanded in partial waves (Suzuki's technique), are *integral operators* acting on the $t$-amplitude; they are diagonal in $\mathcal{J}$ but not in $m_\mathcal{J}$, which couples angular-momentum blocks and inflates memory. The angular-averaged versions (the standard approximation of the nuclear-structure literature) are plain functions and *are* diagonal in $m_\mathcal{J}$, giving a dramatic memory reduction. The authors derive both ladders from the partial-wave expansion and compare. They derive a closed-form $Q_{pp}$ (Eq. 22). Angular-averaged single-particle energies (Eq. 17) are used in **all** calculations.

Memory-reduction trick: exploit is/isotropy — solve $t$-amplitudes at one CM angle, rotate to obtain the rest (Eq. A18), reducing the 2D partial-wave matrix to 1D. Consequence: the 'exact' Pauli runs reach only $J \le 8$ (memory), 'average' reaches $J \le 16$, BHF and PP-LAD reach $J_{\max}\le 24$.

**Results (Sec. IV).** The argument proceeds as a decomposition of the difference between the CC ladder and BHF:
- **Pauli-operator effect** in SNM: exact gives ~0.4–0.5 MeV more binding than 'average' at saturation (consistent with Suzuki's BHF study and Schiller's result; 1.7% of total energy). In pure neutron matter this effect is *smaller* — a useful systematic observation.
- **Hole-hole-ladder effect** (PPHH-LAD vs. PP-LAD): ~0.2 MeV at saturation — smaller than the Pauli-averaging error, and far below the ring-diagram contribution found by Song et al. in the hole-line expansion. Justifies the authors' deferral of the full T₂ treatment.
- **Model dependence** (N²LO vs. NNLLO$_{\rm opt}$): very similar for SNM; for pure neutron matter the N³LO is more *attractive* in the repulsive region, traced to the poorer P-wave phase-shift fit (³P₀ especially). Same pattern seen for the symmetry energy — NNLLO_opt gives much higher symmetry energies at high density. This is a useful diagnostic cross-check against the SCGF finite-T results of Carbone et al.
- Caveats honestly stated: no pairing treatment (gap eq. not solved; T₁ = 0 by momentum conservation), only two-body interactions, three-body forces deferred.

**Conclusions:** first-principles CC-ladder benchmark of nuclear/neutron matter; agreement with BHF within at most ≈6%; hole-hole ladders add 0.7–2%; sets the stage for a genuine CC computation with 3-body forces.

# Feedback

**Strengths in the logical argument.**

1. The BHF ≡ PP-LAD-under-the-continuous-option identification is the cleanest piece of the paper. It's stated as a theorem (Sec. II.B), backed by a numerical check against an independent implementation, and it gives the reader a trustworthy anchor for interpreting every subsequent number: the CC result is *not* some independent estimator that could have a different bug. Good.
2. The decomposition into independent contributions (exact-pauli vs. average-pauli; PPHH vs. PP; model A vs. model B) is well organised and the magnitudes come out with a sensible hierarchy (model dependence ≫ hole-hole ladders > Pauli-averaging). That hierarchical result — "the differences we see are *dominated by the interaction model, not by the method*" — is probably the most physically useful output of the paper and is stated clearly.
3. Internal consistency checks are present: exact vs. average for the HF reference energy (Figs. 5 and 14); the J-convergence of the 'exact' calculation (Fig. 6) is shown at the appropriate density (k_F = 1.8 fm⁻¹) for the right reason (one-pion exchange → model-independent slow convergence).
4. Honest about what they *didn't* do: 3-body forces, ring diagrams, pairing, T₁ in general systems, full T₂ CCD. The "future work" paragraphs are specific, not hand-waving.

**Weaknesses / concerns in the logical argument.**

1. **The title promises coupled-cluster; the content is the CC ladder at T₂.** Authors are candid about this in Sec. I and in the conclusion, but the gap between "CC" and "coupled-ladder approximation with T₁ = 0, all nonlinear T₂ terms dropped, no particle-hole or ring diagrams" is large, and the reader who is not in the field could reasonably be misled by the title. The abstract says "we have performed CC-ladder calculations of symmetric nuclear matter and pure neutron matter including particle-particle and hole-hole diagrams" — which, read carefully, is honest (they call it "CC-ladder"). But the "CC" in the headline is doing a lot of work that the body does not deliver. This is a positioning issue, not a scientific one, but it sets reader expectations incorrectly.

2. **The J-truncation levels are not matched across the comparisons.** Table I compares PPHH/PP/BHF at $J_{\max}=24$ for the correlation, $J_{\max}=8$ for BHF (because BHF is the reference, J_max=8 there means the HF part), and "exact" PPHH at $J\le 8$ is not in the table — so the comparison "PPHH-exact at J=8" vs. "BHF at J_max=8" *is* fair. But the abstract's "at most *almost 6%* difference between our CC calculation and the standard Brueckner-Hartree-Fock approach" is not substantiated in Table I, where at k_F = 2.2 fm⁻¹ PPHH-exact is −22.97 vs. BHF −24.42 = 1.45/24.42 ≈ 5.9% — so the 6% is the 2.2 fm⁻¹ number, i.e., *outside* saturation. At saturation (k_F = 1.8) it is 5.4/25.4 ≈ 2.1%. The phrasing "at most almost 6%" implicitly conveys the high-density value; the saturation value, which is what physicists mean by "the method's accuracy," is smaller. The reader reading the abstract will overestimate the method gap. A more precise phrasing would separate the saturation difference from the high-density difference (or note that the high-density difference is mostly due to the two interactions overbinding — see below).

3. **No direct test that the overbinding of both interactions is a method artefact or a model artefact.** Figs. 7 and 10/11 show both N³LO and NNLLO_opt give saturation at too high a density and bind too deeply. The authors correctly interpret this as "3-body forces are needed" and cite Carbone et al. and their own NNLO_opt+3BF paper. But the *same* logic could be that the ladder method itself underestimates correlations at high density, and they cannot separate that from the interaction. Their defence is that the BHF and CC-ladder (which they prove to be equivalent at the PP level) give essentially the same result — but BHF is also a partial summation, so this is a *consistency* check, not an independent verification that the absolute value is right. The paper's claim ("CC agrees with BHF, therefore the hole-line expansion is convergent") only supports the first-order conclusion; it doesn't establish the magnitude.

4. **The "exact Pauli operators" J-convergence (Fig. 6) and the "average Pauli operators" J-convergence (not shown) are different curves but the figure shows only one.** Because the exact operators make the t-matrix non-diagonal in m_J, the effective convergence in J is different from the angular-averaged case. The authors don't show the 'average' J-convergence at the same k_F, so the reader cannot assess whether J=8 is well-converged for the exact case or whether the 0.4 MeV difference to 'average' at J=8 is partly a convergence artifact. (Fig. 6 looks flat enough between J=6 and J=8 — about 0.1–0.15 MeV in the last two steps, so this is fine, but a one-line statement would make it explicit.)

5. **Small technical gap.** Equation 23 introduces $\Delta\varepsilon(k,k',K)$ with the angular-averaged single-particle energies *inside* the ladder equation while the Pauli operators remain exact; the subsequent derivation in Appendix A10–A17 of the partial-wave form uses $x_{hh}$ (Eq. 15) and $x_{pp}$ (Eq. 16) which are the *exact* step functions. The paper never explicitly states whether Eq. 23 is the "exact Pauli / averaged single-particle energy" mixed version that the rest of the calculation uses — it is implied by "In all calculations we have used angular-averaged input momenta" but the reader has to assemble it from two paragraphs. This is exactly the kind of point that causes implementers to mis-code the ladders, and it should be stated as a single sentence.

6. **Reference to NNLO_opt as a 3-body-force surrogate is not made.** The NNLO_opt potential is a 2-body-only interaction, reparameterised against data (notably nuclear masses, which encode 3-body physics) below 125 MeV. When the paper says the NNLLO_opt "gives better pure-neutron-matter EoS" it is effectively showing that the *effective* attraction baked into the 2-body fit compensates for the missing 3BF; the authors do cite this (Refs. 63, 77) but it could be stated more explicitly in the results discussion.

7. **The claim "the method can be seen as a first step toward CC" deserves a sharper roadmap.** The most obvious next step is full CCD T₂ (no ladders) or at minimum adding the particle-hole (ring) diagrams. The cost of those is not estimated; the memory analysis that gives J≤8 for the 'exact' ladder would give a much tighter bound for the full CCD T₂ equation. A short paragraph on the anticipated scaling would strengthen the paper's value as a *starting point* for the next paper.

8. **Missing: a quantitative statement on the convergence of the BHF G-matrix vs. the CC t-matrix.** The BHF G-matrix equation (Eq. 12) is solved at J_max ≤ 9 (full) and the CC t-matrix at J_max ≤ 24 (correlation). Because they are equivalent, the *same* t-matrix should emerge; do they check that the t-matrix and the G-matrix agree in the equivalent sector? The "internal verification" claim is presumably yes, but it's not shown.

**Verdict.**

The internal logic of the paper is sound and well-structured: it makes a clear theorem (BHF ≡ PP-LAD), builds an implementation around it, decomposes the remaining CC-vs-BHF difference into three named effects with sensible magnitudes, and uses model comparison as a second independent diagnostic. The main weakness is that the headline "CC" over-promises relative to the ladder-only content, and the "6%" abstract number is the worst-case high-density value rather than the saturation value. The J-truncation mismatches between the 'exact' and 'average' runs are handled honestly but not flagged prominently enough. The work is a competent, well-referenced methodological step that any group doing three-body-force nuclear-matter calculations will want to reproduce and extend; it is not a physics result that changes the state of the art.

For scoring: the contribution is a solid methodological paper in a mature field. The formalism (exact Pauli in the ladder via partial-wave expansion, memory-reducing rotation trick, the BHF≡PP-LAD identity) is clean and useful, but it extends existing techniques (Suzuki's BHF, Manzeke's CCD) rather than introducing new machinery. The numerical insight ("at this truncation level, model choice dominates method choice") is interesting but not field-shaping on its own.

- Sub-claims: exact vs. angular-averaged Pauli operator → ~0.4 MeV (~1.7%); including hole–hole ladders → ~0.2 MeV (~1%).

So the claim to audit is: *PPhH-LAD is ~1 MeV (~4%, up to ~6%) more binding than BHF at saturation, because the CC ladder sums correlations that BHF omits.*

## Is the comparison fair?

**Partly, and with a significant confound.**

- **Within the CC/BHF family at a common truncation (Table I) — controlled:** The authors explicitly recompute BHF and all ladder variants at the same cutoffs (J_max = 24 for the HF/Born part, J_max = 8 for the correlation contribution), which is the correct way to isolate the PP–HH effect. At k_F = 1.8 the −24.50 vs −25.47 MeV comparison is internally consistent.
- **But the headline figure (Fig. 7) is not apples-to-apples.** The caption/text say the 'average' correlation energy was taken to J_max ≤ 16 while the 'exact' PPhH-LAD was capped at J_max ≤ 8 because of memory limits ("we were not able to calculate with J_max higher than 8 in the 'exact' approximation"). Fig. 6 shows the exact correlation energy has **not converged** (plateauing and oscillating at J_max = 6–8). So part of the "exact is more binding" offset in Fig. 7 is entangled with the exact ladder being run at a lower, non-converged truncation than the 'average' ladder and the BHF reference (J_max = 24). That weakens the robustness of the 1.7% exact-vs-averaged and the 6% BHF-vs-CC magnitudes.
- **Comparison to external BHF literature is muddied** but handled honestly: they note their Λ³LO result lies ~0.5 MeV below Li et al.'s BHF and attribute it to a real G-matrix vs. complex G-matrix and different singularity treatment. This is a self-inflicted comparison caveat, flagged, not a clean agreement.

## Are the baselines current?

**Adequate for the stated purpose, but not state-of-the-art.** The baseline is conventional BHF with the same chiral interactions (Λ³LO, NNLO_opt), plus a few passing SCGF results (Carbone et al., Hebeler et al.) cited qualitatively. That is the right baseline for *validating a new CC ladder scheme against BHF*, which is the paper's stated goal ("test our method against the standard BHF approach"). However, for a 2013/2021 nuclear-matter paper the field-standard benchmarks of the era are missing: no comparison to three-body many-body perturbation theory (the dominant saturation-point tool then), and no direct quantitative comparison to the hole-line expansion (Song et al.), which is the natural theoretical benchmark for how large the particle-hole/ring corrections are. The absence of those cross-checks limits how strongly the "more binding than BHF" conclusion can be anchored to something independent of the BHF/CC ladder family.

## Are error bars or statistical significance reported?

**None on the computed values.** This is the clearest evidentiary gap:

- No uncertainty is attached to any entry in Table I, and no statistical significance is given (the calculations are deterministic, so "significance" is the wrong frame — but the correct substitute is a *systematic* error estimate, which is absent).
- The box in Fig. 7 is the **experimental** saturation region extrapolated from finite nuclei — it is *not* an error on the authors' calculation, so it should not be read as their uncertainty.
- The only *informal* uncertainty indicators are (a) the convergence plots (Figs. 6, Fig. 7 oscillations in J_max) and (b) the Λ³LO↔NNLO_opt spread of ~0.5–1 MeV (Fig. 10), which is used as a proxy for interaction/EFT-order variation rather than stated as an error band. Tolerances on the fixed-point ladder iteration (Algorithm 1) and on the J_max truncation are not quantified.

Given that the claimed increments (1.7% exact-vs-averaged Pauli; ~1% hole–hole) are of the same order as the observed J_max convergence sensitivity, **the significance of those two smaller effects is questionable** — they sit at the edge of the method's demonstrated convergence without a stated tolerance, and the main BHF-vs-CC figure uses a non-converged "exact" ladder.

## Bottom line

The central claim (CC particle–hole + particle–particle ladders give meaningfully more binding than BHF, a few percent at saturation) is supported by a controlled, same-cutoff comparison (Table I) and by physically sensible, monotonic behavior, so it is credible. But the *quantitative* strength of the claim is overstated relative to the evidence: the headliner figure mixes a non-converged exact ladder with a converged BHF/averaged ladder, the baselines stop at BHF + a couple of SCGF references, and **no error bars, tolerance, or systematic-uncertainty estimate are reported** for any number. The two ~1% sub-effects in particular are within the method's demonstrated truncation sensitivity. This is a careful, well-motivated "first step" formalism paper rather than a measurement with quantified error.

**Scores:** Originality — the derivation of CC ladder equations for infinite matter in coupled relative/CM coordinates, the exact partial-wave Pauli operator treatment, the coupled PP–HH scheme, and the extension to pure neutron matter are genuinely new for the domain (4). Overall — internally consistent and honest about caveats, but the strong quantitative conclusion rests on a non-converged exact ladder in the main figure, lacks any uncertainty quantification, and uses only BHF-class baselines (3). Impact — an enabling method for CC nuclear/neutron-star EOS work, but no new observable and only BHF-level benchmarks keep the standalone impact moderate (3).

The argument chain is: (1) derive a coupled particle‑particle + hole‑hole ladder (PPHH‑LAD) approximation from the CCD amplitude equation; (2) treat the Pauli operator exactly in a partial‑wave expansion (Suzuki‑type); (3) compute equations of state for symmetric nuclear matter (SNM) and neutron matter with N3LO and NNLOopt chiral forces; (4) compare against BHF and attribute the differences to specific, quantified effects: **exact Pauli operators → 1.7–2%**, **hole‑hole ladders → ~1%**, and "at most ~6%" between their "CC" calculation and BHF.

The conclusion that stops being entailed is the move from *measured 0.2–0.4 MeV differences of schemes computed at a fixed, common angular‑momentum cutoff* to *those numbers are the size of the physical effects* (the 1.7–2% and ~1% claims, and the claim that the hole‑hole contribution is "smaller than the error of the angular‑average approximation").

1. **The truncation error is of the same size as the effects being measured.** Table I explicitly states that *all* headline comparisons used "Jmax = 24 for the Hartree‑Fock / Born approximation and **Jmax = 8 for the correlation contribution**," for the *exact* as well as the *average* Pauli variants. So the uncontrolled J‑truncation error is plausibly 0.2–0.5 MeV — the same order as the 0.41 MeV (1.7%) exact‑Pauli effect and the 0.2 MeV hole‑hole effect they purport to resolve.

The quoted 0.4 MeV difference at Jmax = 8 is the difference of two separately mis‑truncated quantities, not the converged difference between exact and averaged Pauli treatments. The paper shows no convergence curve for the 'average' variant, no extrapolation to J → ∞, and no error estimate for either number. The comparison is "at equal truncation," which helps with bookkeeping but does *not* cancel the truncation error, because the errors are scheme‑dependent.

3. **The conclusion compounds the problem by ranking unquantified errors against each other.** "At the saturation density, the contribution coming from including the hole‑hole ladders is smaller than the error of an angular‑average approximation of the Pauli exclusion operators" is a claim that numerical error A < numerical error B, made by a paper that quantifies neither A nor B. Similarly, the "≤ 6% difference between our CC calculation and BHF" is a difference of two uncontrolled truncations, presented as if it were a measure of the CC correction.

The rest of the chain is comparatively solid: the formalism is cleanly derived and honestly caveated (the authors concede the method is "strictly not a coupled‑cluster approximation"); the BHF‑vs‑PP‑LAD implementation check via the bare‑interaction G‑matrix replacement is sensible; and the secondary claim that NNLOopt's more repulsive neutron‑matter EOS stems from poorer 3P0/3P1 phase shifts is reasonably supported by the partial‑wave decompositions of Figs. 12–13 (though that explanation also only isolates the 1S0 + 3P0 waves without a full decomposition, and the note that Carbone et al.'s SCGF comparison shows "slightly more binding" with NNLOopt is in tension with the sign of their own neutron‑matter result, making "rather close" an overstatement). **In one sentence:** the authors resolve 0.2–0.4 MeV effects on a ~‑24 MeV energy using correlation‑part angular‑momentum cutoffs (J ≤ 8) that their own convergence figure shows are still moving by an order of magnitude larger with different, scheme‑dependent convergence behavior — so the 1.7–2% / ~1% attribution of those differences to the exact Pauli operator and to hole‑hole ladders is a claim the presented data do not support.

3. **Two-body forces only.** Every EoS and symmetry-energy conclusion (overbinding, saturation density, high-density symmetry-energy tension — the point where the interaction-model dependence is largest) is made without 3NF, which the authors note but do not quantify. This limits the claims about interactions to "the two-body part of."
4. **Load-bearing arguments deferred to "in preparation" references.** The Argonne v18 hard-core comparison and the NNLO/N3LO P-wave phase-shift analysis are cited as Refs. [73] and [77], "in preparation (2013)." A self-contained submission should either include the AV18 calculation or drop the claim that ladder vs. BHF differences depend on the hardness of the interaction.
5. **Minor issues.** (i) Typographical errors in the abstract ("caluclations") and in the body ("sinlge-particle"). (ii) "At densities lower than 0.1 fm−1" should be fm−3. (iii) The claim that T1 vanishes relies on the free Fermi vacuum and zero total momentum; the standard caveat (it would hold as a consequence of translational invariance with a proper reference, so fine here, but it deserves one sentence). (iv) The BHF comparison uses a Born approximation at J ≤ 24 and full G-matrix at J ≤ 9 — an asymmetry with the ladder cutoffs that is noted but not fully controlled, and it blurs the Fig. 7/8 comparison.

### Recommendation

A solid, technically careful step in bringing CC machinery to infinite matter with chiral forces; the exact-Pauli partial-wave technique will be genuinely useful to the community. As it stands, however, the physical claims are modest (a first CC-ladder EoS), the method is admittedly not really CC yet, and — for a prestigious journal — the absence of any quantitative error estimates on per-cent-level findings, combined with under-converged exact-Pauli results, is a substantive weakness. Recommend **major revision**: add convergence/error estimates, include the AV18 benchmark, and include at least one full-CCD-style diagnostic (or a 2D electron-gas benchmark) to justify the name "coupled-cluster" for the scheme.

I've read the full paper (Baardsen, Ekström, Hagen, Hjorth-Jensen, "Coupled-cluster studies of infinite nuclear matter," arXiv:1306.5681v2, Sept 2021, 18 pp.). Here is my feedback, in the voice of a senior scientist reviewing your work.

## Overall impression

This is a competent, careful piece of many-body physics. You have taken a well-established truncation scheme (CC doubles) and carried it to an unexplored regime — infinite, translationally invariant nuclear matter in a momentum/partial-wave basis — which is a real and useful contribution. The writing is clear, the formalism is internally consistent, and you are honest about what is and is not a strict coupled-cluster result. The biggest risk to the paper's claim is understated: this is a *first step*, and the abstract should sell it as that rather than implying a full CC description of nuclear matter.

## What you did well

- **The ladder derivation from CED (Eq. 7 → Fig. 1 → Fig. 2)** is clean and pedagogical. Showing that the PP–LAD is CCD apart from one term, and PPHH–LAD apart from one plus the non-linear term, is exactly the kind of thing a reader wants to see.
- **The exact vs. angular-averaged Pauli operator comparison** (Sec. III.A vs. III.B) is the methodological heart of the paper and you handle it well. Notably you derive the *closed-form* partial-wave Pauli expression (Eq. 22) and the integral-operator form (Eqs. 20–21, 24–25), and you diagnose *why* the exact operator breaks the $\mathcal{J}$ diagonalization (the Pauli operator is an integral operator, not a scalar). That causal explanation is good science.
- **Verification strategy** is sound: you check that BHF and PP–LAD agree once $G$ is replaced by $v$ (p. 6), and you use a matrix-inversion (Hattel–Tabakin) path for the BHF $G$ equation to avoid the continuous-choice singularities. These cross-checks are what make a numerical paper credible.
- You **quantify the size of neglected effects** (hole-hole ladders 1.7–2%, hole–hole + exact Pauli up to ~6%, PPHH–LAD vs BHF within ~6% / 0.5 MeV) rather than just asserting convergence. Quantification is what distinguishes a mature paper.

## What needs fixing or strengthening

1. **The "coupled-cluster" claim is loose, and you know it.** You state twice (p. 2, p. 14) that because you drop the particle–hole and non-linear diagrams the method "cannot be considered a strictly coupled-cluster approximation." This is a significant qualifier that is buried. For infinite matter the particle–hole ($T_1$) sector vanishes by translation invariance (good, that part is exact), but the *omitted non-linear diagrams in the ladder equations* are a genuine truncation with no systematic error estimate. You cannot, on the basis of this work, put error bars on "6% from BHF" and call it a coupled-cluster energy. Either (a) sharpen the title/abstract to "coupled-cluster ladder approximation," or (b) do at least a one-loop estimate of the first omitted non-linear diagram to *bound* the gap. As it stands, your largest uncertainty source has no number attached.

2. **Convergence is your weakest numerical result and it's presented too casually.** Fig. 6 shows the correlation energy *oscillating* with $J_{max}$, not monotonically approaching a value, and you stop the "exact" calculation at $J_{max}=8$ only because of RAM, with a stated reason (short one-pion range → slow angular convergence). A reviewer will push hard: what is your actual estimate of the $J_{max}\to\infty$ limit for the exact-Pauli case? You can't just say "restricted by memory." Add a Richardson-type or power-law extrapolation in $J_{max}$ for both 'exact' and 'average' so the final binding-energy numbers carry a defensible angular-convergence error, not just an "orders of magnitude smaller amplitude matrix" hand-wave. This directly weakens Table I.

3. **You compare to BHF but not to SCGF with the same observables/inputs.** You cite Carbone *et al.* (SCGF) and the hole-line expansion, but your headline comparison is to BHF. Since SCGF self-consistently solves the *full* off-shell denominators (vs. your HF-level denominators), it is the more natural "how much do the ring/ladder corrections beyond what you have" anchor. A single-paragraph quantitative discussion of *why* your PPHH–LAD sits ~0.5 MeV below BHF while SCGF sits ~1 MeV in the other direction (you touch this but vaguely, citing the hard-core vs. soft-core interaction dependence of ref [74]) is essential. Right now the reader can't tell whether that 0.5 MeV is a Pauli-operator effect, a denominator effect, or an interaction effect.

4. **Symmetry energy (Fig. 14).** A ~25 MeV discrepancy between N³LO and NNLO_opt at high density is large, and you attribute it entirely to $^3P_0$/$^3P_1$ phase-shift quality. But the symmetry energy at high density is also where **three-body forces** should dominate. You explicitly scope 3N forces out (p. 13, 14), which is fine, but you should state more prominently that a rising, large $S(E)$ is *expected* in the absence of an $\rho$- or $\rho^2$-term and that a 2-body-only result is not intended as a prediction of the nuclear matter symmetry energy. As written it overstates the predictive status of a 2-body calculation in a regime known to require 3-body forces.

5. **Technical / minor.**
   - The Hamiltonian in Eq. (1) drops the $\tilde V_{NNN}$ term's explicit $ijk<l$ ordering; be consistent with the ordering convention you use in the 2-body term.
   - In Fig. 5 the curve is labeled "Hartree–Fock" but the axis is labeled "Energy per particle" and the plot looks like it might be the *reference* energy (Eq. 14) — clarify it is $E_{REF}$ (which you elsewhere call the HF energy, an "abuse of language" that you could flag, since HF isn't self-consistent for infinite matter).
   - Eq. 17 uses $k_p=|\pm\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{K}/2|$ and the average $\overline{...}$; make sure the overbar notation is defined before first use — it appears without a definition.
   - The references for "orders of magnitude smaller" $t$-matrix (p. 9) is asserted, not shown; a one-line statement of a typical amplitude ratio would make this concrete.

## Bottom line for a student

The bones of this are a solid, publishable methodological paper and you should be proud of the partial-wave Pauli-operator machinery (Eqs. 20–27) — that's the genuinely reusable piece. Before it is submission-ready, the two things I would insist on are: **(i)** an honest, bounded error estimate for the non-linear ladder diagrams and the $J_{max}$ truncation (both are currently qualitative), and **(ii)** a sharper framing that this is a *ladder* approximation, not full CC, with the missing-sector physics (ring vs. ladder, 3-body forces, self-consistent denominators) discussed quantitatively rather than by citation. Fix those two and it is a strong paper; as it stands it is good but leaves its main uncertainty estimates as footnotes.
