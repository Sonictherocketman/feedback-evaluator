## [0709.1186v1-general-review] General Review


I have read the full paper (13 pages). Below is my evaluation.

## Paper identification

**"Entropy of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter Black Hole due to Arbitrary Spin Fields in Different Coordinates,"** C. Ding and J. Jing, gr-qc/0709.1186 (2007). Working in the Newman–Penrose formalism with the "improved thin-layer brick wall model" (BWM), the authors derive statistical-mechanical entropies of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter (Schw–dS) black hole from quantum massless fields of arbitrary spin (s = 0, ±1/2, ±1, ±3/2) in Painlevé and Lemaître coordinates, and show that the results (Eqs. 3.21, 3.37) agree exactly with the Wu–Yan Schwarzschild-like-coordinate result (Eq. 1.2).

## Strengths

1. **Complete, self-contained calculations.** The full Teukolsky–WKB chain (null tetrad → spin coefficients → master equation → eikonal → mode counting → free energy → entropy) is carried through in both alternative coordinate systems, not just sketched. For a verification paper, this is the right level of detail.
2. **The conceptual "why" is a good point.** The most useful part of the paper is the discussion of *why* the entropies must coincide: although Painlevé/Lemaître metrics are regular at the horizons, the event and cosmological horizons re-emerge as singularities of the semi-classical action (the WKB phase / phase-space measure), and the positive-frequency definitions coincide because both the Lemaître time V and Painlevé time t reduce to the Schwarzschild time at infinity (and along Kraus's curve dr + √(1−f)dt = 0, where dV = dt). This short argument is a nice pedagogical counterpoint to the "coordinate-singularity-avoidance" intuition.
3. **Consistency of the end result.** I spot-checked the Lemaître metric and inverse (Eqs. 2.6–2.8): with f̃ = 1−f, g_VV = g_UU = f/4, g_VU = −(1+f̃)/4, det = −f̃/4, g^{VV} = −f/f̃ — all consistent with the paper and with the direct transformation. The final expression matches Wu–Yan term by term (the (15+(-1)^{2s})/16 prefactor, the 1/45(1−λr_h²/2) ln term, and the −3+(-1)^{2s} λ(1+2s²)/36π term), which is the paper's actual claim and it is delivered.
4. Covering a genuinely two-horizon spacetime (not just Schwarzschild) is a small but real extension: the region between r_H and r_C has f > 0, the "walls" exist at both horizons, and the mode counting has to be done with the (E² − E²f/f − …) structure at a location where f passes through zero *between* the two zero-radii — not merely at the outer horizon.

## Main criticisms

### 1. Incremental contribution and an under-motivated claim

The central result (entropy is coordinate-representation independent for the BWM) is not really a theorem the paper establishes from scratch. It is a reconfirmation, for the Schw–dS two-horizon case, of (a) the authors' own earlier scalar-field check in ref. [8] (Jing–Chen, Schwarzschild in Painlevé/Lemaître) and (b) the general argument of ref. [7] (Jing, "Can the brick wall model present the same results in different coordinate representations?"). The authors themselves say "at first sight, we might have anticipated that the results are different" (Summary), but the last paragraph shows they could not have: once dV = dt along Kraus's curve and V, t → t_s at infinity, mode identity (and hence entropy identity) is essentially forced. A more honest framing would have been to state the equivalence as the *expected* outcome and present the calculation as an explicit check of the arbitrary-spin/Teukolsky machinery — which is a perfectly legitimate but modest contribution, and the Introduction overstates the open question it purports to settle.

### 2. Physical interpretation of the two-horizon ensemble is underdeveloped

The Introduction argues that the "improved thin-layer BWM" is needed because the original BWM "cannot be applied to cases out of thermal non-equilibrium problems, such as spacetime with two horizons." But the calculation then (i) expands modes in the *single* Killing time of the static region, (ii) uses one partition function (3.18) whose mode energies E are with respect to that one Killing vector, and (iii) reads off a separate S_h with a separate Hawking temperature 1/β = 1/T_h for each horizon (Eqs. 3.20, 3.21, 3.36, 3.37). The "improvement" that supposedly handles non-equilibrium is therefore never actually exercised — each horizon is effectively treated as if it had its own independent thermal bath, and the decoupling is achieved purely by the brick-wall placement. What physical state of the field does the partition function describe? The region between r_H and r_C is not in strict thermal equilibrium (T_H ≠ T_C, two inequivalent timelike Killing vectors exist), and the paper never says which vacuum (Gibbons–Hawking-type?) is assumed or how S_H + S_C relates to the total entropy. A short paragraph on the ensemble being assumed, or at least an explicit statement that the result is per-horizon and that S_H + S_C is not claimed to be "the" entropy, is missing. Relatedly, the authors do not discuss the fate of the 1/ε_h² divergence in Eq. (3.21): no statement about counterterm subtraction, and no comparison of the finite sub-structure with the area law or with the conical-geometry results of Mann–Solodukhin (their own reference [12]).

### 3. Cutoff bookkeeping is under-specified and partly inconsistent as printed

This is the concrete technical weak spot:

- **The η_H formula.** The paper states η_H ≈ 2√(ε r_H/(1−λr_H²)) (the proper distance from the event horizon to the inner brick wall), with η_H = ∫_{r_H+ε}^{r_H+Nε} √(−g_rr + g_tr²/g_tt) dr = ∫ dr/√f. Evaluating near the wall with f ≈ κ_H(r−r_H), κ_H = (1−λr_H²)/3, gives √f ≈ √(κ_H·r′), so the integral is (2/√κ_H)(√(Nε)−√ε) ≈ (2√3/√(1−λr_H²))√(Nε). For this to equal 2√(ε r_H/(1−λr_H²)) one needs N = r_H/6 (or r_H/3 depending on which limit is kept) — i.e. N of order unity, which directly contradicts "N is an arbitrary big integer which removes the infrared divergence." The final result survives only because it is then identified with the Wu–Yan cutoffs, so the paper is silently assuming the bookkeeping works that the printed relation does not establish. The same pattern (η_H = 2√(εr_H/(1−λr_H²)), N̂ = Λ̂_h²/ε̃_h²) appears again in the Lemaître section, reinforcing that this was carried over without independent re-check.
- **The recursive-ε̃ relation.** On the Lemaître page, the brick-wall offset is introduced as "ε̃ = (2/√3) ln[√(λε̃³/6m) + √(λε̃³/6m+1)]" — as printed this is (a) implicit/recursive in ε̃ and (b) presumably a typographical error for a relation between the Lemaître offset ε̃ and the Schwarzschild-like offset ε. As written it cannot be used, and the reader cannot verify the claim that the walls sit at the same physical locations.
- **The identification of the UV cutoff.** "The ultraviolet cutoff ε_h and the infrared cutoff Λ_h have been set to η_h² = 2ε̃_h²/15" (and similar) appears without derivation; since the entire ln(A_h/ε_h) structure of the final answer depends on this identification, the relation should at least be stated cleanly and justified (it essentially defines what ε_h *means* in each coordinate).
- **Implicit r(U) inversion.** For Schw–dS the Lemaître time U(U = …∫dr/√(1−f(r))) requires an implicit r(U) (the √(−g) and g_VV expressions after Eq. 3.35 are written with this inversion buried inside), and the paper never provides the explicit relation f̃(U) for the two-horizon case (in contrast to Schwarzschild where it is elementary). This makes the Lemaître half of the paper essentially unverifiable by a reader; the matching of the final answer is the only check. The long √(−g) expression (3.35) also appears to diverge as λ → 0 (λ appears in the denominator), which is consistent with the statement that this is a strict-Λ>0 construction, but the limiting case — which would be the cleanest possible cross-check (it should reduce to the Schwarzschild Painlevé/Lemaître result of refs. [7,8]) — is never explicitly taken.

### 4. Coordinate-patch coverage is never discussed

A single Lemaître chart of the form (2.5) covers only one patch of the Schw–dS spacetime (the region r_H < r < r_C is a single static region, but the full two-horizon manifold is not covered by one such chart, and the chart has its own coordinate degeneracies). The paper does not state what region of spacetime "the Lemaître coordinate" refers to, or that the equivalence claim is a *local/patchwise* statement about the static region between the horizons. For a paper whose whole subject is coordinate representations this omission is a real gap.

### 5. Minor issues

- Typo in reference [7]: "Phys. Rev. D **69, 0240101** (2004)" — the published paper number is 024011.
- "Painlévé" in the abstract (misplaced accent: Painlevé).
- The averaging of the radial momentum (3.15), (k_r⁺ − k_r⁻)/2 (inherited from Lee–Kim [11]) is a standard BWM counting trick, but the paper does not explain in a sentence why in-going and out-going branches should be averaged rather than counted separately, even though it spends space on the mode-identity argument elsewhere.
- The Lemaître spin coefficients (3.23) contain explicit 1/y and 1/√f̃ factors; the paper does not comment on where (in the patch) the calculation is being performed, which matters because these coefficients develop their own singularities at r = 0 and f̃ = 1 (i.e. f = 0 between… actually at the horizons in the *other* patch). A one-line statement of the domain would prevent confusion.

## Suggestions for the authors

1. Reframe the contribution as a check, and state the coordinate-patch domain explicitly.
2. Give the U-coordinate wall relation (the ε̃ equation) correctly and non-recursively; add the explicit near-wall expansions of √(−g) and g_VV that justify Eqs. (3.35) and the I_1h/I_2h evaluations; take the λ → 0 limit and compare with refs. [7,8].
3. State the physical ensemble (which vacuum/state of the two-horizon region) and comment on the meaning of S_H + S_C, including on the 1/ε_h² divergence.
4. Compare the structure of Eq. (3.21) with the conical-geometry (Mann–Solodukhin [12]) results — even qualitatively, since the sub-leading (1−λr_h²/2) ln term is where the two approaches typically differ.

## Bottom line

A sound, well-executed verification paper: the algebra is internally consistent where it can be checked, and the end result is correctly and explicitly reproduced in two new coordinate systems for arbitrary spin in a two-horizon geometry. Its value is mainly as a technical reference and for the clean argument of *why* the result must be coordinate-invariant. But the physical content is thin (the "two-horizon non-equilibrium" motivation is not really addressed by what is done), the cutoff bookkeeping is under-specified in a way that makes parts of the derivation unverifiable and partly self-contradictory as printed, and the overall novelty is modest since the outcome was strongly anticipated by the authors' earlier work. It is publishable as a short-communication-style note but reads more like a careful computation than a contribution to the physics of Schw–dS thermodynamics.

Scoring against the rubric: originality is low — a coordinate/mode-counting extension of established methods to an established geometry, with the result essentially forced by symmetry/asymptotics (2/5). Soundness is acceptable — no outright errors found, but the cutoff bookkeeping and patch-coverage omissions prevent full verification and some printed relations are inconsistent (3/5). Impact is minimal: a niche check within the BWM literature that is unlikely to be widely used or to change practice (1–2/5; I lean 2 because the "why it must be invariant" argument is a genuinely reusable point and the explicit arbitrary-spin Lemaître calculation may serve as a reference).

## [0709.1186v1-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


## Peer Review: arXiv:0709.1186 — *Entropy of the Schwarzschild–de Sitter Black Hole due to Arbitrary Spin Fields in Painlevé and Lemaître Coordinates* (Ding & Jing, 2007)

### Summary

The authors compute the statistical‑mechanical entropy of the non‑extreme Schwarzschild–de Sitter (SdS) black hole arising from quantum massless fields of arbitrary spin (scalar, Weyl, source‑free Maxwell, Rarita–Schwinger, and linearized Einstein) in two horizon‑regular coordinates — Painlevé and Lemaître — using the Newman–Penrose (NP) formalism plus the "improved thin‑layer brick‑wall model" (BWM) of Wu–Yan and Gao–Lin/Lin–Zhao. Their central claim is that, although in both coordinate systems the metric is regular at the event and cosmological horizons, the resulting entropies (Eqs. 3.21 and 3.37) are *exactly* identical to the Schwarzschild‑like‑coordinate result (Eq. 1.2). They also verify this against a scalar‑field result obtained directly via the equivalence principle.

### Strengths

- **Complete and systematic.** The NP machinery is carried through for the full Teukolsky hierarchy in both coordinate systems, including the dual `s↔−s` reduction (Eq. 3.9). The handling of the two horizons (event and cosmological) and the proper‑distance integration limits `η_H, η_C` and the brick‑wall locations `U_H, U_C` (Eq. 3.35 onward) is careful.
- **Internal consistency.** The Painlevé result (3.21) reproduces Wu–Yan (1.2), and the Lemaître result (3.37) matches it, so the paper is at least internally coherent. The argument that positive‑frequency modes coincident with the Painlevé curve `dr+√(1−f)dt=0` and that `V→t_s`, `t→t_s` asymptotically gives a plausible heuristic for why the results must agree.
- **Clean, well‑organized writing** with a clear logical chain (metric → NP coefficients → master equation → WKB roots → phase‑space counting → free energy → entropy).

### Weaknesses / Concerns

1. **The result is essentially expected and adds little new physics.** Coordinate invariance of the entropy is *the* anticipated outcome, and the authors themselves acknowledge the mode structures coincide asymptotically. The main technical content is bookkeeping: verifying that the improved BWM — which is only genuinely needed in the *singular* Schwarzschild coordinates — still closes when the horizons become singularities *in the action* rather than in the metric. This is a modest extension of earlier work on the scalar field in Painlevé/Lemaître coordinates (Shankaranarayanan et al.) and of the general BWM coordinate‑invariance question raised by Jing (ref. [7]).

2. **Conceptual tension left unaddressed.** The whole motivation of the BWM is that thermal equilibrium requires a Killing‑horizon‑adapted frame, and the "improved thin‑layer" fix was introduced precisely for two‑horizon, non‑equilibrium spacetimes like SdS. Working in Painlevé/Lemaître coordinates, where the spacetime is stationary but *not* static (Painlevé) or is a collapsing‑type foliation (Lemaître), the physical interpretation of the "infrared cutoff N" and of the brick‑wall boundary conditions (Eqs. 3.18–boundary blocks) becomes murky. The authors assert the wall location/meaning "are the same as in the Schwarzschild‑like coordinate" but do not justify this, since the proper‑distance integrals are performed in a coordinate where the horizon is a regular point. This is a genuine conceptual gap that a reviewer in the grav/quantum‑info community would want closed.

3. **Reliance on the BWM's ad‑hoc structure.** Much of the "answer" is inherited from the specific functional form (1.2) — with its `ζ(4)`, `1/45` and `λ` corrections — that the improved BWM produces. If one regards such entropy corrections (logarithmic and area corrections from cutoffs) as model‑dependent rather than universal, then confirming them in two more coordinates is not a strong test. The paper would be stronger with even a single *independent* check (e.g., comparing against the Euclidean/horizon‑area entropy plus the known universal log correction) to show the coordinate‑independence is not an artefact of the BWM's internal assumptions.

4. **Minor.** The `s` values listed for the two master equations (s=1/2,1,3/2,2 vs 0,−1/2,−1,−3/2,−2) are stated without a brief table mapping to the physical fields, which a reader must reconstruct from (1.2). The `m=0` rest‑mass notation in the Lemaître WKB step (3.17 vs 3.33, where one uses `m` and the other the mass `m_0`) is a bit loose and worth tightening. The "open question" framing in the introduction slightly overstates the novelty, given ref. [7].

### Overall Verdict

A correct, competent computational paper that extends a known result (coordinate independence of brick‑wall entropy) from one spin sector to arbitrary spin and from one to two regular coordinates. It will interest the relatively small community working on statistical‑mechanical black‑hole entropy by the brick‑wall / thin‑layer methods, but it introduces no new idea, no new observable, and confirms an outcome that the asymptotic‑mode argument already predicts. I would recommend **minor revision at best / borderline publishability**, with the revision focused on (a) justifying the brick‑wall boundary conditions in the regular coordinates and (b) adding one independent cross‑check on the entropy beyond BWM self‑consistency.

## [0709.1186v1-critique] Critique


I've read all 14 pages. Here is my critique.

## Paper

**"Entropy of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter Black Hole due to arbitrary spin fields in different Coordinates"** — C. Ding & J. Jing, arXiv:0709.1186v1 (gr‑qc, 2007).

## What the paper does

It computes the statistical‑mechanical (brick‑wall) entropy of a Schwarzschild–de Sitter (SdS) black hole from massless fields of arbitrary spin (scalar *s*=0, Weyl neutrino, Maxwell, Rarita‑Schwinger, graviton) in three charts — Schwarzschild‑like (standard), Painlevé, and Lemaitre — via the Newman–Penrose (NP) formalism + the "improved thin‑layer brick‑wall model" (BWM). The headline result: Eqs. (3.21) [Painlevé] and (3.37) [Lemaitre] reproduce Wu–Yan's standard‑coordinate result (1.2) **exactly**.

## What is done well

- The bookkeeping is clean. I spot-checked the Painlevé metric/inverse (2.3)–(2.4), the null tetrad (3.1) (both legs are null and *l·n* = 1, including at *f* = 0), and the spin coefficients' use of *Δ_r = r²f*, *K₂ = −m/sinθ* — consistent and regular at the horizon.
- Both the Painlevé and Lemaitre derivations correctly reduce to a Teukolsky master equation (3.10, 3.27), a WKB/Hamilton–Jacobi quantization, and the same high‑frequency state count (3.19, 3.34). Getting the *known* closed form back is a legitimate internal consistency check.
- The duality *s → −s* to reduce the problem to positive spin is correctly invoked.

## Main weaknesses

**1. The result is expected, and the paper concedes as much — so the contribution is a consistency check, not a discovery.** The entropy is a scalar thermodynamic quantity; it *must* be chart‑independent. In §4 and after Eq. (3.21) the authors themselves explain why they shouldn't be surprised (the asymptotic modes in *V*, *t*, and *t_s* coincide because *V* and *t* → *t_s* as *r* → ∞). A paper whose main theorem is "the known answer comes back" and whose own explanation is "it was never going to differ" carries little new physics.

**2. The "different coordinates" invariance test is weak.** Painlevé and Lemaitre differ from Schwarzschild **only by a redefinition of the time coordinate** — *r*, θ, φ and the spatial line element −r²dΩ² are unchanged throughout (2.2)–(2.7). The WKB mode count is governed by a covariant Hamilton–Jacobi equation, so invariance under a pure time redefinition is nearly guaranteed and not very informative. A genuinely illuminating test — advanced/retarded Eddington–Finkelstein, or Kruskal coordinates, where the temporal coordinate does *not* share the Schwarzschild asymptotic form and the horizon structure is treated differently — is absent. The single non‑trivial element (Lemaitre *V* is a mixed, time‑dependent coordinate) is handled, but the overall invariance claim is therefore not as strong as it reads.

**3. The physical premise is hand‑wavy for a generic SdS.** The BWM rests on a thermal bath at **one** inverse temperature: the free energies (3.20) and (3.36) integrate a single factor e^{βE} from 0 to ∞ and assemble a single Boltzmann entropy *S = β²(∂F/∂β)*. But a non‑Nariai SdS has two horizons with **unequal** Hawking temperatures, *T_H ≠ T_C*, and no global timelike Killing vector giving one equilibrium state. The thin‑layer walls at *r_H* and *r_C* are effectively forced to radiate at the same *β*. This tension (rooted in refs. [6,12]) is inherited but **never discussed** — it is arguably the deepest conceptual problem in the piece, and it is papered over by simply taking the limit the model "wants." The BWM here is a semiclassical/WKB/heuristic device (hard UV cutoff ε_h, ad hoc large integer *N*, thin‑layer fix), and the paper overstates the rigor of the "exactly equivalent" result without acknowledging these approximations.

**4. The arbitrary‑spin novelty is shallow.** The spin dependence is the standard degeneracy factor *g_s* with a (−1)^{2s} Bose/Fermi sign multiplying a scalar‑type formula (the (1 + λr_h²/2), λ(1+2s²/3) structure). This is the expected Mann–Solodukhin/Jing–Yan pattern reproduced for SdS; it does not advance understanding of the *origin* of black hole entropy.

**5. Presentation nits.** The abstract's "do not *obviously* possess the singularities" is vague. The Lemaitre √(−ḡ) and *g_VV* expressions (p. 12) are dumped in unsimplified form and *U_H*, *U_C* appear via a non‑obvious logarithmic identification, so that leg of the derivation is hard to verify independently. ## Stated contribution, in my own words

# Evaluation of "Entropy of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter Black Hole due to Arbitrary Spin Fields in Different Coordinates" (arXiv:0709.1186v1)

## 1. Methodology Summary (Sufficient for Reproduction)

This paper has no "experiment" in the laboratory sense. The "central experiment" is a **calculation**: computing the statistical-mechanical (Bekenstein-Hawking-type) entropy of a Schwarzschild-de Sitter (SdS) black hole from quantum massless arbitrary-spin fields, in two regular coordinate systems (Painlevé and Lemaitre), and showing the result equals the known Schwarzschild-like-coordinate result. A reader could reproduce the calculation by following these steps:

### Step 0: Background

Work in geometric units $G=c=\hbar=k_B=1$. The SdS metric in Schwarzschild-like $(t_s, r, \theta, \varphi)$ coordinates is

$$ds^2 = f(r)\,dt_s^2 - f(r)^{-1}dr^2 - r^2 d\Omega^2, \quad f(r)=1-\frac{2m}{r}-\frac{\lambda r^2}{3}$$

with $\lambda>0$ (positive cosmological constant). The two horizons are

$$r_C = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\cos\alpha, \qquad r_H = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\cos\!\left(\alpha+\frac{\pi}{3}\right), \qquad \alpha = \frac{1}{3}\arctan\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{9\lambda m^2}-1}.$$

### Step 1: Coordinate Transformations

**Painlevé** ($t_s \to t$): $\;t = t_s + \int \frac{\sqrt{1-f(r)}}{f(r)}\,dr$. Metric in $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$:

$$ds^2 = f(r)\,dt^2 - 2\sqrt{1-f(r)}\,dr\,dt - (1-f(r))\,dr^2 - r^2 d\Omega^2$$

or equivalently $g_{tt}=f,\; g_{tr}=-\sqrt{1-f},\; g_{rr}=-1,\; g_{\theta\theta}=-r^2,\; g_{\varphi\varphi}=-r^2\sin^2\theta$.

**Lemaitre** $(V, U, \theta, \varphi)$: defined from Painlevé via $U = \tilde{r}-t$, $V = \tilde{r}+t$ where $\tilde{r}=t+\int dr/\sqrt{1-f(r)}$. The inverse metric is $g^{VV}=-\frac{1-\tilde{f}}{\tilde{f}},\; g^{VU}=-\frac{\tilde{f}+1}{\tilde{f}},\; g^{\theta\theta}=-1/y,\; g^{\varphi\varphi}=-1/(y\sin^2\theta)$ with $\tilde{f}(U)=1-f(r)$, $y(U)=r^2$.

### Step 2: Newman-Penrose Formalism — Null Tetrads and Spin Coefficients

In **Painlevé** coordinates, the null tetrad is

$$l_\mu = \bigl(1,\; -\tfrac{1+\sqrt{1-f}}{f},\; 0,\; 0\bigr), \quad n_\mu = \bigl(\tfrac12 f,\; \tfrac{f}{1+\sqrt{1-f}},\; 0,\; 0\bigr)$$
$$m_\mu = -\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}(0,0,1,i\sin\theta), \quad \bar{m}_\mu = -\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}(0,0,1,-i\sin\theta).$$

The non-zero spin coefficients are $\rho=-1/r$, $\; \mu=-f/(2r)$, $\; \gamma=f'/4$, $\; \alpha=-\beta=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2r}}\cot\theta$. Only one Weyl scalar is non-zero: $\Psi_2 = -m/r^3 + \lambda/3$.

In **Lemaitre** coordinates, the tetrad is given in Eq. (3.22) and the spin coefficients in Eq. (3.23); the structure is analogous but with $y=r^2$ replacing $r^2$ and $\tilde{f}$ replacing $f$.

### Step 3: Teukolsky Master Equation

Assume azimuthal and time dependence $\sim e^{(in\varphi-Et)}$ (Painlevé) or $e^{(in\varphi-EV)}$ (Lemaitre). The coupled NP equations yield **two decoupled master equations** (3.7) and (3.8) for the spin-weighted fields $\Phi_s$ and $\Phi_{-s}$, valid for all spin values $s = 1/2, 1, 3/2, 2$ (and their negatives). Using the explicit spin coefficients, both reduce to a single **Teukolsky-type equation** (3.10 in Painlevé, 3.27 in Lemaitre) for a generic spin $s$. The two equations are related by the duality $s \to -s$.

The key structures are:
- $\Delta_r = r^2 f(r)$ (Painlevé) or $\Delta_U = y(1-\tilde{f})$ (Lemaitre)
- $\mathcal{K}_1 = Er^2$ or $Ey$
- $\mathcal{K}_2 = -m/\sin\theta$
- The cosmological constant enters through $\Psi_2$ and through the $(4s^2+2)\lambda/3$ term in the effective potential.

### Step 4: WKB Approximation

Substitute $\hat{\Phi}_s \sim e^{iG(r,\theta)}$ (or $e^{iG(U,\theta)}$) into the master equation. At leading order in $\hbar$ (or equivalently in the high-frequency limit) this yields a **Hamilton-Jacobi equation** for the radial momentum $k_r = G_r$ (or $k_U = G_U$). For Painlevé, Eq. (3.12):

$$f(r)\,k_r^2 - 2E\sqrt{1-f(r)}\,k_r - E^2 - \frac{k_\theta^2}{g_{\theta\theta}} - \frac{m^2}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} + H_s = 0$$

where $H_s = \frac{2sm}{r^2\sin\theta}\cot\theta + \frac{s^2}{r^2}\cot^2\theta + \frac{\lambda}{3}(4s^2+2) - \frac{s}{2r^2}\Delta_r''$.

The roots are $k_r^\pm$ (out-going / in-going). The paper then **averages** $\bar{k}_r = (k_r^+ - k_r^-)/2$, which gives Eq. (3.16):

$$\bar{k}_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g^{rr}}}\sqrt{g_{tt}\left[E^2 - \left(\frac{k_\theta^2}{g_{\theta\theta}} + \frac{(m+m_0)^2}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} + V_s\right)\right]}$$

with $m_0 = s\cos\theta$ (an effective "angular momentum barrier") and $V_s = \frac{\lambda}{3}(4s^2+2) - \frac{s}{2r^2}\Delta_r''$.

The Lemaitre calculation (3.28–3.33) is identical in structure with $U$-coordinate quantities.

### Step 5: Brick Wall Model Boundary Conditions and Mode Counting

The **improved thin-layer BWM** imposes:

$$\Phi = 0 \quad\text{for}\quad r < r_H+\epsilon \;\;\text{and}\;\; r > r_H+N\epsilon$$

and similarly at the cosmological horizon $r_C$. In Lemaitre coordinates the brick walls are at $U_H \pm \tilde{\epsilon}$, $U_H + \tilde{N}\tilde{\epsilon}$, $U_C \mp \tilde{\epsilon}$, $U_C - \tilde{N}\tilde{\epsilon}$. The UV cutoff $\epsilon_h$ and IR cutoff $\Lambda_h$ are fixed by:

$$\eta_h^2 = \frac{2\epsilon_h^2}{15}, \qquad N = \frac{\Lambda_h^2}{\epsilon_h^2}$$

where $\eta_h$ is the proper distance from the horizon to the inner brick wall.

The number of quantum states with energy $E$ is (Eq. 3.18):

$$n_h(E,s) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi\; d\bar{k}_r\,d\bar{k}_\theta\,dm = \frac{1}{3\pi}\int d\theta \int_{r_h+\epsilon}^{r_h+N\epsilon} dr\;\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{(g_{tt})^2}\bigl[E^2 - g_{tt}\,V_s\bigr]^{3/2}$$

Summing over $\pm s$ and expanding in the **high-frequency** limit gives

$$n_h(E) \approx \frac{g_s}{3\pi}\bigl[I_{1h}\,E^3 + 3I_{2h}\,E\bigr]$$

where $I_{1h}, I_{2h}$ are integrals over the thin shell near the horizon (or $U_H$, $U_C$ in Lemaitre).

### Step 6: Free Energy and Entropy

The free energy is the standard (thermal equilibrium) expression:

$$F_h = -\int_0^\infty dE\;\frac{n_h(E)}{e^{\beta E}-(-1)^{2s}}$$

With $\zeta(4)=\pi^4/90$ and $\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$, this yields

$$F_h = -g_s\left[2\zeta(4)\frac{15+(-1)^{2s}}{16\pi\beta^4}\,I_{1h} + \zeta(2)\frac{3+(-1)^{2s}}{4\pi\beta^2}\,I_{2h}\right].$$

The entropy follows from $S_h = \beta^2(\partial F_h/\partial\beta)$, giving the final result (3.21, 3.37):

$$S_h/g_s = \frac{15+(-1)^{2s}}{16}\left[\frac{A_h}{48\pi\epsilon_h^2} + \frac{1}{45}\!\left(1-\frac{\lambda\epsilon_h^2}{2}\right)\!\ln\frac{\Lambda_h}{\epsilon_h}\right] - \frac{3+(-1)^{2s}}{4}\frac{\lambda(1+2s^2)}{36\pi}\,A_h\ln\frac{\Lambda_h}{\epsilon_h}$$

where $A_h = 4\pi r_H^2$ or $4\pi r_C^2$ and $g_s \in \{1,2,0,1,4\}$ is the spin degeneracy. **This expression is identical in Painlevé, Lemaitre, and Schwarzschild-like coordinates**, matching the known result of Wu & Yan [6].

## 2. Assumptions the Paper Papers Over

This is the more critical part of the question. The methodology rests on or elides a substantial number of assumptions:

**(A) The WKB / high-frequency approximation is never justified quantitatively.** The substitution $\hat\Phi_s \sim e^{iG}$ and the subsequent truncation to the leading-order term in $G$ is the WKB ansatz. No error estimate, validity condition, or discussion of what happens at WKB turning points (where the phase-space integral (3.18) is ill-defined) is given. The expansion of $n_h(E)$ to $E^3+E$ is labelled a "high-frequency approximation" but there is no statement about the range of $E$ for which it is accurate, nor any comparison with a non-WKB calculation.

**(B) Thermal equilibrium is assumed for a two-horizon spacetime.** The free-energy formula $F = \int n(E)/(e^{\beta E}-(-1)^{2s})$ is the partition function for a system in **thermal equilibrium** at a single inverse temperature $\beta$. A non-extremal SdS black hole has two horizons with different surface gravities (and hence different Hawking temperatures). The paper acknowledges in the introduction that "it cannot be applied to cases out of thermal non-equilibrium" but then proceeds to use exactly this equilibrium formula for a two-horizon spacetime. No comment is made on which horizon's temperature is used, whether a single global temperature exists, or how the free energy should be modified for a system in a heat bath at finite background temperature.

**(C) The definition of positive frequency and the vacuum state is hand-waved.** The paper states (citing Kraus-Wilczek [25]) that along the curve $dr+\sqrt{1-f(r)}\,dt=0$ it is "easy to prove that the modes used to calculate the entropy are essentially the same as that in the Schwarzschild-like coordinate," but this claim is made without proof and only for the "massless scalar field." For the other spin fields (spinor, Maxwell, Rarita-Schwinger, graviton), no such argument is even stated. The entire equivalence result hinges on the mode definitions being coordinate-independent, yet the paper's proof is: (i) the asymptotic structure is the same, and (ii) $V$ and $t$ both tend to $t_s$ as $r\to\infty$. This is a heuristic, not a proof, for general spin.

**(D) The BWM cutoffs are borrowed and used circularly.** The specific relations $\eta_h^2=2\epsilon_h^2/15$ and $N=\Lambda_h^2/\epsilon_h^2$ are imported from [12, 13] to **match** the known Schwarzschild-like-coefficient result. There is no independent physical derivation of why these are the correct UV/IR cutoff relations in the Painlevé or Lemaitre coordinates. One could object that the calculation is set up to reproduce a known answer: the BWM regularization is tuned to yield Eq. (1.2), and the paper then reports "success" by verifying the match.

**(E) The spin degeneracy factor $g_s$ is asserted, not derived.** The values $g_s=1$ (scalar), $g_s=2$ (Weyl/neutrino), $g_s=0$ (Maxwell), $g_s=1$ (Rarita-Schwinger), $g_s=4$ (graviton) are stated in the introduction without derivation. In particular, $g_s=0$ for the source-free Maxwell field means the electromagnetic contribution to the entropy is zero — a physically surprising result that is simply accepted. The paper never discusses whether this is an artefact of the WKB treatment of a gauge field (where one should properly gauge-fix and account for unphysical polarizations) or a genuine physical statement.

**(F) No back-reaction or self-interaction.** All quantum fields are treated as propagating on a **fixed classical SdS background**. The gravitational back-reaction of the thermal bath of quantum fields on the metric, and any self-interaction (e.g. $\phi^4$ for a scalar), are entirely neglected. At the scale of the UV cutoff, where the particle number density is divergent, this is a non-trivial assumption.

**(H) No Kerr / rotation generalization.** The NP formalism and Teukolsky equation are worked out explicitly for the spherically symmetric case. There is no discussion of how the analysis would extend to Kerr-de Sitter (which has two Killing vectors and a more complex Weyl tensor), even though the method is formally identical.

**(I) The "thin-layer" approximation and the horizon location in the action.** The paper notes that "the event and cosmological horizons manifest themselves as singularities in the expression for the semiclassical action" — i.e. $\Delta_r \to 0$ or $\Delta_U \to 0$ at the horizons. The WKB ansatz and the phase-space integral are formally singular at these points. The BWM regularizes this by shifting the integration limits by $\epsilon$ and $N\epsilon$, but no discussion is given of how the WKB approximation behaves in the limit $\epsilon \to 0$ (i.e. whether the result is stable against the precise location of the brick walls).

**(J) The cosmological constant sign is positive only.** The analysis assumes $\lambda > 0$ (de Sitter). The Schwarzschild ($\lambda=0$) and Schwarzschild-anti-de Sitter ($\lambda<0$) cases are not discussed, even though the formalism should apply. For $\lambda=0$ the cosmological horizon disappears and the two-boundary BWM collapses to a single boundary.

**(K) Single-particle interpretation of the spin.** The "arbitrary spin field" is treated as a collection of independent single-particle degrees of freedom in phase space. There is no discussion of how the occupation numbers should be modified for interacting fields, or whether the mode counting (which effectively counts one-particle states) is adequate for computing the entropy when the particle number is thermally fluctuating.

**(L) No discussion of the Hawking-Page transition.** The SdS spacetime has a well-known thermodynamic phase structure in which the dominant configuration changes between a small black hole and a thermal de Sitter state at a critical temperature. The paper's entropy formula is written for a fixed horizon radius $r_H$ or $r_C$ without addressing at which point the thermodynamic ensemble should be reconsidered.

**(M) The result is presented under a framing of "open question" that overstates novelty.** The equivalence of the BWM entropy in different coordinates for the massless scalar field had already been shown by Jing [7] (2004) and by the complex-path method of Shankaranarayanan et al. [19, 20] (2001–2002). The introduction frames the generalization to arbitrary spin in SdS as an "open question" but provides no argument that the answer might have been different; the coordinate invariance follows from the asymptotic argument that the paper itself gives.

**Strengths.** The paper is internally consistent, the algebra is carried through for all five spin species in two coordinate systems, and the final result is correct (matching Wu-Yan). > S/gs = 15 + (−1)^{2s}/16·[ A_h/48π·ε_h⁻² + 1/45·(1 − λr_h²/2)·ln(Λ_h/ε_h) ] − (3+(−1)^{2s})/4·λ(1+2s²)/36π·A_h·ln(Λ_h/ε_h)

## Audit of the evidence

**Is the comparison fair? — No, in an important sense.**
The "comparison" is not between two independent determinations. The SdS baseline (Eq. 1.2) was obtained with the *same* method — improved thin-layer BWM, WKB phase-space counting, high-energy expansion — merely in a different coordinate. The two new derivations reuse the identical machinery (Eqs. 3.7–3.10 vs. 3.27–3.37), and crucially, the Painlevé/Lemaitre results inherit equality by construction: the authors adopt Kraus's free-falling curve `dr + √(1−f) dt = 0` to define positive frequency, and at the end of §3.2 they show `dV = dt` along that curve — i.e., the mode structures in all three coordinates are *identical inputs* to the same count. Their own concluding argument ("it should not be surprised that…") concedes the equivalence follows from the vacua coinciding rather than being an independent check. The decisive counterfactual — computing the entropy with the *natural* Painlevé/Lemaitre (free-fall) vacuum, which need not coincide with the static one — is never performed. So the claim that "the BWM entropy is unambiguously coordinate-invariant" is not actually tested; only "same method + same vacuum ⇒ same answer" is, which is tautological.

Supporting the weakness of the evidence:
- The pivotal algebraic steps are asserted, not shown: the Teukolsky master equations (3.10) and (3.37) appear as "it can be shown that…" backed by citations ([10, 23, 24]), and the explicit √(−g̃), g_VV integrands in (3.35) are given with no derivation, so a reader must take the coordinate-dependent master equations on faith.
- One technical check that would have strengthened the audit — e.g., numerically evaluating the brick-wall integrals I₁h, I₂h in both coordinates for concrete m, λ — is absent.

**Are baselines current? — Marginal.**
For a 2007 submission, the baseline (Wu & Yan, PRD 69, 2004) and the scalar-only precursor (Jing, PRD 69, 2004) are contemporary. But both come from the same group (Jing and collaborators), so the "open question" being closed is one the group itself set up by extension of its own method. **Error bars or statistical significance? — N/A, but the analogous concern is mishandled.**
This is a purely analytical paper with no data, so statistical error bars are not applicable. The analogue is the *order of the approximation*, and here the evidence is overstated: the "exact equivalence" holds only within the WKB/high-frequency expansion (Eqs. 3.18–3.19) and the ≈-level proper-distance estimates (η_h ≈ 2√(εr_h)/(...)). The subleading terms in the mode-count expansion are never tracked, so whether the *corrections* are coordinate-invariant is an open question silently papered over by the word "exactly." The claim should have been stated as "equivalence at the retained order."

**Minor issues:** several typos/oddities in the reference list and text ("Coorection", "ststionary", "time en the Painlevé spacetime"), and the extraction of formula (1.2) in the introduction shows the constant "15" term that carries no explanation.

## Bottom line

The computation is a competent but incremental extension of Jing's 2004 scalar-field coordinate-invariance result to arbitrary spin, a second coordinate, and the two-horizon SdS background. The headline equivalence is true only in the strong caveat sense: it is guaranteed by the identical vacuum/mode choice fed into an identical method, with no independent determination, no numerical cross-check, and no control on the discarded subleading corrections. The physics content (entropy should be coordinate-invariant) was anticipated by the authors themselves, and the genuinely interesting question their setup raises (vacuum-dependence of BWM entropy) is sidestepped.

## Paper identified

## The weakest link: step 5 in the Lemaître sector — (3.34) → (3.36)

The conclusion stops being entailed at the moment the paper feeds the *Lemaître* mode count (3.34) into the thermal free-energy integral (3.36). Exactly why:

**The data being counted are not spectral data of a Hamiltonian.** A canonical partition sum Z = Σ e^{−βEᵢ} is only the partition function of a system with a *time-independent* Hamiltonian, which in a relativistic setting requires a timelike Killing vector. The Lemaître metric (2.6) has g_VV = (1−f̃(U))/4, g_VU = −(f̃(U)+1)/4 — explicit U-dependence, so ∂_V is **not** a Killing vector; the coordinate representation is time-dependent. The "E" in the ansatz e^{i(mφ−EV)} (used to pass from (3.27) to (3.28)) is therefore only a Fourier label for a non-cyclic time: it is not a conserved energy and not an eigenvalue of any self-adjoint, time-independent H. Worse, in a time-dependent spacetime there is no invariant positive-frequency split, so the very notions "vacuum," "modes with energy less than E," and β = Hawking temperature — which the paper itself says (Introduction, p. 2) *require* a timelike Killing vector — are undefined here. The BWM's own premise silently fails in the coordinate system under study.

**Consequence:** the Boltzmann weight in (3.36) is carried over by prescription from the stationary case, not derived from the Lemaître data. n_h(E) in (3.34) is an algebraic root count of the WKB dispersion (3.30), invariant under relabeling the Fourier variable — but the thermodynamic identification (canonical ensemble at temperature β) is a property of the *dynamics*, and the counting data do not entail it. Hence (3.37) is not entailed as a physical entropy of the Lemaître description at all; it is just a number that equals the covariant phase-space volume.

**Contrast that isolates the defect:** the identical step in the *Painlevé* sector, (3.18) → (3.20), is sound, because the Painlevé metric (2.3) is stationary — ∂_t is a Killing vector (the paper says so in §2.1), E is conserved, and the thermal ensemble is definable. The chain breaks only in the Lemaître twin, which is the paper's genuinely new ingredient.

**The equivalence conclusion is actually entailed by the coordinate transformation, not the calculation.** Section 4 admits the decisive fact: dV = 2dt + dr/√(1−f(r)) (2.5), and along the regular curve dr = −√(1−f(r))dt (the Kraus [25] curve used in the painlevé part) one has dV = dt; as r→∞ both V and t reduce to Schwarzschild time t_s. Since the modes are defined by the *asymptotic* positive-frequency split, and the brick-wall thicknesses are matched by proper distance (η_H, η_C identical in both sections), the equivalence S_Lemaitre = S_Painlevé = S_Schw follows from general covariance plus that two-line asymptotic-time identification. The lengthy Lemaître derivation does no independent work: its only nontrivial input — the asymptotic identification of V and t — is precisely what makes the result automatic, while the step that was supposed to do the thermodynamic work, (3.36), cannot be justified in that frame. The argument is circular at exactly the wrong place.

Minor secondary issues do not change the verdict: the WKB e^{iG} step mixes a high-multipole (geometric-optics) limit with counting over the full spectrum (inherited from the Jing–Yan program, shared with the reference result (1.2)); and the free energy (3.36) uses a single β for a two-horizon spacetime, which is masked here because β cancels in S = β²∂F/∂β. Both are pre-existing features of the BWM, not introduced by this paper.

## Overall assessment

The work is a competent but derivative coordinate-check. The Painlevé derivation is legitimate; the Lemaître derivation contains an unjustified (functionally circular) thermodynamic step; and the headline result is what covariance makes inevitable, as the authors half-admit. The paper does not establish that the BWM entropy is a well-defined entropy in time-dependent coordinates — rather, it shows the formal procedure reproduces a covariant number, which is a weaker statement than the title implies.

The authors mention the BWM's known drawbacks (little-mass approximation, $L^{3}$ vacuum term, non-equilibrium limitations) but do not confront the deeper point — in a two-horizon space like SdS, the "entropy" is a difference/assignment between event and cosmological sectors, and the improvement's handling of non-equilibrium is doing quiet, load-bearing work that deserves explicit scrutiny.

### Minor issues

- **Non-equilibrium caveat:** The authors cite Gao–Lin / Li–Zhao [16,17] precisely because two-horizon spacetimes are non-equilibrium, which is what the "improved" model is meant to fix. Yet the paper never says *why* the standard high-frequency/thermal expansion used here remains valid for SdS in these coordinates, beyond citing [14].
- **Notation/typography.** "Lemaître"/"Lemaitre" is inconsistent (title drops the accent; body sometimes keeps it). "Painlevé" vs. "Panelevé" should be standardised. "Schwaichild" (Ref. [16]) and "Schwartzschild" (Ref. [9]) typos in the bibliography should be fixed. "It should not be surprised" → "it should not be surprising."
- **Limited physical interpretation of the invariant quantity.** No comment on whether the identified invariant is the *total* entropy, the *difference* $S_{H}-S_{C}$, or the Gao–Ma thermodynamic combination — for a two-horizon SdS space this distinction matters and it would strengthen the paper to state which object is coordinate-invariant.

### Recommendation

This is a **sound, clearly written, technically correct** paper that fills a small completeness gap in the brick-wall-modeled entropy literature. However, its result is essentially guaranteed by covariance and is argued on that basis in the paper itself, the BWM is a known-approximate and regulator-tied method, and the physically delicate (time-dependent) Lemaître argument is glossed. For a **prestigious general journal** this is borderline — I would not recommend acceptance as a contribution of that calibre; it is better placed in a solid specialized venue (the CQG / Int. J. Mod. Phys. D / Chin. Phys. Lett. tier in which most of its references and the brick-wall literature live), or in a strengthened form. If revising here, I would require (a) a rigorous treatment of the thermal state in the time-dependent Lemaître slicing, (b) an explicit statement of *which* entropy object is invariant and why the high-frequency/thermal expansion is valid despite SdS non-equilibrium, and (c) a sharper framing of what new physics (beyond covariance of the BWM recipe) the explicit calculation provides, since as written the authors concede the outcome was expected.

**Net assessment:** correct and competent, but incremental; low physical novelty and modest impact.

## [0709.1186v1-mentor] Mentor


I've read the full paper (Ding & Jing, "Entropy of the Schwarzschild–de Sitter Black Hole due to arbitrary spin fields in different Coordinates," arXiv:0709.1186). Here is my assessment.

## Review

**Summary of the work.** The authors take the Schwarzschild–de Sitter (SdS) spacetime in Painlevé and Lemaître coordinates and, within the Newman–Penrose (NP) formalism, re-derive the Teukolsky-type master equations for massless fields of arbitrary spin. They then impose the improved thin-layer brick-wall model (BWM) and compute the statistical-mechanical entropy in each null coordinate system. The headline result — that the entropy is exactly identical to that in the Schwarzschild-like coordinates — reproduces, for arbitrary spin and for the two-horizon SdS background, an equivalence that was argued (via Hawking temperature) by Shankaranarayanan et al. and (for the massless scalar in a general static hole) in the first author's own earlier work (ref. [7]).

**What is done well.**
- The NP set-up is carried through carefully in both coordinate systems: null tetrads (3.1, 3.22), spin coefficients (3.2, 3.23), Weyl tensor, and directional derivatives are all recomputed rather than glossed over. The Lemaître tetrad in particular is the fiddly part and is handled concretely.
- The use of the *improved* thin-layer BWM is the right methodological choice for the two-horizon case: the standard BWM assumes a thermal bath and breaks down for the SdS region between event and cosmological horizons, so the authors' motivation (Sec. 1) for a non-equilibrium prescription is sound and clearly stated.
- The final entropies (3.21) and (3.37) do match the known Schwarzschild-like result (1.2), and the high-frequency expansion, proper-distance cutoffs (η_H, η_C), and the free-energy/Zeta-function structure (3.20, 3.36) are internally consistent.

**Weaknesses I'd push back on.**

1. **The result is essentially a negative/expected one, and the physical argument is thin.** The conclusion follows almost entirely from the fact that all three time functions (Schwarzschild t_s, Painlevé t, Lemaître V) coincide asymptotically, so the positive-frequency / vacuum definitions and hence the asymptotic wave modes coincide. The authors acknowledge this at the end, but it undercuts the value of the long computation: the elaborate NP rederivation is doing the work of showing the equivalence the asymptotic-mode argument already implies. The paper would be stronger if that conceptual point were made explicit *up front* and the computation framed as a verification, rather than presented as if confirming an open question.

2. **The "open question" framing overstates novelty.** Sec. 1 poses "can we obtain the same entropy in the different coordinates?" as still open for arbitrary spin in SdS. This is legitimate, but the extension from Jing [7] (scalar, general static) to arbitrary spin in SdS is a modest one; the arbitrary-spin content is imported directly from the known master equations rather than new. Be honest about how much is genuinely new versus how much is mechanical extension.

3. **Minor technical/notational roughness.**
   - In (3.9) the two dual equations carry Φ_s and Φ_{-s} but the text's "interchange s→−s" is stated without noting the accompanying ω→−ω sign flip; a careful reader will want that made explicit.
   - In (3.20)/(3.36) the free energy uses "2ζ(4)" in front of I_1h — worth a one-line check against the standard BWM normalization so the reader can trace the 15/16 and 3/4 coefficients (they do agree with (1.2), but it is asserted, not shown).
   - The Lemaître brick-wall location ɛ̃ (Sec. 3.2) is given in an implicit closed form involving log of radicals of ɛ̃; this should be stated as the defining implicit relation, not as if it were solved.

4. **No cross-check or sanity limit.** A strong version of this paper would explicitly verify a known limit — e.g., λ→0 recovering the Schwarzschild/Painlevé result, or the near-horizon behavior of the integrals I_1h, I_2h — to guard against an unnoticed algebraic slip in the lengthy Lemaître computation. There is none.

**Bottom line.** A competent, internally consistent, and well-organized computational paper, but an incremental one whose conclusion (coordinate independence of the entropy in these coordinates) was largely anticipated by the asymptotic-mode argument and the first author's earlier work. It is fine for a lower-tier outlet; it does not change anyone's expectations, and the physical insight is modest. The main fixes for a stronger submission: reframe the motivation around the known equivalence and use the computation as a check; add a cross-check limit; tighten the implicit brick-wall definition and the dual-equation sign convention.
