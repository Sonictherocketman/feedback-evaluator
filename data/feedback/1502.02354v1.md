I've now read the full paper (31 pages). Here is my evaluation of "Homological Dimensions Relative to Presolving Subcategories" (Zhaoyong Huang, 2015).

## Summary of the argument

The paper studies a natural question in relative homological algebra: if two homological (co)dimensions are defined relative to a subcategory $\mathscr{C}$ of an abelian category and to a subcategory $\mathscr{S}$ of it, what is the relationship between them? Classical dimensions are defined relative to the projective/flat/injective subcategories (which sit inside the Gorenstein ones), so the author builds a relative *presolving/precosolving* framework (Definition 3.1 / 4.1) and derives criteria for computing and comparing the $\mathscr{S}$- and $\mathscr{C}$-dimensions. Section 4 is the exact dual of Section 3. Section 5 applies the results to special subcategories ($\widetilde{\mathrm{res}\,\mathscr{C}}, \widetilde{\mathrm{cores}\,\mathscr{C}}$) and module categories, recovering and extending results on Gorenstein projective/injective/flat, strongly Gorenstein flat, and FP-injective dimensions. It closes by framing questions/Conjecture 5.18 on finite-projective/injective dimension relations and ties Question 5.17 to the strong Nakayama conjecture (Proposition 5.19).

## Strengths

- **The unification is clean and correct.** The $\mathscr{E}$-presolving/$\mathscr{S}$-dimension framework genuinely subsumes several existing theories (Gorenstein subcategories via [SSW], cotorsion pairs, strongly Gorenstein flat, FP-injective) under one set of lemmas. Example 3.2 and 4.2 document this well.
- **The core technical lemma (Prop 3.3) is the engine** of the dimension-comparison theorems (3.6–3.14), and its proof (pullback diagrams on pages 8) is sound and well-executed. I checked Prop 3.3, Theorems 3.6, 3.8, 3.10 and found no errors — the manipulations are standard and valid.
- **Proposition 5.19** (equivalence between SN(C) holding for $R^{op}$ and the statement that a torsion-free module of pd ≤ 1 is projective) is the paper's most interesting result — a concrete bridge from this homological-dimension framework to a major open problem.

## Weaknesses / critique

1. **Limited self-contained novelty.** A substantial part of the machinery is imported from the author's own earlier paper [Hu] (Huang 2013): Lemma 2.4(1), Theorems 3.2, 3.6, 5.7, 5.8 are all cited rather than proved. The foundational work on proper resolutions and Gorenstein categories is in [H2]/[SSW]; this paper packages and extends them. The genuinely new content is Prop 3.3 plus the comparison theorems (3.10–3.14) and the module applications.

2. **Much of Section 5 is restatement rather than new result.** The author explicitly notes Theorem 5.6(2) = [Z, Thm 2.3], 5.6(6) is "well known," and 5.10(3)/5.10(6) generalize or coincide with [H1, Thm 2.1]/[H2, Thm 2.20/2.22]. While the uniform framework has value, the reader gets the sense of "here is the old result expressed in the new language" more than "here is something new that was previously unproved."

3. **Section 4 adds no work** — it is entirely dual, with proofs omitted. This is legitimate but halves the effective depth.

4. **The hypotheses accumulate.** The comparison theorems (3.10, 3.11, 4.10, 4.11) require a stack of closure conditions (closed under extensions, kernels of appropriate maps, direct summands, generator/cogenerator, $\mathscr{S}\subseteq \mathscr{C}^{\bot}\cap{}^\bot\mathscr{C}$) that are stated without motivating discussion of which are essential. A sharper minimal-hypothesis analysis or a worked example showing the conditions are sharp would strengthen the paper.

5. **The conjecture connection is framed, not advanced.** The GNC/SNC material closes the paper without resolving anything; the questions (5.12–5.17) are left open, which is appropriate, but the overall contribution to these problems is suggestive rather than substantive.

## Overall judgment

A sound, competent unification paper in relative/Gorenstein homological algebra. It is rigorous and well-organized, and it does provide a genuinely usable common framework that recovers several known results in one stroke. Its originality is modest — it is generalization and unification resting heavily on prior work (much of it the author's own) rather than a breakthrough — and its downstream impact is confined to a fairly specialized audience, with only a suggestive (not decisive) link to the Nakayama conjectures.

## Summary

This paper develops the theory of (pre)resolving and (pre)coresolving subcategories in the sense of [SSW, H2, Hu], with emphasis on *relative* versions: a subcategory 𝒯 that is 𝒞-presolving (admits an 𝒞-proper generator and is closed under 𝒞-proper extensions). The main contributions are (i) dimension/codimension comparisons between 𝒞-relative and 𝒯-relative homological dimensions (Section 3), (ii) fully dual results (Section 4), and (iii) applications to module categories — particularly identifications of classical and Gorenstein type dimensions, and questions/conjectures touching on the generalized and strong Nakayama conjectures (Section 5).

## Major Comments

**1. Originality and framing.** The core machinery is largely derivative of the author's own prior paper [Hu] (Propositions 3.3, 3.4 are essentially restatements of the "change of resolutions" arguments from [Hu, Theorem 3.2] and its proof) and of Enochs–Jenda II. The genuinely new element is the *pairing* of two subcategories (a "base" 𝒞 and a "relative" 𝒯) and the comparison theorems (Theorems 3.10, 3.11, 4.10, 4.11) that say when the two relative dimensions coincide. The idea is natural but not deep: it is the expected bookkeeping for relative dimensions, and much of the Section 5 payoff is just re-packaging results already known or already in [Hu], [Z], [H1], [H2] (see Remark 5.7 and Remark 5.11, which admit this explicitly).

**2. Section 4 duality.** A substantial portion of the paper is stated to be "completely dual to that in Section 3 so we only list the results without proofs" (Theorem 3.7's codimension twin, all of Section 4). For a paper of this type that is acceptable to me, but it does inflate the result count; I would rather see a short note explaining which non-mechanical subtleties (if any) arise in dualizing the Hom(𝒞,·)-exact vs Hom(·,𝒞)-exact hypotheses, since the "proper/coprop" asymmetry is worth a sentence.

**3. Weakness in the applications.** Theorem 5.6 and Theorem 5.10 are presented as the centerpiece, but reading them against the references, most assertions are restatements: 5.6(2) *is* [Z, Theorem 2.3]; 5.6(6) *is* [H2, Theorem 2.20]; 5.10(3) generalizes [H1, Theorem 2.1] in a way that is not made precise (which additional hypotheses on R are being removed? The proof in Theorem 5.10 is not actually given in the excerpts I can see — please check; if it is omitted and only stated, this is a real gap). The paper should be more explicit about (a) strict novelty of each item and (b) what degenerates versus its predecessor. As written, the "generalization" claims are asserted rather than clearly differentiated.

**4. Questions 5.12/5.13 are open problems already known in different dress.** Question 5.13 (pd_R A vs T-dim_R A for pd finite) is essentially the standard question behind many variants of the GNC/SNC discussion (compare the "finite pd implies Gorenstein projective" question and the work of Holm and its successors). The paper should acknowledge this literature more fully; framing these as "new questions" overstates the novelty. Proposition 5.19 (SNC for R^op ⇔ projectivity of torsionfree modules of pd ≤ 1) is the single most interesting result in the paper — a crisp equivalence connecting SNC to a concrete module-theoretic statement — but it occupies only half a page and deserves a dedicated treatment: examples, counterexample searches, relationship to the "self-extending" or "1-Gorenstein" literature, and its exact status (is it known for some classes of algebras, e.g., symmetric, self-injective, hereditary?).

**5. Technical nit:** In Theorem 3.10(1) as I read the proof, the claim "K_n ∈ 𝒯 implies the sequence 0 → C_t → … → C_n → K_n → 0 splits" (so K_n is a summand of C_n) uses that K_n ∈ ⁺𝒞; this is fine but the argument hinges on C_n ∈ 𝒞 and K_n ∈ ⁺𝒞 with the Hom(𝒞,·)-exact sequence splitting — this step is stated tersely and I would want it expanded (which long exact Ext sequence vanishes? the relevant argument is that Extⁱ_𝒜(C_i, K_n) = 0 for K_n ∈ ⁺𝒞 and all C_i ∈ 𝒞, which requires the Hom-exactness to make the dimension-shifting legitimate, but the text only says "which implies...").

**6. Theorem 5.9 vs. cited sources.** (1) is cited as [CFH, Lemma 2.17] and (3) as [B1, Theorem 2.3]/[B2, Theorem 2.2] — again the paper essentially reports these results as applications. The contribution of the paper itself to Section 5.2 is the *uniform derivation via the framework of Sections 3/4*, which is a legitimate but modest organizational contribution.

**7. Presentation.** The paper is competently written and the diagrams are clear. However, the heavy dependence on "by [Hu, Theorem 3.2]", "by [Hu, Lemma 2.4(1)]", etc., combined with [Hu] being the author's own recent paper, makes it hard for a reader not in the [SSW]-circle to verify claims independently. For a submission, I would expect at least the key [Hu]-lemma used in the critical Proposition 3.3 proof (the exactness of third/fourth columns in the pullback diagram) to be reproduced or clearly paraphrased rather than cited.

**8. Conventions/typos.** Minor: the paper says "Throughout this paper, 𝒜 is an abelian category and all subcategories of 𝒜 are full and additive" but then some results quietly require more (e.g., enough projectives/injectives in Example 3.2(1), exactness of products/coproducts nowhere stated). The assumption list should be a uniform standing-conditions box rather than piecemeal "unless stated otherwise" notes. I could not spot a concrete typographical error in the pages I read, but the use of "𝒞-coprop" vs "𝒞-coproper" (Definition 2.5(2) uses "𝒞-coprop cogenerator", elsewhere "coproper") should be unified.

## Conclusion

The paper does a clean and useful job of organizing relative homological dimensions over pairs of subcategories and produces a small set of comparison theorems and, in Proposition 5.19, one result of independent interest. But the originality is limited: Sections 3–4 are reorganizations of the author's own prior work and of [H2], and half of the applications in Section 5 are verbatim restatements of literature, in places without clearly stating what is new. The Nakayama-conjecture connections, which the abstract advertises as a highlight, are delivered mostly as questions (5.12–5.17) and one proposition of equivalence (5.19), not as new insight toward the conjectures themselves. I would rate it as a solid, publishable paper in a specialized homological-algebra journal (e.g., J. Pure Appl. Algebra or Commun. Algebra — the house style of the references matches), appropriate as a journal of record but not as a standout contribution to the field. If the author wants a stronger paper, focus should shift to: (a) proving or refuting Question 5.12/5.17 in concrete classes of algebras; (b) expanding Proposition 5.19 and its dual; (c) an honest "novelty vs. [Hu]" comparison table.

**Recommendation:** Accept with minor-to-moderate revisions (clarify what is new, expand the Nakayama section, fill any missing proofs).

As an editor: accept with minor revisions. ## [1502.02354v1-critique] Critique


# Critical Review

**Paper:** *Homological Dimensions Relative to Presolving Subcategories* — Zhaoyong Huang, arXiv:1502.02354v1 (Feb 2015, 31 pp.)

## Summary of Content

The paper develops a framework for comparing homological dimensions defined relative to two different subcategories of an abelian category: a subcategory $\mathscr{C}$ (e.g., projectives) and a "$\mathscr{C}$-presolving" (or "$\mathscr{C}$-precoresolving") subcategory $\mathscr{T}$ that is closed under "proper extensions" and admits a "$\mathscr{C}$-proper generator." The main theorems give criteria for when a $\mathscr{T}$-dimension (or codimension) of an object can be computed via a $\mathscr{C}$-resolution of finite length, and sufficient conditions under which the two dimensions are equal (Theorems 3.10–3.14 and their duals in §4). Section 5 applies this to module categories, recovering equivalence results like $pd_A = Gpd_A$ when $id_A < \infty$, and connecting certain open questions on the generalized Nakayama and strong Nakayama conjectures (Conjecture 5.18, Proposition 5.19).

## Strengths

1. **Systematic and coherent framework.** The two-parameter approach (subcategory $\mathscr{C}$ vs. presolving subcategory $\mathscr{T}$) is a clean and genuinely useful way to organize the comparison of Gorenstein-type dimensions. The resulting comparison theorems (3.10–3.14) package together a range of results that are scattered in the literature (recovering results of Holm, Zhang, Sather-Wagstaff, etc.) in a unified form.

2. **Correct and well-executed proofs.** The diagram-chasing arguments in Propositions 2.3, 3.3, 3.4 and Theorems 3.6, 3.8, 3.10 are standard and, to what I can verify, correct. The inductive argument in Theorem 3.6 and the pullback diagram in Proposition 3.3 are both clean.

3. **Useful connections to important open problems.** The most interesting part of the paper is the end of Section 5: Conjecture 5.18 restates a specific consequence of the GNC in terms of dimension equalities (the $cores\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$-dimension and $\perp RR$-dimension), and Proposition 5.19 gives a clean equivalence between the SNC for $R^{op}$ and a statement about projective dimension of modules with torsion-free dimension $\leq 1$. This is a useful reformulation, even if it does not solve the conjecture.

4. **Clear organization.** The parallel structure of Sections 3 and 4 makes the paper easy to navigate, and the "Example 3.2" and "Example 4.2" lists serve as useful reference tables for checking when a given subcategory is $\mathscr{C}$-resolving.

## Critical Observations

1. **Heavy dependence on the author's previous work [Hu].** The paper is essentially built on top of Huang, *Proper resolutions and Gorenstein categories*, J. Algebra 393 (2013). Virtually every nontrivial step invokes a lemma or theorem from [Hu] by reference, often with phrases like "by [Hu, Lemma 3.1] and Theorem 3.2, it is not difficult to see …" without restating the result. This makes the paper very difficult to read in isolation; a reader who does not have [Hu] at hand cannot verify many claims. The foundational Definitions 2.1, 2.8, and the "proper" formalism are all attributed to [Hu] and not re-developed. This is a significant accessibility issue.

2. **Many proofs omitted by duality.** Section 4 is described as "completely dual to Section 3" and the proofs are literally not given ("so we omit it" in the proof of Theorem 3.7, and similarly for several other results in Section 4). While this is standard practice in this subfield, it makes roughly half the paper a claim of duality without evidence, reducing the total content.

3. **Limited novelty of the main results.** The main comparison theorems (3.10–3.14) are the natural consequences once the presolving-subcategory framework is in place; they are useful but expected. Theorem 5.6 in particular is a package of dimension equivalences many of which are already known (as noted in Remark 5.7: Theorem 5.6(2) is [Z, Theorem 2.3], Theorem 5.6(6) is "well known" [H2, Theorem 2.20]). The genuinely new statements in Theorem 5.6 are the $cores\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(Mod\,R)$-dimension equivalence in part (3) and the strongly Gorenstein flat part in part (5); the rest is primarily repackaging.

4. **Notation is dense and somewhat idiosyncratic.** The paper uses $\mathscr{C}^\perp$ and $^\perp\mathscr{C}$ in a way that can be subtle to parse, and the distinction between "$\mathscr{C}$-proper generator" (Definition 2.5(2)) and "generator for $\mathscr{T}$" (Definition 2.5(1)) is significant but easy to lose. The "proper"/"coproper" terminology adds a layer of abstraction that serves the paper's internal logic but is a barrier to entry. The symbol $\Omega_\mathscr{C}^n$ for the $n$th relative syzygy is fine but the interaction with $\Omega_\mathscr{C}^{-n}$ (cosyzygy) can be confusing without careful attention.

5. **"Vague" application sections.** Fact 5.1 and "Application 5.2" (which says "we will not list these consequences in details") leave the reader to do the work of translating the general theorems into specific dimension equalities for the $res\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}$ and $cores\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}$ subcategories. This is a missed opportunity to demonstrate the framework's utility concretely.

6. **Reliance on a preprint key reference.** [Z] (P. Zhang, *Gorensteinness and Buchweitz theorem*, Preprint 2012) is cited without a publication venue. While common in this field, it makes the provenance of results that build on [Z] (notably Theorem 3.12, Corollary 3.13) harder to verify.

7. **The Conjecture 5.18 is a useful but modest reformulation.** It restates a consequence of the GNC in terms of the $cores\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$-dimension and $\perp RR$-dimension over an artinian algebra. It fits within the paper's framework and is a good question, but it is not a new conjecture in the sense of opening a substantially new direction — it is better understood as a reformulation of a specific low-degree case of the GNC.

## Verdict

This is a solid, correctly-written paper that provides a useful unifying framework for a particular niche in relative homological algebra. The comparison theorems are the kind of result that is useful to the subfield but not deeply novel. The heaviest cost is the heavy dependence on [Hu], which makes the paper hard to use as a standalone reference. The end of Section 5, with its connections to the Nakayama conjectures, is the most interesting part, but it amounts to useful reformulations rather than breakthroughs. The paper is a competent contribution within its subfield, not a field-shaping work.

## [1502.02354v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Stated contribution, in my own words

The paper develops a general framework for comparing *relative* homological (co)dimensions in an arbitrary abelian category ℳ. Its building blocks are:

1. A **two-tier "relative resolution" setup**: given a subcategory ℰ (e.g. projective, flat, or a general additively closed class) and a subcategory 𝒯, 𝒯 is called *ℰ‑presolving* if it admits an ℰ‑proper generator and is closed under ℰ‑proper extensions, and *ℰ‑resolving* if it additionally is closed under kernels of ℰ‑proper epimorphisms (Definition 3.1; dual version 4.1). This deliberately recovers, as special cases, Enochs–Jenda's (pre)coresolving subcategories, the Gorenstein subcategory 𝒢(ℒ) of Sather‑Wagstaff–Sharif–White, cotorsion pairs, and H2‑style (pre)resolving classes.

2. **Comparison machinery**: the technical core is a "one-step reapproximation" lemma (Proposition 3.3/4.3): an exact sequence with two consecutive 𝒯‑terms can be factored into one 𝒯‑term plus one ℰ‑term, preserving relative exactness. From this the author proves (Theorem 3.6/3.7) that 𝒯‑dimension/co‑dimension can be computed by resolutions whose last term lies in 𝒯 and the rest in ℰ; characterizes 𝒩‑cosyzygy/cosyzygy objects in terms of mixed resolutions (Proposition 3.4/4.4); and (Theorems 3.10–3.11, 4.10–4.11) gives sufficient hypotheses under which the 𝒯‑(co)dimension and ℰ‑(co)dimension of an object coincide.

3. **Applications to module categories** (Section 5): recovering and slightly extending known equalities — e.g. the chain pd_R A = SGfd_R A = Gpd_R A (for modules of finite flat/Gorenstein flat dimension, Theorem 5.6), the dual id_R A = GFid_R A =_gid_R A (Theorem 5.10), the pd = torsion‑free dimension identifications over Noetherian rings (Theorem 5.15), plus some "one‑step approximation" corollaries (Gorenstein projective/strongly Gorenstein flat complements in short exact sequences). It closes with questions (5.12, 5.13, 5.16, 5.17) and a conjecture (5.18–5.19) linking the strong generalized Nakayama conjecture (equivalently, GNC) for artin algebras to the coincidence of 𝒯‑ and †R R‑dimensions, i.e. to finite‑dimension phenomena in the cotorsion pair of torsionfree modules.

## Judgement of novelty given the cited prior work

**What is genuinely new:**

- The *terminology and two-tier definition* of (pre/prec)resolving subcategories *relative to a fixed class* ℰ (with the ℰ‑proper exactness qualifiers). The paper's own examples (3.2, 4.2) show this is the common envelope of the E‑resolving classes of Enochs–Jenda, the Gorenstein subcategory G(ℒ) of SSW (which is *not* a new concept, as the paper acknowledges via SSW Corollary 4.5/4.12), and cotorsion‑pair classes. No single cited source presents this exact relative parametrization.
- The **reapproximation lemma** (Prop 3.3/4.3) and its corollaries (Theorems 3.6–3.8, 4.6–4.8) as a *uniform* statement: previously analogous one‑step lemmas appeared case‑by‑case (e.g., AB Lemma 3.12, H2 Thm 2.5, CFE-style functional descriptions). Presenting them once for the whole family is real, if modest, new content.
- The **coincidence criteria** (Theorems 3.10–3.11, 4.10–4.11) that the dimension computed in the larger ℰ‑free class equals the relative one is a genuinely new packaging, though the proof machinery (pullbacks, dimension shifting, [Hu, Thm 3.2]) is standard.
- The **module‑category theorems** (5.6, 5.10, 5.15) are partly new combinations — e.g., Theorem 5.15(1) (id_R A < ∞ ⇒ pd_R A = Gpd_R A = T‑dim_R A over Noetherian rings) is stated as new; Remark 5.7/5.11 honestly note that 5.6(2), 5.10(3), 5.10(6) are *recoveries* of Z, H1, H2.
- The **Nakayama‑conjecture reformulations** (Questions 5.12/5.13/5.16/5.17; Conjecture 5.18–5.19; Proposition 5.19 equivalence of strong GC with a torsionfree/torsionfree‑dimension statement) are a purely new angle tying the whole framework to GNC/SNC for artin algebras — the most interesting (if unproven) part of the paper.

**What is *not* new:**

- The basic idea that homological dimensions are *relative* to a subcategory — that is exactly Enochs–Jenda (EJ2, 2000) and classical Homological Algebra.
- The Gorenstein subcategory G(ℒ) and its pre(resolving) character — SSW (cited); the paper explicitly leans on SSW Corollary 4.5 and Theorem 4.12 rather than proving them.
- Most of the *substantive module‑theoretic* content: the identities pd = Gpd = SGfd under finiteness and their duals, Gorenstein flat/injective/FP‑injective dimensions, ∞‑torsionfree characterizations — the paper itself flags these as generalizations or restatements (see Remark 5.7, 5.11, Corollary 5.14, and the "well known (H2, 2.20)" admission).
- The *methods*: pullback/pushout diagrams, dimension shifting, induction on resolution length, kernel/cokernel closure arguments — all standard tools already deployed in the cited literature. The proofs are correct but do not introduce new techniques.
- The "applications" to res(ℒ) and cores (ℒ) (Fact 5.1, Application 5.2) are immediate substitutions rather than new facts.

**Bottom line:** This is a careful, correct *unification and relative‑generalization* paper in the Gorenstein‑module / relative‑Homological‑Algebra programme. ## [1502.02354v1-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


This is a pure‑mathematics (homological algebra) paper, so there is no *experiment* to reproduce in the empirical sense. The closest analogue is: "given the definitions and lemmas, re‑derive the central comparison results for relative (pre)resolving subcategories." ## Methodology (replicating the central result)

**Setting.** Fix an abelian category $\mathscr A$; throughout, all subcategories are assumed *full and additive* (explicitly stated in §1). Everything else is categorical.

**Core definitions.**
1. For a subcategory $\mathscr C$ of $\mathscr A$ and $n\ge0$, an object $M$ is an *$n$-$\mathscr C$-syzygy* of $A$ if there is an exact sequence $0\to M\to C_{n-1}\to\cdots\to C_0\to A\to0$ with all $C_i\in\mathscr C$ (Def. 2.1). The $\mathscr C$-dimension $\mathscr C\text{-dim }A=\inf\{n\,|\,0\to C_n\to\cdots\to C_0\to A\to0$ with $C_i\in\mathscr C\}$; $\mathscr C$-codimension is the dual with $0\to A\to C^0\to\cdots\to C^n\to0$. Set to $\infty$ if no such $n$ exists.
2. Orthogonals $\mathscr C^\perp=\{A\,|\,\mathrm{Ext}^i_{\mathscr A}(C,A)=0\ \forall C\in\mathscr C, i\ge1\}$ and ${}^\perp\mathscr C$ dually (Def. 2.1).
3. $(\mathscr E,\mathscr F)$-**presolving**: a subcategory $\mathscr F$ is $\mathscr E$-presolving if (i) $\mathscr F$ admits an $\mathscr E$-proper generator and (ii) $\mathscr F$ is closed under $\mathscr E$-proper extensions (Def. 3.1); it is $\mathscr E$-resolving if in addition (iii) $\mathscr F$ is closed under kernels of $\mathscr E$-proper epimorphisms. Dual definitions for (pre)coresolving in §4.

**Engine of the proof.** The technical workhorse is Proposition 3.3: for an exact sequence $0\to M\to T_1\xrightarrow{f}T_0\to A\to0$ (3.1) with $T_0,T_1\in\mathscr F$, there exists a short exact sequence $0\to M\to T\to C\to A\to0$ (3.2) with $T\in\mathscr F$, $C\in\mathscr C$; if (3.1) is $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr A}(X,-)$-exact then so is (3.2). It is proved by a stack of two pullback diagrams plus the generator property $0\to T_0'\to C\to T_0\to0$ and [Hu, Lemma 2.4(1)]. Iterating inductively on $n$ gives Proposition 3.4 (a $T_{n-1}\to\cdots\to T_0$ sequence rewrites as an $n$-$\mathscr C$-resolution whose last syzygy is in $\mathscr F$).

**Main comparison theorems.**
- Theorem 3.6 (criterion): $\mathscr F\text{-dim }A\le n$ iff there is an exact sequence $0\to K_n\to C_{n-1}\to\cdots\to C_0\to A\to0$ with $C_i\in\mathscr C$, $K_n\in\mathscr F$. Proved by induction on $n$ using Prop. 3.3.
- Theorem 3.7 is the dual for codimension (proof stated to be omitted as "dual").
- **Central result, Theorem 3.8**: if $\mathscr F$ is closed under kernels of $\mathscr E$-proper epimorphisms and $\mathscr F\subseteq{}^\perp\mathscr C$, then for $A$ with $\mathscr F\text{-dim }A\le n$: (1) the last syzygy $K_n$ of any $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr A}(\mathscr E,-)$-exact $\mathscr C$-resolution of $A$ lies in $\mathscr F$; (2) for a $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr E,-)$-exact $0\to K\to C\to A\to0$ with $C\in\mathscr C$, $\mathscr F\text{-dim }K\le n-1$. Proof uses [Hu, Thm. 3.2] to build a direct-sum decomposition of $K_n$, the kernel-closedness to put $K_n$ back in $\mathscr F$, then a pullback diagram and Prop. 2.3.
- Theorem 3.10 (equality of dimensions): for $\mathscr F\subseteq\mathscr C^\perp\cap{}^\perp\mathscr C$ with $\mathscr C$ closed under direct summands, $\mathscr F\text{-dim }A=\mathscr C\text{-dim }A$ under three alternative hypotheses; Theorem 3.11 is the codimension analogue.

**Applications (§5).** Instantiating the general theorems with $\mathscr C=\mathscr P(\mathrm{Mod}\,R)$, $\mathscr F=\mathscr G(\mathscr P(\mathrm{Mod}\,R))$ (Gorenstein projectives) or $\mathcal{SGF}(\mathrm{Mod}\,R)$ (strongly Gorenstein flat) yields Theorem 5.6, which collapses the chain of inequalities $\mathrm{pd}_R A\ge\mathrm{SGfd}_R A\ge\mathrm{Gpd}_R A$ equalities under finiteness hypotheses (e.g. "if $\mathrm{pd}_R A<\infty$ then $\mathrm{pd}_R A=\mathrm{SGfd}_R A=\mathrm{Gpd}_R A=\cdots$"). Theorem 5.10 is the dual for injective/FP-injective dimensions. Section 5.3 relates the finite‑generated versions to the strong Nakayama conjecture (Prop. 5.19: for an artinian algebra, $\mathrm{SNC}\iff$ "every $A\in\mathcal T(\mathrm{mod}\,R)$ with $\mathrm{pd}\le1$ is projective").

## Assumptions the method papers over

1. **"Full and additive" everywhere**: the paper declares "all subcategories of $\mathscr A$ are full and additive" once in §1, but most definitions and lemmas never restate it. Closure under kernels/cokernels, direct summands, and extensions are each added as *separate* hypotheses in individual results rather than bundled, so the exact closure profile required is scattered and must be rechecked case by case.
2. **Existence of a generator as a nontrivial axiom**: "admits an $\mathscr E$-proper generator" assumes a subcategory *exists* with the property that every $T\in\mathscr F$ has a $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr A}(\mathscr E,-)$-exact $0\to T'\to C\to T\to0$ with $C\in\mathscr C$. 8. **Choice of minimal presentation**: in §5.3 the transpose $\mathrm{Tr}\,A=\mathrm{Coker}(P_0^*\to P_1^*)$ uses "a projective presentation, chosen minimal when $R$ is artinian." The minimality is what makes Prop. 5.19 work ($B^*=0\Rightarrow B=0$), but the argument's dependence on this choice is not flagged — it is an assumption that fails if a non-minimal presentation is used in a non-artinian setting.
9. **No counterexamples or limits**: the paper proves only sufficient (one-directional) equalities under hypotheses, and never states how sharp they are or gives examples where the dimension comparison strictly fails when an assumption is dropped; the open Questions 5.12/5.13/5.16/5.17 and Conjecture 5.18 are left entirely unaddressed.

But it is largely a synthesis of existing machinery — the core lemma (Prop. 3.3) is a diagram chase, the theorems are inductives over it, and the applications re-prove or re-package standard Gorenstein-dimension equalities rather than establishing new ones. ## My understanding of the paper's logical argument

The paper (Huang, *Homological Dimensions Relative to Presolving Subcategories*, 2015) attacks a stated question in the Introduction: **when two relative homological dimensions are defined relative to a category $\mathcal{C}$ and a subcategory $\mathcal{T}$ of it, what is the relation between the two dimensions?** The projective/flat/injective → Gorenstein projective/flat/injective passage is the motivating example, and the goal is to find a setting where the comparison theorems for those classical pairs are all instances of one unified statement.

The logical skeleton:

1. **Setup (Sec. 2).** Fix an abelian category $\mathcal{A}$ with full, additive subcategories. Uses Enochs–Jenda's [EJ2] relative homology as the base: $\mathcal{C}$-dim/$\mathcal{C}$-codim via left/right $\mathcal{C}$-resolutions, plus the Sather–Wagstaff–Sharif–White (SSW) *Gorenstein category* $\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{C})$ that unifies Gorenstein projective/injective/flat classes. Introduces the key relative notion: a sequence is $\mathcal{E}$-proper if it splits under $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E},-)$, $\mathcal{E}$-coproper if it splits under $\mathrm{Hom}(-,\mathcal{E})$; a subcategory is a $\mathcal{E}$-proper generator/cogenerator.

2. **Definition (Def 3.1).** $\mathcal{T}$ is *$\mathcal{E}$-presolving* if (i) $\mathcal{T}$ has an $\mathcal{E}$-proper generator $\mathcal{C}$, and (ii) $\mathcal{T}$ is closed under $\mathcal{E}$-proper extensions; *$\mathcal{E}$-resolving* adds (iii) closure under kernels of $\mathcal{E}$-proper epimorphisms. 3. **The engine (Prop 3.3 / 3.4).** Given $0\to M\to T_1\to T_0\to A\to 0$ with $T_0,T_1\in\mathcal{T}$, a pull-back diagram produces a mixed resolution $0\to M\to T\to C\to A\to 0$ with $T\in\mathcal{T}$, $C\in\mathcal{C}$ (and the sequence stays $\mathrm{Hom}(X,-)$-exact when the original does). Inducting (Prop 3.4) gives the full computation of a $\mathcal{T}$-resolution and the corosyzygy-equivalence "an object is $n$-$\mathcal{C}$-cosyzygy iff it is $n$-$\mathcal{T}$-cosyzygy."

4. **Comparison (Thms 3.6, 3.8, 3.10, 3.11–3.14).** A $\mathcal{T}$-dimension bound is characterized by a mixed $\mathcal{C}$-resolution with a $\mathcal{T}$-syzygy (Thm 3.6); the $\mathcal{T}$-cosyzygy of a $\mathcal{T}$-resolving object with bounded dimension stays in $\mathcal{T}$ (Thm 3.8); and under $\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^\bot\cap{}^\bot\mathcal{C}$ plus direct-summand closure, $\mathcal{T}$-dim = $\mathcal{C}$-dim (Thm 3.10), with the Gorenstein-category corollaries (3.12–3.14) recovering generalizations of the Holm/Zhang comparison theorems.

5. **Duality (Sec. 4).** A verbatim dual statement for precoresolving/cosing subcategories (Prop 4.3 mirroring Prop 3.3, Thms 4.6–4.14 mirroring 3.6–3.14).

6. **Applications (Sec. 5).** Special subcategories (Fact 5.1) and module categories. The payoff is **Theorem 5.6**, which strings together $\mathrm{pd}_R A$, strongly-Gorenstein-flat dim, Gorenstein-projective dim and the various relative dimensions (cores $\widetilde{\mathcal P}$, $\mathcal G(\mathcal P)$, right/left $\mathcal P$-dim, $(\mathcal F)$-dim), producing a block of equalities whenever the relevant dimension is finite. Corollary 5.8 gives $\mathrm{Gpd}_R A\ge\mathrm{Gfd}_R A$ under mild hypotheses — an answer to an open question of the Gorenstein-flat literature. The closing Questions 5.12–5.17 and Conjecture 5.18 tie the remaining open comparisons to the **strong Nakayama conjecture (SNc)** and **generalized Nakayama conjecture (GNC)**; Prop 5.19 shows Question 5.17 is essentially an equivalent reformulation of SNc for $R^{op}$.

plus a Hom_𝒜(ℰ,·)-exact 0→T→Kₙ→Tₙ→0. Then, putting Kᵢ = Im(Cᵢ→Cᵢ₋₁) for 1≤i≤n−1, the proof asserts "all Kᵢ are objects in 𝒞⊥." That claim is not supported by anything stated:

By dimension shifting on 0→Kᵢ→Cᵢ₋₁→Cᵢ₋₂→0 this amounts to vanishing of Exts of pairs of objects of **𝒞 against each other** (e.g. Ext^{≥2}(𝒞,𝒞)=0) — a self‑orthogonality/relative‑projectivity assumption on 𝒞 that Theorem 3.10 nowhere states.
