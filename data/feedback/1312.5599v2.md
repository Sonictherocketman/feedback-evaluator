## Overall assessment

The paper extends the now-well-established spin-3 chemical-potential black-hole construction to the **spin-4** charge, working at generic rank $\mathcal{N}$ in $\mathfrak{sl}(\mathcal{N})$ Chern-Simons gravity and uplifting to $\mathfrak{hs}[\lambda]$. It then carries out the same two programmatic tasks that made the spin-3 literature fertile: (i) match the perturbative partition function against a free CFT computation at the free points $\lambda=0,1$, and (ii) study the finite-rank → $W_\infty$ limit of the BTZ critical temperature where the perturbative branch ends on a vertical tangent.

The central technical claims are internally consistent and the paper is a careful, well-organized piece of computation:

- **CFT check (Sec. 4).** The $\mathcal{N}=1$ (free bosons, $\mathcal{W}_\infty[1]$) and $\mathcal{N}=0$ (free fermions, $\mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}\to\mathcal{W}_\infty[0]$) partition functions agree with the gravity-side perturbative expansion (4.1)/(4.12) through $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{18})$, once the non-primary spin-4 current is made primary by the shift $\exp[(-2/3)\alpha\,\partial_\tau^2]$ (bosons) or its $7/8$ fermionic analogue. This is a *genuine* two-sided test of $AdS_3/CFT_2$ for a spin-4 source, not merely a reproduction of a known result.

- **$\mathcal{N}=\infty$ (Sec. 5).** The authors correctly *do not* claim a closed resummation; instead the $S_\gamma$ operation with $\gamma=1$ collapses the series to the closed hypergeometric expression $F(\tau,a)$ (5.3), from which an $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{33})$ series is generated in App. D. The explicit caveat that the singularity of $F$ is *not* the radius of convergence of $Z_{\infty}$ (the $S_\gamma$ operator is non-trivial) is the kind of honest qualification that strengthens the work.

- **BTZ critical temperature (Sec. 6).** For spin-3, the BST extrapolation of $T_{BTZ}(\mathcal{N})$ gives $0.02001(1)$ against the analytic $9/(64\pi\sqrt5)=0.0200183…$, a convincing demonstration that the $\lambda=\infty$ BTZ temperature coincides with the branch point of the large-$\lambda$ partition function. For spin-4, with no closed form, the Domb–Sykes slope plus BST ($T_{DS}=0.033184(5)$, $T_{BTZ}(\infty)=0.0331(1)$) are mutually consistent.

- **New physical subtlety.** The spin-4 partition function is a function of $\alpha/τ^3=4π^2\mu T^2$, so *unlike spin-3* it is not even in $\alpha$. The two asymmetric singularities mean one must choose $\mu<0$ to select the convergence radius belonging to the BTZ branch. This is a real, non-obvious observation that changes the spin-3 intuition and is one of the paper's more valuable points.

## Critique / weaknesses

1. **Generality gap at the "main" CFT point.** The novelty is that the authors solve at *generic* $\mathcal{N}$, but they only verify at $\lambda=0,1$. They themselves note (Intro, Conclusions) that an $\lambda$-generic CFT check — the $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^8)$-level comparison done for spin-3 in refs [10–12] — remains open. So the strongest claimed result (a partial duality test) is demonstrated only at the trivial free theories, where agreement is almost tautological given the matching of $c=D$ and the current algebra. The genuinely non-free content is only asserted, never tested.

2. **No closed form for the spin-4 $W_\infty$ case.** This is the clearest limitation, and it is acknowledged. Spin-3 has an exact resummation and an analytically known $T_{BTZ}(\infty)$; spin-4 is stuck with numerics (Domb–Sykes slope + BST extrapolation, $\omega^*\simeq0.75$). The numerical error bars are quoted but never justified — the Domb–Sykes method assumes a simple nearest singularity giving the asymptotic coefficient ratio, and one would like an argument (or at least a fit to a model singularity) that the branch point is a pole-type singularity and not a square-root/finite-order branch, which would invalidate the slope interpretation.

3. **Physical meaning of the transition is still qualitative.** The vertical tangent / branch point is interpreted as the disappearance of the BTZ branch — presumably a first-order transition between the higher-spin-chemistry branch and a BTZ-like branch. 5. **Numerical detail / reproducibility.** $T_{BTZ}(\mathcal{N})$ is given to 16+ digits in the tables (3/6), yet the extrapolated limits are only $\sim0.02001(1)$ and $0.0331(1)$ (three significant figures). 6. **Notation overload.** $\mathcal{N}$ and $\lambda$ are identified ("$\lambda=N$"), and the spin-3 section repeatedly mixes $\mathcal{N}(\lambda)$ normalisation, the $W$-algebra parameter, and the finite $sl(\mathcal{N})$ rank; the first-page statement that the truncation $\mathfrak{hs}[\lambda]\to\mathfrak{sl}(\mathcal{N})$ occurs at $\lambda=\mathcal{N}$ deserves an explicit proof or reference, since it is the bridge that justifies reading finite-$\mathcal{N}$ numerics as samples of a continuous $\lambda$.

These are limitations of the program as much as of this paper.

**Paper:** "Analysis of higher spin black holes with spin-4 chemical potential," M. Beccaria & G. Macorini (arXiv:1312.5599v2, Jan 2014).

### Summary

The paper extends the Kraus–Perlmutter spin-3 higher-spin black hole — a solution sourced by a chemical potential for the spin-3 current — to the spin-4 case. The central technical achievement is a connection/holonomy solution at *generic* sl(𝒩) level, with all normalizations and the chemical potential fixed by the integrability constraint ∂_μ𝓈 ℒ = ∂_τ 𝒲𝓈. Because λ = 𝒩 in the hs[λ] → sl(𝒩) truncation, this yields a spin-4 black hole at generic λ (not just 𝒩 = 4,5 as in prior work). The higher-spin charges are expanded in powers of the chemical potential α as rational functions 𝒥𝓈,𝓃(𝒩) of known degree (eqs. 3.3–3.4), from which the partition function log 𝒵 follows. The authors cross-check the gravity result against a CFT at the free points 𝒩 = 1 (free complex bosons, 𝒲∞[1]) and 𝒩 = 0 (free complex fermions, 𝒲₁₊∞), and they take the 𝒩 → ∞ (Bergshoeff–Blencowe–Stelle) limit, where the partition function resums into a hypergeometric 4F₄ generating function (eq. 5.3). Finally, they analyze the BTZ branch, show its critical temperature T_BTZ(𝒩) converges as 𝒩 → ∞, and identify that limit with the singularity/convergence radius of the partition function, using both Domb–Sykes and Bulirsch–Stoer (BST) extrapolation.

### What is done well

- **The generic-𝒩 spin-4 solution is a genuine advance.** The spin-4 black hole had only been treated at fixed low 𝒩 in the literature (their refs. 16–19); the generic-𝒩 result with subleading connection terms fixed by integrability is clean and reusable.
- **The CFT check is the right check and is done carefully.** Matching to the free-boson and free-fermion theories is the natural test. A detail they handle correctly — the linear W⁽⁴⁾ is *not* a Virasoro primary and must be dressed with a :[W⁽²⁾₀]²: normal-ordering term (eqs. 4.8, 4.15) — shows real understanding, and they verify agreement to 𝒪(𝛼¹⁸).
- **The 𝒩 → ∞ generating function (5.3) is elegant.** Finding a γ (here γ = 1) under the 𝒮𝛾 operator that collapses the expansion into a single hypergeometric 4F₄, with log Z reconstructible from it, is a nice and compact result.
- **The numerical apparatus is solid.** Running two independent estimates (Domb–Sykes on a very long 𝒩 = ∞ series, and BST on the T_BTZ(𝒩) sequence) and getting agreement (T_DS = 0.033184(5) vs BST 0.0331(1)) is convincing. The BST appendix (E) is a good self-contained treatment.
- In the spin-3 case the identification is *exact*: T_BTZ(∞) = 9/(64π√5) coincides with the branch point (2.17) of the closed-form partition function, and the BST sequence converges to it (tables 6.3, Figs. 1–2).

### Issues and weaknesses

1. **A clear factor-of-10 typo in a headline number.** Eq. (6.4) and the surrounding text give the spin-3 BST result as "0.002001(1)" and state "9/(64π√5) = 0.00200183…". But 9/(64π√5) = **0.0200183**, and both the exact fraction in (6.2) and the numerical table (6.3) — whose entries sit at ~0.02 and decrease toward ~0.02 — confirm the limit is ≈ 0.0200183, not 0.0020. So the two decimal statements (the BST value and the decimal expansion of the closed form) each miss a decimal place by ×10. The exact expression is unambiguous and the physics is consistent, but the error sits in the "compares perfectly" comparison of a main result and should be corrected.

2. **The spin-4 result is meaningfully weaker than the abstract suggests, and the paper says so — but the framing still leans on the analogy.** For spin-3, the critical BTZ temperature is *proven* to be the branch point of the closed-form partition function. For spin-4 there is no closed-form resummation of log 𝒵, and — crucially, and correctly pointed out after eq. 5.4 — the critical point of the generating function F(τ,α) in (5.3) does **not** coincide with the convergence radius of log Z_{∞}. So the spin-4 statement that T_BTZ(∞) is "the critical point of the large-λ partition function" rests on the *numerically* found radius of convergence, and the singularity is not tied to any known pole/branch point of a closed expression. The "qualitatively similar" language is fair, but this is a real asymmetry between the two cases that the intro/abstract soft-pedal.

3. **The CFT check is only in the free corner.** All agreement is demonstrated at λ = 0,1 (free bosons/fermions). The genuinely nontrivial duality content — agreement at a non-free minimal-model value of λ — is deferred to future work ("it would be very interesting to repeat the analysis of [12]"). That is the most interesting part of the check and it is precisely what is missing. This confines the duality validation to the easiest limit.

4. **"Generic λ" is really an interpolation from integer 𝒩.** The whole construction relies on smooth λ-dependence and the λ = 𝒩 truncation at integer values. There is no independent check of the formula at non-integer λ. This is standard and well-motivated in the field, but it is an assumption, not a result, and the conclusion that the spin-4 black hole exists "in hs[λ]" at generic λ is only as strong as the assumed smoothness.

5. **Thermodynamic status.** There is a known tension that the holomorphic "entropy" S disagrees with the canonical entropy (their refs. 28–29; clarified in 30–31). The paper footnotes this once but, given that its central objects are *thermodynamic* (BTZ critical temperature, a proposed first-order transition), it would strengthen the paper to address more explicitly under which set of thermodynamic variables the transition and the critical temperature are well defined, rather than leaving the interpretation of the S_𝒩→∞ singularity at the level of "branch merging → first-order (expected) transition."

6. **Resummation is an ansatz.** As with all such hypergeometric resummations, the 4F₄ (5.3) reproduces the computed coefficients (through 𝒪(𝛼³³) in App. D) but this is not a proof of the exact all-order function; in principle higher-order terms could first appear. A sentence acknowledging that the generating function is fixed by the computed terms, not derived, would put it on firmer footing. The "Remarkably … γ = 1 does the job" (eqs. 5.2–5.3) is slightly over-toned, since the mechanism is simply the cancellation of the more-singular terms for that γ.

Minor: the figures are clear but the tables list T_BTZ to 15–20 decimals in a couple of places, which is more precision than the analysis justifies (the spin-4 value is itself only ~4-significant-figure accurate: 0.0331(1)); and the long hand-verification of the appended polynomials is not possible from the paper itself (they are evidently generated by computer algebra — fine, but worth an explicit statement of the tool).

### Overall

This is a careful, well-executed, self-contained technical contribution to a fairly specialized corner (Vasiliev higher-spin gravity / minimal-model AdS₃/CFT₂). It extends known spin-3 methods to a spin-4 black hole at generic λ, performs a correctly-executed free-theory CFT cross-check, and produces a clean hypergeometric generating function at λ = ∞ together with a convincing numerical demonstration that the BTZ critical temperature converges and equals the partition function's convergence radius. Nothing is unsound; the main concrete defect is the factor-of-10 decimal typo in the spin-3 BST comparison, and the main conceptual caveat is that the clean analytic "BTZ temperature = partition-function singularity" story is fully solid only for spin-3, while the duality check is restricted to the free points. Impact on the wider field is modest — this will serve as a useful reference within the higher-spin community rather than changing how the field approaches the problem.

# Critique: *Analysis of higher spin black holes with spin-4 chemical potential* (Beccaria & Macorini, arXiv:1312.5599v2)

## What the paper does

Working within the AdS₃/CFT₂ Vasiliev higher-spin correspondence, the authors extend the previously-known sl(N)/spin-3 black hole (a BTZ-like solution with a chemical potential `a` sourcing the spin-3 charge `Wℳ`) to a **spin-4 black hole**: a solution with chemical potential `ā` coupled to the spin-4 charge `𝒥₄`. The results:

1. A general sl(𝒩) construction of the solution (Eqs. 3.1–3.2), generalizing the earlier fixed-𝒩=4,5 work to arbitrary 𝒩, with an uplift to hs[λ] at λ=𝒩.
2. Higher-spin charges expanded in the chemical potential: `𝒥_s = Σ 𝒥_{s,n}(𝒩) aⁿ/τ^{3n+s}`, where the coefficients are explicit **rational functions of 𝒩** with a clean degree pattern (Eq. 3.4).
3. A CFT check at λ=0,1 (free complex fermions/bosons), matching the gravity-side partition function to O(aᵅ¹⁸).
4. An N→∞ (λ=∞, BBS-limit) partition function, re-summed into a closed generating-function form via an S_γ rescaling operation with γ=1 (Eqs. 5.2–5.4).
5. A study of the BTZ critical temperature (where the BTZ branch of holonomy solutions develops a vertical tangent): for spin-3 they show via 60-digit numerics + Bulirsch-Stoer extrapolation that `T_BTZ(𝒩) → 9/(64π√5)` as 𝒩→∞, matching the known branch-point of the closed-form large-𝒩 partition function. For spin-4 (no closed form) they use Domb-Sykes ratio analysis on the perturbative series and BST extrapolation to get `T_BTZ(∞) ≈ 0.033184(5)` / `0.0331(1)`, concluding it likewise equals the radius of convergence of the large-λ partition function.

## Strengths

- **Genuine technical advance.** The first general sl(𝒩) (hence hs[λ]) solution with a *spin-4* chemical potential, with the charge series as clean rational functions of 𝒩, is a useful and non-trivial computation that prior work had only done at fixed 𝒩=4,5.
- **Careful numerics.** 60-digit precision, Bulirsch-Stoer extrapolation with a free fit parameter ω*, Domb-Sykes cross-check, and an explicit table of `T_BTZ(𝒩)`. The two *independent* methods (BST on `T_BTZ(𝒩)` vs. Domb-Sykes on the N=∞ series) agreeing to 0.0331(1) vs 0.033184(5) is reassuring.
- **The λ-dependence connection is the interesting physics.** Showing that the BTZ branch's endpoint for all integer λ converges to the branch-point of the λ=∞ partition function is a nice confirmation that the singularity is real and not an expansion artifact. This generalizes and strengthens the spin-3 story (refs. [7],[14]).
- Well-organized, with transparent appendices carrying the heavy rational-function data, and honest about what could not be done analytically.

## Weaknesses

**1. Incremental within a fixed program.** The core novelty is "repeat for spin-4 what Beccaria–Macorini already did (refs. [7],[14]) for spin-3." The CFT check the abstract sells as "novel" is at λ=0,1 — the *free* theories — where agreement is expected; the genuinely hard generic-λ check (ref [12]) is deferred to future work. The conceptual content is modest.

**2. The closed-form resummation is asserted, not understood.** In Sec. 5 the authors say they "did not succeed in finding a resummation" of the raw N=∞ series, but then note "remarkably, one finds that γ=1 does the job." This is presented almost as an accident. There is no explanation for *why* γ=1, and no proof that the S_γ-transformed series F(τ,α) is uniquely the correct resummation. The statement (right after Eq. 5.4) that the critical point of F "does not coincide with the radius of convergence of log Z_{N=∞} as one could naively guess" is flagged but not analyzed beyond "check simple model functions."

**3. The spin-4 critical temperature stays numerical.** This is the main asymmetry versus the exact spin-3 result, yet the paper does not explain *why* the spin-4 case resists analytic treatment. The Domb-Sykes value 0.033184(5) rests on a finite (O(a³³)) series and extrapolation; the Domb-Sykes *systematic* error (assumption that the nearest-to-origin singularity is the one measured) is not bounded, even though Fig. 4 shows two nearly-symmetric singularities and the paper must *choose* the left one. The identification of `T_BTZ(∞)` with "the critical point of the large-λ partition function" is therefore indirect, not a theorem.

**4. The sign-of-μ subtlety is under-motivated (Sec. 6.2).** For spin-4, `α/τ³ = 4π²μT` so the sign of μ selects which side of the perturbative expansion is explored, and the authors must pick μ<0 to connect the convergence radius to the BTZ branch. The justification is "we analysed this issue and found that μ<0 is correct" — a one-line statement for what is a non-trivial physical branch choice. Why μ<0 is the physical branch rather than μ>0 deserves more.

**5. Foundational context is not engaged with (footnote 1).** The whole "higher-spin black hole as a thermodynamic state of a unitary CFT" picture was, at the time of writing, in flux (the entropy discrepancy of [28,29]; the thermodynamically consistent redefinition of [30,31]). The paper treats its objects as legitimate black holes by fiat and footnotes the tension. This constrains the physical significance of the result.

**6. The solution is an ansatz, not a derivation.** The form of `a₊` (Eq. 3.1) with its specific normalizations is *posited*, then verified by integrability. There is no uniqueness argument; the "subleading" terms of `a₋` (Eq. 3.2) are left implicit, fixed "a posteriori" by integrability. Standard in this literature, but it means one has not *shown* that this is the unique regular solution.

- The infinite sums in Eq. (3.1) are a notational convenience (only finitely many independent charges), which could be made clearer.
- The large integer coefficients in Eqs. (4.1), (4.12), (5.1) are fine but hard to audit by eye; a machine-readable cross-check of the series against the CFT side (e.g., the free-boson/fermion integrals) would strengthen the "checked to O(aᵅ¹⁸)" claim.

## Summary

A solid, careful, technically competent extension of the authors' own spin-3 black-hole work to the spin-4 sector. It delivers the general sl(𝒩)/hs[λ] solution with spin-4 chemical potential, a free-theory duality check, and a consistent numerical identification of the BTZ critical temperature with the large-λ partition-function critical point. It is sound and useful to the higher-spin community, but its novelty is limited (spin-3 → spin-4), the central analytic resummation is asserted rather than derived, the spin-4 critical value remains purely numerical, and the deeper physical reading of the critical point and of the "black hole" itself is left shallow.

**What is done well.** The construction is rigorous: the solution is not asserted but *forced* by holonomy + flatness + integrability, with the integrability conditions doing real work (fixing subleading terms and producing the 𝒥₄,𝓃 = −(n+1)/(3n+4)𝒯ₙ₊₁ relation). 3. **The physical meaning of the critical temperature remains thin.** The introduction promises to "elucidate" the physics of the λ=∞ critical point, but the paper lands on identifying it with a mathematical singularity / radius of convergence and describing the BTZ branch as ending there (a presumably first-order transition). It does not establish what phases it separates or rigorously the order of the transition. The "elucidation" is that the *locus* is well-defined and reachable from two sides — reasonable for a mathematical object, but the physical narrative is not completed.

4. **The Domb–Sykes application is a bit out of nominal domain.** Domb–Sykes assumes a single dominant singularity, but Fig. 4 shows there are two (μ>0 / μ<0). **Minor.** The CFT matching (Step 3) depends on a chain of rescalings (W⁽⁴⁾ zero-mode insertion, S_γ, the 2/3→7/8 fermion replacement); the O(α¹⁸) depth makes a spurious agreement unlikely, but the reader must trust that the two series are conventionally independent before the rescaling is applied. The paper would be strengthened by one explicit statement that the gravity and CFT series agree *before* any rescaling is imposed.

**Net.** A careful, internally consistent, moderately incremental extension of an established program. The integrability construction and the λ=0,1 CFT check are rigorous and novel (the main contribution); the λ=∞ critical-temperature identification is plausible, honestly presented, but rests on numerical extrapolation plus a smoothness assumption rather than an analytic theorem, and the physical interpretation of that temperature is not fully delivered. Solid, sound, mid-field impact.

The core results are sound and impressively high-precision; the λ = 0, 1 CFT checks through order 18 are exact and are the paper's strongest, least-correlated evidence. The critical-temperature claims rest on two caveats: (i) the spin-3 "prediction" is a check against the authors' own earlier closed form rather than an independent baseline, and (ii) the spin-4 conclusion depends on a sign-convention choice and on two partially correlated estimators with informally assigned error bars and no statistical test. ## [1312.5599v2-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Evaluation of "Analysis of higher spin black holes with spin-4 chemical potential"
**Beccaria & Macorini (2014, arXiv:1312.5599v2)**

## Summary

The paper constructs a higher spin black hole in sl(𝒩) Chern–Simons gravity with a chemical potential coupled to the spin-4 charge, computes the perturbation series for the partition function and higher-spin charges to high order, and verifies it against free CFT at 𝒩 = 0, 1. The headline result is that the critical temperature of the BTZ branch, T_BTZ(𝒩), converges as 𝒩 → ∞ to the radius of convergence of the partition function's perturbative expansion — mirroring the spin-3 result of earlier work.

## The Single Weakest Link

The conclusion — *"we proved that the critical BTZ temperature converges to a critical point of the large-λ partition function"* in the spin-4 case — rests on a **numerical coincidence between two estimates that are not independent**, and the gap between what is shown and what is claimed is the step where the argument stops being entailed by the data.

Here is the precise chain and where it fails:

1. **What is computed independently.** The BTZ branch is found by solving the holonomy constraints (eq. 2.9) as a real function of T at fixed μ, scanning N = 4, …, 13. The branch terminates at a vertical tangent, giving T_BTZ(N) (table 6.8). This is a *real-domain bifurcation* of the holonomy equations.

2. **What is computed from the same expansion.** The partition function series log Z_{𝒩=∞} (eq. 5.1, extended to 𝒪(α³³) in Appendix D) is *derived from the same perturbative expansion of the holonomy solution* that produces the BTZ branch. A Domb–Sykes analysis of this series yields T_DS = 0.033184(5) (eq. 6.7).

3. **The "agreement."** A Bulirsch–Stoer extrapolation of T_BTZ(N) gives T_BTZ(∞) = 0.0331(1) (eq. 6.9). These agree at the ∼1 % level. The paper states this "is in full agreement" and uses it to "prove" that T_BTZ(∞) equals the radius of convergence.

**Why this is not a proof:**

- **Circularity of the data source.** Both T_BTZ(∞) and T_DS are extracted from the same perturbative data generated by the holonomy equations. In the spin-3 case, the verification is genuinely independent: the closed-form expression (2.14) for log Z_{𝒩=∞} is analytically factorised, and the branch point at |α/τ²| = 9/(32√5) (eq. 2.17) is found by direct algebra on that formula, then compared to the BST extrapolation of the numerically computed BTZ branch. The spin-4 case has no such exact expression, so the Domb–Sykes number is a *different numerical method applied to the same series*, not an independent measurement.

- **The Domb–Sykes method identifies the nearest singularity; it cannot exclude a closer one.** The Domb–Sykes technique fits the ratio of successive coefficients to identify |α*/τ³|. With a finite series (18 terms, or 33 in App. D) and known systematic bias from the finite N and the two asymmetric singularities (mentioned in §6.2), a genuinely closer complex singularity that is not on the real axis would be missed. There is no argument that the real branch point is the *nearest* singularity.

- **The BST extrapolation itself is sensitive to the assumed convergence form.** T_BTZ(∞) = 0.0331(1) assumes algebraic convergence T_BTZ(N) = T_∞ + c/N^ω with ω ≈ 0.75. In Figure 6, the N = 13 point (T_BTZ ≈ 0.0392) is still ∼15 % above the extrapolated value, far from asymptotic. If sub-leading corrections (exponential, logarithmic, or oscillatory) are present, the BST value carries a systematic error much larger than the stated (1).

- **A real branch point need not be the dominant singularity.** The perturbation series for L(α/τ³) and log Z(α/τ³) terminates in radius at the *closest* complex singularity of the function. The BTZ branch terminates at the *real* branch point of the holonomy solution. These coincide only if no closer complex singularity exists. The paper does not prove this. In the spin-3 case the algebraic structure of the hypergeometric expression (2.14) makes it verifiable; in the spin-4 case it is an unproven assumption.

In short, the paper's spin-4 "proof" of T_BTZ(∞) = radius of convergence is really a consistency check: two different numerical extraction methods, applied to the same perturbation series, give the same answer to 2 %. The data does not entail the stronger claim that the nearest singularity of the partition function is the BTZ branch point — it is compatible with a closer complex singularity that the current analysis cannot resolve.

## Broader Assessment

**Strengths:** Clean construction of the general 𝒩 solution; high-order expansion checked against free CFT at 𝒩 = 0, 1 (up to 𝒪(α¹⁸)); useful BST and Domb–Sykes methodology; careful discussion of the sign of μ and the two singularities.

**Weaknesses:** (i) The overconfident "proved" language for the spin-4 result; (ii) no argument for the truncation from integer 𝒩 to the continuous hs[λ] theory beyond the standard λ = 𝒩 identification; (iii) the physical interpretation of the critical point (first-order transition? branch of saddle points?) is stated but not derived from a free-energy analysis; (iv) the CFT computation at generic λ is not performed, only at the known free values 𝒩 = 0, 1.

**Originality:** A natural extension of the Beccaria–Macorini spin-3 analysis to the next chemical potential. The techniques, setup, and structure follow prior work closely.

**Impact:** Confirms the spin-3 pattern at spin 4, which is expected. The main contribution is the data set and the numerical precision at spin 4, of interest mainly within the higher-spin / 3D-gravity community.

## Peer Review

**Title:** Analysis of higher spin black holes with spin-4 chemical potential
**Authors:** M. Beccaria, G. Macorini (arXiv:1312.5599v2)
**Target venue:** JHEP

### Summary

The authors study higher-spin black holes in $sl(\mathcal{N})\oplus sl(\mathcal{N})$ Chern–SimonS gravity, uplifting to $hs[\lambda]$, now with a chemical potential coupled to the **spin-4** charge (a natural extension of the previously studied spin-3 case). They derive the higher-spin charges and the partition function as high-order expansions in the chemical potential $\alpha$, with the exact $\lambda=\mathcal{N}$ dependence made explicit. They match results against free CFTs at $\lambda=0,1$, obtain a "generating function" $F(\tau,\alpha)$ for the $\lambda=\infty$ (Bergshoeff–Blencowe–Stelle) partition function, and analyse the critical BTZ temperature $T_{BTZ}(\lambda)$ for both the spin-3 and spin-4 black holes, arguing it converges to a finite $T_{BTZ}(\infty)$.

### Strengths

1. **Technical competence.** The holonomy-integrability conditions ($\partial_\mu \mathcal{L}=\partial_\tau \mathcal{W}_s$) are used consistently to fix the subleading terms of the connection and to determine the normalisations (e.g. the $\kappa(\mathcal{N})$ choice and the coefficient of the linear term in $a_-$). The high-order expansions and the agreement with the free-boson/fermion CFT calculation to $O(\alpha^{18})$ (Eq. 4.1 vs. 4.11, and Eq. 4.12 vs. 4.15) provide a genuine internal consistency check.

2. **The spin-4 black hole at generic $\mathcal{N}$ is new.** Prior work on a spin-4 source was confined to fixed low $\mathcal{N}=4,5$ (refs. 16–19); the generic-$\mathcal{N}$ form (Eqs. 3.1–3.2) together with the clean $\mathcal{N}\to\infty$ limits is a legitimate new construction.

3. **The $T_{BTZ}\to$ critical-point identification.** Showing numerically (spin-3) that $T_{BTZ}(\mathcal{N}\to\infty)$ equals the analytically known branch point $9/(64\pi\sqrt5)$ — with a Bulirsch–Stoer estimate $0.0200(1)$ matching $0.0200183\ldots$ — is a nice, concrete link between two previously separate findings. Extending the analysis to the spin-4 case is a reasonable step.

### Weaknesses / concerns

1. **Incremental character.** The paper is predominantly a systematic replication of the spin-3 analysis for the spin-4 channel, reusing the holonomy machinery, the $\kappa(\mathcal{N})$ normalisation, the $\mathcal{N}=\lambda$ truncation, and the BST/Domb–Sykes numerics. The conceptual novelty is modest; most of the machinery is inherited from [7,14,20].

2. **Unjustified resummation.** The central closed form $F(\tau,\alpha)$ of Eq. (5.3) is obtained not by principle but by *trial*: the authors try a deformed operator $S_\gamma$ and find that "$\gamma=1$ happens" to collapse the series. There is no argument for why $\gamma=1$, and $F$ is shown only to *generate the same series*, not to be the true analytic sum of the partition function. This is the paper's main "analytic" result at $\lambda=\infty$ and it currently stands as a plausible ansatz, not a derivation. As the authors concede, no closed form is available for the spin-4 case either, so the comparison with spin-3 is asymmetric.

3. **Numerical-only spin-4 critical temperature.** $T_{DS}=0.033184(5)$ (Eq. 6.7) and the BST value $0.0331(1)$ (Eq. 6.9) carry only modest precision and rest on the Domb–Sykes radius-of-convergence interpretation plus a branch/sign choice for $\mu$. The claim that the critical temperature equals the radius of convergence is therefore a numerical pattern, not a rigorous identification. The finite-precision table entries (10-digit values whose last digits are not guaranteed to be reliable without error bars) would benefit from quoted uncertainties.

4. **Thin physical interpretation.** The introduction promises that the physical meaning of the $\lambda=\infty$ critical point "will be elucidated in this paper," but the elucidation is essentially the observation that two quantities coincide. The expected first-order transition where the BTZ branch ceases to exist is described but not mechanistically explained (no free energy computation, no phase diagram). The canonical-entropy mismatch (footnote 1) is cited but not confronted, which is an open thread in the broader literature.

5. **Duality left untested at generic $\lambda$.** The CFT cross-checks are only performed at $\lambda=0,1$ (free theories). The authors acknowledge that a generic-$\lambda$ comparison (à la [12]) would be "very interesting" but do not do it, so the duality verification does not extend to the generic $\lambda$ region that is the actual focus.

### Recommendation

The paper is a clean, careful technical computation that achieves what it sets out to do; I found no internal inconsistencies in the derivations. However, the contribution is an incremental extension of an established program, the headline analytic result ($\gamma=1$ resummation) is not justified on principle, several key numbers are low-precision and interpretation-dependent, and the promised physical interpretation of the critical point is underdeveloped. This is well within the normal quality band for JHEP, but for a *prestigious* outlet it is solidly good rather than standout. A revised version that motivates $\gamma=1$, improves the spin-4 numerical uncertainties, and supplies a free-energy/phase-diagram discussion of the critical point would strengthen it materially.

### Scores

- **Originality (3/5):** Meaningful new computations (spin-4 black hole at generic $\mathcal{N}$; $T_{BTZ}$ convergence for both spins; the $\lambda=\infty$ series generator) but largely an extension of the established spin-3 framework and its methods.
- **Overall (3/5):** Sound, consistent, and clearly written, with a few soft spots: an unjustified resummation, numerical-only spin-4 results of modest precision, and a physical interpretation that is more suggestive than explanatory.
- **Impact (2/5):** Adds useful data and a consistency picture to a niche area of higher-spin/black-hole physics, but does not change how the field approaches the problem.

## Overall assessment

This is a clean, well-executed, self-contained paper in AdS₃ higher-spin gravity. The core idea is modest in scope but carried out rigorously: the authors take the well-trodden machinery for the spin-3 black hole and apply it to a black hole whose chemical potential sources the **spin-4** charge, promoting sl(3)/sl(4) results to sl(N) and hence to hs[λ]. The two payoffs are (i) a *new* partial Ad₃C/CFT₂ check at λ = 0, 1 (free theories) that had not been done before, and (ii) a careful numerical study of the BTZ critical temperature T_BTZ(λ) as λ → ∞ for both spin-3 and spin-4. The writing is clear, the equations are consistent, and the appendices carry the explicit rational-function data needed for a reader to reproduce the low-charge sector.

## What is done well

1. **The construction is the right one.** Solving the smoothness/holonomy conditions (2.9) at generic N, and using the integrability relations (2.11) to fix the *subleading* terms in the connection, is a non-trivial technical step and is done cleanly. The result that all *odd* spin charges vanish by parity for an even-spin-4 source is a nice consistency check.

2. **The CFT comparison is a genuine contribution.** Matching the spin-4 partition function to free bosons (N=1, eqs. 4.1–4.11) and free fermions (N=0, eqs. 4.12–4.15) up to O(α¹⁸), including the non-trivial correction from the W₀⁽²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²²² squared term to make the spin-4 current a Virasoro primary (the S_γ operator, 4.8), is a solid, verifiable partial check of the duality. This is the strongest new physics in the paper.

3. **The spin-3 T_BTZ story is satisfying.** Proving that the analytic critical point (2.17), 9/(32√5) (in |α/τ²|), equals the BTZ limit T_BTZ(∞)=9/(64π√5), and that the bulirsch–Stoer extrapolation of the numerics (0.02001) matches the analytic value to the quoted digits, is a coherent closed loop. The vanishing of the vertical tangent in the N→∞ limit (finite derivatives in 6.5/6.6) is a nice touch.

## What I would push back on

1. **The headline claim about spin-4 is weaker than the prose suggests.** In spin-3 there is a closed form (2.14), so the identification of the BTZ branch endpoint with the partition-function critical point is essentially a *proof*. In spin-4 you *do not have a closed form* for Z_{N=∞}; you have (a) a resummation F(τ,α) in a *different* variable (5.3), and (b) a long power series in the appendix. Crucially, you **yourself** state (Sec. 5) that the critical point of F does *not* coincide with the radius of convergence of Z. So the conclusion in 6.2 — that T_BTZ(∞) equals the partition-function convergence radius, "proving that, again, it agrees" — is really a *numerical* statement (Domb–Sykes, 6.7, 0.03318) cross-checked against BST (6.9, 0.0331). That is acceptable, but please soften the "proving" language: it is strong circumstantial numerical evidence, not a demonstration. The spin-3 and spin-4 results are *not* at the same rigor level, and the abstract/conclusions blur that distinction.

2. **The physical interpretation is asserted, not elucidated.** The introduction promises the "physical meaning [of the critical point] will be elucidated in this paper." What is actually delivered is a consistency statement (T_BTZ(∞) = critical point). But the conceptual question — *why* should the endpoint of the perturbative BTZ branch coincide with the nearest singularity of the CFT partition function? Is this a genuine first-order transition, an onset of the BTZ→higher-spin branch merger, or an artifact of the perturbative/saddle-point approximation that breaks down there? — is left open, only gestured at via the "second branch merges with the BTZ branch." For a student paper I would want a dedicated paragraph on *what phase* lives on the other side, and whether the non-analyticity signals instability or merely the breakdown of the branch.

3. **The spin-4 N=0,1 partition functions are effectively black boxes.** Equations (4.1), (4.12), (5.1) dump O(α¹⁸) polynomials with enormous integer numerators. The only check given is that the high-temperature limit reproduces them. I'd like at least: (a) the derivation of the Bernoulli-number resummations (4.7)/(4.14) spelled out, or a reference with the free-spin-4 version, and (b) an independent low-order check, e.g. that your CFT coefficient of α⁶ in (4.1) equals the gravity coefficient of J₄ in (3.6) at N=1. Right now a reader cannot *verify* the spin-4 CFT input at all.

4. **The spin-4 sign-of-μ subtlety (Sec. 6.2) deserves to be foregrounded, not buried.** Because in spin-4 the partition function depends on α/τ³ (odd), the sign of μ determines which side of the singularity you probe, and you had to *argue* that μ<0 selects the correct convergence radius. This is a genuinely interesting conceptual difference from spin-3 (where the function is even in α/τ²). It is important but is presented almost in passing near the end. A short explicit comparison — "in spin-3 the radius is μ-independent; in spin-4 the two singularity branches are inequivalent and we must pick μ<0" — would be a real conceptual contribution and should be more prominent, ideally stated in the abstract.

5. **Numerical hygiene / figures.** Figure 3 (Domb–Sykes, T_BTZ) shows visible scatter in the 1/N points, and the caption lists N up to 3000 but the trend is not manifestly linear; the extrapolation error 0.033184(5) is reassuring only because BST independently confirms it. Please show the actual linearity of the Domb–Sykes plot (Domb–Sykes *requires* plotting against 1/N) and a statement of how far out in N the linear regime holds. The 55-digit numbers in tables (6.3)/(6.8) are theatrical; four sig figs would do.

6. **Minor.** The abstract and Sec. 6.2 say the picture is "qualitatively similar" but never quantify the spin-4 vs spin-3 difference (spin-4 T is roughly 1.65× spin-3's: 0.0331 vs 0.0200). A one-line comment on the N-dependence (is it ∼1/N, as Fig. 2/6 suggest?) would help.

## Bottom line

This is a competent, publication-level paper: the spin-4 free-CFT duality check is a real (if modest) novelty, the numerics are done carefully with two independent methods agreeing, and the writing is good. It is, however, an *extension of existing methods to a new charge* rather than a conceptual advance, and the central spin-4 claim rests on numerics where the spin-3 one is a proof — a distinction the paper does not make sharp enough. The physical meaning of the critical point, promised in the intro, is identified but not really explained. Fix the points above (especially #1's language, #2's physics, #3's verifiability, and #4's prominence of the sign-μ issue) and this becomes a strong contribution to the higher-spin black-hole thermodynamics literature. As it stands it is solid but incremental, more confirmatory than discovery.
