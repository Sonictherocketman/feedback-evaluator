## [1712.03939v1-general-review] General Review


# Review: "Inhomogeneous modes in the PT-symmetric quantum cosmology" (Novikov, arXiv:1712.03939)

## Summary

The paper extends the "PTom" program — describing phantom energy by a complexified field $\tilde\Phi = -i\xi$ so that the kinetic energy is positive and the potential is complex — beyond homogeneous minisuperspace. Using Dirac quantization of a two-field (quintessence + PTom) model on GR, a Born–Oppenheimer/WKB split of the Wheeler–DeWitt equation, and the Fring–Moussa time-dependent pseudo-Hermitian formalism, it derives that longitudinal inhomogeneous modes are governed in WKB-time by a time-dependent effective Hamiltonian $\hat H_2^{(n)} = (\eta_n)^{-1}\hat h^{(n)}\eta_n - i(\eta_n)^{-1}\partial_\tau \eta_n$, then restricts to a model with exact homogeneous solutions and reduces the determination of the metric operator $\eta_n = e^{X_n}$ — with $X_n$ in the three-dimensional algebra $\{\tilde p^2, \tilde\Phi^2, \tilde p\tilde\Phi+\tilde\Phi\tilde p\}$ — to the ODE system (30).

## Strengths

1. **Clear, coherent programmatic logic.** The chain of reasoning (ghost problem → complexification → classical solutions on the imaginary axis with real observables → quantum treatment of real-direction perturbations) is well motivated, and each ingredient (BO quantization of cosmological perturbations [6–13], time-dependent pseudo-Hermitian dynamics [14]) is a recognized tool.
2. **The algebraic reduction (28)–(30) is the genuinely nice piece.** The observation that the fluctuation Hamiltonian is quadratic, hence that a quadratic (three-parameter) dressing operator is a sufficient and closed ansatz, with the adjoint-representation machinery (29), is elegant and turns an intractable operator problem into three ODEs. This is a useful methodological pointer for the community working on this approach.
3. The constraint analysis (11)–(15), the inhomogeneous Hamiltonian/momentum constraints, and the WKB-time construction (21)–(23) follow the standard recipe, and the separation into modes, gauge fixing, and IR-box regularization are handled in the usual way.

## Major concerns

1. **The central claim is never established.** The abstract promises a treatment of inhomogeneous modes in terms of a time-dependent pseudo-Hermitian Hamiltonian, and the motivation is *solving the phantom instability*. What the paper actually delivers is the ODE system (30) — with no solutions shown, no figure, no spectrum. Neither (a) the $\eta$-norm being positive-definite for the constructed $\eta_n$, (b) the conserved norm in WKB-time, nor (c) the positive-definiteness/stability of the effective mode Hamiltonian off the background is verified. In particular, the paper never shows that the PTom construction actually removes the gradient instabilities of the underlying non-PT two-field model — the comparison with the standard phantom/quintom instability structure, which is the entire point of the exercise, is absent. As it stands the paper is a framework, yet it is written (abstract, final paragraph) as if the mechanism were demonstrated.

2. **The $\kappa \to 0$ limit is singular for the chosen model.** With the parameter relations (25), $\dot{\mathcal D} \equiv M_{\tilde\Phi\tilde\Phi}/\mathcal D - 6\kappa^2 = 0$ and $\bar{\mathcal D} = 12\kappa^2$ identically, so in the "weak gravity limit" where (26)–(27) are quoted, both kinetic operators in (27) vanish: $\hat H_2$ is $O(\kappa^2)$, while (26) only discards $O(\kappa)$. The bookkeeping is inconsistent, and (26)–(27) cannot be reproduced from (13) plus (25) as written. Presumably an $O(\kappa^0)$ piece is missing from the definition of $\dot{\mathcal D}$ (a typo), but this needs to be corrected, because the whole analysis of the inhomogeneous modes lives inside a limit that is degenerate as stated.

3. **The weak-gravity limit silently removes the quantum-gravity content.** All conclusions are drawn after sending $\kappa \to 0$ with $V/\kappa^3 \to \infty$, i.e., a QFT on a classical background. In that limit the BO/WKB and time-dependent-PT apparatus are no longer doing any work — the same oscillator problem could be set up classically. The paper should say what remains genuinely tied to quantum gravity, or acknowledge that the result is background-field theory with a non-standard (complex) measure.

4. **Gauge dependence is never discussed.** The longitudinal scalar perturbations are gauge-dependent variables, and the partial gauge $h = E = 0$ leaves residual time-reparametrization/slide freedom ($v, s$). The effective Hamiltonian, $\eta_n$, and the norm (31)–(32) are expressed in raw fluctuation variables; no gauge-invariant observable is constructed or shown invariant. Given that the paper's selling point is a well-defined probabilistic (unitary, norm-preserving) dynamics, the absence of a statement that the results are gauge-independent is a serious conceptual gap, especially since the cited BO literature [11, 12] uses dressed/gauge-invariant operators.

5. **No derivation of the key equations.** Equation (30) is invoked from "the NLO of (24)" without any shown algebra, and there is no discussion of what order counts as NLO (in the background deviation $\delta\tilde\Phi$? — that reading makes the ordering internally consistent, but this should be stated) and of whether truncating $\eta$ to the three-generator subspace is justified to that order. Similarly, the norm preservation (31) should at least be demonstrated in the three-parameter case (a short direct check) rather than asserted.

6. **Stability of the imaginary-direction modes is not addressed.** The PTom mechanism claims that fluctuations along the *real* direction are healthy (positive oscillator). The original phantom ghost lives along the other, imaginary direction of the field space. The paper assumes the minisuperspace wave packets are "strongly concentrated near $\tilde\Phi = i\xi_{\text{class}}$" but never analyzes whether each inhomogeneous mode is stable against imaginary deviations. Without that, "solving the issues of phantom cosmology" remains an assertion.

7. **No phenomenology.** Nothing links the inhomogeneous mode dynamics to observable quantities (power spectra, signatures distinguishing PTom from quintom/phantom). Even a sketch of what the mode equations would predict — and where they would differ from standard phantom models — is missing.

## Minor issues

- The notation is confusing in places: $\dot{\mathcal D}$, $\bar{\mathcal D}$, $\tilde V$, $V$ and the tilde structure of $\delta\tilde\Phi$ versus the mode variable $\tilde\phi^{(n)}$ in (28)–(30); (31) and (32) use the same bracket for $\eta$-norm and flat product, and (32) as written lacks the $\eta^\dagger\eta$ insertion it ought to contain.
- Typos: "IR regulazation", "remaing", "costraints", "lays beyound the scope".
- Eq. (23) defines $G^{AB}$ with upper indices and calls it "the metric" — for clarity it should be called the supermetric/inverse metric, as it is used as such in (22).
- The intro's example (2) (Bender–Boyd) is fine, but the claim that such Hamiltonians "generate unitary evolution" should be phrased with the usual caveats (real spectrum only inside the unbroken PT phase; unitarity with respect to the $\eta$-norm).

## Overall judgment

This is a competent, well-motivated framework paper whose core deliverable — a verified positive, norm-preserving dynamics for the inhomogeneous modes of a PTom cosmology — is not achieved: it stops at a system of ODEs that is never solved, in a limit that is degenerate as written, for gauge-dependent variables, with the physical (stability/observational) content never addressed. The algebraic dressing-operator method is worth keeping; the claim structure needs to be toned down to match the content and the model definitions (25) checked. As a journal article I would recommend rejection of this v1 in favor of a substantially expanded version solving at least one mode explicitly, proving the norm is positive and conserved, and discussing gauge invariance.

## Review: *Inhomogeneous modes in the PT-symmetric quantum cosmology* (Novikov, 2017)

### What the paper does
The author extends his earlier homogeneous "quintom" study ([5], with Andrianov & Lan) from the minisuperspace sector to the **longitudinal inhomogeneous (tensor/vector-independent scalar) modes**. The plan is: build a two-field quintessence-phantom action, replace the wrong-sign field by an imaginary one ($\tilde\phi=-i\xi$) to get a non-Hermitian ("PTom") cosmology, Dirac-quantize the ADM constraint system, solve the Wheeler–DeWitt equation via a Born–Oppenheimer (BO) split of mini- vs. super-space, and treat the fluctuation sector as a time-dependent **pseudo-Hermitian** ($\eta$-related) effective Hamiltonian in Kiefer-style WKB-time. A specific potential is chosen to admit exact solutions.

It is a well-organized, internally consistent research note, and it is the logical next step of a coherent program.

### What is done well
- The chain of techniques is applied correctly and in line with the cited literature: ADT/ADM constraints (12)–(15), WdW → BO (18)–(19), WKB mini-superspace wavefunctional (20), WKB-time operator (22)–(23), and the Fring–Moussa / Andrianov pseudo-Hermitian effective-Hamiltonian construction (24).
- Choosing an exactly-solvable potential (25) and showing clean decoupling in the weak-gravity limit (26)–(27) is sensible for a short illustrative note.
- The observation that the non-Hermitian piece reduces, for a purely-imaginary classical trajectory, to a time-dependent Hermitian oscillator (leading to the concrete $\eta$ ansatz (28) and ODE system (30)–(31)) is correct and usefully specific.

### Main concerns

1. **The paper delivers a framework, not a result.** The "main result" is the setup plus the ODEs (30)–(31), whose solutions are only said to "can be found numerically using exact classical solutions $\xi_{\rm class}$ found in [5]." There is **no numerical solution, no explicit spectrum, no plot, and — critically — no check that the norm (31)–(32) is actually positive-definite**. The genuinely substantive content (wavepacket dynamics, the probability measure) is explicitly deferred to "future work." As a *note* this is a work-in-progress rather than a completed piece.

2. **The central physical claim is asserted, not established.** The whole motivation is that PT-symmetry cures the phantom instabilities / gives a positive effective Hamiltonian in the WKB region. But for the full longitudinal sector the treatment is mixed: $\Phi$ and $\phi$ are "ordinary Hermitian fields" while $\hat H^{(n)}_{\tilde\phi,2}$ (27) is the non-Hermitian time-dependent part "similar to the problem in [16,17]" that the authors say they sidestep with a "somewhat different approach." That $\eta$'s time-dependent contribution is precisely what is *not* guaranteed to be positive — it is the open Swanson non-observability question. So the total conserved "probability" (32) is not shown to be a valid positive measure. The paper names "PTom" and invokes positivity, but the guarantee is left as an assumption.

3. **The "different approach" is thin novelty.** The machinery (finite-algebra $\eta$ ansatz, $e^{\rm ad}$ operator-exponential identities (29), ODE for $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$) is the standard pseudo-Hermiticity / Swanson program in a new parametrization — "different" in presentation more than in method.

4. **The interesting simplification hides a weakening of the gravity story.** The clean decoupling and Hermiticity arguments kick in only in the **weak-gravity limit $\kappa\to0$** (26), which undercuts the "quantum cosmology" framing at the moment it would matter most.

5. **Standard but unexamined idealizations:** backreaction is dropped ($\hat H_0\check\Psi_2^{(n)}=0$); the partial gauge $h=E=0$ (9) is stated rather than justified against residual gauge; no connection to observed perturbation spectra (expected here, but it caps impact). Presentation is dense for five pages and would benefit from signposts on which steps are routine and which carry content.

### Bottom line
A competent and technically correct **short note** that correctly extends the author's own homogeneous result to inhomogeneous longitudinal modes using a well-understood (non-PT-symmetry) toolkit plus the Fring–Moussa pseudo-Hermitian construction. Its principal limitation is that it stops at the framework: no solution, no numerics, and — most importantly — no demonstration of its own headline claim of a positive-definite, real-spectrum evolution for the full fluctuation sector. It reads as an honest progress note toward a larger paper, and it would be substantially stronger with even one worked example (explicit $\xi_{\rm class}$, solved ODEs, and a verified positive norm).

**Recommendation:** acceptable as a short proceedings note / letter; for a full journal paper, add an explicit solution and a check of norm positivity, and state precisely under which conditions the PT-symmetry positivity claim holds.

# Critique: "Inhomogeneous modes in the PT-symmetric quantum cosmology" (Novikov, arXiv:1712.03939v1)

## What the paper does

The note extends the author's earlier PT-symmetric (P-Tom) phantom-field quantum cosmology of the homogeneous minisuperspace [ref. 5] to *longitudinal scalar perturbations*. The plan is: combine a positive-kinetic quintessence field Φ with an imaginary-kinetic "P-Tom" field Φ̺̊ = −iξ; write the ADM Hamiltonian and momentum constraints to quadratic order in the fluctuations (12–15); Dirac-quantize to get the Wheeler–DeWitt (WdW) constraint system (16–17); factorize the wavefunction with a Born–Oppenheimer–WKB ansatz for the minisuperspace (18, 20); and reinterpret the quadratic fluctuation Hamiltonian H̄₂⁽ⁿ⁾ as a **time-dependent pseudo-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian** in WKB-time (24), following Fling–Moussa [14]. In the weak-gravity κ→0 limit the two scalar sectors decouple into time-dependent oscillators (26–27), and the similarity operator ηₙ that makes the imaginary sector unitary is parameterized (28), giving a small 3-ODE system for its coefficients (30).

## Main criticisms

**1. No result is actually produced.** This is the central problem. The paper is sold as "we elucidate how this approach may work for the longitudinal inhomogeneous modes," but it stops at "the solutions can be found numerically using exact classical solutions ξ_class found in [5]" (after eq. 30). There is no numerical demonstration, no eigenvalue spectrum, no power spectrum of perturbations, no CMB prediction, and the explicitly deferred "wide wavepacket case and implications … will be considered in the future work" (last line) means the physically meaningful output is entirely absent. A short note can set up a formalism, but the current text delivers an unfilled recipe rather than physics.

**2. The claimed advantage is asserted, not shown.** The whole motivation is that the pseudo-Hermitian reformulation tames the negative-kinetic instability by yielding a "positively definite effective Hamiltonian" (eq. 21 comment, abstract). But that claim holds only "near classical trajectory" and for the specific exactly-soluble potential (25), and it is never actually verified for the concrete H̄_φ̊,2 in (27), which is just a time-dependent oscillator with frequency ∝ e^{3ρ}. The reader is not shown that the PT construction buys anything over a plain time-dependent oscillator in the κ→0 limit, so the core selling point is unsubstantiated.

**3. Observability is hand-waved.** The paper concedes (after eq. 24) that the time-dependent non-Hermitian Hamiltonian "may have complex eigenvalues but still describe some unitary evolution with respect to the norm ⟨ψ|η|ψ⟩." That this is consistent with the cited Fling–Moussa construction is asserted; no condition is checked that the effective ηₙ(ρ) of (28) has a positive-definite spectrum (so that the inner product is actually a norm) along the trajectory. Without that, unitarity is not established and the "PT-symmetric = stable" thesis remains a hope.

**4. Gauge and constraint handling are incomplete.** A "partial gauge fix" h = E = 0 is imposed (9), yet the full set of constraints — the inhomogeneous Hamiltonian H⁽ⁿ⁾ (13) and the momentum constraint H_s⁽ⁿ⁾ (15) — are written as "≈ 0" and never eliminated, and the Dirac procedure for second-class vs. first-class treatment of the non-dynamical l_ρ is described only as "as usual in GR." For a perturbation paper the gauge choice is consequential (the physical, gauge-invariant combination should reduce this to the Mukhanov–Sasaki form); the paper never reduces to, or reproduces, the known gauge-invariant result, so there is no sanity check that the PT apparatus deforms a known-correct baseline.

**5. Missing comparison with standard quantum cosmology.** There is no check in the decoupled limit (27) that the Φ-sector reproduces the standard quantum Mukhanov–Sasaki / power-spectrum result (cf. refs. [11,12]). Without that baseline, nothing in the paper can be judged as a consistent perturbation theory — it is free-standing. The finite-volume IR regularization to a volume V with Laplacian eigenfunctions (10) also appears only to make the algebra finite; the V→∞ limit for continuous k is never taken or even discussed.

**6. Presentation.** The scope statement is honest about its thinness ("this short paper"), but that framing papers over the gaps above. Technical issues: the symmetry statement (6) lists i↦−i but nowhere justifies how the measure and the WKB-time vector (22) are invariant under it; the finite-algebra A and the operator-adjoint identities (29) are invoked without proof; and the reduction from "non-observable complex Hamiltonian" to "unitary evolution" is the paper's premise yet receives only one paragraph.

## What I would have wanted
- A concrete worked computation: give ξ_class(ρ) from [5], integrate (30) numerically, and show the pseudo-Hermitian spectrum and/or the fluctuation norm (31) remain real and conserved.
- Recovery of the standard gauge-invariant perturbation equation in the limit Φ̺̊ → 0 (or imaginary-sectored-off), to prove the formalism is a deformation of a known-correct theory.
- An explicit proof that ηₙ is positive-definite along the trajectory so the inner product is a norm.
- A statement of what observable (e.g., the perturbation power spectrum) the PT approach would predict and where it differs from the ordinary (Big-Rip-destined) phantom treatment.

None of these appear, so the paper as written reads as a methodology outline that has not yet produced the physics it advertises.

## Bottom line
A plausible and internally mostly consistent formal scaffold, but an essentially result-free short note: the key claims (stabilization by a positive effective Hamiltonian, unitary evolution of a non-observable operator) are asserted rather than demonstrated, no perturbation observable is computed, and there is no check against the standard gauge-invariant limit.

## [1712.03939v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


# Evaluation: "Inhomogeneous modes in the PT-symmetric quantum cosmology" (Novikov, arXiv:1712.03939v1)

## Stated contribution, in my own words

The paper addresses a specific gap in a particular research program. Phantom (negative-kinetic-energy) scalar fields are classically pathologically unstable, and the line of work by Bender, Andrianov, and collaborators proposes an escape route: describe the phantom with a classically equivalent *complex* field, so that the theory can be made physically consistent as a PT-symmetric / pseudo-Hermitian quantum theory with a non-standard, η-dependent inner product. The preceding paper in this line (Andrianov–Lan–Novikov, ref. [5]) had worked this out only for the **spatially homogeneous minisuperspace**. The present paper's job, and the author says so plainly ("in this short paper we elucidate how this approach may work for the longitudinal inhomogeneous modes"), is to extend that framework to the **longitudinal scalar perturbation sector**.

Concretely, the author considers a two-field model — a normal quintessence field Φ plus a "PTom" phantom field Φ̌ with a Hermitian-looking but imaginary kinetic term — quantizes the constraint system in the Wheeler–DeWitt equation, applies a Born–Oppenheimer separation between the minisuperspace and the mode functions, and uses a WKB (Brout–Kiefer) time along the classical trajectory as the evolution parameter. For a specific solvable potential (borrowed from ref. [5]), the quadratic fluctuation Hamiltonian becomes a time-dependent pseudo-Hermitian operator. The technical core is the construction of the metric/norm operator ηₙ for the modes, which the author does via an ansatz (eq. 28) whose parameters αₙ, βₙ, γₙ are determined by a first-order ODE system (eq. 30) solvable numerically from the exact classical phantom solution; in the weak-gravity limit the non-Hermitian partner mode decouples and reduces to an ordinary Hermitian time-dependent oscillator, and the η-norm of the mode wavefunction is shown to be conserved in WKB-time.

## Judgment on novelty, relative to the cited prior work

**What is genuinely new here:**

1. **The perturbation-sector extension itself.** Nowhere in refs [1]–[18] had the longitudinal inhomogeneous modes of a PT-symmetric phantom cosmology been treated. Applying the Born–Oppenheimer + WKB machinery to modes of *phantom* fields (the non-Hermitian partner oscillator, not just the ordinary one) is new.
2. **The norm construction for the modes.** The ηₙ operator (eq. 28) and the derived ODE system (eq. 30) for its parameters are new — the author explicitly contrasts this with the Fring–Moussa approach (refs [16, 17]) for time-dependent Swanson-type Hamiltonians, choosing instead to anchor the solution in the exact imaginary classical trajectory Φ̌ ≈ iξ_class. This particular move is defensible and not previously published.
3. **The decoupling result in the weak-gravity limit** (eqs. 26–27): that the problematic non-Hermitian sector simplifies to a Hermitian oscillator for purely imaginary classical phantom solutions is a concrete structural observation not present in the earlier works.

**What is *not* new:**

- The **PT/pseudo-Hermitian framework** — Bender [2] is a 2007 review; nothing new here.
- The **core physical idea** — representing phantom energy via a complex scalar with a non-Hermitian Lagrangian to obtain unitary-looking evolution in a non-Hermitian form. This is precisely what Andrianov, Cannata, and Kamenshchik already did in 2006 in refs [3] and [4] ("Phantom universe from CPT symmetric QFT," "Complex Lagrangians and phantom cosmology"). **5. No comparison to the standard result.** The ordinary $\phi$ sector ($\hat H_{\phi,2}^{(n)}$) is just a free massive scalar in a time‑dependent FRW metric. You know the standard QFT‑in‑curved‑spacetime answer for its mode functions and power spectrum. A good version of this paper uses that as a **sanity check**: it should at minimum confirm that the WKB/Born–Oppenheimer machinery reduces to the known result in the $\kappa\to0$ limit. Its absence makes it harder to trust the more exotic $\tilde\phi$ sector, which has no such cross‑check. (The decoupling also drops the tensor/vector sectors by the "at the quadratic order transverse modes decouple" assertion — fine, but it means there's no cross‑check for those either; say so.)

**6. Minor but real: typos and notation.** "regulazation", "remaing", "correspondingly/correspondingly", and a flip between $\tilde\phi_{\tilde\phi}$ and $\bar\phi$ in the kinetic terms. Also $\mathcal D\tilde={\mathcal D}+\bar{\mathcal D}$ is left undefined until (25) and $\tilde{\mathcal D}$ is never named consistently. None is fatal, but at this length the paper should be clean.
