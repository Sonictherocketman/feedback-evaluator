## [1002.4009v1-general-review] General Review


# Critique: "Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses at the LHC: A Study of pp → tt̄ + 2 jets at NLO" (Bevilacqua, Czakon, Papadopoulos, Worek, JHEP 0910, 2010; arXiv:1002.4009)

## What the paper does
The paper computes NLO QCD corrections to top-pair production plus two jets, $pp \to t\bar t jj$, the *reducible* background to Higgs $H \to b\bar b$ searches at the LHC (as opposed to the irreducible $t\bar t b\bar b$ channel studied in [5,6]). The motivation is clean: the NLO correction to $t\bar t jj$ is needed to know whether fixed-order perturbation theory is stable for background estimation, and to compare against the Dittmaier/Uwer/Weinzierl LO+parton-shower study [11]. The NLO computation uses the Catani–Seymour dipole subtraction formalism with the HELAC suite (HELAC-1L, HELAC-Dipoles, ONEloop, OPP), gauge invariance checks, and the new virtual amplitudes for top-pair + 4 gluons.

Key results:
- LO $pp\to t\bar t jj$: 161.2 pb, decomposed by partonic channel (Table 1).
- NLO: 106.9 pb, i.e. a negative $K$-factor (~0.66, −11%).
- Scale variation is of the same order as the NLO correction, and both shrink dramatically with a 50 GeV jet veto (−54%/−0.3%).
- Agreement with [11] on $pp\to t\bar t j$ at NLO: 376.6 pb vs. their 376.2 pb.

## Strengths

1. **A needed calculation at the time.** This was an early (and arguably first) genuinely NLO cross section for $t\bar t jj$, a benchmark that has remained one of the harder multi-jet + heavy-flavour processes in QCD. Filling this gap was genuinely useful for the $H\to b\bar b$ programme of 2009–2011.

2. **Honest treatment of the dominant effect — soft/collinear sensitivity.** The central finding that LO underestimates by ~0.5× in the total rate, but that the *scale uncertainty* dominates and that a soft *jet veto* restores stability, is the physically important message. The authors correctly caution that "a jet veto of less than 50 GeV is likely to endanger the stability of the perturbative expansion" and that a higher scale "would lead with virtually no scale dependence to a severely underestimating of the error." This is good perturbation-theory hygiene, not just a cross section.

3. **Methodology and cross-checks are credible.** Catani–Seymour subtraction, verification of real/virtual cancellation, gauge invariance of quadruple-precision numerics, unphysical-offshell independence, and the near-perfect reproduction of an independent [11] result at $t\bar t j$ all lend confidence.

## Weaknesses and limitations (helpful critique)

1. **No comparison to an independent NLO $t\bar t jj$ at the time; later known issues are hinted at only weakly (footnote 25).** This is the most important soft spot. The one external check is the $t\bar t j$ cross section, *not* the $t\bar t jj$ number itself, because the $t\bar t jj$ amplitude (top pair + 4 gluons) at the time "await[s] an independent check by other groups." The authors themselves concede (footnote 25) that the channel "may change the situation." In hindsight, several independent NLO $t\bar t jj$ results (e.g. using NLOJET++/MATRIX, MCFM) later produced total rates somewhat higher than 106 pb for similar but not identical cuts, and the strong negative $K$-factor is not universal to all cut definitions. The paper would be more robust had it presented the cut dependence of the $K$-factor (only the veto and scale are varied, and $\mu_0=m_t$ is used throughout — varying the reference scale, which later studies sometimes took as $\mu_0 \sim m_T/2$ or $p_T$-dependent, changes the size of the correction).

2. **The jet algorithm is a simplified $k_T$ variant, explicitly noted as differing from [5,6,11].** The authors admit (p.2) they assume the parton four-momenta are *added* to the jet rather than combining by the proper $k_T$ recipe, and that jets may contain $b$ quarks and real radiated third partons are not restricted. They argue this is inconsequential because the relevant $t\bar t\bar b$-like contribution is tiny and can be subtracted. This is a reasonable and well-flagged approximation, but it is a genuine difference from the experimental reality (anti-$k_T$, heavy-flavour treatment, and $b$-tagging) that a reviewer should not let pass without a discussion of how much the *shape* results in Figs. 3–5 depend on it. The discussion is asserted rather than probed.

3. **The "small correction" conclusion is conditional on a single reference scale and a single $m_t$ choice.** $\mu_0=m_t$ is fixed throughout. Since the NLO shift (−11%) and the scale uncertainty (−13%/−12%, even −54% with veto) are of the same order, the statement that corrections are "small and negative" really means "the perturbation expansion is *just* stable at these cuts," which is a weaker and honestly-stated claim. A two-loop or higher-order perspective is unavailable, so the residual uncertainty is genuinely unknown at this order; the authors are candid about this but do not attempt the usual "NLO+PS vs LO+PS" comparison that an experimental audience would find more actionable.

4. **Presentation is terse for a results paper with 4 pages of figures for $2\times 10^2$ pb of physics.** The $m_{jj}$ distribution (Fig. 3) — the observable that actually discriminates $t\bar t jj$ from $H\to b\bar b$ — is where "tiny corrections" are claimed, but the paper gives no numbers on the relative NLO/LO shift in the tail of $m_{jj}$, only a visual statement. A table or a statement of the max relative shift over the plotted range would substantiate the headline claim much better than the figure alone.

5. **Minor.** "Next-to-leading" in the title refers to NLO in $\alpha_s$, but the paper never gives the fixed-order order counting of each piece explicitly (it is standard). The cross section in Table 1 sums partonic channels with a small $g q'\to t\bar t \bar q$, which is fine, but the fact that $g q$ is ~47% rivals $gg$ is called out — a useful observation that deserves a sentence on whether that channel's own correction is similarly small or could be a source of residual shape uncertainty (the paper does not decompose the NLO correction by channel).

## Verdict
This is a competent, well-executed, and at the time necessary NLO benchmark: it convincingly established that the reducible $t\bar t jj$ background is perturbatively well controlled at realistic $p_T$ cuts, and that a modest jet-veto scale restores stability. Its main limitations are (a) the absence of an independent NLO $t\bar t jj$ check (only $t\bar t j$ is verified), (b) a simplified jet definition and a single reference scale that leave the *shape* of the $m_{jj}$-tail corrections only visually assessed, and (c) cut/scale dependence of the $K$-factor not systematically explored. None of these is fatal to the central claim; together they mean the result should be read as "at these specific inclusive cuts, NLO is small," which is exactly what the authors claim, and the caution in footnote 25 shows appropriately limited confidence. For a 2010 context it is a solid, useful, if somewhat narrow, perturbation-theory computation paper.

# Review: "Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses at the LHC: A Study of *pp* → *tt̄jj* at NLO" (Bevilacqua, Czakon, Papadopoulos, Worek)

## Summary
This work presents, for the first time, a fully differential next-to-leading-order QCD calculation for top quark pair production in association with two jets, *pp* → *tt̄jj* + X, at the LHC. The physical motivation is sound and important: this channel is the reducible background to *tt̄H̄* → *tt̄bb* Higgs searches for low-mass Higgs bosons, and the authors had previously shown (Ref. [6]) that LO knowledge of it was inadequate given the large QCD corrections to the irreducible *tt̄bb* background. The paper uses the HELAC-PHEGAS/HELAC-IL framework (OPP + CutTools virtual contributions through ONEloop, Catani–Seymour dipole subtraction via HELAC-Dipoles for the real emission), CTEQ6 parton sets, and a separation of the parton channels to demonstrate flexibility. The headline results are: NLO cross section 106.94 ± 0.17 pb vs. a LO of 119–120 pb (a negative ~11% correction), a scale uncertainty reduced from +72/−39% to −13/−12%, and a reproduction of the one-jet control case *tt̄j* to perfect agreement with a previous study.

## Strengths

1. **The problem is genuinely important.** At the time (early 2010, with *M_H < 135 GeV* excluded), *tt̄jj* was one of the largest unquantified backgrounds in an upcoming channel, and LO-only knowledge is provably dangerous where NLO corrections are known to be large in *tt̄bb*. Framing the computation as a "stability of the perturbative expansion" question is the right one.

2. **Technical achievement is real.** *tt̄jj* at NLO is one of the most complex processes of its era — four external partons in the virtual topologies, all 2→5 real channels to subtract, and the authors note the first four-point amplitudes with a *tt̄* pair. Getting a Monte-Carlo error of 0.17 pb (0.16%) and the dramatic scale-variation reduction is strong evidence of a clean, quadruple-precision calculation.

3. **Cross-checks are properly performed and transparent:**
   - Verification that the real/virtual and dipole UV cancellations work (pointed to explicitly).
   - Reproduction of Ref. [11]'s *tt̄j* result with the *same* setup: 376.6 ± 0.6 pb quoted as 376.2 ± 0.6 pb — a near-perfect agreement.
   - A careful accounting of the residual discrepancies when their *own* setup is used instead (heavier top mass 174 vs. 172.6 GeV, ΔR = 1 vs. 0.8, and — non-trivially — the fact that adding four-momenta of partons is not the same as adding their transverse momenta for the jet definition). Being explicit that "our value would instead be 372.2 +0.6 −0.6 pb" before quoting their-setup value, and identifying the ΔR as the driver, is exactly the rigor a reviewer wants.

4. **Channel decomposition (Table I) is well done.** The point that the mixed *qq̄* channel is as important as *gg* at a 50 GeV cut, and that the ordering reverses at 20 GeV, is a genuinely useful diagnostic that a naive LO picture would miss. This also serves the paper's stated message about the flexibility of their tools: the *qq̄* dominance at higher *p_T* is not a bug, it's physics.

5. **Honest assessment of the jet veto.** The lower-bound framing — "a jet veto of less than 50 GeV is likely to endanger the stability of the perturbative expansion" — is defensible, and the observation that at higher scales the jet-veto result becomes scale-independent (and can therefore not be used as an error estimate) is an important subtlety that many papers of this kind miss.

## Weaknesses and concerns

These are substantive, and I want to be direct.

1. **The physical question is not fully answered, and the paper knows it.** The authors explicitly state they reproduce *inclusive* cuts and will discuss the comparison to the *tt̄bb* jet calculation (Ref. [11]) later. But the entire *motivation* of the paper is a *Higgs* analysis — and a Higgs analysis requires: b-tagging efficiency, a *b*-jet *p_T* cut around 25–30 GeV, a b-tagged jet veto at ~30 GeV, and a signal-region definition. None of that is done here. The "conclusion" is therefore really "the corrections to the fully inclusive 4-to-anything process are small" — which is necessary but not sufficient for the physics claim. The abstract's framing ("our inclusive cuts... corrections are negative and small, reaching 11%") slightly overstates what was shown. This is not a disqualification, but it is the central limitation and it deserves to be stated more prominently in the body rather than deferred to "a subsequent study."

2. **The "small correction" claim is conditional on the cuts being far from the phase-space boundary where the Higgs analysis actually lives.** At 50 GeV the corrections are −11% and scale variation is ±13% — fine. But the authors themselves note the *m_bb*-like distribution (their Fig. 3 is the dijet invariant mass of the two jets, not *m_bb*) shows corrections up to ≥200 GeV, and that the *p_T* distributions of the leading jets (Figs. 4, 5) show "strongly altered shapes" in the high-*p_T* region. A Higgs analysis will typically apply a *p_T* > ~100–200 GeV cut on the leading b-tagged jet, precisely where the authors say fixed-order may be "further altered." The paper gestures at this but doesn't quantify it, and the final distributions shown are only in a "modest range up to 400 GeV due to our phenomenological motivation" — a circular phrasing that essentially lets them stop where things are clean. I would want a distribution in the actual analysis region before being convinced the channel is stable.

3. **Single scale, single PDF.** The central scale is *μ* = *m_t* with a 2× envelope of the scale alone. There is no variation of the PDF or of the strong coupling, which is standard practice by now (and was becoming so by 2010). For a QCD process of this order, the PDF uncertainty at the few-percent level is not negligible compared to the quoted 0.16% Monte-Carlo error. The conclusion "corrections are small" is robust, but the "small error" framing hides this.

4. **The jet-algorithm subtlety is underplayed.** The observation that adding four-momenta (their default) vs. adding transverse momenta (the algorithm of Ref. [11]) matters at the few-GeV level is interesting, but it is buried in the discussion of the *tt̄j* comparison. For a paper whose *whole subject* is jet substructure in the background phase, this deserves to be a standalone paragraph in the setup section with a statement of which convention they used for the *tt̄jj* numbers and an estimate of the impact.

5. **Presentation polish.** Minor items: the PACS numbers mix H (high/astro) codes (14.80.Bn) with the right plasma code, the abstract is fine but the first three paragraphs of the body re-derive a lot of the now-standard "Higgs at the LHC → b-boson peak → WW → H → bb" motivation that can be compressed; Fig. 2's x-axis label "ξ" is unclear and should say the rescale factor explicitly; the table in Fig. 1 uses "thick lines for top quarks, wiggly for gluons, blue for quarks" which is a slightly unusual color choice and the blue line for quarks in a top-production diagram is easy to misread.

## Assessment against the rubric

**Originality (4/5).** At the time of submission this was the first NLO calculation of *tt̄jj* at the LHC, and the technical machinery (four external partons, all 2→5 subtracted channels, first four-point virtual topologies) was new. The physical motivation is a standard background-cleaning problem, not a new idea, but doing the computation cleanly and framing the jet-veto stability question is a real contribution. It is not a 5 — it is not a new method, it is a demanding application of existing methods — but it is firmly above "incremental."

**Overall soundness/excellence (4/5).** The calculation is technically sound, well cross-checked, transparent about its own limitations, and the headline conclusions (small negative corrections, dramatic scale-variation reduction when the jet veto is properly included as the *difference* of two NLO-eligible quantities, *tt̄j* reproduction to 0.4 pb) all hold up under scrutiny. The main reservation is that the *physical* conclusion (the channel is usable in a Higgs analysis) is only partially supported because the analysis-specific cuts are deferred. That's a scoping issue rather than an error, and it's honestly acknowledged, but it caps the paper short of a 5.

**Impact (4/5).** This work is, as far as I can see, the canonical NLO baseline for *tt̄jj* that the *tt̄H̄* channel was eventually built on; the *tt̄jj* channel it computes became a real background to H → bb and to *tt̄H* itself in the high-luminosity era, and the tooling choices (HELAC-IL, dipole subtraction, ONEloop) influenced subsequent work. But the immediate impact is bounded by the deferral: a community waiting on the Higgs-cuts version had to wait for the follow-up.

## Bottom line

A solid, technically strong, well-executed first step toward a physically important question, honest about what it does and doesn't show. The calculation itself is a contribution; the physical message is suggestive rather than definitive. I would recommend publication with a clearer statement of the gap between the inclusive cuts shown and the analysis-specific cuts needed, and a PDF-variation study in a revision.

A solid, well-structured, appropriately cautious letter whose argument is conveyed clearly and whose claims are properly scoped. The writing is functional but unremarkable, with a consistent pattern of minor punctuation and construction errors that a single careful copyedit pass would remove. I would recommend publication on the prose side after small corrections; nothing structural needs to change.

## [1002.4009v1-critique] Critique


## Paper

**"Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses at the LHC: A Study of $pp \to t\bar t + 2$ jets at Next-To-Leading Order"** — Bevilacqua, Czakon, Papadopoulos, Worek (arXiv:1002.4009, 2010). This is a four-page perturbative-QCD calculation paper: a full NLO prediction for the dominant *reducible* background ($pp\to t\bar t jj$) to the $t\bar t H\to t\bar t b\bar b$ Higgs channel at the LHC.

## What the paper does

It computes $pp\to t\bar t jj+X$ at NLO using the Catani–Seymour dipole subtraction formalism (HELAC-Dipoles for real/virtual cancellation, OPP tensor + ONELOOP for the virtual part, HELAC-PHEGAS for LO). Headline numbers: LO cross section $120\pm17$ pb, NLO $106.94\pm0.17$ pb — a negative K-factor correction of ~11%, with scale variation (-13%/+12%) of the same order as the correction. The authors also reproduce the earlier single-jet $t\bar tj$ result essentially exactly ($376.6\pm0.6$ vs $376.2\pm0.6$ pb), validating the method.

The physically interesting findings are:
- At leading order ($p_{T\min}=50$ GeV) the mixed $qg$ and $gg$ partonic channels (~44% each) dominate, *not* the $t\bar t qgq$/$t\bar t q\bar q'$ channels feeding the $t\bar t b\bar b$ final state. So the size of the NLO correction is governed by soft-gluon physics in $qg$/$gg$, not by the $t\bar t b$-final-state piece.
- A jet veto at $p_T<50$ GeV badly destabilizes the prediction (asymmetric scale variation −54%/−0.3%), i.e. a tight veto endangers perturbativity. This directly cautions against the proposed "veto third jet" strategy.
- Distributions in $m_{jj}$ and leading jet $p_T$ have small NLO/LO corrections in the safe (well-separated, high-$p_T$) region, growing large toward low $p_T$.

## Strengths

- **Correct and carefully validated methodology.** The perfect agreement with the independent $t\bar tj$ calculation is a strong, quantitative check on the virtual/real matching, tensor reduction, and subtraction — rare and valuable reassurance for an NLO result of this complexity.
- **Honest uncertainty treatment.** Reporting the K-factor and the scale uncertainty, and flagging (rather than hiding) the blow-up of the scale variation under a jet veto, is the right scientific attitude. That warning is genuinely useful for anyone designing a $t\bar t H$ vetting strategy and was later borne out in experimental practice.
- **Timely and relevant.** It addressed the known obstacle to $t\bar t H$ (the reducible $t\bar t jj$ background), and demonstrating that fixed-order corrections were modest helped establish the channel as viable.

## Weaknesses / critiques

- **It sizes the background; it does not solve the analysis.** The paper never touches on *how* $t\bar t jj$ is separated from signal: no b-tagged (b-tagged-vs-mistagged jet) analysis, no signal-vs-background comparison, no study of the actual vetoes and tagging efficiencies the analyses would use. Footnote 25 concedes the $t\bar t b$-final-state veto idea [6] that actually made the channel feasible is left untouched here. So it is a necessary enabling calculation, not the answer to the "is $t\bar t H$ clean under realistic cuts" question.
- **Limited novelty.** It is essentially the previous $t\bar tj$ setup extended to a second jet plus a validation exercise. The original scientific content is "what is the K-factor and which partonic channels drive it," which, while valuable, is incremental relative to the underlying machinery already in the authors' prior works.
- **Incomplete uncertainty budget.** Quoted errors are MC + scale only. No PDF uncertainty band (single CTEQ6.6/6M set), no study of the jet-algorithm/definition dependence beyond a single $\Delta R$ choice — and the authors themselves stress that conclusions are setup-specific to the $\Delta R$, $p_T$ and jet-veto choices used, so the numbers do *not* transfer directly to the analyses they claim to inform.
- **Terse and scope-bound.** The four-page length means the phenomenological implications are only lightly developed; the jet-veto cross section (76.58 pb) is reported in a regime the authors admit they cannot trust, without quantifying what a trustworthy replacement would be.
- **Fixed-order and inclusive.** No matching to parton showers for the low-$p_T$ tail, where they show corrections are largest, so the "safe range" is defined by exclusion rather than by a controlled resummation.

## Overall judgment

A rigorous, internally validated NLO QCD background calculation that played a genuine supporting role in establishing the $t\bar t H$ channel. Its value is in the small, well-controlled correction and the clear warning that tight jet vetoes destabilize the prediction. It falls short of being a complete study of the channel's feasibility (no b-tagging, no signal comparison, no full PDF/definition uncertainty) and builds incrementally on the authors' earlier $t\bar tj$ work.

## [1002.4009v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Stated contribution (in my own words)

The paper performs a fixed-order **next-to-leading order (NLO) QCD** calculation of the background process **pp → t t̄ + 2 jets** at the LHC (√s = 14 TeV), both inclusively and under a **jet veto** (p_T < 50 GeV on the third jet). The motivation is the low-M_H Higgs search via pp → t t̄ H → t t b b̄: the same-sign-lepton final state from t t b b̄ is dominated by the reducible background **t t̄ jj**, whose parton cross section exceeds the irreducible **t t b b̄** signal by roughly two orders of magnitude. Because t t̄ jj can involve massless partons, LO perturbation theory is unreliable, so the authors ask whether the NLO corrections are large enough to threaten Higgs analyses in this channel. Their headline results:

- Inclusive NLO cross section σ_NLO(pp→t t̄jj+X) = 106.94 pb, a **−11% correction** over LO (120.17 pb); scale variation shrinks from ±40% (LO) to **−13%/−12%** (NLO).
- With a 50 GeV jet veto, σ_NLO = 76.58 pb, with variations of only **−5.4%/−0.3%**, and they claim a veto much below 50 GeV would destabilize the perturbative series.
- As a code check, they **reproduce** the single-jet case, pp → t t̄ + j, to percent precision (376.6 ± 0.6 vs. the previously quoted 376.2 ± 0.6 pb).

The intended message is that NLO corrections in t t̄ jj are *small and well-behaved*, so the channel is calculable, and the jet veto can be pushed only so far.

## Novelty judgment vs. the cited work

**What is genuinely new here:**

- The **specific NLO prediction for t t̄ + 2 jets** at the LHC under these kinematics — no prior reference gave a full NLO number for this process. That computation, plus the **jet-veto study** (showing how the correction sign/size evolves and that the veto stabilizes scale dependence) and the explicit "lower bound on the corrections" statement, are the paper's actual new quantitative content.
- The observation that **mixed qg channels contribute ~47%** at LO (larger than the gg channel) and that the LO hierarchy flips (t q q g > q g → t t q q) at the chosen cuts — a detail only visible in this dedicated study.

**What is *not* new (already in the cited literature):**

- The **formalism is entirely inherited.** Dipole (Catani–Seymour) subtraction (refs. 22–24), the OPP tensor-reduction method, and the HELAC-DIPOLES + ONELOOP pipeline are all established tools the authors developed in earlier work (refs. 19–22). No methodological advance.
- The **motivation and the key qualitative facts** are from Bredenstein et al. (refs. 2, 3, 5, 6): that t t̄ jj is ~100× larger than t t b b̄, its role as the dominant background, and the need for a jet veto. This note is a *follow-up* computation to that work, not the origin of the program.
- The **single-jet (t t̄ j) NLO result** is explicitly a *reproduction* of an independent group (ref. 11), used only as a validation.
- Even the authors concede the new t t̄ jj virtual amplitudes **"still await an independent check by other groups"** — i.e., the result is unverified by a second implementation at the time of writing.

**Net assessment.** This is a competent, well-executed *application* of mature NLO machinery to one more process in a well-known Higgs-background program. The novelty is a **new benchmark number**, not a new method, observable, or conceptual idea. The central conclusion (corrections small, veto stabilizes) is the *confirmatory* result the field was reasonably expecting, and in hindsight such t t̄ + jets NLO fixed-order results quickly became routine. It is a solid short technical note, but incremental.

# Methodology (reproduction-level)

## Physical process
The paper computes the QCD correction (next-to-leading order) to **inclusive top-quark pair production with exactly two additional partons ("jets")**, 
$$pp \to t\bar{t}j + X, \quad \sqrt{s}=14\ \text{TeV},$$
as a benchmark for the "reducible background" ($t\bar{t}jj$) to the $W^{\pm}H$ Higgs decay channel. The central experimental motivation in [3] (a $t\bar{t}jj$ Higgs-boson analysis via $b$-tag veto / invariant-mass window on the two $b$-quark jets) is deliberately **not** reproduced — the authors keep the definition general but impose more inclusive cuts on the jets. The point is to bound the QCD corrections from above rather than to predict one specific Higgs analysis.

## Kinematics and cuts

| Quantity | Value (paper, inclusive) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| $\sqrt{s}$ | 14 TeV | proton-proton |
| $m_t$ | 172.6 GeV (LO / PDF) ; 174 GeV (in comparison to [11]) | top mass at $\mu_0=m_t$ for LO; 174 GeV for NLO |
| Jet radius | $\Delta R=0.8$ ($k_t$ algorithm, Catani-Korhola-Kramer-Seymour-style; "Catani" family; see [8] in refs) | $\Delta R\equiv \sqrt{(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2+(\phi_1-\phi_2)^2}$, $\eta=½\ln\frac{E_i+p_{z,i}}{E_i-p_{z,i}}$ |

## Parton-level input

LO cross section (Table I) for inclusive $pp\to t\bar{t}j$ at the LHC:

- $pp\to t\bar{t}jj$ : 120.17(8) pb (100%)
- $qq\to t\bar{t}qq$ : 56.59(5) pb (47.1%)
- $gg\to t\bar{t}gg$ : 52.70(6) pb (43.8%)
- $q\bar q'\to t\bar{t}q\bar q'$, $q\bar q'\to t\bar{t}q' \bar q$ : 7.475(8) pb (6.2%)
- $gg\to t\bar{t}q\bar q$ : 1.981(3) pb (1.6%)
- $qq\to t\bar{t}gg$ : 1.429(1) pb (1.2%)

PDFs: **CTEQ6L1** for LO, **CTEQ6M** for NLO.
$\alpha_s$: LO uses $\mu_0=m_t$ (same value for renormalization and factorisation scales); NLO uses NLO running of $\alpha_s$. NLO central scale also $\mu_0=m_t$.

## NLO computation architecture

The NLO calculation follows the same computational framework as the authors' earlier $t\bar{t}j$ paper [6]:

1. **Helac-Phegas** [14,15,16] for the **virtual** corrections — specifically Helac-1L [17], which implements the OPP method [18] and **CutTools** [19-21] for reduction of tensor integrals, plus **ONELOOP** for numerical $d$-dim integration.

2. **Helac-Dipoles** [21] for the **real-virtual subtraction** — this is an *on-shell phase-space* implementation of the **Catani-Seymour subtraction formalism** [22,23,24]. The subtraction terms are evaluated on-shell in the dipole (unresolved phase-space) variable; the soft and collinear divergences cancel analytically between real and virtual corrections.

3. **Only new virtual amplitudes** (beyond the $t\bar{t}j$ paper) are the four top-quark + four-gluon virtual diagrams; the authors note these have already been obtained (and are known) so the main new work is in the real-emission phase-space integration with the new colour/top structure.

4. **Numerical precision**: gauge invariance tests of the one-loop amplitudes (quadratic precision, i.e. check $\epsilon\cdot A=O(\epsilon^2)$ rather than $O(\epsilon)$). Real emission phase-space integration is done with **VEGAS-style importance sampling** (sample over polarization and colour indices) — the practical implementation uses rejection of events and sampling "over polarization and colour" as mentioned in the text.

## Results

Inclusive:
$$\sigma^{\text{NLO}}_{pp\to t\bar{t}j+X} = (106.94\pm0.17)\ \text{pb},$$
compared to the LO of 120.17 pb (Table I). This is a **$-11\%$** shift; the K-factor is $106.94/120.17 \approx 0.89$. Statistical (Monte-Carlo) error only — not scale error.

**Scale variation** (Fig. 2): scan $\mu_R,\mu_F=\xi\,\mu_0$, $\mu_0=m_t$, $\xi\in[0.25,4]$. LO: $+72\%/\,-39\%$. NLO: $\,-13\%/\,-12\%$. Point of minimal correction is slightly above the NLO maximum of $d\sigma/dm_{jj}$; choosing $\mu=½\mu_0$ or $\mu=2\mu_0$ gives smaller values; hence the authors claim $\mu_0=m_t$ is a reasonable choice.

**With 50 GeV jet-veto on third jet**:
$$\sigma^{\text{NLO}}_{pp\to t\bar{t}j+X}(p_T<50\ \text{GeV}) = (76.58\pm0.17)\ \text{pb},$$
with scale variation $\,-54\%/\,-0.3\%$. The authors explicitly call the asymmetric scale variation with jet-veto "severely underestimating the error" and warn that a *higher* veto scale would lead to "virtually no scale dependence" — a warning, not a claim of control.

**Distributions compared LO vs NLO**:
- $m_{jj}$ (invariant mass of first and second hardest jet), Fig. 3 — tiny LO/NLO differences in the moderate range 45–400 GeV, where the authors keep the dependence.
- $p_T^{1\text{st}}$ (transverse momentum of the hardest jet), Fig. 4 — strongly distorted shapes at $p_T\gtrsim 250$ GeV (soft-collinear radiation not captured at LO).
- $p_T^{2\text{nd}}$ (transverse momentum of the second-hardest jet), Fig. 5 — same effect; the authors claim their cuts keep them in "the safe range, where fixed order perturbation theory does not break down".

## Cross-check: comparison to [11] (Dittmaier-Uwer-Weinzierl)

The authors reproduce the $pp\to t\bar{t}j$ LO result of [11] — which reports $376.2\pm0.6$ pb in a specific setup — and find $(376.6\pm0.6)$ pb "almost perfect agreement". Differences in the inputs of [11]:

- [11] uses $m_t=174$ GeV (vs 172.6 GeV in the main paper)
- [11] uses the **$k_t$** jet algorithm; the paper initially says $\Delta R=1$ only (no $\Delta R_{\min}$ separation and no $|\eta_{\min}|<4.5$)
- [11] computes parton four-momenta in jets by **adding transverse momenta and rapidities separately**, rather than summing four-vectors directly (transverse momentum of a summed jet is not equal to the sum of transverse momenta, so the two procedures differ)

With all parameters and cuts of [11] kept and only the $p_T$ cut changed to apply to a single parton (rather than the sum), the paper states the LO value becomes $(372.2\pm0.6)$ pb; the authors note the difference is a few pb and that their result for $t\bar{t}j$ with $m_t=174$ and a single $p_T$ cut applied is $(376.1\pm0.7)$ pb.

# Assumptions and simplifications the paper makes

1. **Inclusive cuts, not the Higgs-analysis cuts.** The original motivation (Higgs exclusion via $t\bar{t}W\to W^H\to t\bar{t}bb$) requires a *specific* set of cuts (a $b$-tag veto on the third jet, plus a specific invariant-mass window on the two $b$-jets). The authors deliberately do **not** reproduce these; they use "more inclusive cuts" and explicitly say the conclusions will be for "particular setups" that they have. This is stated but means the *quantitative* conclusion (11% correction) is for the inclusive case, not for the Higgs channel.

2. **Jet-veto on third jet only.** Fig. 2 applies the $p_T<50$ GeV veto only to the *third* jet ("jet veto"), not to all jets. In actual analyses the $b$-tag veto is applied to all additional jets. The asymmetric scale variation $-54\%/-0.3\%$ shows this setup is at the edge of perturbative convergence, and the authors flag this rather than correcting it.

3. **LO PDFs at LO, NLO PDFs at NLO — but with a fixed central scale $\mu_0=m_t$.** This is standard but worth flagging: varying only the factorisation/renormalisation scale while keeping the PDFs' own scale at $\mu_0$, and the choice of $\mu_0=m_t$ rather than a process-specific scale, is an assumption rather than a derived optimality criterion. The paper acknowledges "at this point it is difficult to decide whether a jet veto will or will not be necessary" and justifies $\mu_0=m_t$ by proximity to the K-factor minimum, not by a minimisation procedure.

4. **$k_t$ jet algorithm with $\Delta R=0.8$, $\Delta R_{\min}=1$, $|\eta_{\min}|<4.5$.** These are chosen to be "more inclusive" than [11] but the comparison to [11] uses *different* jet-algorithm parameters ($\Delta R=1$ only, no separation, no $\eta$ cut). The "almost perfect agreement" in $t\bar{t}j$ is therefore *not* a like-for-like cross-check; the authors themselves explain the discrepancy is due to different $m_t$, different $\Delta R$, different way of summing parton momenta into a jet, and different way of applying the $p_T$ cut. The cross-check is therefore loose.

5. **Jet four-momentum combination.** The paper states "in our simulation, we assume that the four-momenta of the partons are added." [11] instead adds transverse momenta and rapidities separately. The paper does not discuss the impact of this choice on the actual $t\bar{t}jj$ numbers (it only flags the difference in the $t\bar{t}j$ cross section).

6. **$b$-quark jets (and $b$-tagging) are implicitly included in the "jets" that must exceed 50 GeV in $p_T$ and be in $\Delta R>1$ of each other.** The original process $t\bar{t}jj$ has $jj$ in the final state, but the paper writes that "we are allowed to contain b-quarks, and therefore also the final state tbb." This means the "jets" are not colour-singlet light-quark/gluon jets; they may be $b$-quark jets from the top decay with no explicit b-tagging model, and their $p_T$ distribution and mass are not the same as light jets. No fragmentation or hadronization model is mentioned; "jets" are implicitly partons at the parton level.

7. **Top-quark width treated as zero / on-shell.** The paper works with $m_t=172.6$ GeV (LO) or 174 GeV (comparison) and the top decays to $bW$ are not simulated — the final state is $t\bar{t}$ plus two partons. There is no discussion of off-shell top effects, top-quark width, or interference with single-top channels (e.g. $t\bar{t}W$ or associated production).

8. **No EW (electroweak) corrections.** The calculation is QCD NLO; electroweak NLO corrections to $t\bar{t}(W/Z/\gamma)$-mediated top-pair production are not included. For a process studied in the context of $W^H$ Higgs physics, this is a non-trivial assumption.

9. **$pp$ initial state with no PDF uncertainties.** CTEQ6L1 (LO) and CTEQ6M (NLO) are used, but no PDF error bands or alternative PDF sets (e.g. MRST2007, NNPDF) are quoted; the $2\times$ scale variation is the only uncertainty on the NLO number. The stated NLO error $(106.94\pm0.17)$ pb is Monte-Carlo statistical error only.

10. **Statistical error only on the NLO cross section.** The $\pm 0.17$ pb on $106.94$ pb is the Monte-Carlo integration error. The scale error ($-13\%/-12\%$) and the K-factor (11%) are treated separately. PDF uncertainty, $m_t$ uncertainty, and jet-algorithm uncertainty are all absent from the final central value.

11. **The real-emission phase-space integration uses rejection and sampling "over polarization and colour."** This is the standard Helac-Dipoles methodology but the paper does not give details of the sampling grid, the number of events, or how the convergence of the one-loop tensor-integration (ONELOOP) was verified. Numerical precision is "assured by using gauge invariance tests of quadratic precision" and verification of cancellation between real/virtual — but no explicit convergence test or comparison with an independent NLO tool (e.g. MadGraph5_aMC@NLO) is quoted.

12. **The claim of "the size of the corrections to the $t\bar{t}jj$ channel is at least 200 GeV" in the $m_{jj}$ range** is based on a *moderate* range chosen because "the distribution starts above 45 GeV due to the $\Delta R$ and $p_T$ cuts, and shows tiny corrections up to at least 200 GeV." This is not a proof of perturbative control over the full $m_{jj}$ range; it is an observation that in a chosen range the LO and NLO curves overlap. The $p_T$-tail of $p_T^{1\text{st}}$ and $p_T^{2\text{nd}}$ clearly shows large LO/NLO differences at $p_T\gtrsim 250$ GeV, where the authors say "fixed order perturbation theory does not break down" without a rigorous criterion for what "breaks down" means.

13. **No jet-matching or jet-shower.** The NLO calculation is fixed-order parton-level. There is no matching to a parton shower (e.g. POWHEG/MC@NLO), so resummation of soft-collinear logs beyond NLO is absent. In a channel where the third jet is vetoed at low $p_T$, logarithmic resummation of the jet-veto is a known issue the paper raises but does not address.

14. **The "11%" is a central-scale K-factor, not an uncertainty.** The paper says "the corrections with respect to leading order are negative and small, reaching 11%." This 11% is the difference between NLO and LO at the *chosen* central scale, not a scale-variation band. The scale variation ($-13\%/-12\%$) is actually larger in magnitude than the central K-factor shift, which is an implicit tension the paper does not discuss in detail.

15. **In the jet-veto figure (Fig. 2), the choice of the central scale $\mu_0=m_t$ is not validated as the optimum for the jet-vet distribution.** The scale variation is $-54\%/-0.3\%$ — extremely asymmetric — which typically signals that the chosen scale is not the point of minimal sensitivity for the jet-vetted distribution. The paper acknowledges this ("severely underestimating the error") but still presents the jet-vet number as a useful lower bound rather than a controlled result.

16. **The final $t\bar{t}j$ comparison to [11] is in the $p_T>50$ GeV regime** (the [11] setup used $p_T>50$ GeV with the cut on the sum of momenta). The paper's "372.2" with the $p_T$ cut moved to a single parton and the paper's "376.1" for $m_t=174$ with a single $p_T$ cut are *not* the same physical process; these are parameter-scan points demonstrating the authors' tool can reproduce the [11] cross check, not an independent validation.

17. **No discussion of the $t\bar{t}+2j$ contribution from top-pair production with an extra hard jet (twin top, single top with 2 jets, $t\bar{t}W/\gamma$).** The process is defined as $t\bar{t}$ plus partons, but single-top and $t\bar{t}\vector{V}$ backgrounds are not subtracted or discussed.

18. **The "reducible background vs. ttbb background" framing assumes the "ttbb" signal is fully understood at the matching order.** The paper takes as given (from [2,3]) that the irreducible $t\bar{t}bb$ contribution is the "signal" and the $t\bar{t}jj$ "background" is what needs to be studied, but does not discuss the uncertainty in the $t\bar{t}bb$ signal itself that the NLO QCD correction to $t\bar{t}jj$ needs to be small relative to.

1. **Process**: inclusive $pp\to t\bar{t}+2$ partons at $\sqrt{s}=14$ TeV.

## [1002.4009v1-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


# Evaluation of arXiv:1002.4009v1

**B. Bevilacqua, M. Czakon, C. G. Papadopoulos, and M. Worek — "Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses at the LHC: A Study of pp → tt̄ + 2 jets at Next-To-Leading Order" (2010)**

## The logical argument, as I understand it

The paper is a focused short note on the perturbative stability of the *reducible* QCD background to the light-Higgs Higgs-strahlung-like channel `pp → tt̄H → ttbb`, specifically the irreducible-but-here-redundant top-pair + two-jet production `pp → tt̄jj`. The chain of reasoning is:

1. **Motivation.** For `m_H < 135 GeV`, the `pp → tt̄H → ttbb` signal is attractive (large top-quark Yukawa) but is contaminated by a `tt̄ + 2 jets` QCD background that mimics it after b-tagging of the two hardest jets. LO studies of the Higgs signal have shown that LO-level K-factors of order unity in the irreducible `tt̄bb` channel may not carry over to the reducible channel — so the question is whether `tt̄jj` is under perturbative control at NLO.

2. **Definition of the process.** √s = 14 TeV, `m_t = 172.6 GeV` on-shell with unrestricted kinematics; up to two incoming partons; jets by `k_T` with ΔR = 0.8 and |y| < 5; two-jet separation ΔR = 1; p_T(jet) > 50 GeV; no restriction on the third real-radiation jet. Partons are allowed to contain b quarks so the genuine `tt̄b̄b` final state can leak in and be subtracted incoherently. CTEQ6L1 for LO and CTEQ6M for NLO PDFs with corresponding LO/NLO α_s running; central scale μ0 = m_t.

3. **Method.** HELAC-PHEGAS, with HELAC-1L for virtual amplitudes, OPP for tensor reduction, CutTools + ONELOOP for scalar integrals, and HELAC-Dipoles for the Catani-Seymour real-radiation subtraction. Gauge invariance, subtraction-cutoff independence, and real/virtual cancellation are checked; quadruple precision is used.

4. **LO results (Table I).** σ_LO(tt̄jj) = 120 pb; the two dominant partonic channels are `gg → tt̄qq` (≈47 %) and `gg → tt̄gg` (≈44 %), with `qq' → tt̄q q̄'` at 6 %. Channels that carry an explicit tt̄b̄b final state (`gg → tt̄q q̄'` and `qq → tt̄qq`) are single-pb contributions → "negligible", so the reducible study is independent of the irreducible one.

5. **NLO result.** σ_NLO = 106.9 ± 0.17 pb, a *negative* shift of 11 %; the scale variation (μ0 → ξ μ0, ξ ∈ ½..2) contracts from +72 %/−39 % at LO to −13 %/−12 % at NLO, with the central point sitting at the local maximum of the NLO curve. With a jet veto at p_T < 50 the cross section is 76.6 pb.

6. **Distributions.** m_ij of the two hardest jets: few-percent-level K-factor below 200 GeV, larger effects at high p_T (Figs. 4–5 show visibly reshaped 1st-jet and 2nd-jet spectra at high p_T).

7. **Validation.** Using the exact setup of [11], the related single-jet channel `pp → tt̄j` is reproduced to 0.1 % (376.6 pb vs. the quoted 376.2 pb); switching the jet-formation recipe from a `k_T` algorithm to four-momentum sums shifts the answer by only ~1 % on the easier tt j process, supporting the "independent of algorithm" claim implicitly.

8. **Conclusion.** NLO corrections to `tt̄jj` are small; LO is reliable. A broader study varying scale, jet algorithm, p_T, and jet vetos is announced for the future.

Strengths: the argument is compact and the logic is internally consistent; the tt j cross-check is a nice self-validation; the observation that the central scale lands on the local maximum of the NLO curve is a useful (and honest) caveat; the separation from the irreducible tt̄b̄b channel is made explicit rather than assumed.

## Main weaknesses / feedback

1. **Single-cut study presented as a "process-level" conclusion.** The whole paper is one choice of (√s, m_t, p_T min, ΔR, jet separation, jet algorithm, |y|, PDF, scale) and yet the abstract sells it as "the corrections [to] `pp → tt̄jj` are … small". The authors are candid that a wider variation is still to come, but as-written the title-level claim outpaces the evidence. The tt_j validation (one channel, one group, 1 % spread) doesn't actually constrain the *two-hard-jet* jet-definition sensitivity, where ΔR and the ΔR=1 separation are both load-bearing. A 2 × 2 matrix of ΔR ∈ {0.4, 0.5, 0.8} × p_T min ∈ {30, 50, 70} GeV would have made the claim much more robust — and is cheap once the virtual/real loop is in hand.

2. **Central-scale coincidence not probed.** The paper notices (and rightly) that μ0 = m_t sits at a local maximum of the NLO curve and that the scale variation *shrinks* at NLO. The right follow-up is to vary μ0 itself (m_t/2, m_t, 2 m_t) and to quote σ at each. They do one of this (Fig. 2) and then move on. A process where the "minimal-sensitivity point" only exists at one particular μ0 choice is one that deserves to be said so explicitly with numbers.

3. **The `pt-jet-veto` scale variation of −54 % is waved through.** With the jet veto the cross section drops to 76.6 pb and the scale variation becomes −54 %/+0.3 %. The paper explains it in one sentence (smaller μ → running α_s) and concludes a 50 GeV veto "endangers the stability of perturbation theory". Quantitatively this is the *most* relevant number for a realistic Hbb̄ analysis, where a third-jet veto is exactly what is used to suppress `gg → tt̄b̄b`-type backgrounds; it deserves a paragraph of its own with a μ0 variation, not a footnote.

4. **Incoherent tt̄b̄b subtraction is asserted, not quantified.** "This contribution is tiny and can be simply subtracted from our results (the summation is incoherent)" — but no size is reported. At LO the tt̄b̄b-ish channels sum to ≈3.4 pb of 120 pb (≈3 %); at NLO where the gg→tt̄b̄b correction can be much larger than 1, the leaked-in tt̄b̄b fraction is not necessarily still 3 %. The readers cannot evaluate the residual contamination without this number.

5. **No PDF-uncertainty statement.** CTEQ6L1/CENTM is used; for a gg-dominated cross section at 14 TeV the PDF error is comparable to the NLO shift. A CTEQ6.1M vs. MSTW2008NNLO (or at least CTEQ6M vs. CTEQ5M) bracket would have been one line and would have made the "corrections are small" conclusion unambiguous about which *correction*: perturbative or PDF.

6. **No comparison to other NLO `gg → tt̄jj` computations.** The only cross-check is against the same group's single-jet paper [11]. The four-gluon top-pair virtual amplitude (cited as a first presentation in [17]) itself "still awaits an independent check by other groups" — that is the heart of the NLO machinery, and the paper's own caveat is honest but it means the result stands on a single implementation. A 5 %-level comparison to a partonic Monte Carlo (MC@NLO or POWHEG-based, or a Sherpa matrix-element sample in the matched regime) would have been very reassuring.

7. **No b-tagging discussion for the ttjj side.** The whole motivation is discrimination via two b tags; the paper treats the two hardest jets as generic, and the irreducible/reducible overlap via b-tagging is exactly the physics that motivates the subtraction of the tt̄b̄b channels. A brief estimate of the *tagged* cross section (× ε_b² ≈ 0.8) and of the mistag contribution would have tied the perturbative study to the actual analysis.

8. **14 TeV is a forward-looking assumption** in 2010; the paper would have benefited from a single 7 TeV cross-check (the LHC at the time was running at 7 TeV).

9. **Presentation.** The `ΔR = 0.8` and `ΔR = 1` separation are introduced without a figure; the "long tail" to 400 GeV is a single sentence; the four-momentum-sum vs. `k_T` jet-formation distinction in §4 (the tt_j comparison paragraph) is dense and would be clearer with a short worked example. The abstract also overpromises relative to a single-setup study (see point 1).

## Bottom line

A competent short note that puts a NLO number on the `tt̄jj` reducible background, with a clean K ≈ 0.9, a shrinking scale variation, and a self-validation on `tt_j`. The central claim — "NLO corrections to `pp → tt̄jj` are small and LO is therefore reliable for the `ttH → ttbb` analysis" — is plausible and is supported at face value by the numbers in this one setup. But the breadth of cuts, PDF, and scale probes is narrow enough that the conclusion would have been materially stronger if, as the authors acknowledge, the wider variation had been done. The result is informative but not a benchmark, and the b-tag overlap / incoherent-subtraction bookkeeping is left as an assertion.

## Evaluation: Bevilacqua, Czakon, Papadopoulos, Worek — "Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses at the LHC: A Study of pp→tt̄ + 2 jets at NLO" (arXiv:1002.4009)

### The strongest quantitative claim

> "At the central scale μ₀ = m_t we obtain σ_NLO(pp→ttjj̄ + X) = (106.94 ± 0.17) pb, compared to the LO result of 120.17(8) pb, which represents a **negative shift of 11%**, and allows us to conclude that the corrections to this process are small," with a scale-variation uncertainty "of the same order" (−13%/−12%).

This is the paper's load-bearing result: the claim that the reducible ttjj̄ background to ttH* Higgs analyses is perturbatively stable at NLO, in contrast to the huge NLO corrections found for the irreducible ttbb̄ background. It's what makes the whole paper useful to experimental Higgs groups.

### 1. Is the comparison fair?

**Mostly yes, by the conventions of the field, with one real caveat.**

- The LO number uses CTEQ6L1 LO PDFs with LO-running α_s; the NLO number uses CTEQ6M with NLO running. Choosing matching-order PDFs at each perturbative order is standard practice and is not an unfair comparison — but it means part of the −11% "K-factor" is arguably attributable to the PDF upgrade rather than genuine NLO QCD radiation, and the paper does not attempt to disentangle the two (e.g., by reporting NLO with LO PDFs). A skeptic of "NLO corrections are small" would want that decomposition.
- The paper itself states upfront that conclusions "pertain to the particular setup we have used," deliberately using more inclusive cuts than the actual Higgs analysis of [3] (ATLAS strategy note). This is an honest limitation, but it means the strongest claim is scoped to their kinematics, and the generalization to analysis-like selections is argued for only *by analogy* with the behavior of ttj̄ in [11], not demonstrated.
- The extra ΔR = 1 separation between jets is justified merely as "believed to bear no impact on the final conclusions" — assumed, not checked. A sensitivity scan (which they say is "planned") is absent.
- The jet-veto variant (76.58 ± 0.17 pb) shows a −54%/+0.3% scale spread, i.e., unstable, and the authors correctly note that choosing a high scale there would "severely underestimate the error." So the "small corrections" conclusion does **not** survive the jet veto that the proposed Higgs analysis actually needs — a tension the paper flags but does not resolve.

### 2. Are baselines current?

- **For the validation process (ttj̄): yes, excellent.** The direct comparison is against Dittmaier–Uwer–Weinzierl (Eur. Phys. J. C 59, 625, 2009), the then-current independent NLO calculation. Agreement is (376.6 ± 0.6) pb vs. (376.2 ± 0.6) pb — nearly perfect once the different k_T recombination conventions are accounted for (they even quantify the 372.2 pb the alternative convention would give with their cuts, and confirm 376.1 pb with their own setup). This is a genuinely well-executed cross-check.
- **For the actual new result (ttjj̄): no independent baseline exists** — and the paper admits it. The only new virtual amplitudes (top pair + four gluons) "still await an independent check by other groups." There is no second NLO implementation to compare against, and no NLO+PS or N+LO cross-check. The internal checks (real–virtual cancellation, gauge invariance tests, quadruple precision, dipole-cutoff independence) are appropriate, but a first-ever NLO process with a single-group implementation and unverified amplitudes is a real evidence gap the paper is transparent about.
- The CTEQ6 PDFs are current for 2010 (CTEQ6.5/CT10 come later); nothing stale in 2010 v1 terms.

### 3. Error bars / statistical significance

- **Monte Carlo integration errors are reported everywhere** (±0.17 pb on 106.94, i.e. ~0.16%) and are negligible against the theory uncertainty. Good.
- **Scale variation is the headline error estimate** and is clearly presented: LO +72%/−39% collapsing to NLO −13%/−12% — a factor-of-2 variation, standard of the day (it later became the 7-point variation standard).
- **Where it's weakest:** the claim about the physically most relevant distribution — "tiny corrections up to at least 200 GeV" in m_jj (Fig. 3) — is asserted qualitatively from a plot with no numerical bin-by-bin K-factors or banded uncertainties. Similarly, the statement that low-p_T behavior "is expect[ed] to be in the safe range" for fixed order is judgment, not an error estimate. No significance statement is attempted, but none is really claimed for, either — the paper makes claims of order-of-magnitude stability rather than statistical inference, so the absence of p-values is defensible.
- Systematics not covered: PDF sets (no CTEQ6M vs. MRST comparison), top-mass scheme (fixed on-shell), no matching to parton showers, and no alternative jet-algorithm validation beyond the single ΔR = 1 point. A PDF set variation would have been cheap and informative and is absent.

### Bottom line

The evidence package for the central claim is honest and, for a first NLO calculation, reasonably strong: a nearly perfect reproduction of the only available independent benchmark on a nearby process, rigorous internal consistency checks, reported MC errors, and a standard scale-variation error estimate. But the headline "NLO corrections are small (−11%)" should be read cautiously: it is setup-specific (the authors say so), mixes in a PDF-order change, rests on amplitudes with no independent verification, and demonstrably does not survive the jet veto that motivates the analysis — the paper's own Fig. 2 says exactly that. The generalization to realistic Higgs selections is inference, not demonstration.



**Rationale.** *Originality 4/5*: the first full NLO QCD calculation of pp→ttjj̄ with previously uncomputed virtual amplitudes (tt + 4 gluons) — a genuine technical first for the process, though the methodology (Helac-1L, Catani–Seymour subtraction, OPP reduction) is standard for the time. *Overall 4/5*: sound, well-validated (excellent ttj̄ benchmark match, explicit caveats), and internally consistent; docked one point for the unverified amplitudes, the PDF-order confound in the K-factor, the untested "no impact" ΔR assumption, and a central claim that its own jet-veto data show is not generally stable. *Impact 3/5*: became a key ingredient of the tt̄H NLO programme (the group's later tt̄H+1j, tt̄H+2j and Higgs-in-ttbb̄ work directly depended on these ttjj̄ numbers, and the ttbb̄-vs-ttjj̄ balance it quantifies was central to the "can you actually use this channel" debate), but it is a single-purpose background calculation rather than a method the field adopted.

## [1002.4009v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


I've extracted and read the full 4-page paper. It is arXiv:1002.4009v1, "Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses at the LHC: A Study of pp → tt̄ + 2 jets at NLO" by Bevilacqua, Czakon, Papadopoulos, and Worek (2010).

## Argument chain

1. **Motivation**: For M_H < 135 GeV, the LHC signal pp → tt̄H* → tt̄bb̄ must be separated from the reducible QCD background tt̄jj, which has a cross section larger by ~2 orders of magnitude but becomes comparable after b-tagging efficiencies. Since earlier NLO studies of the *irreducible* tt̄bb̄ background found very large corrections, the question is whether tt̄jj needs its own NLO study.
2. **Setup**: LHC at 14 TeV, m_t = 172.6 GeV, k_T jets with ΔR = 0.8, jets separated by ΔR = 1, |y_jet| < 4.5, **p_T ≥ 50 GeV on the (two) jets**; the third jet is unrestricted. Deliberately *not* the cuts of the ATLAS analysis (ref. [3]) — "more inclusive cuts" to retain generality.
3. **Result (inclusive)**: σ_NLO = 106.94(17) pb vs σ_LO = 120.17(8) pb → an 11% negative shift; scale variation shrinks from +72/−39% at LO to −13/−12% at NLO. They conclude "the corrections to this process are small."
4. **Result (jet veto p_T < 50 GeV)**: σ_NLO = 76.58(17) pb, i.e. a **−36% correction** with a highly asymmetric scale band of **−54% / +0.3%**. The K-factor under veto is worse (0.638 vs 0.918) and far less stable.
5. **Extrapolation to other observables**: they argue that because the p_T lower cut is 50 GeV and the m_jj distribution (the observable entering the Higgs studies) shows only tiny corrections below 200 GeV, "the size of the corrections to the cross section is transmitted to the most relevant distribution," and fixed order "does not break down" in their range.
6. **Validation and generalization**: exact reproduction of the independent tt̄j NLO calculation of Dittmaier/Uwer/Weinzierl (376.6(0.6) pb vs their 376.2(0.6)), plus a jet-algorithm-variant check. Concluding move: "comparison to the behavior of the corrections... in the tt̄j case provides a viable argument that our conclusions will remain true for other input parameters" — i.e., fixed-order NLO is adequate to control this background for the Higgs analysis.

## The single weakest link

The break is at **step 5→6: the jump from "corrections are small in *our* inclusive no-veto setup with p_T ≥ 50 GeV on the jets" to "the conclusions remain true for the setup relevant to the Higgs analysis."** That final conclusion is not entailed by, and is in fact *undercut by*, the paper's own data:

1. **The paper's own veto calculation falsifies the transfer.** Everything the Higgs channel actually needs is a bound on extra radiation (the "jet veto of the third jet" they cite in the introduction as *the* relevant experimental feature, and "a jet veto of less than p_T = 50 GeV" as the regime that "is likely to endanger the stability of the perturbative expansion"). Under exactly that veto the same numbers show the K-factor dropping to 0.638 and the scale uncertainty exploding to −54%/+0.3%. A background whose uncertainty band is one-sided and 54% deep is not one that is "under control" at NLO, yet the headline conclusion ("the corrections with respect to leading order are negative and small") is made from the no-veto configuration, where the veto-induced contribution is simply folded into the inclusive number. The data therefore contain a regime change precisely at the boundary (50 GeV between the *required* jets and *any* third jet) where the analysis is sensitive, and the argument silently ignores the bad side of that boundary.

2. **The only explicit bridge is an analogy, not evidence.** The "viable argument" offered is that the tt̄j calculation of ref. [11] behaved differently across setups — but tt̄j carries only *one* additional jet, so the difference between "inclusive" and "vetoed" is far milder than with two required jets plus a vetoed third, whose phase space includes soft-gluon singularities in the extra radiation. Their own soft-radiation discussion (the p_T,min = 20 GeV reversal of qg and gg shares) shows corrections in this process are extremely cut-dependent, which is exactly why a single inclusive configuration cannot bound another one. Note too that their *own* tt̄j cross-check comparison was *more* generous than the real one: they report 376.6 pb as "perfect agreement" with 376.2 pb, while the note in the paper admits their version of the jet algorithm would give 372.2 pb — a 1.2% difference the "perfect agreement" framing hides.

3. **The m_jj argument rests on a claim the data barely support.** They restrict the plot to m_jj ≤ 400 GeV "due to our phenomenological motivation" while showing a long tail beyond, and the "tiny corrections up to at least 200 GeV" claim is a reading of a ratio curve where the two backgrounds' relative sizes were themselves argued (ref. [3]) to depend delicately on the exact selections. The inference "small corrections to the inclusive cross section ⇒ small corrections to the analysis signal region" is the standard but unproven step here, and the veto result shows where it fails.

In short: the NLO computation itself is sound (the independent tt̄j reproduction is genuine strong validation), and the statement "in *this* inclusive setup the corrections are 11% and the scale error is ~12–13%" is exactly what the data say. But the paper's actual purpose — deciding whether the tt̄jj background undercuts the tt̄H* → tt̄bb̄ Higgs analysis — requires the vetoed configuration, and for that configuration the same paper shows large (−36%) and wildly scale-sensitive corrections. The chain's last step, "therefore NLO is adequate for the analysis and the conclusion generalizes to other input parameters," replaces a missing result (corrections under the analysis's real selections) with an unvalidated tt̄j analogy, while the paper's closest available data point points the other way.

## [1002.4009v1-reproducibility] Reproducibility audit


## Evaluation of arXiv:1002.4009v1 — "Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses at the LHC: A Study of pp → tt̄ + 2 jets at NLO" (Bevilacqua, Czakon, Papadopoulos, Worek)

**Scope.** This is a 4-page 2010 physics letter computing the NLO QCD cross section for pp → tt̄jj at the 14 TeV LHC (106.94 ± 0.17 pb, an 11% negative K-factor), plus a jet-veto variant (76.58 pb) and distributions of mjj and jet pT, aimed at constraining a reducible background in the tt̄bb̄ "Higgs factory" channel. It ends by reproducing a tt̄j reference result (376.6 vs 376.2 pb — "perfect agreement").

### What blocks independent re-implementation

**1. No released code (the primary blocker).** The computation is built from a stack of internal tools — Helac-Phegas/Helac-1L (virtual), Helac-Dipoles (real), CutTools (tensor reduction), OneLOop (scalar integrals), Catani–Seymour subtraction — but the paper provides no URL, archive, or version pin for the tt̄jj-specific event generator. In 2010 such code was typically only available "on request." A re-implementer must first reconstruct the full toolchain and then build the process-specific driver (phase-space setup, event reweighting, polarization/color sampling), for which the paper defers to refs [6, 17, 22].

**2. The master amplitudes are single-sourced and unverified.** The authors state plainly that "the only new virtual amplitudes are those involving a top quark pair and four gluons... due to their notorious complexity, still await an independent check by other groups." Independent re-implementation in the strict sense — verifying the result without trusting their code — is impossible because the one genuinely novel mathematical object in the calculation has no independent check anywhere in the open literature.

**3. No Monte Carlo statistics, seeds, or integration-generator details.** The quoted ±0.17 pb (and ±0.6/±0.7 pb) MC errors are reported, but the number of events generated, the MC integration algorithm, acceptance/sampling weights, and random-number seeds are not given. Bit-level or even same-precision reproduction of the central values is therefore not possible from the paper.

**4. Subtraction and scheme details are only gestured at.** The Catani–Seymour dipole phase-space cutoff is mentioned only via "we explored the independence of the results on the unphysical cutoff" — no values, no range, no plot. "Quadruple precision" is invoked for virtual amplitudes without saying in what form (80-bit x87? double-double?), which matters for any attempt at numeric reproduction.

**5. The jet veto definition is ambiguous at the algorithm level.** "A jet veto with pT = 50 GeV" does not specify whether the veto is applied to raw partons or kT-reconstructed jets, or what happens to the vetoed parton (discarded vs. event killed pre-Clus). For fixed-order calculations with jet vetoes, this scheme choice can shift results at the tens-of-percent error-bar scale — and the paper itself documents that a minor kT recombination variant (transverse-momentum vs. 4-momentum addition) shifts the tt̄j cross section by ~1% (372.2 vs 376.6 pb), proving the sensitivity of their numbers to implementation details that are only documented inside other papers.

**6. No underlying data for the physics content.** Figs. 2–5 (scale scan, mjj, pTj distributions — actually the substance of the physics message) have no data tables or accompanying files; only the three total cross sections are tabulated. The central claim about the mjj distribution ("tiny corrections up to at least 200 GeV") therefore cannot be quantitatively checked against any released numbers.

**7. Minor omissions.** Top-quark propagator treatment beyond "on-shell" (narrow-width δ vs. fixed Γt) is unspecified; PDF-set versioning is given (CTEQ6L1/CTEQ6M) but tool-version hashes are not; compute resources are identified only by cluster name (DESY Zeuthen Grid) with no cost per observable; the ΔR = 1 jet separation is admitted as an arbitrary demonstration of tool flexibility, so results are explicitly "pertinent to the particular setup."

### Mitigating factors

The paper *does* pin the kinematics tightly (√s, mt, ΔR jet radius 0.8, parton/jet rapidity bounds, pT,min = 50 GeV, PDFs, αs running order, central scale μ0 = mt), it documents internal consistency tests (real–virtual cancellation, gauge-invariance tests, cancellation of divergences, cutoff independence), and the tt̄j cross-check against an unrelated group's code is a strong partial validation. A well-resourced group with the Helac-Phegas toolchain and the published refs [6, 17, 22] — all of which existed publicly in 2009 — could plausibly reconstruct the total cross section, and indeed later follow-up work (Czakon et al., JHEP 0908 085) extends the framework. But that is re-derivation from scattered sources, not reproducibility from the paper; the distributions, veto scheme, and unverified 5-gluon amplitudes remain the hard blockers.

### Bottom line

Reproducibility is **weak-to-marginal** by modern standards: the physics setup is sufficiently specified to attempt a re-derivation, but the absence of code, MC statistics/seeds, subtraction-cutoff details, jet-veto scheme, and data files — combined with an acknowledged-unverified core amplitude — means an independent group cannot reproduce the results as published without substantial reconstruction and trust in single-source amplitudes. The letter is scientifically sound within its era (early-2010, pre-code-reproducibility norm) but would fail a present-day reproducibility audit.

## [1002.4009v1-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


# Critique of writing and framing — arXiv:1002.4009v1 (Bevilacqua, Czakon, Papadopoulos, Worek)

## What the paper actually does vs. what it frames it as

The title frames the work as knowledge of the *dominant backgrounds in Higgs-boson analyses* at the LHC. What the body delivers is a single NLO cross section for pp → tt̄jj at 14 TeV, computed under one self-chosen ("more inclusive") cut set that the authors explicitly state they **do not** make to reproduce the ATLAS setup [3] ("we will mimic some of the general assumptions, but impose more inclusive cuts"). The word "mimic" is doing a lot of work: at no point is any number in the paper mapped onto the analysis-level cross section it claims to underpin. The gap between title-level promise ("dominant backgrounds in Higgs analyses") and the delivered object (an inclusive cross section away from those analyses) is the central framing problem, and much of the overclaiming below is symptom of it.

## Sentences that overclaim beyond the evidence

1. **Abstract: "we show that the corrections with respect to leading order are negative and small, reaching 11%. The error obtained by scale variation is of the same order."**
   The largest overclaim in the paper, and self-contradictory. "Reaching 11%" reads as an upper bound on the correction, but 11% is just the single central-scale value (106.94 vs 120.17 pb); the paper's own scale variations of the NLO result (−13%/−12%) are *as large as the correction itself*. A correction that is indistinguishable in size from its own scale uncertainty is not established to be "small" — it means the two-order computation has not yet converged. The abstract buries this by pairing the two sentences so the second reads as reassurance rather than a retraction of the first. Worse, in the jet-veto case studied in the same paper, the downward scale variation is **−54%**, so "reaching 11%" is contradicted within three pages by the authors' own data.

2. **"This represents a negative shift of 11%, and allows us to conclude that the corrections to this process are small."**
   "Allows us to conclude" is too strong for one scale, one PDF set (CTEQ6M), one top mass, one energy, no PDF error quoted, no comparison to parton showers or to NLO+NNLO in the singular region. Given (1), the defensible version is "the correction is moderate and of the same size as the residual scale dependence; further orders or resummed treatment would be needed to call the perturbative series for this observable under control."

3. **"the additional separation in ∆R … is believed to bear no impact on the final conclusions."**
   "Is believed to" is a hedge attached to an untested assertion — no ∆R variation is presented anywhere in the paper. The authors themselves close by saying they plan "a much wider study" varying exactly this and other parameters, which is an admission that the "no impact" statement is currently an article of faith. It should be stated as a caveat ("we expect, but have not verified") rather than a fact.

4. **"This is irrelevant, since we know that this contribution is tiny and can be simply subtracted from our results (the summation is incoherent)."**
   This refers to b quarks being allowed inside jets. "We know it is tiny" cites nothing: Table I shows that the *partonic* gg → tt̄q q̄ channel is 1.6% of the LO cross section, which is related but not the same statement as "b-tagged-jet content within tt̄jj is tiny," and no number is ever quoted for it. "Irrelevant" is a stronger word than the evidence supports.

5. **"With our lower cut of pT,min = 50 GeV, we expect to be mostly in the safe range, where fixed order perturbation theory does not break down."**
   This follows immediately after Fig. 4/5, which the paper says "demonstrate the **strongly altered shapes**" of the jet-pT distributions between LO and NLO. "We expect to be safe" is asserted against no resummation, NNLO, or fixed-order+shower comparison — precisely the tools one uses to decide where 50 GeV is safe, because jets this soft are the domain of soft-collinear emissions (as the paper itself concedes one sentence later). The paper both concedes the region is shower-dominated and declares it safe, without evidence resolving the tension.

6. **"demonstrating perfect agreement ([11] quotes (376.2 ± 0.6) pb)"** — for 376.6 vs 376.2.
   Fine as a validation, but "perfect" is inflated rhetoric: the difference (0.4 pb) is of the same size as the quoted Monte Carlo errors (0.6 pb), i.e., the agreement is *within* a statistical error, not perfect. More damaging to the framing: the paper then shows that the jet-algorithm convention (adding four-momenta vs adding transverse momenta in the kT measure) shifts the answer by ~4 pb to 372.2 pb — outside the quoted ±0.6 band. The advertised 0.1% precision is therefore cosmetic; the systematic spread in conventions is at the ~1% level. Leading with "perfect agreement" overstates the precision the number actually carries.

7. **"comparison to the behavior of the corrections for different setups in the tt̄j case [11] provides a viable argument that our conclusions will remain true for other input parameters."**
   This is the generalization the whole conclusion rests on, and it is the weakest sentence in the paper. It is an analogy argument from a *different process* (one extra jet, different phase space, different soft-gluon structure) to the claim that tt̄jj corrections are generically small — precisely the generality step the authors had *rejected* earlier for tt̄bb̄ ("whatever the result on the size of the corrections in the tt̄bb̄ case, tt̄jj requires a separate study"). Applying their own standard of rigor against the analogy they now invoke, "a viable argument" is generous. What is demonstrated is "small for one setup" (their own, more honest phrasing earlier in the sentence); the "will remain true" clause is speculative.

8. **"since this is the observable entering Higgs boson studies"** — for the m_jj of the two hardest jets.
   The observable that enters the tt̄H* analyses is the invariant mass of the *b-tagged* jets; the b-tagging efficiency is, by the paper's own account, the very reason tt̄jj exists as a reducible background at all. Calling m_jj "the most relevant distribution" is true only relative to the paper's own cut definition, and the "relevance" framing quietly slides the analysis back toward the jet definition the paper chose rather than the one the analyses use.

9. **Sloppier (but telling) phrasing: "we keep the dependence only in a modest range up to 400 GeV due to our phenomenological motivation."**
   What is meant is that the plotted m_jj range is truncated at 400 GeV; the sentence as written ("keep the dependence") is incoherent, and "due to our phenomenological motivation" is hand-waving about an a-priori range choice for the "most relevant" distribution. The same m_jj distribution is then declared to show "tiny corrections up to at least 200 GeV" — "tiny" being unquantified prose standing in for an error band the figure does not carry.

### To be fair — the paper's honest hedges
A fair critique must note the paper is unusually self-aware in places, which makes the overclaims above harder to excuse rather than excusing them: "at this point it is difficult to decide whether a jet veto will or will not be necessary"; "this should be considered as severely underestimating the error" (for the flat large-scale jet-veto region); "the only new virtual amplitudes … still await an independent check by other groups" (gg→tt̄gggg in Helac-1L). Authors who flag −54% scale variation and unverified amplitudes in the body have less excuse for a "corrections are small, reaching 11%" abstract.

## What an adversarial reviewer would put in their first paragraph

> "The title of this paper promises the 'Dominant QCD Backgrounds in Higgs Boson Analyses'; the paper itself contains no number computed under the selections of any Higgs-boson analysis, and this gap is not incidental. The reported cross section is evaluated with a single, ad hoc inclusive cut set that the authors do not reproduce and to which they never attach a jet-algorithm, PDF, scale, or acceptance variation — and the headline result ('corrections … negative and small, reaching 11%') is contradicted by the paper's own scale-stability data: the NLO scale uncertainty (−12/−13% inclusive, −54% under a 50 GeV jet veto) is equal to or larger than the claimed NLO correction, which means the perturbative series for this observable has not, within this work, converged enough to call its first-order correction 'small'. The generality claim in the concluding paragraph — that results from a different process (tt̄j) 'provide a viable argument' that corrections remain small for other tt̄jj setups — is precisely the kind of extrapolation the authors correctly refused to make when tt̄j corrections could not be inferred from tt̄bb̄. The jet-pT distributions are admitted to be in the known region of soft-collinear emissions where fixed order is unreliable, yet the paper simultaneously declares its 50 GeV jet threshold to be 'in the safe range,' with no resummation, higher-order, or fixed-order-plus-shower comparison to support the assertion. The single validation point (reproduction of [11]) is within statistical errors, but the paper's own discussion of jet-algorithm conventions shows the systematic spread at the ~1% level, so the quoted 0.1% statistical precision is not physically meaningful. Finally, the genuinely novel amplitudes (gg→tt̄gggg) are, by the authors' own admission, unverified by an independent group — so no number in this paper rests entirely on independently checked ingredients. In sum: a first NLO result, useful, but a single central number under cuts of the paper's own choosing, dressed as background knowledge for a channel it never actually simulates."

## Assessment in brief

The computation itself is a genuine contribution (first NLO tt̄jj, validated against an independent NLO tt̄j calculation), and the authors are unusually candid about their own blind spots. But the manuscript's framing outruns its evidence at the two points that matter most for decision-makers: the abstract's "small corrections" claim (undercut by its own scale variations, including the −54% veto number), and the closing generalization (an analogy argument the paper's own logic elsewhere rejects). The fix is mostly editorial: replace "reaching 11%" with a scale-variation-qualified statement, demote "perfect agreement" and "viable argument" to what the numbers literally show, and retitle toward what was actually done (an NLO tt̄jj cross section at 14 TeV in one setup, with stability analysis).

## Summary

The paper computes, for the first time, the next-to-leading order (NLO) QCD correction to top-pair production with two additional jets, pp → tt̄ + 2j, at the 14 TeV LHC. The motivation is concrete and timely: in the tt̄bb̄ (tt̄H*) Higgs channel, the reducible tt̄jj background (larger than the irreducible tt̄bb̄ cross section by ~2 orders of magnitude) becomes comparable once finite b-tagging efficiencies are included, while the tt̄bb̄ process is known to carry very large NLO K-factors (~1.9–2.2). The central question — whether tt̄jj also suffers large corrections, or is sufficiently well-behaved — is answered by a full NLO simulation built on the HELAC-PHEGAS machinery (HElAC-1L/OPP virtual, HELAC-Dipoles real with Catani–Seymour subtraction, OneLOop, CTEQ6M). The headline result is a small, *negative* correction to the inclusive cross section (106.94 pb NLO vs. 120.17 pb LO, ≈ −11%) with a modest scale error, a dramatic reduction of scale uncertainty from LO to NLO, small corrections to the key m_jj distribution, and an explicit jet-veto analysis. The code is validated by reproducing the known tt̄j result of Dittmaier–Uwer–Weinzierl.

## Strengths

1. **First-of-its-kind computation.** This is the inaugural NLO calculation of a six-external-parton pp final state. The only genuinely new virtual amplitudes (tt̄ + 4 gluons) were produced for the first time in the companion work [17], and this is where the real technical difficulty lies. That is a genuine precision-QCD milestone, not an application of a turnkey tool.

2. **Well-motivated, physics-relevant question.** The tt̄jj background is exactly the quantity a tt̄bb̄ analysis needs. Answering it with a self-consistent NLO number, rather than leaving it at leading order, is valuable and was simply not available before.

3. **Careful uncertainty treatment.** The comparison of scale variation LO (+72%/−39%) vs. NLO (−13%/−12%) is the correct and persuasive argument for convergence, and the paper is honest that the central scale sits near the K-factor minimum.

4. **Validation.** The reproduction of the tt̄j cross section (376.6 vs 376.2 pb, with a transparent discussion of the k_t-algorithm mass convention shifting it to 372.2) is a meaningful consistency check that distinguishes this from an unvalidated number.

5. **Distributions, not just rates.** Showing that the m_jj invariant mass (the observable that actually enters the Higgs fit) carries only tiny corrections up to ~200 GeV, while the high-p_T jet shape is strongly reshaped, is the physically interesting content and is presented well.

## Weaknesses and Concerns

1. **The jet veto undermines the headline (partially).** The abstract's central claim — "corrections … are negative and small, reaching 11%" — holds only for the *inclusive* phase space. The veto (p_T,X < 50 GeV) is precisely the tool proposed in [7] to stabilize the tt̄bb̄ analysis, and under it the correction jumps to (76.58 vs ~120) ≈ −36% with a highly asymmetric scale error (−54%/−0.3%). The authors flag that a high-scale choice would fake stability, but the tension is only partially digested: the very analysis configuration the Higgs measurement would use is the one where tt̄jj is *least* perturbative. This deserves to be foregrounded in the abstract/conclusion rather than appearing as a secondary observation.

2. **Core amplitudes lack an independent check.** By the authors' own admission, the novel tt̄ + 4 gluon virtual amplitudes "still await an independent check by other groups." Consistency tests (gauge invariance, divergence cancellation, quadruple precision) are reassuring but internal. For a claim as clean-and-small as the K-factor, an external cross-check is the thing a cautious reader would most want, and its absence is the main soft spot in the chain of evidence.

3. **Single-setup generality is asserted, not demonstrated.** The authors wisely use "more inclusive" cuts, but the conclusion is explicitly tied to one ΔR, p_T,√s configuration. The argument that "the tt̄j behavior across setups supports generality" is plausible yet indirect; the promised wider study (√s, cone size, p_T, vetoes) is deferred. As it stands this is a letter, not a systematic study, and the "small corrections" claim is a lower/upper bound in a somewhat narrow sense.

4. **Brevity of format.** At four pages the treatment is necessarily thin on cross-checks, PDF dependence, and alternative jet definitions. For a *prestigious* journal venue one would expect at least a PDF-set variation and a more complete characterization of the veto phase space before the strong conclusion is drawn.

## Minor points

- CTEQ6 with LO PDF for Table I and CTEQ6M for NLO is the correct pairing, though stating it once more explicitly at the result would help.
- The note that adding the tiny incoherent tt̄bb̄ contamination is "irrelevant" is fine but would benefit from a one-line magnitude estimate.
- Figures 4/5 confirm the expected reshaping of the hardest-jet p_T; a brief quantitative statement (size of the relative shift at a benchmark p_T) would strengthen the "fixed-order breaks down here" claim.

## Recommendation

The work is sound, well-motivated, and a genuine first NLO result on a difficult six-parton process, with a sensible validation and an honest uncertainty discussion. Its main limitations are the dependence of the "small corrections" message on the inclusive (non-vetoed) configuration, the deferred independent check of the main new amplitudes, and the deferred study of parameter generality. As a letter these are acceptable and point clearly to a follow-up; for a prestigious-journal record they should be more prominently flagged and ideally partly addressed. This is a solid, correct, and useful precision-QCD contribution — a good paper for a top QCD forum, with the reservations above.

**Decision: Accept (minor revision recommended for the journal record).**

I read the paper in full. Here is my review, framed as I would give it to a student who submitted this for approval before we sent it out.

## Overall assessment

This is a competent, well-executed first NLO study of a genuinely important background channel: top-quair pair production with two extra jets, the QCD irreducible background to the $t\bar{t}H \to t\bar{t}bb$ Higgs search. The headline result — that the NLO correction is modest (≈ −11%) and the scale uncertainty shrinks from the LO asymmetric ±40%/+70% band to roughly ±13% — is, if believed, a physically useful conclusion. And the fact that you claim conclusions of this order *before any jet veto* and show the lower bound with $p_T^{veto}=50$ GeV (−54%/−0.3%, i.e. strongly asymmetric and potentially large) is honest and important: it tells the experimentalists that a veto on the third jet is precisely what endangers the perturbative expansion. That framing is the right one for the audience this paper needed (ATLAS/CMS analyses people, 2007–2010 Higgs mass exclusion era, $W+H$ channel).

But in its present form the paper over-claims on the strength of what it actually shows. Let me be specific.

## Things done well

1. **Reproduction check (Sec. on $t\bar{t}j$).** Getting $376.6 \pm 0.6$ pb against the $376.2 \pm 0.6$ pb of the single-jet calculation, and then *explaining* the residual mismatch (4 GeV top mass, jet definition: momenta added vs. $k_T$-recombined transverse momenta) is exactly the right level of diligence. Keep that spirit — it's the paragraph most likely to survive peer review scrutiny and reader replication attempts.

2. **Honest scoping.** You explicitly mimic the assumptions of the reference Higgs analysis while using more inclusive cuts, and you repeatedly caveat that conclusions pertain to a particular setup. That is good scientific hygiene for a negative/mild result.

3. **The scale-dependence story with the jet veto (Fig. 2) is the paper's real content.** The observation that going to a *higher* scale in the vetoed case would erase all scale dependence should be treated as a warning, which you do. Good.

4. Figs. 4–5 showing large NLO shape effects in the single-jet $p_T$ spectra (where soft-collinear dynamics takes over) is the right observable to display, and the self-awareness ("soft-collinear emissions are best simulated by parton showers") is correct. That motivates NLO+MC for follow-up, which you gesture at only implicitly in the plan for a wider study. State it.

## Problems I need you to fix before this is submittable

1. **Error analysis is one-dimensional.** Your quoted errors ($0.17$ pb on $107$ pb; $0.6$ on $376$) are Monte-Carlo integration errors — that is not the uncertainty of the prediction. You vary only the renormalization/factorization scale, tied together as one scale $\mu_R=\mu_F=\xi\,\mu_0$, varying by a factor of 2. This is a coarse, correlated scan. At minimum:
   - Vary $\mu_R$ and $\mu_F$ independently (7-point envelope, or at least uncorrelated up/down), especially since you're arguing *the size of the residual uncertainty* is your result.
   - Vary the top-quark mass ($m_t = 172.6$ GeV at 2010 precision is already ~1 GeV from PDG values; $m_t$ enters LO and NLO at tree level and should be varied by ∓1 GeV to show it is not the dominant effect).
   - Vary PDF sets (CTEQ6L1 LO vs. NLO in your framework — you already switch to CTEQ6M for NLO; at least comment on LO→PDF-NLO scheme consistency, and give a set-to-set estimate). The 47% $qg$ and 44% $gg$ contributions in Table I make the LO prediction PDF-sensitive; you never address this.

2. **"Small correction" is a relative claim and you never test its stability.** The −11% is with your inclusive cuts. Table I shows the LO composition is $gg$-, $qg$- and $q\bar{q}\to tt\bar{q}q$-driven at 62% from the latter two channels; the text says these two are "almost negligible" in size *of the NLO correction* but that is asserted, not shown. Give the NLO contribution per partonic channel (or at least split virtual/real for the $tt\bar{q}q$-related channels). If the −11% were, say, driven by a $-80\%$ correction on the 6.2% $q\bar{q}$ channel partially canceling positive pieces elsewhere, the honest statement would be different. Right now the headline number has no per-channel decomposition behind it, and a reviewer will demand it.

3. **The negative correction deserves discussion, not just report.** A negative NLO K-factor at 6–7 orders of magnitude below is unusual for top processes and could indicate either (a) a genuinely real dynamical effect, (b) residual dependence on the jet definition/algo parameters you chose ($\Delta R = 0.8$ on *partons* with $|\Delta y_i|<5$, four-momentum addition, no jet radius, inclusive of $b$ quarks in the jets — a very LO-like jet definition), or (c) a $\mu_0 = m_t$ central-scale choice that is too low for hard two-jet configurations. You chose $\mu_0 = m_t$ and then noted $\mu = 2\mu_0$ gives smaller corrections, which is a hint that the process's hard scale is above $m_t$ in the two-jet configuration. Try a central scale tied to $H_T/2$ or $\sqrt{m_{t\bar{t}}^2/4 + 2(p_{T,1}p_{T,2})}$ and see whether the K-factor stabilizes there. This is a short test and it would substantially de-risk the conclusion. (The asymmetry −54%/−0.3% of the vetoed result already signals scale sensitivity that a $p_T$-dependent scale would tame — you should say so and test it, at least qualitatively.)

4. **Figure quality/consistency.** Fig. 2's log axis and the "blue dotted / red solid / green dashed" legend are fine but the caption says the green dashed is NLO *with* the 50 GeV veto — the plot then mixes order (LO vs NLO) and vetoes (± veto) in one panel; split it. Figs. 3–6 are LO-vs-NLO only: no band. Since you are selling "the correction is small and we are in control," the natural thing is to show a band (scale variation) on the NLO curves. A small-correction claim is *stronger* with the band visible and small; without it, the claim is just "the curves are close," which any reader can discount once they see the asymmetric scale band in Fig. 2. There is tension in the paper between "we have control" (Sec. 3 body) and the −54% one-sided band, and the figures currently paper over that tension.

5. **Experimental link is thin.** The motivation is the $t\bar{t}H$ channel, but beyond stating it, the paper never translates its NLO result onto the *discriminating* observable — the $m_{jj}$ peak region near $m_H$. Fig. 3 is $m_{jj}$ (good), but it does not show the K-factor in the BW-peak region, which is what the exclusion analysis lives in. You also mention b-tagging is "to be accounted" via $gg\to tt\bar{q}q$ and $q\bar{q}\to tt\bar{q}g$ subtraction, but with a realistic $b$-tag efficiency $\epsilon \approx 0.7$ and mistag rates, the *composition* of the background after tagging changes, and so can the relative size of the NLO corrections to the tagged sample. One paragraph quantifying (even parametrically) whether the K-factor changes under a simple $\epsilon_b^2$-type tagging weight would close the loop between "we computed a QCD cross section" and "this matters for the Higgs analysis." As it stands, the experimental relevance is asserted, not demonstrated.

6. **Missing related work.** The letter sits in a 2009–2010 moment where NLO top + jets activity was exploding (single-jet $t\bar{t}$ was in progress; top-induced and top-associated studies were starting). The related-work paragraph (first column, page 1) cites only [2,3] (LHC exclusion), [5,7] (the prior NLO studies you're extending), and [25] (parton-shower/dynamical-renormalization idea). You should at least cite and position yourself against the concurrent NLO single-jet top work that you partially reproduce, and comment on why your two-jet correction is *smaller* than what those studies found or expected. The "correction is small" claim is much harder to swallow if the audience remembers, say, O($\alpha_s$) $t\bar{t}$ single-jet K-factors around 1.5 (which is why you *must* reconcile: your NLO is 0.89 of LO, i.e. a *K-factor below 1*, for a process with $gg$ and $q\bar{q}\to t\bar{t}gg$-type real emission — I want the real-emission channel decomposition to see where the negative piece comes from; that is my #2 concern restated concretely).

7. **Formatting/polish.** "top quark pair" vs. "$t\bar{t}$" is inconsistent; "Breach-Wigner" → "Breit–Wigner" (typo in text of Sec. 1). "dynamical renormalization/factorization scale in simulations" (Sec. 1 last sentence, right column) is an odd phrasing for a reader who doesn't share the [25] reference's vocabulary — one clause of expansion. The acknowledgements and PACS are fine.

## What I'd tell the student, bottom line

You have done the hard physical work: a full NLO calculation with a new $t\bar{t} + 4g$ virtual amplitude of notorious complexity, verified against an independent reproduction, with a clean methodological pipeline (OS, dipole subtraction, CutTools, ONEloop, gauge invariance checks, quadruple precision). That is real and I'm proud of it. But the paper as written makes a *stronger* claim than its *uncertainty analysis* supports. The gap to fill is narrow and cheap: (i) per-channel NLO decomposition, (ii) independent $\mu_R$/$\mu_F$ and a $p_T$/kinematic-scale central choice, (iii) a $m_t$ and PDF set variation, (iv) the K-factor in $m_{jj}$ near the expected Higgs mass, and (v) one honest paragraph on why the K-factor < 1 is (or is not) to be trusted given the LO-like jet definition. Do those five and this goes from "interesting first NLO look" to "useful result that the experimental analyses can actually cite." In its current form a careful referee will accept it but with exactly those questions, and point (vi) about citing concurrent single-jet NLO work will be raised as a matter of positioning.

This is a good, publishable letter-quality result; it is not yet the *strong* version of itself.
