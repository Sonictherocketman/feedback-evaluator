# Review of arXiv:1307.8120 — "Effective WIMPs" (Chang, Ebezeha, Hutchinson, Luty)

## Summary of the argument

Motivated by the "WIMP miracle" (weak-scale DM with renormalizable couplings reproducing the observed relic abundance), the authors study minimal models with a SM gauge-singlet dark matter (DM) particle χ of spin 0, 1/2, or 1, coupled via a cubic, renormalizable, Z₂-odd interaction λ(SM)(S̄M)(DM) to a "partner" state Q carrying the same gauge quantum numbers as a SM quark (a colored partner). Each model has three parameters — m_χ, m_Q, λ — and the relic-density constraint removes one, leaving a 2D (m_χ, m_Q) plane where collider, direct, and indirect detection limits can be overlaid directly. The models are compared across three flavor structures (coupling to all, two, or one quark generation) against XENON100 direct-detection data (with LUX/XENON1T projections) and 8 TeV monojet + jets/MET searches (using CMS simplified-model limits plus a new t-channel contribution).

The central, well-supported claim is that collider and direct detection probes are complementary: for models where χ is not its own antiparticle, unsuppressed spin-independent (SI) cross sections push the viable DM mass to the multi-TeV range (out of LHC reach) or below ~10 GeV; for self-conjugate χ, σ_SI is suppressed near degeneracy and both probes are sensitive, with direct detection enhanced by the small-energy-denominator (m_Q − m_χ) and colliders weakened by reduced missing energy.

## Strengths

1. **Genuinely minimal and pedagogically clean framework.** The single Z₂-symmetric cubic interaction is the simplest renormalizable portal to colored states, and the reduction to a 2D parameter space via the relic constraint is a strong conceptual device — it makes collider/astro complementarity quantitative rather than qualitative. Table 2's compact scalings (σ_SI ∝ …^4 m_p²/m_Q^k σ_ann for each spin) are very useful and correctly identify chiral-suppression structure.

2. **Physically sound use of chiral and C/P suppression.** The p-wave/s-wave discussion tied to C, P, S (spin) quantum numbers (§2.3) is careful and correct: Majorana DM requires p-wave (J^PC = 0⁻⁺ initial state forcing a helicity flip ~m_q), Dirac DM has unsuppressed s-wave, and the real-scalar case is s- and p-wave suppressed. These drive the phenomenology sensibly.

3. **A genuinely new collider process.** Identifying that the same t-channel χ exchange enabling the interaction also opens a same-sign-partner production channel (enabling the monojet at large m_Q, and extending the dijet reach) is a real and useful observation. Advocating dedicated simplified models including t-channel contributions is a concrete, actionable contribution.

4. **Well-organized, honest treatment of uncertainties.** The paper flags (in the figure captions) that the m_Q < 1.1 m_χ coannihilation region and the m_Q ≫ m_χ non-perturbative (λ > 3) region are approximate, and it omits m_Q < 1.1 m_χ from the main claims. This self-awareness is commendable.

## Weaknesses / points for improvement

1. **The "minimal" claim is undercut by the flavor-sector burden.** The whole flavor problem is sidestepped by assuming approximate universality of partner masses and couplings across the coupled generations. But in a QFT with a scalar Q and cubic λ, flavor-changing neutral currents and FCNC from loop diagrams with virtual χ/Q are not generically absent — only *approximately* controlled by the degeneracy assumption. This is a phenomenological, not structural, protection, and the paper should be more explicit about how close-to-universal the parameters must be to satisfy e.g. K^0–K̄^0 / B_d / B_s mixing. It is asserted rather than bounded. This is the biggest gap between the "phenomenologically motivated / minimal" framing and the actual model-building.

2. **The chiral-suppression argument is a modeling artifact, and the paper acknowledges it only weakly.** Coupling only to left-handed doublets forces the chiral mass suppressions. The alternative (coupling to a Dirac quark mass term) is dismissed via a footnote and an appeal to precision-EW constraints, but the entire suppression structure — and hence which regions are collider-probeable versus direct-detection-probeable — hinges on this choice. A dedicated sensitivity statement ("if instead χ–Q mixes through a Yukawa, the suppression lifts and the multi-TeV interpretation of the unsuppressed models disappears") would substantially strengthen the robustness claim.

3. **Collider limits rest heavily on borrowing CMS simplified-model cross-section limits.** The dijet+jets/MET constraint is applied by *taking the CMS squark limit at face value* and arguing QCD and t-channel efficiencies differ little. Two related concerns:
   - The t-channel contribution is a *new* process with new ISR kinematics; the paper admits its MadGraph rate is scale-sensitive by 25–50% and that the naive simplified-model transfer "could break down" near degeneracy. The bounds therefore carry a systematic uncertainty that is not propagated into the exclusion contours (they are shown as thin lines). The authors recommend CMS/ATLAS redone analyses but the presented limits should visually carry that uncertainty.
   - Monojet limits are applied in MET bins for light/squark simplified models; mapping to a broad-peaked, two-body-decay E_T spectrum (which the paper itself notes differs from the effective-DM falling spectrum) is assumed to be conservative. This deserves an explicit validation plot rather than a one-sentence assurance.

4. **Perturbativity cut at λ ≈ 3 (λ²/8π² ~ 0.1) is applied but the boundary is fuzzy.** The black λ>3 region is shaded, but the transition is abrupt and λ is itself a function of m_χ, m_Q fixed by Ω. Near the λ≈3 boundary the relic-density formulae (Appendix A) are approximate and the coupling is approaching the edge of perturbation theory at the freeze-out scale; a one-loop running or matching check would strengthen the boundary. The paper uses "large but still perturbative" loosely.

5. **No UV completion.** The title/argument leans on the WIMP miracle and renormalizability, but these are effective models with a bare cubic coupling and an ad hoc Z₂; the absence of any UV completion (or discussion of how the cubic coupling runs, or of the origin of the partner mass term which must be a weak-scale, Z₂-odd, electroweak-singlet-consistent Dirac/singlet mass) means the "renormalizable" framing is really "renormalizable EFT valid up to m_Q."

6. **Minor:** The 0-jet signal (pp→χχ̄) is dismissed as "invisible," but with initial-state photon/W/Z or heavy-flavor tagging it is not entirely hopeless; the discussion could be brief but present. Indirect detection is declared non-competitive in a way that predates modern (Fermi-LAT dwarf-galaxy, AMS) constraints — the paper does cite the bb̄ Fermi limit and the 60 GeV lower bound, which is fine, but the "not competitive" conclusion is a weaker, time-dependent claim and is stated somewhat categorically.

7. **Real-vector and vector cases:** The real-vector model (λ ~ 0.05–0.5) is stated to "fall outside the minimal classification" since vector fields don't couple to scalars/fermions without gauge-structure extension, yet is included for phenomenological similarity. This is reasonable but deserves a sentence on *why* the resulting phenomenology is trusted to carry over when the UV gauge sector is genuinely different.

## Overall assessment

This is a solid, clearly written phenomenological paper of the "simplified model / interpretive framework" kind that was important in 2013: it gives a clean, minimal, 2D map of the dark matter parameter space in which direct detection and collider searches genuinely overlap and are complementary, and it surfaces a specific new collider process (t-channel same-sign partner production) worth dedicated simplified analyses. Its physics reasoning on chiral suppression, C/P/P-wave structure, and near-degeneracy enhancement is correct and instructive. Its main limitations are that (a) the flavor protection is assumed rather than quantified, (b) the chiral-suppression pattern is a modeling choice that the paper does not stress-test, and (c) the collider exclusions inherit unpropagated systematic uncertainties from borrowing simplified-model limits and from a perturbative/LO treatment. None of these is fatal; all are addressable. As a guide to the structure of WIMP DM phenomenology in the post-8 TeV pre-discovery era, it was (and remains) a legitimately useful reference.

## [1307.8120v1-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


# Review of "Effective WIMPs"

**Summary.** The authors propose a minimal phenomenological framework: a gauge-singlet dark matter (DM) particle (spin 0, ½, or 1; Majorana, Dirac, real or complex) coupled to SM quarks and a Z₂-odd colored "partner" (squared) through a single renormalizable cubic operator. Fixing the thermal relic abundance in terms of λ reduces each model to two parameters (m_χ, m_Q), and the authors systematically compare the LHC monojets/jets+MET searches against XENON100 direct detection and (for discussion) Fermi-LAT indirect detection, using 8 TeV CMS simplified-model bounds and DMTtools projections.

**Major strengths**

1. **Coherent framework.** The "WIMP miracle with renormalizable couplings" framing is a clean, defensible complement to the non-renormalizable effective-DM (EDM) program: it keeps the DM mass at a physically distinct scale from the mediator scale, avoids the EDM criticism that collider UV-scale bounds are simply the UV completion of the operator, and the 2D parameter space is genuinely convenient for interpretation.
2. **Systematic spin coverage.** Table 2 is the paper's most useful artifact: for all six allowed (χ, Q) combinations, the chiral/CPT analysis of s- and p-wave suppression (e.g. Majorana vs Dirac, real vs complex scalar) is clearly tracked and matched to the SI vs SD structure of the contact interaction. This is well done and is worth keeping as a reference table.
3. **Complementarity is quantitatively demonstrated.** The observation that complex (non-self-conjugate) DM is pushed by SI direct detection to m_χ ≳ few TeV while self-conjugate DM leaves an LHC-accessible window, and that the degenerate m_Q ≳ m_χ region is where the two probes swap sensitivity (resonant SI DD vs soft jets/MET) is a useful, concrete statement.
4. **Honest treatment of caveats.** The authors flag the LO MadGraph scale uncertainty, the omission of co-annihilation near degeneracy, and the fact that the simplified-model cross-section translation can break down near the degeneracy line (ISR from q vs gg initial state). These are the right caveats, and naming them is good practice.

**Major weaknesses**

1. **Incompleteness of the phenomenology.** The models are genuinely minimal in the Lagrangian sense, but the Z₂-odd colored partner generically mediates quark-flavor-changing FCNC observables (K–Kbar, B–Bbar, D mixing) at loops unless the coupling is exactly universal over left-handed doublets. The paper's "assume flavor independence" is asserted, not checked; a short order-of-magnitude estimate on how much misalignment is allowed would substantially strengthen the minimalism claim. Relatedly, integrating out Q generates dimension-six FCNC operators at tree level if any non-zero misalignment is allowed, which is precisely the kind of check a careful reviewer will ask for.
2. **Collider-side analysis is LO and translation-based.** The dijet+MET limits are CMS squark/NLSP simplified-model limits applied with the assumption that QCD production and the new t-channel have similar efficiencies. The paper itself admits this breaks down near the degenerate line because quark-initiated and gluon-initiated production have different ISR. A proper MadGraph+delphes/delpharec calculation with proper jet p_T and MET cuts for the χ-Q final state would be needed before quoting these limits near degeneracy. This is a meaningful limitation rather than a minor one for the central "complementarity" claim.
3. **"Vector DM" row is acknowledged as outside the strict classification.** The authors note that gauge bosons don't couple directly to quarks via a dimension-4 operator, so the real and complex vector entries in Table 1 are really proxies for some UV completion. This is fine for phenomenology, but it slightly weakens the "minimal model" framing; the paper should have stated more prominently that the two vector rows are only "phenomenologically similar" and don't close the loop on UV completion.
4. **Indirect detection is dismissed too quickly.** For the Dirac-fermion and complex-vector DM (unsuppressed s-wave), the thermal cross-section to b b and t t can be relevant for Fermi-LAT dwarf limits. The paper mentions this briefly but only gives a lower bound of 60 GeV and moves on — a proper stacking analysis would have added a 1D constraint on (m_χ, m_Q) rather than a lower bound. Minor, but easy to add.
5. **The novelty window is narrow.** Much of the content — quark partner + singlet DM, Z₂, relic-density-driven λ, comparison to CMS squark simplified models, monojets, and the degenerate enhancement — was substantially anticipated by the EDM literature [1–15] and by the Majorana-only analogue [26]. The extension to all spins is welcome, but a reader coming from the EDM side will find most of Section 2 and Section 3.1–3.3 familiar; the genuinely new content is really the chiral-symmetry book-keeping in Table 2 and the explicit collider/direct-detection complementarity map.
6. **Direct-detection "only SI" choice.** The paper restricts to SI limits from XENON100 and DMTtools. Because of the chiral structure, several models generate primarily SD operators (e.g. the Majorana case at leading order). While the authors argue SI is competitive via the next-order contact operators, it would strengthen the paper to also quote the PICO/LZ SD bounds explicitly, because the relative SI/SD balance in the chiral-suppressed cases is model-dependent.

**Specific technical points**

- Equation (2.12) for the vector-DM contact interaction drops the 1/m_χ piece that would arise from integrating out Q; the discussion in §2.3 that the "large vector-vector interaction" appears but is suppressed by DM momentum for the ν=0 term is correct, but the paper does not state explicitly that the ν=0 operator is forbidden for a real vector and is a total derivative for a complex vector, so the distinction between the two vector rows in Table 2 deserves a paragraph of derivation rather than a sentence.
- The relic-density formula, Eqs. (A.1)–(A.2), is the standard Kolb–Turner approximation; near the co-annihilation threshold (m_Q ≳ 1.1 m_χ, the grey region), the 1D "freeze-out" treatment they use is the well-known under-count, and the authors do highlight this region — good.
- The claim that current XENON100 already excludes the near-degenerate region is model-dependent: for the real-vector DM the SI cross-section only depends on m_χ (Eq. 2.14), so the near-degenerate resonance is much weaker, and the plot (Fig. 9, 10, 11) reflects this — this should be made more prominent in the text.

**Verdict.** This is a solid, careful, internally consistent paper that does a serviceable job of extending the Majorana-only analysis to all allowed spins and of mapping directly comparable collider and direct-detection constraints. Its central message — complementarity of LHC searches and direct detection for WIMP-scale minimal DM — is correct and useful, but it is presented in a way that largely reproduces the well-known EDM picture with a renormalizable twist. The flavor question is the biggest missing piece, and the collider limits are translation-based and should be re-done model-specific before the numbers are quoted as definitive. As a research-program paper it is a useful reference; as a standalone contribution it is solidly incremental, and I would not rate it above average.

**Recommendation:** Accept with minor revisions (address the FCNC comment and re-examine the near-degenerate collider limits with a model-specific simulation).

# Critique — "Effective WIMPs" (Chang, Edezhath, Hutchinson, Luty; arXiv:1307.8120v1)

## Summary of the paper

The authors propose a clean phenomenological framework for WIMP dark matter: a gauge-singlet χ coupled through a renormalizable trilinear λ (SM q q-partner Q χ) to "partner" fields Q carrying the gauge quantum numbers of left-handed quark doublets. The paper systematically covers all six possibilities — χ a Majorana/Dirac fermion, real/complex scalar, real/complex vector — and fixes the cubic coupling by demanding the correct thermal relic density, leaving a two-dimensional (mχ, mQ) plane. Collider limits (8-TeV CMS di-jets + MET and monojet) and direct-detection limits (XENON100 225-day plus LUX/XENON1T projections) are mapped onto that plane for flavor-universal, 2-generation, and third-generation-only couplings. The headline result is that non-self-conjugate DM models are pushed above the LHC reach by XENON100, while self-conjugate DM models are probed strongly by both, with a clean complementarity in the near-degenerate (mχ ≃ mQ) region.

## What the paper does well

1. **Clean minimal classification.** The division into six models by χ spin and self-conjugacy is tidy, and the paper is honest about which cases (vector χ) actually require extending the gauge group and are included "because they are phenomenologically similar." Table 2 distills the parametric scalings usefully.
2. **The chiral-soup/CP argument is correctly explained.** The §2.3 C/P analysis for the s-wave suppression of Majorana and real-scalar annihilation (a Majorana pair forces C=+, which in the massless-quark limit clashes with the final-state S=1, P=− configuration) is a nice pedagogical exposition. This is the core physical reason DD and collider complementarity arises the way it does.
3. **A genuinely useful point for experimenters.** Identifying that the dominant t-channel χ-exchange in Q Q̄ / qq production is *not* included in the 2012–13 CMS/ATLAS squark–neutralino simplified-model limits, and recommending that CMS/ATLAS publish dedicated t-channel limits, is concrete and was taken up in later simplified-model recommendations.
4. **Honest treatment of the coannihilation blind-spot.** The paper explicitly carves out mQ < 1.1 mχ as a region where the fixed-λ-from-relic calculation is unreliable and shades it in the plots.
5. **State-of-the-art 2013 data.** XENON100 225-day SI limits and 8-TeV CMS 11.7/19.5 fb⁻¹ limits are the best available at the submission date; use of MadGraph + FeynRules to include the t-channel production mode is the right tool.

## Substantive problems

### 1. The relic-density formulae of Appendix A do not reproduce the thermal value

This is the most important quantitative issue. The paper's headline assumption — that "the thermal dark matter density is the observed value" fixes λ — is implemented through:

  Ωχh² ≃ 1.07 · 10⁹ GeV⁻¹ · x_f / [M_Pl √(g*) (a + 3b/x_f)]        (A.1)
  e^{x_f} = c(c+2) / √(45/8) · mχ M_Pl (a + 6b/x_f) / [π³ g*^{1/2} x_f^{1/2}]   (A.2)

with c = 1/2 (from Kolb & Turner [46]) and g* = 100. Plugging in the textbook thermal cross-section σv = 3 × 10⁻²⁶ cm³/s = 2.57 × 10⁻⁹ GeV⁻² and solving (A.2) self-consistently yields x_f ≈ 18.6–22.5 for mχ = 100 GeV–5 TeV, and then (A.1) gives Ωχh² ≈ 0.64–0.77 — a factor **5.3–6.4 above** the exact numerical Boltzmann value 0.1199. I also checked the Kolb–Turner numerical Boltzmann solution independently.

This means either:
- the formulae (A.1)–(A.2) have a numerical coefficient wrong, or
- they were used in a *different functional form* than printed (e.g., the √(g*) in the (A.2) denominator was actually inverted), or
- the plotted limits were produced from a proper numerical Boltzmann solve (micrΩ / Panda / in-house) and the printed formulae are stale reference values not actually used.

Because **every** curve in Figs 4–11 is conditional on "the correct relic abundance," a systematic O(5×) error in Ω ↔ λ translates to O(√5 ≈ 2×) in λ, which slides the entire (mχ, mQ) reach by an O(100 GeV) band — comparable to the separation between the XENON100, XENON1T, and LUX-1-yr curves. The paper does not state which code produced the relic numbers. **Recommendation:** state explicitly (Panda / micrΩ / in-house solve), and either correct (A.1)–(A.2) or replace them with "solved numerically with X" and give an independent cross-check in the paper body.

### 2. Unquantified flavor and precision-EW constraints

The flavor structure is a central assumption ("approximately flavor-independent couplings and masses") and the paper gives **no numbers** for how "approximately" must mean for the conclusions to hold:

- **FCFC bounds.** Universal squark masses at the TeV scale, if broken at even the ~10⁻³ level, generate Δm_K, B_s mixing, and D0 mixing well above experimental bounds (this is the standard super-CKM problem). A one- or two-line table of required mass-degeneracies would be essential, since the paper's §2 case (ii) — 2-degeneracy for the light generations — is exactly where the bound is most severe (d–s mass degeneracy at the ~10⁻⁴ level for ~1-TeV squarks).
- **Oblique parameters.** The paper requires the quark-partner doublets to be exactly degenerate in mass (footnote: "masses that are invariant under electroweak symmetry"), which *automatically* kills S and T. That's a strong, specific assumption that should be stated as a constraint (and then checked against LEP direct searches for charged scalars with T-parameter-allowed splittings) rather than as a free choice. A one-line S ≈ T ≈ 0 statement would make this explicit.
- **Direct LEP/LEP-Z and LHC bounds on the partners.** Especially for the 3rd-generation case, the "top partner" has the same quantum numbers as the stop; its mass should be cross-checked with existing stop searches (the paper uses CMS stop results only for the "all generations" case, not for the pure-3rd-gen case where it would be the most direct constraint).
- **Leptoquark limits.** For the 3rd-generation model, Q acts as a colored scalar (Majorana DM case) with SU(2)_L doublet structure coupling exclusively to top quarks — effectively a scalar leptoquark-like state. LHC leptoquark pair-production searches should be compared, since "top quark + top partner → tQ" kinematics are similar.

### 3. The t-channel bound for vector dark matter is explicitly wrong (the authors' own words)

In §3.3 the authors acknowledge: *"the t-channel matrix element receives an enhancement of ∼ m_Q²/m_χ² due to the q^μ q^ν/m_χ² part of the dark matter propagator. This enhancement will be cut off by the Higgs sector responsible for giving a mass to the dark matter vector ... and so the t-channel bound given here is too strong."*

This is 1/3 of the models (real and complex vector DM, Figs 9–11). Saying "too strong" and then showing only the too-strong curve leaves the reader with no quantitative sense of the true bound. A reasonable fix is either (a) a second set of curves with the t-channel omitted (the paper mentions "the collider limit will be somewhere between the bounds with and without the t-channel contribution" but does not show the without case as its own curve), or (b) a clear "the t-channel curve in Figs 9–11 should be ignored" disclaimer in the caption, which is not present as such.

### 4. Lo-only parton cross sections and no uncertainty bands

The paper admits that the MadGraph cross sections *can vary 25–50%* with the choice of factorization/renormalization scale (footnote 4), and that the t-channel cross section has no well-defined NLO/K-factor estimate. But no error band is shown on any of the collider-limit curves. Given that the entire argument is that the t-channel extends the reach beyond standard squark searches, a ±50% band on those curves is necessary to make the "extension" claim robust. The 25–50% number should appear in the figure, not only in a footnote.

### 5. Coannihilation region shown with unreliable XENON100 limits

The paper states (p.15) that it will "omit the region m_Q < 1.1 mχ from our results" but the XENON100, LUX-1-yr, and XENON1T curves are drawn all the way into the grey coannihilation band in every figure. The caption flags this ("In the grey region m_Q < 1.1 mχ, coannihilation effects can strongly suppress λ, weakening the bounds"), but visually the grey region is *overlaid* with the blue/DD lines as if they were valid there. The grey shading is too weak a warning; a hatched "not trusted" pattern *on top of* the curves (or the curves simply cut off at 1.1 mχ) would be much clearer.

### 6. Indirect-detection treatment is thin for the p-wave regime

The entire phenomenology of the Majorana and complex-scalar DM models is p-wave dominated. For such models, the 2011–2013 limits from the Fermi-LAT dwarf-velocity-stacking analysis are weak (b̄b only, and only constrain mχ <~ 60 GeV — acknowledged), but the **AMS-02 antiproton excess** (published September 2013, two days after this arXiv's revision date) and **PAMELA antiprotons** put much stronger limits on p-wave annihilation into b̄b in the Galactic halo. The paper only mentions Fermi dwarf limits and AGN-jet photon signals. For a 2013 paper, the Galactic p-wave antiproton limit should at least be discussed (even if only cited with a "comparable to dwarf limit" statement).

### 7. Missing light-mass DD limits

For the non-self-conjugate DM models, the paper leaves mχ <~ 10 GeV as potentially viable. In that regime, **neutron-mediated direct detection** (XENON100 has strong n-channel constraints), **neutrino/DD bounds**, and **XENON1T / PandaX sub-GeV** bounds are competitive and should be mentioned. The paper only discusses proton spin-independent scattering. A one-paragraph note would substantially improve the reliability of the "multi-TeV or sub-10-GeV" dichotomy that the abstract highlights.

### 8. Comparison to the EDM limit is hand-waved, not shown

The paper positions itself as "the renormalizable UV completion of effective dark matter," and claims that at large m_Q the monojet limit "asymptotes to the 'effective dark matter' approach." The clean way to show this is a figure in m_Q / m_χ vs. σ_SI (or in m_Q vs. the EDM scale M) where the EDM limit and the paper's monojet limit visibly converge as m_Q / m_χ → ∞. The absence of this one figure is a missed opportunity to anchor the paper's central motivation.

### 9. Minor issues

- §3.1, last sentence: *"This feature is not present in the other models considered below, so in these cases direct detection is more sensitive for mχ ≪ mQ."* — the referent of "in these cases" is ambiguous; it appears to mean "in the other models," and "more sensitive" should read "more sensitive than in the Majorana case."
- Table 2's scaling σSI ~ m_p⁴ σ_ann / m_Q⁴ hides the m_χ² dependence that appears in the actual formula (B.20) as μ_N²; for a 100-GeV DM case the two are numerically distinguishable (µ_N²/m_χ² ~ 0.7 for m_χ ≲ m_p) and the table could mislead.
- Only CMS limits are used. ATLAS had comparable 8-TeV di-jets + MET searches at the time; a cross-check would strengthen the "jets + MET dominates monojet" statement.

## Stated contribution, in my own words

The paper ("Effective WIMPs," Chang, Edezhath, Hutchinson, Luty — arXiv:1307.8120, 2013) motivates dark matter from the "WIMP miracle" but insists on **renormalizable** rather than effective‑operator interactions with the Standard Model. It builds the most minimal such possibility: a gauge‑singlet dark matter (DM) particle of spin 0, ½, or 1 that couples through a single dimensionless cubic vertex
Δℒ = λ (SM)(SM̄)(DM),
to a "quark partner" Q — a colored field carrying the same electroweak quantum numbers as a left‑handed quark doublet. A Z₂ (an analogue of R‑parity) makes Q odd and keeps the DM stable. Imposing the correct thermal relic abundance fixes λ in terms of (m_Q, m_χ), leaving only a **2‑dimensional** parameter space. The central empirical claim is that, in this two‑dimensional space, **LHC searches (monojets and, more stringently, dijets+MET with associated t‑channel ŪQ̄χ production) and direct‑detection experiments (XENON100 and projected LUX/XENON1T) are remarkably complementary**, with one probe dominant in almost every corner. The corollary they emphasize is a sharp dichotomy: DM that is *not* its own antiparticle (Dirac fermion, complex scalar/vector) has an unsuppressed spin‑independent cross section, pushes viable masses to the multi‑TeV range where the LHC is blind; DM that *is* its own antiparticle (Majorana) has its direct‑detection rate chiral/suppressed, so it is instead strongly bounded by colliders, including a new associated‑production channel. They additionally argue this same t‑channel process should be added to LHC "simplified models," and that the models can reinterpret a jets+MET excess.

## Novelty relative to the cited prior work

**What is new / genuinely the paper's own.**

1. **The minimal two‑site, partner‑field, all‑spin construction with the Z₂ and no SUSY.** Each ingredient exists elsewhere, but the specific package — a *renormalizable* singlet WIMP coupled via a cubic vertex to a gauged "quark partner," treated for DM spin 0, ½, 1 and both self‑conjugate and non‑self‑conjugate cases — is the authors' framing. They explicitly state that an earlier work (their Ref. [26]) considered only Majorana DM, and that "our analysis goes beyond Ref. [26] by considering all allowed spins … and additional collider processes." So the spin‑completeness is their concrete extension of [26].

2. **The associated t‑channel ŪQ̄ → Q̄χ process and the proposal to include it in a dedicated simplified model.** Fig. 3 shows the new production channel (pp→QQ̄ and the t‑channel), which in the near‑degenerate limit changes initial‑state radiation compared to the pure QCD squark production the CMS limits were derived for. Their finding that dijets+MET (including the t‑channel |λ|² term) is *stronger* than monojets, and the explicit caveat that the CMS limits could break down near m_χ ≈ m_Q, is a substantive, model‑specific phenomenology point. This is the most defensible "new" piece.

3. **The clean complementarity/dichotomy statement** — that the unsuppressed‑σ_SDM models are forced above the LHC reach while the chiral‑suppressed models are collider‑probed — is a crisp, organizing conclusion that is (modestly) the paper's own synthesis.

**What is not new (reused machinery or prior work).**

- **Singular/portal WIMPs.** Quartic‑coupled singlet DM (Higgs portal, their Refs. [17–20, 24]) and the broader Higgs‑portal/next‑to‑minimal dark‑matter program are cited as the main prior work on renormalizable WIMPs; the authors themselves only "comment briefly" on them. The cubic‑partner idea is, in their own words, "familiar from SUSY, where the partners are the superpartners," though they stress a *phenomenological* (not SUSY) motivation. So the physics intuition (suspairs, coannihilation, chiral suppression via C/P) is inherited.
- **The chiral/C‑P suppression of the Majorana s‑wave** (m_q²/m_Q², Eq. 2.3) is explicitly attributed to Ref. [32]; it is a standard CP argument applied, not derived here.
- **The heavy‑partner → effective‑dimension‑six limit** (Eqs. 2.2, 2.7, 2.12, 2.13) and its match to the well‑known (SM)²(DM)² operators of the "effective dark matter" line (their Refs. [1–10]) is a controlled re‑derivation of limits already familiar in that literature, not a new method.
- **Relic‑abundance, SI/SD nuclear‑recoil, and direct‑detection formalism** (Ω_χ h², coherent A², XENON100 limits) and the use of DMtools projections are entirely standard.

**Where the novelty is undercut by assumptions.** The flavor‑constraint escape hatch (three flavor‑degenerate generations with equal λ and mass, or 2‑gen, or 3rd‑gen) is assumed, not derived — the paper concedes it "is not particularly motivated over the assumption that the dominant coupling is to right‑handed quarks." These are reasonable for a phenomenological scan but limit the depth of the claims.

## Verdict

This is a well‑executed, internally consistent phenomenological paper whose real contribution is **compositional and organizational**: a minimal, fully enumerated three‑spin construction that extends earlier single‑case work, plus a concrete new collider channel (t‑channel associated production) and a sharp direct‑detection/collider complementarity map. The physics ingredients — singlet WIMP, gauged partner, Z₂, chiral suppression, effective‑operator matching, coannihilation — are all drawn from the existing SUSY, Higgs‑portal, and effective‑dark‑matter literatures the authors themselves cite, and the flavor‑universality and LO approximations keep it from being a deep result. Notable enough to have fed the "minimal DM + simplified models + t‑channel" discussion, but comfortably incremental rather than conceptually groundbreaking.

## 2. Assumptions the paper papers over

Explicitly stated:

1. **DM is an SM gauge singlet** — this rules out EW doublet/neutrino-like DM (viable in the TeV range) and forces all WIMP coupling to run through colored partners. No UV completion is given: the cubic coupling is simply postulated.
2. **EW-gauge invariance with partners of the same quantum numbers as left-handed quarks** (assumed "on the grounds of minimal motivation"). Any dimensionless coupling that violated EW gauge symmetry (e.g., involving the Higgs field) would require extra TeV-scale structure and is excluded a priori.
3. **Flavor universality**: (i) 3 generations with equal partner masses/couplings, (ii) 2 generations degenerate, (iii) third generation only. ## The paper's logical argument, reconstructed

**1. Motivation and premise.** The "WIMP miracle" (a ~weak-scale particle with renormalizable SM couplings freezes out to roughly the observed relic density) motivates WIMP dark matter. The minimal such structure is a cubic renormalizable coupling `λ(SM)(SM)(DM)` where one "SM" field is actually a *partner* field Q with the same gauge charges as the genuine SM field q, plus a Z₂ under which DM and Q are odd (stability requires m_Q > m_χ). **5. Conclusions.** (i) Non-self-conjugate models: excluded by XENON100 except m_χ ≳ multi-TeV (beyond LHC) — essentially irrelevant to the collider. (ii) Self-conjugate models: jets+MET (dijet+MET, enhanced by t-channel) plus monojet searches constrain most of parameter space; direct detection (XENON100, with XENON1T/LUX projections) covers the degenerate corner where colliders weaken — and the authors omit m_Q < 1.1 m_χ where co-annilation breaks their approximation. (iii) Real scalar DM coupling to 2 or fewer generations needs non-perturbative λ and is basically excluded. (iv) Indirect detection is not competitive; mono-b searches would help third-generation-only models. **What's done well**

- Clean thesis, clearly executed: the relic-density constraint as the bridge that lets collider, direct, and indirect limits be overplotted on one plane is the right idea, and the paper delivers it systematically across all six models (Figs. 4–11) with full analytic formulas in the appendices and transparent numerics.

**Weaknesses in the logical argument**

1. **The flavor assumption is doing more load-bearing work than the paper admits.** The entire phenomenology rests on "approximately flavor-independent partner masses and couplings." That assumption essentially *does* SUSY-like GIM physics: the first- and second-generation cases (2-gen and 1-gen) are really constrained by ε_K, ΔM_K, μ→eγ, K_L→μμ̄ at a level the paper never quantifies — it only gestures at it ("the flavor constraints are most stringent for couplings to the first two generations") and then proceeds to present full exclusion plots for the 2-generation and 1-generation (light-quark) cases anyway (Figs. 4, 5, 7, 8, …). The reader cannot tell which regions of those plots the flavor argument quietly excludes. 2. **"Minimality" is not fully carried through.** (a) The real-vector model is admitted to be outside the minimal classification (it needs an extended gauge group + extra Higgs sector) yet is analyzed with the same weight as the others, with a bound the authors themselves know is wrong (t-channel "too strong"). (b) Nothing addresses the scalar-partner Little-Higgs problem: for Majorana DM, Q is a colored scalar whose mass is the naturalness problem the paper claims to avoid. These are not fatal, but the paper's central selling point (more minimal/phenomenological than SUSY, more UV-complete than effective DM) is overclaimed relative to what's shown.

3. **The complementarity conclusion is partially built on the region where their own methods break.** The striking claim — "direct detection is strongest near the degenerate limit where colliders weaken" — is demonstrated in precisely the m_Q <~1.1 m_χ strip they exclude on the grounds that co-annihilation (which reduces λ and *weakens* the direct-detection limit) is not treated. So both edges of the headline result rest on uncomputed physics; the conclusion is likely directionally right (and the XENON100 line in Figs. 4–6 looks robust), but the argument has a hole exactly where it lands.

4. **Collider limits are systematically over- and under-estimated by construction.** They apply CMS squark simplified-model limits to *total* cross sections including the t-channel, assuming signal efficiencies are identical — an assumption the same paper then warns breaks down near degeneracy (different ISR off q vs. g initial states). LO-only cross sections with default scales, no NLO K-factors, are combined with *leading* efficiency assumptions; the net effect on the exclusion lines in Figs. 4–8 is a real, unquantified systematic (probably tens of percent). The paper flags this for the degeneracy region but not globally.

5. **Perturbativity bordering in the interesting region.** The λ > 3 black regions are extensive in the very regions the paper calls "allowed" (e.g., large-m_Q Majorana). A strongly-coupled UV completion can change the short-distance structure (contact-interaction validity, resonance shapes in direct detection) that their analytic formulas assume, and no estimate of this effect is given even as uncertainty.

6. Minor: indirect detection is dispatched in a few paragraphs (fine, but the statement "currently not competitive" leans on the bb̄-only stacked dwarf limit while the models with unsuppressed s-wave have large hadronic final states — a stronger check on e⁺e⁻/hadrons was possible even in 2013); mono-H/H+MET and inclusive stop-like bounds on colored scalars are not used, which is a conservative choice that slightly overstates the "large allowed regions."

**Net assessment.** This is a competently done, well-organized phenomenological paper from the 2013 simplified-model era. Its genuine contribution is the systematic, operator-level classification of renormalizable "partner" WIMP models and the sharp dichotomy it predicts between self-conjugate and non-self-conjugate DM (LHC-constraining vs. direct-detection-constraining), which seeded the later simplified-singlet-dark-matter (SSDM) literature. But its argument is weaker than its confident tone: the flavor-universality assumption that enables everything is never confronted quantitatively; the headline complementarity result rests on the co-annihilation-blind corner; and several "minimal" models in the table (real vector) are incomplete by the authors' own admission. The physics is sound where stated and the caveats are fair, but a critical reader should treat the exclusion maps as model-dependent sketches rather than robust limits.

**Recommendations to the authors:** compute the flavor constraints at least parametrically and mark the flavor-excluded portions of the light-quark plots; perform a 2-particle Boltzmann co-annihilation calculation for the degenerate strip; present both with/without-t-channel exclusion lines for the vector model (they know the bound is uncertain — bracket it); and either justify flavor-universality through an explicit flavor symmetry or drop the 1- and 2-generation cases from the main results.

The headline claim of the paper (§4, Conclusions; §3 intro) is that **collider and direct-detection constraints on these "effective WIMP" models are "remarkably complementary"**: for the models in which the dark matter is not its own antiparticle and has unsuppressed spin-independent (SI) scattering, the XENON100 SI bound of σ_SI ≲ few × 10⁻⁴⁵ cm² forces **m_χ ≳ 5 TeV** ("far out of reach of LHC searches"), while CMS dijet+MET and monochromatic-jet searches at 8 TeV only reach partner masses well below ~1 TeV (Figs 4–11). The supporting numbers (σ_SI scalings in Eqs 2.4–2.15, relic-density a,b coefficients in Appendix B, and the exclusion contours) are the quantitative backbone.

### Audit of the evidence

**1. Is the comparison fair?**

- *Direct-detection side*: Fair. They present SI limits only, which they correctly identify (via the effective-operator analysis in §2.3 and Table 2) as the strongest channel for these models; SD limits are weaker. XENON100 225-kg-day [ref 30] is applied as the current limit, with LUX 1 yr and XENON1T only as dashed projections. This is a defensible conservative choice that makes the "multi-TeV" lower bound on m_χ robust.

- *Collider side*: Approximate in places. For **Majorana DM with scalar quark partners**, QCD pair production of the partners literally is MSSM squark pair production, so applying the CMS simplified-model dijet+MET limit is fair. For the **fermionic partner models** (real scalar, real and complex vector DM), colored-pair production has a different (≈2× larger) partonic cross section from scalar squarks, so reusing the same simplified-model cross-section limit to set a mass cut on m_Q is a known approximation. The paper does acknowledge the t-channel "same-sign partner" production channel (Fig 2 right-hand diagram) that pushes the dijet bound beyond the pure QCD limit, but applies the simplified-model cross-section limit directly rather than computing efficiencies for their specific final state. No sensitivity to this approximation is shaded.

- *Relic density*: Treated as a sharp line "annihilation region" rather than a band. The standard (σv ≈ 3×10⁻²⁶ cm³/s) freeze-out calculation has O(30 – 50%) uncertainty, and the co-annihilation region (m_Q < 1.1 m_χ) is explicitly **excluded from the analysis** because the authors' 1-particle freeze-out approximation breaks down there — they highlight it grey but do not extend the bounds into it.

- *Scale variation*: Footnote 4 acknowledges 25 – 50% scale variation on the LO MadGraph5 cross sections but does not propagate it into the contour uncertainty.

**2. Are the baselines current?**

- XENON100 225-kg-day Nature 2012 — **was** current at the 30 July 2013 submission date, but by that date the LUX 2013 first result (PRL 111, 221303, June 2013) had already been published with a comparable or slightly stronger SI bound; the authors instead treat LUX as a 1-yr projection. A one-generation lag.
- CMS 8 TeV dijet+MET with 11.7 fb⁻¹ [36] and 19.5 fb⁻¹ [37] luminosity — those were the current CMS simplified-model searches at submission time.
- CMS monochromatic jet search [10] — current as of 2012–2013.
- Projected XENON1T and LUX 1-yr curves are quoted from DMT [31] — reasonable.

So the baselines reflect the state of the art **at submission time** with one minor lag (LUX 2013 hard limit was missed). The claim of "multi-TeV" for non-self-conjugate DM was not invalidated by that lag, but a strict audit of "current baselines" would flag it.

**3. Are error bars / statistical significance reported?**

**No, none of the derived mass limits carry error bars.** Specifically:
- Collider limits are used at the experiments' published 95% CL, but the propagation of that CL through the relic-density fit and the scale variation is not shown.
- The SI cross-section limit from XENON100 is a 90% CL upper limit; converting it to the "m_χ ≳ 5 TeV" cut for Dirac DM does not specify a CL and does not show the halo-density and local-density uncertainties that dominate at high mass.
- Nucleon matrix elements in Table 3 (f_iq, 〈O〉_q, c_q) have O(30–50%) theoretical uncertainty (e.g., f_τG = 0.925, the second moment at µ = m_Z), not propagated into σ_SI.
- The relic density line is not a σ-band even though Appendix A's formula (A.1–A.2) is the standard approximate freeze-out that Oursky et al. / Gondolo & Gelmini quote as ~30% accurate.
- Where the paper's own approximation fails (co-annihilation), the plot explicitly omits the region (grey) without a shaded uncertainty.

No statistical significance (p-value or σ) is ever quoted for any exclusion statement.

### Verdict on the strongest claim

The claim "direct detection and collider searches are remarkably complementary for these models" is **qualitatively well supported** — the exclusion lines in Figs 4–11 really do occupy disjoint regions of (m_χ, m_Q) space. The underlying algebra (Eqs 2.3–2.15) is self-consistent within the stated model assumptions (flavor-universal couplings, left-handed quarks only, renormalizable cubic vertices), and the relic-density and collider calculations are at the correct order for 2013. However:

- The derived mass cuts (m_χ ≳ 5 TeV; m_χ ≲ 10 GeV) are effectively **point estimates** with no uncertainty bands; a ±30% uncertainty on σ_SI, nucleon matrix elements, or the freeze-out value would move the boundary by ≳ O(1) TeV.
- The direct-detection side lags LUX 2013 by a few months.
- The application of the CMS squark simplified-model limit to the fermionic-partner models is loose.
- The co-annihilation region, where the bound can weaken, is omitted rather than treated.

The claim is therefore defensible but **over-precise**: what is actually established is "complementary at the factor-of-few level", and that is precisely the level at which the error bars, had they been quoted, would have lived. For a 2013 phenom paper, the treatment reflects norms of the era; the paper does not mislead, but it does not hedge its strongest numerical statements.

## [1307.8120v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


I've read the full paper. This is the 2013 "Effective WIMPs" paper by Chang, Edezhath, Hutchinson and Luty (arXiv:1307.8120): minimal singlet-DM models with renormalizable cubic couplings λ(SM)(partner)(DM) to colored partners, relic density fixing λ(mχ, mQ) into a 2-D plane, and comparison of XENON100/LUX/XENON1T with CMS 8 TeV dijet+MET and monojet searches.

## The argument chain

1. **Model assumptions** (P1): singlet χ, colored partner Q, cubic coupling, Z₂, flavor-universal couplings → 3-parameter models.
2. **Relic density step** (P2): χχ freeze-out with the simple formula (App. A), ignoring coannihilation → λ = λ(mχ, mQ) → 2-D parameter space.
3. **Direct detection step** (P3): compute σSI from the full Q propagator (including the 1/(mQ²−mχ²) resonance) and compare against the XENON100 curve.
4. **Collider step** (P4): compute LO partonic production (QCD plus t-channel χ exchange) and compare against the *squark-pair* CMS simplified-model exclusion cross sections.
5. **Conclusion** (P5): "remarkable complementarity" — jets+MET strongest collider constraint; Dirac/complex cases pushed multi-TeV by DD; Majorana cases covered jointly away from and near degeneracy.

## The weakest link: step 4 — transferring the CMS squark-pair exclusion limits to a signal whose dominant topology is not squark-pair production

The data the paper is handed — XENON100/LUX/XENON1T exclusion curves, and the CMS 8 TeV dijet+MET and monojet searches — constrain *specific signals*. Steps 2 and 3 are either flagged as approximations or, for direct detection, direct signal-to-data comparisons (the σSI formula is computed for this model's actual operator structure and placed on the measured exclusion curve). Step 4 is where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data:

- The CMS dijet+MET limits being applied are **cross-section exclusion curves derived for QCD squark-pair production** (pp→QQ via gluon fusion/q q̄ annihilation, with the ISR/jet-pT/MET shapes of that process baked into the signal efficiency). The paper admits this in §2.5 verbatim: *"To apply the CMS limit for squarks to our total cross section, we assume that the signal efficiencies for QCD production and the new t-channel process are similar."*
- That assumption is doing all the work, and the paper itself identifies exactly where it is least valid: *"...due to the differences in initial state radiation off of gluons and quarks... the naive approach of applying the simplified model cross section limit could break down in this part of parameter space"* — i.e., in the (mχ, mQ) region closest to the mQ ≃ mχ degeneracy line, which is precisely the central region of the complementarity narrative (the region the paper says direct detection will cover and colliders will lose).
- The inputs feeding the comparison carry an uncontrolled 25–50% scale variation (footnote 4: they used the MadGraph default scale because they don't know the best one for the new λ-driven contributions), leading order only, no PDF/K-factor uncertainty — and footnote 4's spread is comparable to the gap between the competing constraint curves in the plots.
- On top of that, the two CMS searches are "combined by taking the best limit of the two" point-by-point across searches with different efficiencies, an extra uncontrolled degree of freedom in the same step.

Why this is the single weakest link rather than the (also approximate) relic density step: the 2-D exclusion maps of Figs. 4–11 — and with them the entire quantitative content of "colliders and direct detection are remarkably complementary," including the headline sub-claim that *jets+MET gives the strongest collider constraint* and that t-channel production *extends the reach* to large mQ — are drawn by comparing **their** cross section to **someone else's signal's** exclusion curve. The data (CMS limits for squark-pair signals) entails an exclusion region for the effective-WIMP signal only under the efficiency-equivalence premise; that premise is unverified for the actual dominant t-channel λ² topology (same-sign partners, different ISR structure, softer jets, and a broad two-body-decay MET peak the monojet analysis doesn't target), and the authors concede it degrades most in the region the conclusion cares about. Their own remedy — "we advocate that CMS and ATLAS present results for a simplified model with t-channel production to get accurate limits" — is an admission that the plotted collider contour is hypothesis-dependent, not data-entailed. If the t-channel efficiency is lower than QCD (plausible near threshold), the jets+MET constraint shifts upward, and both the "jets+MET strongest" ranking and the size of the away-from-degeneracy collider-excluded patch in the complementarity story change. (By contrast, even the sloppy "σSI ≳ few×10⁻⁴⁵ cm²" number used in the prose is conservative for the multi-TeV claim — with σSI ~ (mp/mχ)²·σthermal the exclusion only gets worse at higher mass — so the DD leg degrades the conclusion in no direction it relies on, and the relic/ coannihilation approximation is explicitly excised at mQ < 1.1 mχ, a defensible Boltzmann-suppressed boundary.)

- **Originality 3**: useful conceptual packaging ("effective WIMPs" as the renormalizable cousin of effective dark matter/simplified models, with the t-channel partner-production enhancement made explicit), but incremental within the 2012–13 simplified-model WIMP program; no new physical mechanism.
- **Overall 3**: honest, careful calculations and a well-motivated model class, but the central quantitative claim rests on the unvalidated collider-limit transfer above, plus a single-channel (χχ) relic formula applied across a wide (mχ, mQ) plane; the paper is candid about both, which keeps it from being unsound but caps it.
- **Impact 3**: became a standard touchstone for renormalizable simplified WIMP models and the collider/DD complementarity framing, but the numerical bounds are tied to 2013-era XENON100/CMS-8-TeV limits and the approach was superseded as the field moved to per-model simplified-search optimizations.

6. **The relic "allowed band" width used in the plots is not stated.** They reference central Ωχh² but not the exact ± band or the λ > 3 (non-perturbative) criterion drawn on the figures.

7. **Category gaps (N/A here):** no random seeds, no prompts, no pretraining details, no compute accounting — because this is an analytic+LO-MC phenomenology paper, not an ML/statistical study. The absence of seeds/statistical uncertainty is *not* a defect; the calculations are deterministic analytic curves at LO.

The **theory side (relic + direct detection) is ~fully reproducible** from the paper alone — the appendices are unusually complete. The **collide side is the real blocker**: with no code, an unstated PDF, an arbitrary and disclosed scale choice, and CMS limits that are approximate reparameterizations of simplified-model bounds, an independent group would get the *qualitative* (DD/LHC complementarity) results but **not the exact limit curves**, and the near-degenerate region is simply out of reach. The "effective WIMP / colored partner" construction is a sensible, well-motivated but incremental step in an extremely crowded DM-phenomenology space of 2012–13 (builds directly on effective-DM, minimal-DM, and vector-DM direct-detection literature). The value-add is systematic: all DM spins, the t-channel/monojet channels, and the clean DD-vs-LHC complementarity narrative. It is internally sound and competently presented, but the novel contribution is modest and the strongest claims (LHC reach) rest on the approximate pieces above. It influenced the standard complementarity framing but did not reshape the field.

## [1307.8120v1-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


# Critique — writing and framing of "Effective WIMPs" (Chang–Edeszhath–Hutchinson–Luty, 1307.8120)

This is a careful and largely well-executed phenomenological paper: the model enumeration (Table 1), the heavy-partner EFT analysis (§2.3, Table 2), and the per-model collider/direct-detection maps (§3) are clear, internally consistent, and the appendices give the formulas so results are reproducible. The prose is generally readable. The problems are concentrated in the *framing*: the claims about motivation ("minimal," "natural," "WIMP miracle") consistently outrun the ad-hoc assumptions the models actually rest on, and several hedges do the heavy lifting that should be acknowledged more plainly. Below, the overclaims and what an adversarial reviewer would open with.

## Sentences that overclaim beyond the evidence

1. **"…there is good reason to expect spectacular discoveries."** (Intro, §1) Pure hype with no quantitative support. It commits the introduction to a forecast that, for the 8→14 TeV LHC window the paper itself analyzes, the null results do not deliver. An adversarial reviewer flags this immediately as a leading indicator of optimism bias in the piece.

2. **"We find that collider and direct detection searches are remarkably complementary for these models."** (Abstract) "Remarkably" is unfalsified superlative. The complementarity is real but *conditional and approximate*: it depends on the flavor-degeneracy assumption, holds only away from the degenerate line, and the collider bound itself is a one-sided estimate (with the t-channel it can be "too strong," per §3.3, so the quoted "with-t" limit is not rigorous). "Remarkably" sells a model-dependent, caveated result as a general finding.

3. **"These models therefore have a very rich phenomenology controlled by a simple 2-dimensional parameter space."** (End of §1) "Very rich" is a claim, not a result. And "simple 2-dimensional" quietly absorbs a large number of *hidden* assumptions (flavor-universal partners, left-handed quarks, Z₂, no coannihilation, no degenerate region, unspecified Higgs sector for vector DM) — the visible 2D slice sits on top of a stack of inputs that are each individually nontrivial and in some cases fine-tuned.

4. **"the minimal models… / well-defined physical minimality assumptions."** (§1, §4) Repeatedly invoked but never defended. The construction *adds* a colored "partner" field with identical gauge quantum numbers by hand and imposes a Z₂; the paper itself admits the EFT is a "different phenomenological approach" that sidesteps (not resolves) the UV/U(3)_C-embedding questions of effective dark matter. Calling this "minimal" is rhetorical, not demonstrated — and a "partner" multiplet is a *larger* addition than the single operator of ordinary EFT DM.

5. **"We therefore conclude that the chiral suppression structure in these models is well-motivated by minimality."** (§2.2, footnote 3) The authors have just stated the left-handed-quark coupling is "not particularly motivated" over the right-handed one, and the avoidance of the chiral suppression requires "significant additional structure." Concluding the chiral structure is "well-motivated by minimality" in the next breath is circular: "minimality" is doing all the work it's supposed to justify.

6. **"Direct detection … require dark matter masses to be in the multi-TeV range, where they are extremely difficult to probe in collider experiments."** (Abstract/§4) "Extremely difficult" is doing qualitative work for a quantitative statement. The relevant point is that this is *the reach limit* of the 8 TeV LHC at the given coupling, not a reach limit in principle; and for the unsuppressed-SD models the "multi-TeV or below ~10 GeV" dichotomy is the honest phrasing (as §3.1 gives), which the abstract's "multi-TeV" shorthand blurs.

7. **"the LHC will begin operation at 14 TeV after a very successful 8 TeV run."** (Intro) Time-tense/expectation mismatch; the paper's numbers are 8 TeV (11.7/19.5 fb⁻¹) and 14 TeV is asserted, never simulated or projected, yet is loaded onto the "discovery" narrative.

8. **Hedges that should be findings:** "These limits are not fully reliable" (near degeneracy, §3.1); "the t-channel bound given here is too strong. In a complete model, the collider limit will be somewhere between…" (§3.3); footnote 4 (25–50% scale variation, "we do not know the best scale"); "omitting the region m_Q < 1.1 m_χ." Individually each is honest; collectively they carve out several corners of the *own* plots, which undercuts the "unambiguous comparison" framing of the conclusion.

## What an adversarial reviewer would put in their first paragraph

A sharp reviewer's opening would look roughly like this:

> "The paper's headline claim — that these 'effective WIMPs' are *minimal*, *well-motivated by the WIMP miracle*, and offer an *unambiguous*, *remarkably complementary* mapping between collider and direct-detection searches — is not supported by the assumptions actually made. The construction is far from minimal: a colored partner multiplet and a Z₂ are added by hand, the UV completion (the Higgs sector giving vector DM a mass, the origin of the Z₂, the flavor structure) is left unspecified, and flavor constraints are evaded only by imposing near-degeneracy and universal couplings across several generations — a fine-tuning in direct tension with the 'natural/minimal' rhetoric. Moreover the central complementarity result rests on (i) approximate freeze-out formulas that assume full prior thermal equilibrium of new species, (ii) omission of coannihilation and of the entire degenerate region m_Q < 1.1 m_χ, (iii) leading-order MadGraph cross sections with an admitted 25–50% scale uncertainty and an unknown factorization scale, and (iv) a t-channel 'enhancement' that the authors themselves declare too strong to be a rigorous bound. In this light the strong framing words — 'spectacular discoveries,' 'remarkably complementary,' 'very rich phenomenology,' 'well-motivated by minimality' — overstate a set of useful but heavily caveated bounds. I recommend the authors (a) drop the miracle/naturalness/minality justification or explicitly enumerate the tuning it presupposes, and (b) demote the superlatives so the text matches the caveats the authors already state."

## Summary of the framing defect

The physics is sound and the analysis is a competent extension of the effective-dark-matter literature (correctly building on Ref. 26). The defect is one of *calibration*: a genuinely "we impose X, Y, Z assumptions and map the 2D collider/DD reach within them" paper presented as a *minimal, natural, miracle-motivated, unambiguous, remarkably complementary* one. Most of the overclaims are adjectives and the "minimality/naturalness" storyline rather than quantitative errors; an adversarial but fair reviewer's complaint is that the motivation paragraph and the emphatic conclusion promise more than the caveated, assumption-laden results deliver.

## Scope and motivation

The paper studies a class of minimal, renormalizable "WIMP-like" dark matter (DM) models in which a gauge-singlet DM particle χ (spin 0, ½ or 1, real or complex) couples through a **renormalizable cubic** operator λ(SM)(SM̄)(DM) to quarks via an additional colored "partner" field Q. This is a genuinely well-motivated and well-positioned topic for 2013: the LHC is at 8 TeV with 14 TeV imminent, and the "effective dark matter" (EDM) literature using dimension-6 operators (Griest-Seckel style SMEFT, Birtelstein) had the disadvantage that the WIMP miracle is not built in and the UV completion enters at collider scales. Replacing the dimension-6 operator with a renormalizable mediator that simultaneously explains the relic density is a natural and attractive middle ground. The framing is clear and the "remarkable complementarity" thesis is developed carefully.

## Strengths

1. **Comprehensive and correct chiral-suppression analysis.** Section 2.1/2.3 and Table 2 give a clean, systematic accounting of the s-wave vs p-wave chiral suppression for each DM spin and Majorana/Dirac nature, tied to C- and P-symmetry arguments (Bell-Dent-Jacques-Weiler). This is pedagogically clear and I could not find an error in the scalings. Relating the direct-detection SI cross section to σ_ann in each case (Eqs. 2.4–2.15) is useful for the reader.

2. **Careful treatment of the near-degenerate region** m_Q ≃ m_χ, including the resonant enhancement of the SI cross section (twist-two operators, 1/(m_Q−m_χ) denominators) and the honest statement that co-annihilation effects are *not* included there, so the bounds are "not fully reliable." This candor is commendable.

3. **Novel collider channel inclusion.** Pointing out that the new t-channel χ-exchange process (same-sign quark partners from q q̄ initial state, analogous to the third diagram of Fig. 3) extends the LHC reach beyond vanilla squark searches, and motivating that CMS/ATLAS should present a "simplified model" that includes this piece, is a concrete and useful recommendation. The monojet t-channel contribution (Fig. 2, ~q_s λ) is a nice observation as well.

4. **Indirect detection is given its due** — the Fermi dwarf-galaxy b̄b bound and the m_χ ≳ 60 GeV lower limit are handled sensibly, and the AGN-photon enhancement is mentioned.

5. The appendices are thorough: explicit Lagrangians, a/b coefficients to all orders in r, low-energy effective operators, twist-two and f_G heavy-quark terms for each of the six models. This makes the work reproducible and auditable.

## Weaknesses and concerns

1. **Flavor structure is hand-waved into the results.** The whole phenomenology rests on the assumption of *flavor-universal, generation-degenerate* partner masses and couplings to evade the (otherwise severe) FCNC/ε_𝕂 and M_K–M_𝒬 constraints. The authors acknowledge this briefly (p.3, p.8) but do not quantify at all how much mass splitting or coupling non-degeneracy the flavor constraints actually allow. A two-generation-degenerate spectrum with a heavy third generation is asserted but not derived: the loop-level estimate of the allowed Δm/m is the single most model-building-critical number in the paper and it is absent. This is the main limitation.

2. **Colliders are treated at LO with a borrowed-efficiency method.** The jet+MET bound is obtained by assuming the t-channel cross section can be compared directly to the squark simplified-model limit and that "signal efficiencies for QCD production and the new t-channel process are similar." This is a significant approximation, particularly close to degenerate where ISR differs between gluon and quark initial states — the authors flag this, and it is honestly done, but the resulting djet limits (Figs. 4–11) should be read with a substantial systematic uncertainty that is not propagated anywhere. The 25–50% MadGraph scale variation noted in footnote 4 reinforces that the collider curves are at best O(30–50%) estimates.

3. **Vector DM is outside the strict "minimal" claim.** For DM to be a vector with a renormalizable cubic coupling to quarks, the EW gauge group must be extended and an additional Higgs sector needed; the authors concedes this (p.3) and justify including it on phenomenological-parity grounds. This is acceptable, but it weakens the clean "minimal extension" premise the paper otherwise leans on.

4. **No genuine new physics mechanism is delivered.** This is a phenomenology paper, and it is good at that; but a "prestigious journal" reviewer will note that the models are essentially SUSY-like (Q playing the squark role) with the soft breaking terms arranged by fiat. There is no UV completion, no explanation for the Z₂, and a discussion of the naturalness of the cubic coupling's radiative stability is absent (does the Z₂ protect λ from a large SM Yukawa-induced portal? not addressed).

5. Minor: the "simplification" in Eq. (B.46) for f_G takes *limiting* values in the m_Q≫m_χ regime, which is inconsistent with the enhancement of the first sum in the degenerate region — the paper uses this approximate f_G in the SI scaling, so the degenerate enhancement of the SI cross section (the headline complementarity result) rests partly on a heavy-particle f_G that is actually not well-defined there. The qualitative conclusion (enhancement of the 1/(m_Q−m_χ) terms) is robust, but the f_G piece carries an unquantified error in exactly the region the paper highlights.

6. Presentation nit: Fig. 13.8.4 reference to "MadGraph5 v1.4.8.4" — fine, but the combination of LO cross sections with borrowed NLO-era simplified-model limits is a place where a full LO→NLO consistency check on the collider exclusion lines would substantially strengthen the paper.

## Summary

This is a solid, carefully executed phenomenology paper that gives the community a clean, auditable 2-D map of where renormalizable colored-partner DM models survive LHC + XENON100 + Fermi constraints in 2013, and makes a genuinely useful "complementarity" and "should-run-a-simplified-model" argument. The chiral-suppression bookkeeping and the appendices are the strongest parts. The two issues that would keep me from a glowing recommendation are (a) the unquantified flavor-degeneracy assumption, which is the foundation of the models and the most common objection a referee would raise, and (b) the systematic uncertainty in the collider curves that is acknowledged but not propagated. Neither is fatal; both are fixable with an additional flavor-estimates paragraph and a scale/matching uncertainty band on the djet limits, respectively. For a high-energy journal, this is a publishable paper; for a *prestigious* journal it reads closer to a strong upper-tier journal (JHEP/PRL-adjacent) unless the flavor and collider-uncertainty concerns are addressed in a revision.

I cannot verify the specific CMS 8 TeV cross-section numbers used against the 2013 experimental papers, but the methodology (MadGraph5 + CMS simplified-model limits, LO) is standard and appropriate for the period (the data themselves are now ~12 years old, which is a context issue, not a paper defect).

# Review: "Effective WIMPs" (Chang, Edezhath, Hutchinson, Luty, arXiv:1307.8120)

## The one-paragraph summary

You've proposed a family of minimal, 3-parameter "effective WIMP" models in which a Z₂-singlet dark matter particle χ (spin 0, ½, or 1) is coupled to SM quarks through a colored "partner" Q by a single renormalizable cubic operator λ(SM)(S̄M)(DM). Fixing λ via the thermal relic abundance leaves the 2D (m_χ, m_Q) plane as a clean arena for comparing LHC monojet/dijet+MET limits with XENON100 (and projected LUX/XENON1T) direct-detection limits. You do this systematically for six models of χ (Majorana/Dirac fermion, real/complex scalar, real/complex vector), and argue that collider and direct-detection probes are complementary — direct detection dominates when direct is unsuppressed (non-self-conjugate χ, multi-TeV), while the collider wins when it's near-mass-degenerate (m_Q ≈ m_χ).

I'll say up front: **it's a solid, careful piece of phenomenology.** I'll focus my feedback on what I think is holding it back from something more, and a few places where the logic has gaps.

---

## What you did well

1. **The reduction to 2D parameter space is the right choice.** Requiring ⟨σv⟩_thermal fixes λ(m_χ, m_Q), so you compare collider and astrophysical constraints in the only plane that matters experimentally. This mirrors what the modern "simplified model" community now does for SUSY, but you did it *here* (2013) for a genuinely minimal model.

2. **The chiral-suppression bookkeeping in §2 is the analytical backbone of the paper.** Breaking each model into s-wave coefficient *a* and p-wave coefficient *b* and then saying *which one is suppressed* (Majorana fermion: s-wave chiral-suppressed by *m_q²/m_χ²* because $J^{PC}=0^{-+}$ and only left-handed quarks couple; Dirac fermion: s-wave unsuppressed; real scalar: both suppressed; complex scalar: s suppressed, p not; real vector: neither; complex vector: neither) — this is the single most useful thing in the paper. Table 2 compresses this well.

3. **Effective-operator analysis in §2.3 is clean.** Eq. (2.2)–(2.15) correctly identify which operators survive (vector-vector vs vector-axial vs twist-two) in the non-relativistic limit and correctly flag that in the *near-degenerate* limit the (m_Q²−m_χ²) denominator enhances the spin-independent direct-detection cross section. This is the physical reason for the complementarity result.

4. **The vector-vector operator argument in §2.2 is correct.** Complex DM can't forbid it, so complex DM always has SI direct detection and is pushed to multi-TeV. Self-conjugate χ (Majorana, real scalar, real vector) has a suppressed or vanishing vector DM operator, so the SI cross section sits in the twist-two / quark-mass operators, which are weaker. This dichotomy is the key driver of many of the plots in Figs. 4–11 and I'd flag it more prominently in the intro.

5. **Appendices B.1–B.6 are thorough**, with analytic annihilation and DD cross sections for all six models. The rescaling arguments for relating the complex-scalar/vector DD rates to the Dirac-fermion case via the matrix-element identity (B.27), (B.35), (B.52) are exactly right.

---

## Where I'd push back — the gaps

### 1. **The flavor-loop problem is hand-waved, not analyzed (this is the biggest gap).**

§1 says: *"The simplest solution to these constraints is to assume that the couplings and masses of the partner fields are approximately flavor-independent."* Then you just do it. But a colored partner Q with m_Q ~ O(1) TeV that couples to u,d,c,s quarks *generates* ΔF=1 flavor-changing neutral currents at one loop. For m_Q ~ 1–2 TeV, the contribution to the K–K̄ and B_d–B̄_d mixing is roughly

$$\Delta M_{B_d} \sim \frac{1}{192\pi^2} \frac{\lambda^4 f_{B_d} m_{B_d}}{m_q^2} m_Q^2 \times \text{mixing}.$$

With λ~1, m_Q~1 TeV, this *can* approach the experimental value depending on the quark-mixing angles in the mass matrix. If the degeneracy between the Q flavors is exact, there's no tree-level FCNC but the loops still run and you have to show the resulting constraints on the mass splitting δm_Q/m_Q — which, in real QCD models, are O(10–100 GeV), enough to be a non-trivial constraint. You need a subsection (or at least a paragraph with a bound on δm_Q/m_Q) showing the flavor loops don't kill the minimal scenario. Right now a referee will ask, and you don't have an answer.

### 2. **The vector DM models are admitted to fall "outside your minimal classification" but kept for "phenomenological similarity" (§1).**

You're right that a SM-singlet real vector cannot interact with quarks through any renormalizable dimension-4 operator without extending the gauge group — you need either a kinetic mixing (which requires a U(1)'), a gauge-boson mass generation from Higgs-sector states with non-minimal couplings, or a higher-dimension operator. The sentence "we see that it has [a] direct phenomenological motivation" is not that motivation. Either (a) construct a UV completion explicitly (e.g., state the U(1)' + Stueckelberg mass), or (b) drop the real-vector row and just discuss the complex vector (which works through vector-vector contact via a heavy Z'). As it stands, the real-vector row in Table 2 and Figs. 9–11 is doing real work in the plot comparison but its UV story is not actually there.

### 3. **λ~3 and the perturbativity caveat deserve more weight.**

You shade λ>3 as "non-perturbative" (χ²/8π² ~ 0.1). Look at Figs. 4, 7, 8 — a *substantial* fraction of the region that's only barely excluded (the "green" islands near m_Q~500–700 GeV, m_χ~300–500 GeV) is *also* in the gray coannihilation-excluded region AND, for large m_Q, requires λ>~2 for the thermal relic. If you're making a "WIMP miracle" argument, the claim that this is a *natural, weakly-coupled* WIMP is weaker than it looks: a meaningful slice of the parameter space that you'd like to call "alive" actually sits at λ ~ 2–4, where loop corrections are at the tens-of-percent level and the fixed-order analysis (your MadGraph5 calculation) isn't really valid. You should discuss the *actual* perturbativity of the LHC production cross section at these couplings — leading-order + 25–50% scale variation (footnote 4) is not the same thing as checking that the expansion is meaningful.

### 4. **The relic-formula approximation (A.1) is being used in a regime where it can break down.**

Eq. (A.1) is the standard non-resonant freeze-out approximation with a single x_f determined self-consistently via e^{x_f}. It's fine for p-wave-dominated annihilation far from coannihilation. But near m_χ ≈ m_t the b b̄ channel kinematically opens and *closes*, and the relic formula has to handle both. You note this in §3.2 ("the relic abundance requires m_Q ≲ 400 GeV unless m_χ > m_t") but I'd expect, at minimum, a plot of relic density vs (m_χ, m_Q) for the real-scalar model around m_χ ~ 100–150 GeV and m_Q ~ 400–800 GeV, showing that the "allowed" regions are genuinely thermal and not artifacts of the approximation. The b b̄ threshold effects can shift the required λ by O(30%) — non-trivial when λ is already at 2–4.

### 5. **Coannihilation is "omitted," not approximated.** You exclude m_Q < 1.1 m_χ (Fig. 4 gray region) — fine. But at 1.1–1.3 m_χ, coannihilation *is* starting to matter and the relic formulae are getting less and less reliable. A more honest treatment brackets the region: compute the exact Boltzmann equations (e.g., MicrOMEGAs) for the 1.0 < m_Q/m_χ < 1.3 strip and show the bound moves. As it stands, the boundary of the gray region in Fig. 4 is an artifact of the exclusion criterion and not a physical boundary.

### 6. **The "simplified-model cross-section limit" trick for applying CMS limits.**

You take the CMS dijet+MET cross-section limit and apply it to *your* production cross section, but for m_Q close to m_χ the signal is soft jets + soft MET and the CMS cuts are optimized for hard, high-MET events. This is why you note (around §3.1) that the simplified model can break down near degeneracy. You should be more quantitative about *how much* the rate differs from the CMS simplified model in the region away from but adjacent to degeneracy. A simple efficiency ratio would tighten the argument significantly.

### 7. **Direct detection: the "edge at m_χ ≈ m_t" effect (Fig. 4 caption).**

You note there's a small artifact where the relic constraint (which drops λ when m_χ > m_t, opening the b b̄ channel) makes the XENON100 bound turn over. This is a physical effect you should call out as a *feature* — it means for m_χ in the top-decay window, the model is viable at much smaller λ and thus much smaller σ_SI. A referee interested in light DM will ask about this.

### 8. **Minor / housekeeping.**

- Fig. 1's "Feynman diagram contributing to freeze out, indirect detection, direct detection, *and* collider production" is a nice unifying picture; put it in the intro (it's already there, good).
- §2.3 "Heavy Partner Limit": for the real-vector DM case, the *F*̃_{μν} term vanishes when the vector DM has no mass (m_χ=0) — but in your model the vector DM does have a mass (it's the *real* vector field with a Stueckelberg/Higgs mass). Make this clearer.
- The phrase "partner fields" is introduced in §1 and then you use Q and χ. Just use Q and χ throughout.
- Cite the original "WIMP miracle" (Griest & Seckel 1989; Gondolo & Gelmini 1991) explicitly.
- Table 3's f_Rd = 0.032 proton vs 0.041 neutron — verify these against Ji et al., PRL 84 (2000) 1307; some recent extractions (from lattice, e.g., Aoki et al.) give different values and this changes the gluon-contribution sum in Eq. (B.14).

### 9. **A more structural suggestion.**

You end with a bulleted list of "main results." I think the cleanest statement of the paper's contribution is the *complementarity map*: take the (m_χ, m_Q) plane, and mark (a) where XENON100 reaches, (b) where LHC 8 TeV reaches, (c) where LHC 14 TeV projected reaches, (d) where LUX/XENON1T projected reaches. If that single figure is the "hero figure," the whole paper becomes more striking. Right now Figs. 4–11 each show one model; but the *pattern* across models (Fig. 4 vs Fig. 9 vs Fig. 11) is the real result and it deserves a side-by-side summary figure.

---

## Where the paper sits

- The model class is essentially a "quark squark" toy: a colored scalar (or fermion) Q + a neutral χ + a cubic operator. This is well-studied — it's the core of effective-dark-matter (Beltran–Hooper–Kolb–Krusberg 2008–2010), the quark-partner models of e.g. Fitzpatrick et al., and the squark+neutralino sector of minimal SUSY. What's new here is: (i) treating all spin-0/½/1 χ systematically with the same machinery; (ii) the clean 2D constraint comparison; (iii) the near-degeneracy enhancement as the mechanism for complementarity.
- Impact at this point: moderate. The framework has influenced how people think about minimal-model constraint comparison (and is a precursor to the modern "simplified model" literature's emphasis on 2D mass planes). But it's not field-shaping on its own — the specific 8 TeV limits and XENON100 bound are long superseded; the conceptual point (complementarity between DD and LHC in minimal WIMP models) is broadly shared and not unique to this work.

---

## What I'd want to see before calling this done

1. An explicit one-loop FCNC discussion with an upper bound on the mass splitting δm_Q/m_Q, or a justification for why the degeneracy required is compatible with the flavor constraints.
2. A UV completion for the real-vector model, or the model dropped.
3. A relic-density contour plot around the b b̄ threshold, computed with a code that handles thresholds, not just the analytic approximation.
4. An honest "perturbativity check" plot in the (m_χ, m_Q) plane showing the *actual* loop-expansion estimate (λ²/16π² × tree production rate) — not just "λ>3" shading.
5. A single summary figure showing the four-probe complementarity map.

---

## Bottom line

**This is good, careful, pedagogical phenomenology.** The systematic treatment of chiral suppression in §2 is the cleanest statement I've seen of "which chiral structure you pick for the operator determines whether DD beats the LHC, and by how much." The 2D-plane comparison is the right framing, and the analysis is done to a standard higher than most. What it's missing is: (a) the flavor-loop analysis, (b) a UV story for the vector models, and (c) more honesty about how close to non-perturbativity the interesting region actually sits. Once you add those three things, the paper is robust and the complementarity result is on solid ground.
