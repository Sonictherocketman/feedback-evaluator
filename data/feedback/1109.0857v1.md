# Review

**Paper:** *"Electrical field induced shift of the Mott Metal–Insulator transition in thin films"*, D. Nasr Esfahani, L. Covaci, F. M. Peeters (arXiv:1109.0857).

## What the paper does

The authors apply a **site‑dependent (inhomogeneous) Gutzwiller approximation (GA)** to a single‑band Hubbard model at half filling, in a slab geometry (finite in *z*, translational invariance in *x,y*). A linear one‑body potential profile `v(i) ∈ [−v/2, +v/2]` stands in for a perpendicular electric field. They minimize the GA energy functional over the local double‑occupancy weight `g_i`, the spin‑ and site‑dependent "fugacity" parameters `ζ_{iσ}`, and the non‑interacting state `|φ₀⟩`, solving the resulting nonlinear saddle‑point equations with a MinPack trust‑region‑dogleg method on an 8×8 in‑plane k‑grid. The diagnostic is the **site‑resolved quasiparticle weight** `q_{iσ}` (inverse mass renormalization).

Headline claims:
1. A perpendicular field shifts the MIT **to higher U for the surface** — surface stays metallic while the bulk insulates for `U > U_c^bulk` (16t, Brinkman–Rice 3D value).
2. The quasiparticle weight peaks **a few layers below the surface**, not on the top layer.
3. Above `U_c` a **dead insulating zone** forms in the center before the field is fully screened, trapping charge at the edges.
4. Contrast with the `v=0` slab (Nourafkan–Marsiglio): there the surface goes insulating *first*.

## Strengths

- **Clean, honest framing of limitations.** The authors repeatedly state that GA cannot describe the insulating state and is meant to approach `U_c` from below, that the method is qualitative, and that they do *not* solve Poisson. This candor is good practice.
- **Nice variational bookkeeping (Sec. II).** The observation that a single‑body potential adds **no new variational parameters** — because the `ζ_{iσ}` can be absorbed into the fugacities, leaving the standard d=∞ renormalizations unchanged — is a genuinely correct and useful point. It makes the extension of GA to site potentials principled rather than ad hoc.
- **Physically plausible phenomenology.** The "peak a few layers in" behavior is a sensible competition between (a) surface sites having lower coordination (hence lower kinetic energy, smaller `q`) and (b) field‑induced local doping raising `q`. The particle–hole (electron/hole) symmetry of the charge profile across the slab (Fig. 2b) is the right sign.
- **Coherent, well‑motivated story** connecting to LAO/STO, V₂O₂, and bad‑metal/correlated‑insulator interfaces, with a concrete (if speculative) device suggestion.

## Main concerns

**1. The result is described for `U > U_c`, using a method that is only justified up to `U_c`.**
This is the most serious internal tension. The abstract's central claim — a *dead insulating region* and a *shifted critical point* for the surface — rests entirely on figures at `U = 16.0000 … 16.0625`, i.e. **above** the Brinkman–Rice `U_c = 16t`. Yet the introduction concedes GA "cannot give any information about the insulating state." Above `U_c` the quasiparticle picture on which GA is founded breaks down, and `q → 0` is precisely where the approximations are least reliable. The authors should state explicitly how much of their "insulating zone" is a trustworthy GA prediction versus an extrapolation of the variational energy functional outside its domain of validity. As written, the headline sits in the regime the authors themselves flag as unusable.

**2. Confusion between an *imposed* field and *screening*.**
The whole narrative is framed in the language of screening ("the field is totally screened only for the central sites"; `v_eff = v_i + λ_{iσ} + Λ → 0` in the center = screening, Fig. 2c). But the field is **fixed by an imposed linear profile**, not computed self‑consistently (no Poisson, no long‑range Coulomb). In a true equilibrium conductor under a battery, the *bulk* field is exactly zero — the drop is confined to Thomas–Fermi skins — so an imposed *linear* internal profile is not what a metal or a doped surface does. There is a circularity: the field is imposed, but then its "screening" (the vanishing of `v_eff`) is reported as a result. The interesting physics (unscreesed potential persisting into an insulating core) is real, but the physical meaning of `v_eff` should be stated more carefully: it is the self‑consistent effective potential in the trial Hamiltonian, and only in certain parameter regimes can that be equated with a screening statement. This deserves a sharper paragraph.

**3. "Metallic" is asserted from `q ≠ 0` alone — no transport is computed.**
The central experimental hook (use the metallic surface for transport, block the *z* direction) is never backed by any conductance calculation. A finite quasiparticle weight indicates a finite DOS / mass renormalization, not a connected metallic channel; near a transition a finite `q` can still be incoherent or percolation‑limited. Given that the paper's whole motivation is *transport in a thin film*, at least one Landauer/Boltzmann estimate of in‑plane vs. out‑of‑plane conductance would substantially strengthen it.

**4. Modest incremental content relative to known building blocks.**
Much of the spatial structure follows from two facts the GA already encodes: (i) `q` is a **minimum at half filling `n=1`** (so field‑induced local doping automatically raises `q`), and (ii) surface sites have reduced coordination. The "doping of the surface by the field" is elegant, but the paper should be transparent that the main new observation is the *geometry* (peak a few layers in; symmetric two‑sided profile; central dead zone; the *direction* of the surface‑vs‑bulk transition shift relative to the `v=0` result of Refs. [20,21]), rather than new correlation physics. That framing would make the contribution clearer.

**5. Regime restrictions are underemphasized as to generality.**
- **Exactly half filling.** All "doping" is field‑induced, yet the conclusion invokes "underdoped/overdoped Mott insulator" language. A single off‑half‑filling point would test how robust the conclusions are.
- **Paramagnetic only, and cubic lattice with one `t`.** Real V₂O₂ is antiferromagnetic; `U_c=16t` is the paramagnetic 3D Brinkman–Rice value. No attempt is made to map `t`, `U`, and `v` onto the actual material (what gate voltage does `v=2t` correspond to?). The field/breakdown‑field comparison in the conclusion is therefore qualitative to the point of being non‑informative.
- **Fixed‑`v`, varying‑thickness, and `v`=2t at fixed thickness are the main sweeps.** There is no sensitivity/robustness analysis: no finite‑temperature behavior, no comparison to inhomogeneous DMFT (the obvious benchmark they name but never run), and no explicit demonstration that the 8×8 in‑plane grid and the Nz discretization are converged beyond a statement that "the energy is well converged." MinPack is a local solver on a *dense, finite‑difference* Jacobian over ~300+ variational parameters — a strongly nonlinear functional — so the question of local‑minimum vs. global‑minimum (and the reported failure of the iterative scheme for `U > 4t`) is worth discussing more.

**6. Presentation.**
There are a fairly large number of typos that slightly undermine confidence ("trail wave function," "cites," "matalic," "Jacobian/Jacobin," "specially"), and some figures carry duplicated subplot labels. Not fatal, but worth a careful proofread, especially given that the argument depends heavily on the reader trusting the numerical figures.

## Overall assessment

This is a clean, competently executed, and honest qualitative study. It extends the Gutzwiller method cleanly to site potentials, produces a plausible and physically well‑motivated spatial picture (field‑doped metallic skins, a central dead insulating zone, and a reversal of which side goes insulating first relative to the field‑free slab), and is clearly written about its own limitations. However, its central claims live in the `U > U_c` regime the method is admittedly weakest in, the screening language is looser than the imposed‑field model warrants, and the paper's transport motivation is never turned into a number. The physics is reasonable and partly follows from well‑known features of the GA renormalization plus surface coordination, so the genuine novelty is the geometry of the effect rather than new correlated‑matter physics.

**Recommendation:** the work is suitable in a short‑communications/letter form after (a) an explicit caveat on how far above `U_c` the claims are made and on the imposed‑vs‑screened‑field distinction, (b) at least a coarse finite‑`T` or transport estimate supporting "metallic," and (c) a stronger justification of the numerical convergence/global‑minimum claim.

# Review

**Title:** Electrical field induced shift of the Mott Metal-Insulator transition in thin films
**Authors:** D. Nasr Esfahani, L. Covi, F. M. Peeters (cond-mat.str-el, 2011)

## Summarizing the contribution

The authors extend the Gutzwiller approximation to an *inhomogeneous* single-band Hubbard model in a slab geometry, adding a linear (rigid) on-site potential profile to represent an electric field applied perpendicular to the film. Because an on-site potential is a one-body term, no new variational correlations are needed — a point they argue and justify. Solving the resulting saddle-point equations, they report that a perpendicular field (a) shifts the critical Hubbard repulsion of the *central* sites to higher U, (b) localizes charge near the surfaces (charge trapping), and (c) keeps the surface layers metallic (in the absence of a field the surface is the first to go insulating) while the bulk forms a "dead insulating zone" for U above the (shifted) critical value.

## What is done well

1. **Clean variational argument.** The claim that introducing on-site potentials does not require new Gutzwiller variational parameters is the right one, and that they prove it rather than assert it is a genuine strength. The whole self-consistent scheme (renormalized hoppings, saddle-point conditions, MinPack Newton solution) is internally consistent and standard.

2. **A coherent, physically motivated picture.** The emergent spatial differentiation — metallic surface, insulating interior, surface charge trapping pushing U_c up — is a sensible and interesting mechanism, and the discussion connects it to the known "bad-metal / overdoped Mott" edge regimes (refs. 23, 24).

3. **Reasonable honesty about scope.** The limitations (rigid potential, no Poisson–Schrödinger coupling, qualitative nature, slab size) are stated rather than papered over.

## Substantive concerns

1. **The rigid-potential assumption is in tension with the insulating state the method predicts.** The whole calculation keeps the applied profile linear even as the bulk is *driven into a Mott insulator*. A Mott insulator screens poorly; a real field across an insulating film would bend/saturate, and the band bending would be quantitatively different. So the central conclusion (field-induced spatial differentiation, charge trapping) inherits an assumption that is at its weakest precisely in the insulating regime where the interesting physics appears. This is the single biggest physical caveat, and it is only gestured at, not resolved.

2. **No benchmarking at any level.** There is no comparison to DMFT, exact diagonalization / DCA, or even a field-free numerical check against a known inhomogeneous reference, and no comparison to the electrical-field MIT measurements they cite. The Gutzwiller approximation is a known-strong-limit, coarse tool (they acknowledge it gives little about low-energy excitations). With a method of limited quantitative reliability *plus* an idealized potential and zero temperature, the results stay purely qualitative; a single quantitative anchor would materially strengthen the paper.

3. **Zero temperature, and the experimental link is loose.** The cited experimental electric-field-driven transitions occur at finite T and often in specific regimes (first-order, structural). The ground-state, half-filled paramagnetic model is a clean abstraction, but the discussion of its relevance to thin-film transport (surface states as 2D channels, lower breakdown field) is speculative and not quantitatively connected.

4. **Figure noise / finite-size presentation.** Several panels (notably Fig. 6(a)) show point-to-point fluctuations of a magnitude that suggests the N_z dependence and the N_k = 8×8 grid are limiting resolution; the finite-thickness "asymptotic" argument (conclusions, §IV, and discussion around Fig. 5) is acknowledged as not asymptotic ("we did not obtain a clear asymptotic behavior") — so the scaling claim for thick slabs is tentative and should be downgraded.

## Minor issues

- **Consistent misspelling.** "Hartree" is rendered "Harttree" throughout, and "Gutzwiller" is once "Gutzwiler." Non-native English more generally, but intelligible.
- The claim "predict the critical Hubbard repulsion … U_c = 16t for 3D" is a standard Brinkman–Rice Gutzwiller number for the specific lattice; it is presented as a validation but it is essentially the method's built-in output, so it is weak evidence of accuracy.
- The charge redistribution in Fig. 1(b) (≈1.03 → 0.97) is small; the strength of the "charge frustration" argument would benefit from a larger or more systematic field sweep in the text.

## Verdict

This is a competent, self-consistent, and reasonably honest piece of theoretical work that extends the Gutzwiller framework to a field-driven inhomogeneous geometry and extracts a physically sensible qualitative result. It is, however, a modest and niche contribution: purely qualitative, resting on a rigid-potential assumption that is most strained in the insulating regime it studies, and lacking any quantitative benchmark against a more accurate method or against experiment. It reads well as a workshop or mid-tier-journal letter; it would need a quantitative anchor (a field-free inhomogeneous benchmark or DMFT comparison) and a more physical treatment of screening to be convincing of the *specific* shift in U_c.

# Critique: *Electrical field induced shift of the Mott Metal‑Insulator transition in thin films* (Esfahani, Covaci, Peeters; arXiv:1109.0857)

## What the paper attempts
The authors study a single‑band Hubbard model in a slab (translational invariance in *x*, *y*; finite in *z*) with a perpendicular electric field modelled as a **linear potential profile** from $-v/2$ to $+v/2$. The tool is an **inhomogeneous Gutzwiller approximation (GA)**, extended (Section II) to absorb on‑site potentials by a change of variables ($\zeta_{i\sigma}$, fugacities) that recovers the $d=\infty$ renormalization factors without introducing new correlation variational parameters. They then solve the resulting saddle‑point equations (Eqs. 19–22) self‑consistently and report the spatial profiles of quasiparticle weight $q_i$, charge density $n_i$ and effective potential. Headline claims: (i) the MIT shifts to higher $U$; (ii) $q_i$ is maximal a few layers beneath each surface; (iii) for $U>U_c=16t$ a central "dead insulating zone" forms, charge is trapped at the edges, and the surface stays metallic while the bulk insulates; (iv) without field, the surface insulates *before* the bulk.

## Strengths
- **Well‑motivated, timely problem.** Field‑tunable Mott physics in VO₂ and at the LAO/STO interface is a real and important thread; the device relevance (using 2D doped surface channels while the bulk stays insulating) is a sensible framing.
- **Clean, honest methodological contribution.** The variational argument that a single‑body on‑site term needs *no* new variational parameter — proven by the change of variables, and checked against Gebhard — is neat and correct in spirit. The GA is a reasonable, cheaper‑than‑DMFT choice precisely because it handles inhomogeneity tractably, and the authors are upfront about its limits (no higher/lower Hubbard bands, no description of the insulating side, only approach to $U_c$ from below).
- **Consistent picture that agrees with known surface physics.** The no‑field case (surface insulating first, $q$ maximal at the centre) matches Potthoff–Nolting and Nourafkan–Marsiglio, which lends credibility to their inhomogeneous solver.
- **Decent breadth of numerics** (profiles, $U_c^{\text{slab}}$ vs thickness, vs $v$, edge charge accumulation).

## Major weaknesses

**1. The perpendicular field is imposed, not self‑consistent — the central physical assumption is under‑examined.** They fix the linear potential $v_i$ and do *not* self‑consistently include long‑range Coulomb / Poisson–Schrödinger coupling (explicitly deferred). A perpendicular field in any material with finite screening is itself the *result* of boundary conditions and response; in a metal it is screened to the surface, in an insulator it drops across the gap. Imposing the full linear profile regardless of the phase the slab ends up in, and then *reading screening off the output*, is circular: the boundary condition already presupposes an insulating response. This is not a bug but it means the "screening" claim in the abstract is partly an input, and the quantitative strength of any "field effect" cannot be trusted without a Poisson loop.

**2. The "shifted MIT" is largely the ordinary Mott‑doping phase diagram sampled across the slab.** The potential makes one edge electron‑doped and the other hole‑doped. $q$ is maximal away from half‑filling by construction, so doped layers are automatically more metallic. Their own Fig. 2(b/c) confirms $q$ is maximal exactly where $|n-1|$ is maximal. Stated this way, the "field‑induced shift" is just *site‑dependent doping*, not a novel field mechanism. A reviewer will push hard on whether "charge frustration raises $q$" (repeated verbatim in the text) is anything more than re‑deriving the horizontal axis of the Mott phase diagram — and it reads that way. The genuinely nontrivial content (does screening *reinforce* the doped region in a feedback loop?) is precisely what item 1 says was not done.

**3. The central "dead zone" is substantially a finite‑size / constrained‑$v$ artifact, and the bulk limit is not reached.** $v$ is held fixed while thickness $N$ is varied, so the field per layer falls as $1/N$; central sites drift toward $n=1$ simply by geometry, hence are near the Mott point and insulate for $U>16t$. That makes the thickness dependence (Fig. 5) an artifact of fixing $v$ rather than fixing a field. They openly admit they did **not** obtain an asymptotic, thickness‑converged solution ("we did not obtain a clear asymptotic behaviour… one *may* get… for very thick slabs"), so the signature result rests on $N=100$ and extrapolation. Fixing the field per unit length instead of the total drop would arguably remove the dead zone.

**4. No independent benchmark.** Everything is self‑consistent within GA. There is no cross‑check of $q$ or $U_c$ against DQMC, cluster (CT‑QMC), or DMFT — even for the uniform limit — so the only anchor is the known Brinkman–Rice $U_c=16t$. For a first‑order/strongly non‑linear regime where metastable solutions are common, the absence of an accuracy benchmark compounds the next concern.

**5. Numerical robustness is thin.** MinPack (dog‑leg) with a **finite‑difference, dense Jacobian** over $\sim 2N\times 3$ variational parameters; their cheaper iterative scheme diverged for $U>4t$. There is no uniqueness/metastability analysis, no k‑grid (8×8 — small) convergence study, and no statement that the found solution is the global minimum. For a Mott transition with possible coextraneous metallic/insulating solutions, the reported $q$ and "critical" $U$ could be solution‑dependent, yet this is never assessed.

**6. Abstract overstates / garbles the screening result.** "the electric field will be totally screened only for central cites [sites]" is hard to parse and not clearly supported by Fig. 2(c); the central claim that screening completes *only* at the centre for $U>U_c$ should be stated and quantified (a screening length, a ratio), not left to a hand‑wave.

## Minor issues
Heavy typos/grammar ("cites"→sites, "metalic", "Jacobin" vs "Jacobian", "in short", repeated sentences, e.g. the $q$‑source paragraph) lower the polish. The device conclusions ("field needed is well below breakdown," "protecting surface states from leakage") are asserted without any real‑unit estimate: putting $t\sim0.1\,$eV, $v=2t$ across $100\,\times\,a$ Å already gives $\sim$GPa/V‑scale fields near breakdown, so that claim is unverified. The introduction's tie‑in to VO₂/LAO‑STO is never made quantitative (no mapping $U,t,v$ onto a real material), so the motivation and the calculation feel decoupled.

## Bottom line
A competent, internally consistent inhomogeneous‑GA study on a relevant geometry, and a clean methodological extension of GA to on‑site potentials. But the central physics reduces mostly to *doping‑induced* site dependence of $q$ rather than a new field mechanism; the perpendicular field is imposed rather than screened self‑consistently; the signature dead‑zone result is an admitted non‑converged, constrained‑$v$, finite‑size effect; and there is no external benchmark or robustness/unique‑solution check. The claims in the abstract outrun what the modelling and numerics firmly establish. Solid incremental work, not a strong result.

# Evaluation of arXiv:1109.0857v1 — "Electrical field induced shift of the Mott Metal-Insulator transition in thin films" (Esfahani, Covaci, Peeters, 2011)

## The paper's stated contribution, in my own words

The authors ask what happens to the Mott metal–insulator transition (MIT) in a thin film of a correlated material when an electric field is applied **perpendicular** to the film. They model this as a single-band half-filled Hubbard slab (translationally invariant in-plane, finitely thick in z) with a linearly varying on-site potential, and solve it **ground-state-in the inhomogeneous Gutzwiller approximation (GA)**, tracking the site-resolved quasiparticle weight *q_i* as a diagnostic of local metallicity (insulating where *q* → 0, i.e., divergent mass renormalization).

Their central claim: the field does two things that **invert the usual field-free surface picture**. (i) It drives charge away from local half-filling near the surfaces (electron-rich on one face, hole-rich on the other); since the Gutzwiller renormalization factor is *minimal at half-filling* (the Brinkman–Rice mechanism), that charge frustration keeps *q* nonzero at the surfaces, so **surface layers stay metallic past the bulk critical U_c ≈ 16t**. (ii) The bulk/center becomes the first region to go insulating, forming a "dead zone," which then traps the field-induced charge at the surfaces and shifts the *effective* MIT of the surface channels to still higher U — with the shift growing with voltage and slab thickness. They frame this as relevant to transport in thin films: in-plane surface conduction shielded by a field-protected insulating bulk, at fields below dielectric breakdown (motivated by VO₂, LAO/STO, Ni(S,Se)₂).

## What is genuinely new

1. **The specific physical configuration: a perpendicular external field on a correlated slab, studied within inhomogeneous GA.** None of the cited prior work treats this setup. The closest cited neighbors each lack one ingredient: Potthoff & Nolting (PRB 59, 2549, 1999) and Nourafkan & Marsiglio (PRB 83, 155116, 2011) treat field-*free* surfaces/interfaces of the half-filled Hubbard model (the "surface becomes insulating before the bulk" baseline the paper contrasts with); Borghi–Fabrizio–Tosatti (2010) and Helmes–Costi–Rosch (2008) treat interface-induced metallization from an adjacent bad metal, not an applied electrostatic potential. So "field-driven charge frustration keeps the *surface* metallic while the *bulk* Mott-insulates in the same film" is a result not contained in the cited literature, and the reversal of the field-free surface ordering is a legitimately fresh qualitative finding.
2. **A minor technical point:** showing that adding site-dependent single-body potentials requires *no new variational parameters* in the d=∞ Gutzwiller wave function (absorbed by redefining fugacity factors, Eqs. 4–6). This is correct and worth stating, but it is a short reparametrization exercise, not a methodological advance.

## What is *not* new

1. **The machinery is standard.** Slab geometry + linear potential + site-resolved inhomogeneous GA + self-consistent saddle-point iteration is a routine combination of the Gutzwiller formalism of Metzner–Vollhardt/Gebhard, Brinkman–Rice, and the inhomogeneous GA already used on Hubbard surfaces (Potthoff–Nolting). The numerical scheme (MINPACK trust-region dogleg, 8×8 k-grid) is off-the-shelf.

## Understanding of the paper's logical argument

**arXiv:1109.0857 — "Electrical field induced shift of the Mott Metal-Insulator transition in thin films" (Nasr Esfahani, Covaci, Peeters, 2011).**

**Goal.** Motivated by electric-field-driven MITs in VO₂ and at the LaAlO₃/SrTiO₃ interface, the authors ask whether a static electric field perpendicular to a thin film of a strongly correlated (Hubbard) material spatially reshapes the metal–insulator transition.

**Method.** They use a position-dependent Gutzwiller approximation (GA) on a single-band Hubbard slab (translational invariance in *x,y*; *N_z* layers in *z*) with a *linear* background potential profile *v_i* from −*v*/2 to +*v*/2. Two technical claims frame the rest:

1. Adding on-site potentials does **not** introduce new correlation variational parameters — shown via a change of variables (ζ_iσ = g_i^{β_iσ}) that reproduces the *d → ∞* GA renormalization factors, so minimization is over |φ₀⟩ and the local *g_i* only.
2. The energy functional (with Lagrange multipliers *λ_iσ*, Λ, *E*) yields saddle-point equations (19)–(22), solved self-consistently with a MinPack trust-region dogleg solver and a finite-difference Jacobian on an 8×8 *k*-grid.

**Core chain of reasoning (results).**
- Because the Gutzwiller quasiparticle weight *q_iσ* is minimal at half-filling (Eq. 8–9), any field-induced local density deviation away from *n_i* = 1 increases *q*.

- For *U*>U_c (bulk U_c = 16t) the central layers' *q* collapses to a near-zero "dead insulating zone," while the charged surface layers keep finite *q* — i.e. the surface MIT is shifted to higher *U*.

- Physical implication: perpendicular transport is cut off by the dead zone while the surface stays conductive, so the field needed to form surface states is below the breakdown field.

## Feedback

The logical argument is internally coherent and the GA extension (a fixed background potential does not add variational parameters) is a clean, correct point. But several gaps weaken the case:

1. **The phase-separated solution is never shown to be the ground state.** This is the most serious issue. The "metallic surface + insulating center" configuration is a local minimum found by starting from the metallic solution. It directly competes with a *fully screened, homogeneous insulating* state for U > U_c. The paper never compares their energies, so the central claim — "the surface remains metallic because charge is trapped" — is an assertion about a variational local minimum, not about the actual ground state. Without that comparison, the headline result rests on an unverified assumption about which minimum the dogleg solver converged to.

2. **The fixed background potential is in tension with the screening narrative.** The authors explicitly do *not* couple to Poisson's equation and impose a *linear* profile. Yet they then discuss "screening" and "charge trapping" as if the field self-consistently redistributes. A fixed external field will keep charging a now-insulating film; the physical field at the surface is not what this setup represents. The effective-potential plot (Fig. 2c) is a self-consistent *q*-weighted quantity, not a true dielectric screening response. The screening claims should be softened or the Poisson step actually performed.

Minor: typos ("central cites," "cites"), and a dense, somewhat circular prose in the results discussion.

**Bottom line:** a competent, incremental application of an established inhomogeneous-Gutzwiller method to a reasonably novel slab+field geometry, yielding a plausible and visually clear picture of field-induced surface states and a central dead zone. ## Audit

### 1. Is the comparison fair?

**Partially.** The v = 0 vs. v = 2 t comparison in Figs. 3 and 5 is *internally* fair — same N_z = 100, same 8 × 8 in-plane k-grid, same solver, same Minpack tolerance (though the tolerance is never actually stated). The criterion "maximal quasiparticle weight Z_max = 5×10⁻³" is arbitrary and not tied to any physical threshold. Crucially, **no long-range Coulomb / Poisson–Schrödinger self-consistency is solved** — the field is imposed as a rigid linear profile, so v_i is effectively an external control parameter, not a physical voltage. The paper makes **no attempt to calibrate or correct** this known systematic error, and never quotes a DQMC, DMRG-on-a-slab, or DFT+DMFT number on the same geometry. For a 2011 theoretical paper, the reference list (mostly 1998–2010, including Husmann 2000, Cavilia 2008, Potthoff 1999) is period-appropriate rather than "current," and no *numerical* baseline is invoked beyond the v = 0 GA limit.

**None, and the framework for reporting them is absent.** GA is a deterministic variational method — there is no ensemble, so MC error bars don't apply — but the authors equally

- don't state the energy convergence tolerance of Minpack,
- don't test the 8×8 k-grid with a finer mesh (no grid-convergence table),
- don't vary N_z around a single value to show discretization error (Fig. 5 *does* sweep thickness but treats it as physics, not as a numerical error estimate),
- never attach an uncertainty to U_c = 16 t or to the 0.3 t shift in Fig. 5,
- report Z_max = 5×10⁻³ as a *lower bound* for U_c^slab (an honest qualifier) but then present the v dependence as if it were a measured transition line.

In short: **no error bars and no statistical-significance test of any kind are reported anywhere in the paper.**

- N_z = 100 is thin in physical units; the asymptotic "extremely thick slab" limit the authors claim is unreachable is never numerically demonstrated.
- Only a single band and half filling are treated; the V₂O₂ experiment they cite is multi-orbital and slightly off half-filling, so any quantitative comparison of "0.3 t ≡ X volts" is not attempted and arguably cannot be.
- Long-range Coulomb and band-structure effects (Cavilia's structural transition) are excluded by construction.

## Bottom line

The paper is a clean, internally consistent, but **single-method** theoretical study. The headline number (electric-field shift of U_c) rests on (a) an arbitrary Z_max threshold, (b) no independent benchmark for U_c itself, and (c) no numerical error analysis of any kind. It is well-motivated and qualitatively plausible, but the quantitative claim it leans on most should not be read as a precision prediction.

## [1109.0857v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Evaluation: "Electrical field induced shift of the Mott Metal-Insulator transition in thin films"
## Nasr Esfahani, Covaci, and Peeters (2011)

## The Single Weakest Link: From Spatial Inhomogeneity to "Shifted Transition"

The paper's argument chain runs:

1. **Model**: Single-band Hubbard model + externally imposed linear on-site potential (the electric field), slab of $N_z$ sites.
2. **Method**: Site-resolved Gutzwiller approximation, exact in the $d \to \infty$ limit; no new variational parameters needed for on-site potentials.
3. **Observation**: For $U > U_c^{\text{bulk}} \approx 16t$, the quasiparticle weight $Z_i$ at the slab center collapses to $\sim 0$ while $Z_i$ in 1–2 surface layers remains finite ($\sim 5\times10^{-3}$ to $5\times10^{-2}$, depending on $v$). They call the center a "dead insulating zone."
4. **Definition of a new critical point**: $U_c^{\text{slab}}$ is the value of $U$ at which $Z_{\max} \equiv \max_i Z_i = 5\times10^{-3}$ (Fig. 5). By construction, $U_c^{\text{slab}} > U_c^{\text{bulk}}$.
5. **Conclusion (title/abstract)**: "The metal–insulator transition is shifted towards higher Hubbard repulsions by applying an electric field."

**The step where the conclusion is not entailed by the data is 3 → 5.** The data unambiguously demonstrate *spatial inhomogeneity*: at a fixed $U > 16t$, the surface layers are metallic and the bulk center is insulating. This is a real, computed, internally consistent result. However, a "shift of the Mott transition" is a homogeneous-system concept — a value of $U$ at which $Z_\rho \to 0$ *uniformly* in space, corresponding to a genuine change in the bulk thermodynamic phase. In the presence of the imposed linear potential, the system is *never* uniform: $Z_{\min}$ (at the center) reaches zero at essentially the same $U$ as in the homogeneous case, while $Z_{\max}$ (at the surface) lingers.

The "shift" $U_c^{\text{slab}} - U_c^{\text{bulk}}$ is therefore an artifact of (a) choosing the *spatial maximum* of $Z_i$ as the observable that defines the critical point, and (b) imposing an arbitrary threshold ($5\times10^{-3}$) on it. Had the authors tracked $Z_{\min}$ (center) or $\bar{Z}$ (thickness average), no shift—or a much smaller, threshold-dependent one—would emerge. Fig. 5 confirms this: the "shift" is on the order of $0.03$–$0.15\,t$ depending on $N_z$ and $v$, essentially at the resolution level of the threshold choice. The paper never varies the threshold to demonstrate that $U_c^{\text{slab}}$ is robust to it.

More fundamentally, the paper itself concedes (p. 7): *"when measuring an I–V curve only the z-axis conductivity will show metallic behavior because the z-axis conductivity will be dominated by the bulk insulating layer."* In other words, the authors' own result says the *film* does not become a better conductor at higher $U$; it becomes more insulating in the transport-relevant direction. The "shift" is really a statement about the *localization length* of the metallic surface states, not about where the film insulizes as a whole.

### Why this matters for the other claims

The "screening is incomplete for $U < U_c^{\text{slab}}$" claim (abstract) and the "charge trapping" interpretation are actually *consistent with* the inhomogeneity picture, and are not overreached. But the title, first sentence of the abstract, and the concluding "we therefore show that … the MTT has been shifted for edge sites" overstate what follows from the data. The supported physical conclusion is: **an electric field creates a spatially graded metallic-to-insulating profile whose metallic cap persists above $U_c^{\text{bulk}}$** — an interesting result, but not a "shift of the Mott transition" in any standard thermodynamic or transport sense.

### Secondary (weaker) concerns, not the primary break

- The potential is imposed linearly, not self-consistently solved via Poisson's equation; the authors acknowledge this. The qualitative picture likely survives, so this is secondary.
- The infinite-$d$ limit is used for a nominally 3D system (they note $U_c = 16t$ in 3D GA vs. $\sim 20t$ in DMFT, so the absolute $U_c$ is off). This affects the magnitude but not the qualitative inhomogeneity prediction.
- The 8×8 $k$-grid is reasonable for the slab geometry but limits quantitative accuracy in the underdoped/overdoped surface regime.

None of these is as damaging as the conceptual slippage from "inhomogeneous $Z$ profile" to "shifted $U_c$."

## Summary Assessment

The paper is a competent qualitative application of a well-known method to a reasonable physical geometry. The inhomogeneous-Gutzwiller machinery is correctly implemented, the figures are clear, and the "underdoped/overdoped" analogy for the surface charge redistribution is a genuinely nice observation. However, the central headline claim — that the electric field *shifts* the Mott MIT — is not what the computed data actually show. The data show a spatial crossover that *overlaps with and extends slightly past* the bulk homogeneous transition, and the paper's own transport analysis undercuts the "metallic film" interpretation. The result is a paper whose title and abstract overstate the conclusion by one rhetorical step relative to what the figures establish.

## Bottom line and what I'd want before this is publishable

The physics is real and the mechanism is correct; the result (field-induced reversal of surface/bulk MIT via surface doping) is a solid, moderately novel finding — a good first paper. But the **gap between the model and the claims** (screening without Poisson; fixed-tilt boundary vs. the two-terminal experiment; arbitrary *U*_c threshold; *U* > 16*t* solutions whose stability is unverified; no *k*-grid or multi-start convergence) is what a reviewer will hit you with. The highest-value fixes, in order:

1. Add *one* self-consistent Poisson point (or a bound on the screening correction) and reword the screening language.
2. Replace/justify the Z = 5×10⁻³ threshold with a stability criterion + a sensitivity check.
3. Add *k*-grid and multi-initial-guess convergence checks, and verify the *U* > 16*t* central-site solutions are the global minimum.
4. Reconcile and clarify the *U* > *U*_c statement; connect *v* to real volts; fix the typos.

Do 1 and 3, and this goes from "plausible" to "defensible." I'm glad you're doing this — the inhomogeneous GA is the right tool and you've used it carefully; the issue is mostly about *not over-claiming* what the fixed-potential, un-screened model can support.
