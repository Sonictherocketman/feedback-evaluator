## Review: "*4f*-derived Fermi Surfaces of CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ near the QCP: Resonant Soft‑X‑ray ARPES Study"

### Summary of the argument
The authors use soft‑X‑ray ARPES in the Ce 3d→4f resonance region to ask whether the Ce 4f electrons stay itinerant (participating in FS formation) on both sides of the QCP at x_c ≈ 0.07. They compare the paramagnetic/AFM composition CeRu₂Si₂ (x = 0, below x_c) with the ferromagnetic CeRu₂(Si₀.₈₂Ge₀.₁₈)₂ (x = 0.18, above x_c), using LaRu₂Si₂ as a 4f‑free reference. They find strongly *c–f*‑hybridized, 4f‑derived FS in both compounds (made visible by the resonant enhancement), and they interpret the *absence* of a reconstructive change of the FS across the critical composition as evidence that the SDW‑QCP description (no FS reconstruction, itinerant criticality) is preferable to a local‑quantum‑criticality (LQC) model.

The methodological core is sound and the experiment is clearly executed: the off‑resonance/on‑resonance contrast is a clean way to isolate the 4f‑weighted heavy quasiparticle FS, and the resonant feature is correctly mapped to the expected zone centers.

### Main weaknesses

**1. The central inference is weaker than it is presented (most important).**
The headline logic is: *no discontinuous FS change across x_c ⇒ SDW rather than LQC.* But the LQC model predicts a **Kondo‑crossover‑like, continuous evolution** of the FS near T₀ — not a sharp jump. In that case one should *expect* little visible change in the FS at a single temperature T ≈ 20 K ≈ T₀. So the null result is, if anything, more consistent with a gradual LQC crossover than it is fatal to it. Distinguishing the two pictures really requires showing the FS is *static* under the LQC‑predicted crossover regime, which demands (a) a temperature scan through T₀ and T_N at/near x_c, and (b) a composition scan in the narrow window around x_c. Neither is done. The paper conflates "no discontinuity" with "no change"; the former is uninformative about a continuously evolving FS.

**2. The two measured compositions are both far from x_c.**
x = 0 is 0.07 below and x = 0.18 is ~0.11 above the critical point. The LQC crossover physics is concentrated in a narrow band around x_c and near T₀. Probing only the two endpoints means the crossover region where the models actually differ is unmeasured. "Above and below the critical composition" is a somewhat generous phrasing for "well outside it."

**3. Conflation of three distinct questions.**
The paper blurs (a) whether the 4f electrons are itinerant, (b) whether the FS reconstructs/is discontinuous across the transition, and (c) which universality class (SDW vs LQC) applies. Demonstrating 4f itinerancy on both sides is meaningful for (a) and speaks to a "local‑moment vs itinerant" picture, but it is not a direct discriminator between SDW and LQC, which is a statement about the *critical* behavior of the coupled system. The conclusion that "SDW‑QCP is suitable" overclaims relative to what the data establish.

**4. The "circular FS ⇒ 4f delocalized" identification is indirect and partly circular.**
The shape‑based argument (circular vs the La square shape) leans entirely on the DFT bands of Ref. [7], while the very band with the *largest* 4f weight (band 4, the heaviest branch) is the one that is **not observed** in the off‑resonance data. So the FS with the biggest 4f content is precisely the one missing from the identification, and the assignment rests on a calculated band shape that may not capture Kondo‑renormalized heavy masses. A more quantitative handle — e.g., the resonant enhancement ratio as a 4f spectral‑weight proxy, or line‑shape analysis — would make the 4f‑participation claim quantitative rather than qualitative.

**5. Limited experimental leverage on the crossover.**
Only a single energy resolution (120–160 meV) and a single temperature (≈20 K ≈ T₀) are used. No temperature series, no composition series, and no quantitative spectral‑weight measurement means the paper cannot rule out a small but real LQC‑type crossover. The "weak at X, strong at Z" resonance is treated qualitatively.

### Minor points
- Reliance on DFT [7] from 1992/93 and on the LaRu₂Si₂ reference is reasonable but should be tempered given DFT's known difficulty with the heavy Kondo band.
- The phrase in the abstract "consequently indicating a delocalized nature of the 4f electrons in both compounds" is fine, but the follow‑on SDW claim (last sentence of abstract) is the step that does not follow tightly.

### What would strengthen the paper
- A temperature scan of the FS just above and below T₀ at a composition at/near x_c (a Kondo‑crossover test).
- A composition scan in x ≈ 0.02–0.12 to bracket the crossover window.
- A quantitative 4f spectral‑weight analysis (resonance ratio) rather than shape comparison.
- An explicit statement of the observable that would falsify their SDW claim under this measurement scheme.

### Assessment
This is a competent, well‑motivated experimental contribution with a nice use of resonant soft‑X‑ray ARPES, and it is broadly consistent with prior dHVA and band‑structure work. However, its main conclusion — that SDW is favored over LQC — rests on a null result whose expected signature (a *continuous* crossover) the data never actually probe, compounded by a small sample set far from x_c and a single temperature. The physics content is good; the interpretive leap is overclaimed. Recommend revision with a more cautious framing of the SDW/LQC claim and the temperature/composition scans above.

## Summary of the work

The authors use soft-x-ray angle-resolved photoemission (ARPES), including a Ce 3d→4f resonant measurement at 881 eV, to map the 4f-derived Fermi surfaces (FSs) of CeRu₂Si₂ (paramagnetic, H_N ≈ 7.7 T, T₀ ≈ 20 K) and CeRu₂(Si₀.₈₂Ge₀.₁₈)₂ on either side of the critical composition x_c ≈ 0.07. They find a strong c–f hybridized character in the band structures/FSs of both Ce compounds and, crucially, no FS-surface reconstruction when crossing the critical composition. They argue this absence of a clear FS change favors a spin-density-wave (SDW) QCP over a local-quantum-criticality (LQC) scenario, the latter of which is expected to produce an FS crossover at the QCP.

## Strengths

1. **Good use of resonant ARPES.** The Ce 3d→4f resonance at ~881 eV is the right tool for amplifying the 4f contribution, which is precisely why standard VUV ARPES was inconclusive (Refs. [21], [22] give conflicting conclusions). Comparing off-resonance vs. on-resonance images to isolate the heavy quasiparticle FS is a sensible, well-motivated design.

2. **Direct measurement around the QCP.** Measuring two compositions straddling x_c at low temperature (20 K, near/above T_N and ~T₀) and observing the 4f participation in band formation is a genuinely relevant experiment. The tube-like (CeRu₂Si₂) vs. closed-ellipsoid (LaRu₂Si₂) 3D FS difference is a clean, citable structural distinction.

3. **The core logic is defensible.** The argument that a *discontinuous* FS collapse (required by the LQC Kondo-screening crossover exactly at x_c) is absent in the paramagnetically-doped material is a real, published argument in the field, and they state it clearly.

## Weaknesses / concerns

1. **The central conclusion is under-determined by the data.** The headline claim "SDW rather than LQC" rests on a single negative observation: *no FS reconstruction between x = 0 and x = 0.18*. There are several legitimate escape routes the paper does not address:
   - The QCP is at x_c = 0.07 while the samples are at x = 0 and 0.18 — i.e., both **well outside** the critical window (by ~4.5% and ~11% in composition). A gradual or narrow-criticality L QC crossover could easily be missed between two compositions that straddle but do not bracket x_c tightly. A single intermediate composition (near 0.07) would be exactly what's needed to distinguish the models, and its absence weakens the "more appropriate than LQC" claim considerably.
   - The authors measure at 20 K ≈ T₀. In the SDW picture the FS reconstruction is expected *at/above* T₀ (which is what they test), but the *local* crossover that would signal LQC is in principle temperature- and field-dependent. The single temperature makes the model assignment tentative, yet the abstract states SDW is "more appropriate" with a confidence ("suggests ... suggests") that is not matched by the experimental coverage.
   - No magnetic measurements (magnetoresistance, specific heat, susceptibility, transport) tie these two compositions to the phase diagram. The paper leans entirely on Refs. [5],[9]-[12] for the QCP location.

2. **Band assignment is acknowledged as not conclusive (their words, p.3).** The assignment of the square BZ-boundary FS to band 4 is "not conclusive because the hole FS of band 4 is considered to be the heaviest FS branch... the absence of this FS branch may deny the delocalized nature." This is the one assignment that directly bears on the 4f-delocalization argument, and they flag its own circularity (the strongest 4f band is the one not clearly seen). This should temper the "4f electrons are itinerant" statement.

3. **Resonance "enhancement" is modest.** They describe the 0.1 eV feature as "remarkably enhanced on resonance, weak near the Z point but distinct near the X point." Whether this is a clean resonance signature of 4f character, or a geometry/intensity effect, is not quantified (no energy-intensity ratio, no background-subtracted resonance map across the X–Z line). An off-resonance normalization would strengthen the c–f hybridization claim.

4. **Sample quality / surface sensitivity.** These are 4142-type compounds; the [001] surface of the Ge-substituted solid solution could surface-segregate, and soft-x-ray ARPES (high photon energy, deeper inelastic mean free path) is actually somewhat more forgiving, but no in-situ quality check (surface roughness, cleave quality, sample degradation) is reported. For a 5-page letter this is a minor point, but it matters for quantitative FS extraction.

5. **Presentation.** It's a competent but terse letter. The schematic in Fig. 1 and the model-vs-data logic could have been made more explicit — what observation *would* discriminate SDW from LQC in these specific bands is buried in the discussion rather than stated up front as a falsifiable prediction.

## Bottom line

A solid, methodologically sound resonant-ARPES measurement that convincingly establishes strong 4f (c–f hybridized) character in the FSs of CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ on both sides of the QCP. The step from that solid measurement to the broader "SDW rather than LQC" conclusion is, however, a real but somewhat overstated inference: it's based on a negative result between two compositions that both lie outside the immediate critical window, at a single temperature, with the most decision-relevant band assignment flagged by the authors themselves as inconclusive. The data supports a cautious "no clear FS reconstruction between x = 0 and 0.18, *consistent with* SDW"; it does not yet *strongly favor* SDW over LQC. The proposed future work (measuring near x_c at the magnetic boundaries) is exactly the right experiment that would close this gap.

A useful, citable contribution to the Ce heavy-fermion QCP literature, but the title-level claim outruns the evidence somewhat.

## [0906.3078v1-critique] Critique


# Critique: *4f-derived Fermi Surfaces of CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ near the Quantum Critical Point: Resonant Soft X-ray ARPES Study* (arXiv:0906.3078v1)

## What the paper does well

- **Motivation is sound and timely.** Direct observation of FS reconstruction at a heavy-fermion QCP distinguishes the SDW and local-quantum-criticality (LQC) pictures, and the authors correctly note the failure of prior attempts. Resonant (Ce 3d→4f) soft-x-ray ARPES is a legitimate lever to enhance 4f signal that VUV ARPES (Denlinger et al.) lacked, and the LaRu₂Si₂ reference is a necessary experimental control.
- **The clean, quantitative comparison is good.** The off-resonance FS images (Fig. 3): square (La-like) vs. circular (Ce-like) FS around the Z point is a crisp, visually unambiguous difference, and it agrees with the 20-year-old band calculations of Yamagami et al.
- **Honest acknowledgment of one key failure.** The paper explicitly notes (Sec. 3, last paragraph of the off-resonance discussion) that the *expected* heaviest band-4 hole FS is **not** observed off-resonance, and that its absence "may deny the delocalized nature of the Ce 4f electrons." That is an important caveat for a paper whose headline claim is 4f itinerancy — the authors then patch it with the on-resonance observation, as discussed below.

## Major problems

### 1. The two measured compositions do not bracket the QCP (central logical flaw)
The critical composition is x_c ≈ 0.07. The authors measure x = 0 and **x = 0.18** — a composition that is *2.5× beyond* x_c deep inside the antiferromagnetic regime (AFM phase extends to x = 0.57). "Absence of FS reconstruction across the critical composition" is therefore not what the data show; what the data show is absence of reconstruction over an interval that straddles but substantially overshoots x_c, with a gap (0.07 ≤ x ≤ 0.18 containing nothing) and nothing measured at x_c itself. A continuous transition in heavy-FS volume could still occur at 0 < x < 0.07, or as a gradual crossover on the AFM side. Title claim ("near the QCP") overstates what the composition sampling justifies.

### 2. The temperature is exactly the one that cannot discriminate SDW from LQC — and the conclusion is drawn anyway
The LQC model predicts FS collapse only below the Fermi-liquid scale T_F, which in the LQC scenario decorrelates from the Kondo scale T₀ and can lie *below* T_N. The measurements are made at 20 K ≈ T₀, i.e. precisely in the regime where the two models make **no distinguishable FS prediction**. The authors acknowledge the measurement is "at a temperature comparable to T₀" in a passing clause, then argue that "it is natural to think that the Ce 4f electrons keep their itinerancy" when cooling below T₀. That argument is a priori circular: the entire content of the LQC hypothesis is precisely that a Fermi-liquid breakdown *can* happen at a sub-T₀ scale with no conventional phase transition. A null result at T ~ T₀ is uninformative against LQC; the paper's main conclusion ("SDW is more appropriate than LQC") is not supported by the data as measured, and the authors themselves flag the needed follow-up (measure below T_N) in closing.

### 3. The on-resonance argument has a significant unexplained anomaly
The strongest evidence for 4f itinerancy in the AFM-side compound is the on-resonance FS (Fig. 5(e,f)). But the authors write: "Surprisingly, the large circular FS surrounding the Z point in the off-resonance images becomes invisible in the on-resonance images. This **might be due to** the weakness of the resonance enhancement near the X point." Two issues:

- The large circular FS is the *main feature* of the off-resonance Ce data, and it vanishes under the very enhancement designed to boost 4f signal, while the smaller new FS survives. The offered explanation is speculatively hedged ("might") and, if true, implies the resonance enhancement is so momentum-space selective that much of the FS is unobservable at resonance — undermining the reliability of the residual on-resonance feature attribution.
- The "additional bands" appear only after mapping *secondary derivatives* of momentum-distribution curves with normalization. That is aggressive image processing for evidence that is load-bearing for the central claim.

### 4. The k_z assignment is circular
On-resonance k_z values (0.29π/c, 0.37π/c) rest on an *assumed* 12 eV inner potential borrowed from refs. [21, 22]. The on-resonance "heavy FS" is then identified because its size/shape matches the calculated band-4 hole FS "at k_z = 0.3π/c away from the Γ-Z-X plane" — i.e., identification by matching to a calculation at exactly the k_z the assumed inner potential predicts. An alternative, mundane reading: the new EF crossing at the X–Z midpoint simply reflects the fact that on- and off-resonance data are cut at *different k_z*, and no band assignment at all is required. The paper does not rule this out.

### 5. Composition and sample details
Only a single doped composition (x = 0.18, from the sample-growth procedure of Ref. 6) stands in for "CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ near the QCP." No information on actual x (e.g., from XRD/lattice parameter), site disorder, or inhomogeneity is given, despite the argument hinging on where that composition sits relative to x_c = 0.07. Given that x_c itself was established from transport/magnetization in one body of work, composition uncertainty of a few percent is material.

## Minor issues

- **Energy resolution 120–170 meV** is coarse for the 4f spectral features; the energy-resolution difference between CeRu₂Si₂ and the Ge sample (120 vs 160 meV at resonance) is not justified.
- **Apparent contradiction left hanging:** off-resonance 4f FS *not* seen (caveat above), on-resonance large FS *disappears*. The net picture — which parts of the FS are actually 4f-derived — is weaker than the abstract's flat "strong 4f-electron character was observed… indicating delocalized nature in both compounds."
- The abstract and conclusion read as if the LQC question was decided; the body concedes it wasn't. The abstract should carry the caveat.
- **Provenance oddity:** the arXiv stamp is June 2009 but the PDF dates July 14, 2021 (a late arXiv republication of older work); the "Dated: July 14, 2021" line in the manuscript reflects that. Not a scientific defect, but worth noting that the field context (refs. up to 2008) is that of 2009.

## Bottom line

A competent, well-instrumented experiment with one genuinely informative result (clean Ce vs. La FS difference, consistent with itinerant 4f in nonmagnetic CeRu₂Si₂) and one interesting observation (on-resonance FS near the BZ boundary in the AFM compound). But the paper's headline contribution — SDW vs. LQC — is drawn from data at a temperature and composition that, by the models' own logic, cannot discriminate the two. The two most vulnerable steps (on-resonance FS attribution and k_z identification) are each individually hedged in the paper; together they carry a conclusion stated without the hedge. The follow-up the authors list (below-T_N resonant ARPES as a function of x across x_c) is exactly the experiment needed to make the claim.

## [0906.3078v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


I have read the full paper (Okané et al., *4f*-derived Fermi surfaces of CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ near the quantum critical point: resonant soft X-ray ARPES study, arXiv:0906.3078v1, submitted 2009). My evaluation follows.

## The paper's stated contribution, in my own words

The authors set out to settle, with a direct Fermi-surface (FS) measurement, two questions about the Kondo quantum critical point at x_c ≈ 0.07 of the heavy-fermion series CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂: (i) whether the Ce 4f electrons are itinerant (participating in the FS) in the paramagnetic state on both sides of the critical composition, and (ii) which critical model — spin-density-wave (SDW) vs. local quantum criticality (LQC) — applies, since LQC predicts a 4f-localization / FS reconstruction crossover near the QCP while SDW does not.

Their experimental contribution is Ce 3d→4f **resonant** soft X-ray ARPES at SPring-8 (BL23SU) on three single crystals at T ≈ 20 K (above the Néel temperature T_N of the x = 0.18 compound, at/below the Kondo crossover T₀): CeRu₂Si₂ (x = 0, paramagnetic side), CeRu₂(Si₀.₈₂Ge₀.₁₈)₂ (x = 0.18, ordered side measured in the paramagnetic state), and LaRu₂Si₂ as a 4f-less reference. By combining off-resonance angle-scanning with on-resonance measurements and hν-scanning (which resolves k_z), they (a) confirm the FS of CeRu₂Si₂ is clearly different from LaRu₂Si₂'s — a tube-like band-5 electron FS vs. closed ellipsoids — and (b) reveal, *only* under 4f resonance enhancement, an additional heavy-quasiparticle FS inside the Z-center square boundary matching the calculated strongly c-f hybridized band-4 hole FS. Crucially, the x = 0.18 compound's FS (both off-resonance and on-resonance) is essentially identical to that of x = 0: there is **no FS reconstruction across x_c**, which they take as evidence favoring the SDW over the LQC scenario, arguing that the absence of reconstruction at T ≈ T₀ strongly implies its persistence on cooling toward T_N since no phase transition intervenes.

## Is it novel relative to the cited prior work?

**What is new:**

1. **The straddling-the-QCP comparison itself.** Yano et al. [22] (soft X-ray ARPES, cited and acknowledged) compared the *endpoints* of the phase diagram — paramagnetic CeRu₂Si₂ (delocalized FS) vs. paramagnetic CeRu₂Ge₂ (localized FS). That cannot distinguish SDW from LQC, because localization appears anyway at the far Ge end. The present work's genuinely new act is measuring a composition *just above* x_c (x = 0.18) in the paramagnetic state and directly comparing it with x = 0. The observation of no FS change between these two points is the first direct spectroscopic probe of the LQC-predicted reconstruction window in this family.
2. **Technical: element-specific resonant isolation of the 4f-derived bands.** Denlinger et al. [21] (VUV ARPES) had *failed* to resolve any FS difference between CeRu₂Si₂ and LaRu₂Si₂; the authors explicitly frame their result as resolving that discrepancy. The use of the Ce 3d→4f resonance to enhance the strongly c-f hybridized band-4 FS, plus hν-scanning to access the k_z-dependent 3D FS shape (tube vs. ellipsoid as the diagnostic), is a meaningful experimental advance over both prior ARPES studies.
3. **Resolving the band assignment:** linking the on-resonance FS to the specific calculated band-4 hole FS at k_z = 0.3π/c is a concrete new identification, not just a qualitative "4f character" statement.

**What is *not* new:**

1. **The framework and the inference logic.** The SDW-vs-LQC decision criterion (does the 4f-delocalized FS survive across the QCP?) comes directly from the LQC program (Si et al. [12,13] and the review [1]); the itinerant-vs-localized dichotomy, the dHvA evidence (band-4 heavy FS in CeRu₂Si₂ [8]; localized 4f in CeRu₂Ge₂ [9–11]), and the band-structure predictions [7] are all pre-existing. The paper applies an established logic with better data.
2. **The delocalized FS of CeRu₂Si₂ in the paramagnetic state** was already claimed by Yano et al. [22]; the authors confirm but do not discover it (they mainly fix Denlinger's non-observation). Confirmation with a resonance-enhanced technique is an extension, not a novelty, per se.
3. **Conclusions about the LQC scenario remain circumstantial within the paper's own bounds.** The reconstruction LQC predicts occurs at T well *below* T₀; the measurements were made at T ≈ T₀, and the authors acknowledge (conceding it to "future studies") that the crossover *below* T_N was not measured. The step "no reconstruction at T ≈ T₀ ⇒ SDW" is a reasonable continuity argument but is inference, not direct observation. This weakens the headline claim substantially.
4. **Internal caveats the paper itself raises**: the heaviest band-4 hole FS is *not* seen off-resonance, leaving part of the large Z-surrounding FS unassigned; only one composition above x_c is measured; the result is a negative result (absence of reconstruction) in a single material family, so it cannot arbitrate the field-wide SDW-vs-LQC debate the introduction advertises.

## Verdict

This is a competent, technically improved, first-of-its-kind measurement in an actively debated system: it fills a real gap (the Yano/Denlinger discrepancy; the missing above-x_c paramagnetic FS) with clean comparison to band-structure calculations, and its no-reconstruction result is a legitimately novel data point against LQC in this family. But the intellectual content — the criterion, the dichotomy, the expected physics — is inherited from the cited LQC/SDW literature and from Yano et al.'s own earlier ARPES; the contribution is a strong experimental follow-through rather than a new idea. The central model-selection claim rests on a negative result measured at the wrong edge of the temperature window (T ≈ T₀ rather than just above T_N), and in a single family, which limits both its internal weight and its field impact. ## [0906.3078v1-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


# Evaluation of arXiv:0906.3078v1 — *"4f-derived Fermi Surfaces of CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ near the Quantum Critical Point: Resonant Soft X-ray ARPES Study"* (Okan et al.)

## 1. Central question and approach

The paper asks, in the paramagnetic heavy-fermion series CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ which orders at x_c ≈ 0.07, whether the Ce 4f Fermi surface reconstructs when crossing x_c — a **spin-density-wave (SDW)** quantum critical point should show no Fermi-surface (FS) change (the heavy FS smoothly evolves across the QCP at any T > T_N), whereas **local quantum criticality (LQC)** should show a FS crossover (heavy FS → light FS) *above* T_N, just below the Kondo temperature T₀. The authors measure the FSs with soft X-ray ARPES and report: (i) the FSs of CeRu₂Si₂ and LaRu₂Si₂ differ (4f itinerant in the Ce compound) and (ii) no FS reconstruction occurs going x = 0 → 0.18, which they take as evidence for the SDW scenario.

## 2. Reproducibility-level method

**Samples and preparation.** Single crystals of CeRu₂Si₂, CeRu₂(Si₀.₈₂Ge₀.₁₈)₂ and LaRu₂Si₂, grown by the procedure of Ref. 6 (Sugi, *PRL* 101, 056401 (2008)). Clean surfaces by *in situ* cleavage along [001]. Sample temperature 20 K (above T_N ≈ 8 K of the x = 0.18 compound; comparable to/below T₀ ≈ 20–25 K of CeRu₂Si₂). Beamline BL23SU at SPring-8 (proposal 2008A3822).

**Two complementary measurement modes:**

1. *Angle-scanning, "off-resonance":* photon energy chosen per compound (hν = 765, 860, 855 eV for LaRu₂Si₂, CeRu₂Si₂, and CeRu₂(Si₀.₈₂Ge₀.₁₈)₂ respectively) so that the final-state kz lands on the Γ–X–Z plane, using an assumed inner potential of 12 eV (borrowed from Refs. 21, 22). Energy resolution 170 meV (770–870 eV). This maps dispersions along X–Z and Γ–X, and constant-energy (near-E_F) maps in k_x–k_y.

2. *hν-scanning, "on-resonance":* sweep hν over 750–870 eV to map FS intensity vs (k_xy, k_z) using the same flat-band-final-state/kz–mapping; separately, angle scanning at the Ce 3d→4f resonance (hν = 881 eV, 120 meV resolution for CeRu₂Si₂, 160 meV for the x = 0.18 compound) to enhance 4f photoemission. The resonance corresponds to k_z = 0.29π/c (CeRu₂Si₂) and 0.37π/c (x = 0.18) — *different* k_z planes for the two Ce compounds, a subtlety the paper notes but does not correct for.

**Analysis.** FS images are intensity maps around E_F in k_x–k_y (constant k_z) and (k_xy, k_z) (constant-energy contour over hν), integrated near the Fermi energy. For the on-resonance data, second-derivative images with momentum-normalized MDCs were made to reveal weak EF-crossing bands. Experimental sheets are compared to 1992 DFT/LDA band-structure/FS calculations of CeRu₂Si₂ and LaRu₂Si₂ (Yamagami, JPSJ 61, 2388 and 62, 592) — those DFT FS surfaces (bands 1–4 holes + band 5 electron) are essentially part of the "measurement" since the assignment of experimental FSs relies on them.

## 3. Assumptions the methodology papers over

- **Flat final-state / inner potential = 12 eV.** The whole hν-scanning FS mapping, and the claim that the two "off-resonance" hν values select the Γ–X–Z plane, rest on the standard ARPES final-state-model assumption with an inner potential adopted from earlier work on different CeRu₂X₂ compounds. A 1–2 eV shift in inner potential moves the sampled k_z by ≳ 0.1π/c, moving the comparison off the planes the DFT reference FSs were cut on. This is never tested.

- **T = 20 K is the right temperature — and is actually the weakest link of the claim.** LQC predicts a heavy→light FS change that *onsets below T₀*; here T ≈ T₀. So the experiment sits exactly at the temperature where an LQC crossover may just begin and may grow on cooling. The authors' argument that "it is natural to think the 4f electrons keep their itinerancy on cooling from T₀ to just above T_N" is, by their own admission (the "future studies" paragraph — a crossover below T_N "should be examined"), an unmeasured extrapolation. The absence of FS reconstruction is therefore a *null result at one temperature*, but is reported in the abstract/conclusion as a positive evidence that "SDW is more appropriate than LQC."

- **Two compositions, straddling the QCP coarsely.** x = 0 and x = 0.18 vs. x_c ≈ 0.07; there is no point within a few percent of x_c, and — critically — no measurement on the *magnetically ordered* side at finite x between x_c and 0.18 at any temperature above T_N either. A gradual reconstruction over 2 × 100 K and 18 at.% composition change would not be visible in two-point comparison.

- **FS identification from a constant-E_F intensity window.** With 120–170 meV energy resolution and Fermi-function broadening at 20 K (k_B T ≈ 1.7 meV but the resolution dominates), any small FS branch, thin tube, or reconstruction fragment with little spectral weight near E_F is below detection. The paper openly notes that the "hole FS of band 4" — the heaviest branch, the one that *should* show the largest 4f content — is **not observed off-resonance**, and concedes "the above assignment for the Ce compounds is not conclusive." The delocalization conclusion then leans almost entirely on the on-resonance data.

- **"On-resonance ⇒ 4f character."** The identification of the extra on-resonance EF-crossing bands as "strongly c-f hybridized heavy-quasiparticle FS" uses the heuristic that resonant enhancement = 4f. But Ce 3d→4f resonance ARPES is not a pure 4f projector: the final-state multiplet physics mixes in conduction-band photoemission channels, and the paper itself notes the resonance enhancement is *weak near X* and *invisible to the large circular FS around Z* in the on-resonance images. The large off-resonance FS goes away on resonance, while a new FS "just inside the square BZ boundary around Z" appears; assigning that to band 4 by size-and-shape match with the 1992 DFT surface at k_z ≈ 0.3π/c is an inference, not an identification.

- **Different hν (hence different k_z) for the two Ce compounds on resonance.** k_z = 0.29π/c vs 0.37π/c. The side-by-side conclusion "the large circular FS ... is almost identical to that of CeRu₂Si₂" / "tube-like shape as in CeRu₂Si₂" compares FSs from *different k_z slices*, and the paper does not control for k_z dependence.

- **Different energy resolution per compound (120 vs 160 meV on resonance, 170 meV off resonance)** is treated as unimportant; a 50 meV difference is enough to blur or reveal a weak EF-crossing band, and it is again correlated with the sample identity.

- **DFT band structure (1992) is silently assumed to be reliable enough for one-to-one FS assignment.** No DFT (or other) check is done for the Ge-substituted compound; all band/FS numbering and the k_z-slice predictions in the x = 0.18 sample are inherited from the end-point calculations.

- **In situ cleaved surface is assumed bulk-representative.** No check for surface reconstruction, dead layer (120–170 meV resolution ≈ a few Å⁻¹ in k_z, but mean-free-path effects at these hν still bias toward a few Å), or residual non-stoichiometry. Growth composition x = 0.18 is taken at face value; no chemical analysis is reported. Pressure/dosing are not given.

- **The LaRu₂Si₂ reference plays a double role** — it is both the "4f absent" baseline and the DFT anchor — so part of the "difference between Ce and La" that the paper reports is a comparison between the *same sample's* ARPES and the *same DFT* surface (Fig. 3(a) vs 3(d) "agrees well"), which is circular as a validation.

- **Temperature comparability.** "20 K is comparable to T₀ of CeRu₂Si₂" is stated loosely; T₀ is cited as 20–25 K, so the data are exactly at or above the Kondo-crossover temperature, not safely below it. Combined with the absence of low-T data, the SDW-vs-LQC verdict is made on the borderline case.

- **No magnetic-domain / field control.** CeRu₂(Si₀.₈₂Ge₀.₁₈)₂ is antiferromagnetic below T_N ≈ 8 K; the 20 K measurement is ~12 K above T_N, so proximity to T_N may already perturb the paramagnetic state, but no temperature series is taken to check for pre-transitional FS smearing.

## 4. Bottom line

The experiment itself is a competent, fairly standard soft X-ray resonant ARPES study, and it *does* show what it claims at the resolution and temperature used: a large 4f-heavy FS in CeRu₂Si₂ that is absent in LaRu₂Si₂, and no *observable* change between x = 0 and x = 0.18 at 20 K. But the headline conclusion — that SDW, not LQC, describes the QCP at x_c ≈ 0.07 — is a **two-point, one-temperature null result** made at T ≈ T₀, where the LQC signature is precisely expected to be most subtle. The paper is candid enough to flag the missing band-4 FS and the need for below-T_N measurements, which is why the abstract's stronger claim is the over-statement, not the body. The methodological assumptions above (flat final state with a borrowed 12 eV inner potential, 20 K temperature, two compositions only, constant-E_F integration at 120–170 meV, k_z mismatch between the two Ce samples on resonance, and assignment-by-shape-match to a 1992 DFT surface) collectively mean the experiment is *consistent with* both SDW and slow-onset LQC and does not discriminate between them. A low-T (few-K) ARPES series in x between 0 and 0.28 would resolve the question.

## My understanding of the paper's logical argument

The paper sits in the heavy-fermion/quantum-critical-point (QCP) literature and tries to settle a specific, foundational question: does the QCP of CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ at x_c ≈ 0.07 follow the **spin-density-wave (SDW) QCP scenario** (no Fermi-surface [FS] reconstruction; Kondo screening collapses) or the **local quantum-criticality (LQC) scenario** (a sudden heavy→light FS reconstruction, with the Ce 4f electrons becoming localized on one side and itinerant on the other)?

The logical chain proceeds as:

1. **Setup.** CeRu₂Si₂ (x = 0) is antiferromagnetic; Ge substitution drives it through x_c = 0.07 into ferromagnetism. The QCP is the Kondo-screening (T₀ ≈ 20 K) → magnetic-transition crossover, so it is the ideal probe. Distinguishing the models hinges on whether the 4f electrons stay itinerant (join the FS) or localize across the transition.

2. **Technique.** Because 4f contribution to the FS is hard to see, they use **soft x-ray resonant ARPES** in the Ce 3d→4f region (CeRu₂Si₂ at 881 eV, x=0.18 at 870 eV) to enhance the 4f photoemission, cross-checked with an off-resonance mode and against **LaRu₂Si₂** (reference in which the 4f electrons do *not* participate in the FS).

## The weakest link: from a null FS comparison at T ≈ T₀ to "SDW rather than LQC"

**The argument chain, compressed:**
1. The LQC scenario predicts a Fermi-surface reconstruction (Kondo breakdown: delocalized → localized 4f) as the critical composition x_c = 0.07 is crossed; the SDW-QCP scenario does not (paper's Fig. 1).
2. Soft-x-ray ARPEs on x = 0 (paramagnetic side) and x = 0.18 (antiferromagnetic side) in the paramagnetic state show the same large, strongly c–f-hybridized, 4f-heavy Fermi surface, and both differ clearly from LaRu₂Si₂ — so the 4f electrons are itinerant on both sides.
3. **Therefore ("It can be claimed that the SDW-QCP model is suitable ... rather than the LQC model"), LQC is disfavored and SDW is preferred.**

**Step 3 is where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data.**

The decisive LQC prediction is about the *ground-state / low-temperature* FS on the antiferromagnetic side: heavy FS in the paramagnetic phase, light (local-moment) FS once the Kondo breakdown is crossed. But the experiment was performed at 20 K, which the authors themselves call "comparable to T₀," the Kondo crossover temperature — i.e., exactly at the edge of the breakdown crossover (their own Fig. 1 draws the LQC "FS transition" line running from the QCP *up to* T₀). Above (or right at) T* ~ T₀, screened itinerant 4f is what the LQC scenario itself predicts even on the AF side. The measurements were thus made in precisely the regime where SDW and LQC make the **same** prediction: a heavy FS. To distinguish them, one would have needed T well below T* but above T_N (~8 K) on the AF side, or a scan down to T→0 across x_c — neither was done.

The gap is bridged by an *unmeasured premise*, stated as "it is natural to think that the Ce 4f electrons keep their itinerancy without a phase transition" on cooling from T₀ to just above T_N — i.e., the paper assumes the very thing in question (that no LQC reconstruction develops below T₀) and then cites the absence of a reconstruction at T₀ as evidence for SDW. That is an absence-of-evidence inference, and it fails for concrete reasons:

1. **Temperature sits on the crossover itself.** No null result at T ≈ T₀ can rule out a reconstruction that is *defined* to occur on the LQC line, which the paper's own schematic places between the QCP and T₀.
2. **The positive test actually failed, and was explained away as an instrumental limit.** In Fig. 5 the strongly c–f hybridized heavy hole FS predicted on the AF side *does not appear* on resonance; the authors attribute its absence to "the weakness of the resonance enhancement near the X Point" and openly call the band-4 assignment "not conclusive." A null result traced to limited sensitivity cannot be converted into evidence for the competing model.
3. **Resolution is far too coarse for the relevant scales.** 120–160 meV ≈ 1,400 K resolution versus a Kondo scale of k_B·T₀ ≈ 1.7 meV — FS evolution of the kind produced by a Kondo breakdown (meV-scale renormalizations, mass divergence, FSL-broken dispersions) would simply not be visible.
4. Only one composition on each side, both far from x_c, is used; even a genuine LQC crossover in |x − x_c| would not necessarily manifest at x = 0 vs x = 0.18.

So the data support an entirely defensible, weaker conclusion — *no sudden FS change was detected between these two compositions at 20 K* — but they do not entail the modal claim that "the SDW-QCP model is more appropriate." All the experiment actually establishes is that the 4f remains itinerant up to T₀, which is consistent with the LQC picture above its breakdown line and does not adjudicate the controversy below it, as the authors' own "future studies" line implicitly concedes.

# Peer Review

**Paper:** T. Okane *et al.*, "4f-derived Fermi Surfaces of CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ near the Quantum Critical Point: Resonant Soft X-ray ARPES Study" (arXiv:0906.3078v1)

## Summary of the work

The authors use angle-resolved photoemission in the Ce 3d→4f resonant soft-X-ray regime (Spring-8, BL23SU) to probe the Ce 4f character of the Fermi surface (FS) of the heavy-fermion metamagnet CeRu₂(Si₁₋ₓGeₓ)₂ on either side of its magnetic QCP (x_c ≈ 0.07). Two paramagnetic-side/AF-side Ce compositions (x = 0 and x = 0.18) are measured, with LaRu₂Si₂ (localized 4f, no heavy FS) as reference. Off-resonance and on-resonance FS maps are compared, and the measured dispersions are matched to prior band-structure calculations. The headline result: a strongly 4f-hybridized heavy-quasiparticle FS appears on resonance in *both* Ce compounds, so 4f electrons remain itinerant across the critical composition—interpreted as evidence favoring a spin-density-wave (SDW) QCP over the local-quantum-criticality (LQC) scenario that predicts a Kondo/FS crossover below T₀.

## Strengths

1. **Technically demanding and well-motivated method.** Resonant soft-X-ray ARPES across the Ce 3d→4f edge is a genuine experimental achievement here, and it is the right tool for the physics question: it selectively enhances 4f spectral weight and lets the authors separate c–f hybridized bands from bands of predominantly Ru 4d character. The use of on- vs. off-resonance contrast is a clean and clever diagnostic.

2. **Appropriate reference system.** Including LaRu₂Si₂ (4f localized, 4f do not join the FS) anchors the interpretation of what "4f participation" looks like in this material family, which strengthens the itinerant-vs-localized argument.

3. **A genuinely interesting observation.** The on-resonance emergence of an additional FS pocket (the "band 4" heavy-quasiparticle surface near k_z ≈ 0.37π/c) that is essentially absent off-resonance, together with the simultaneous *suppression* of the large Ru-4d-dominated Z-point FS on resonance, is a non-trivial and physically informative result. Derivative spectra to pull out the extra dispersive band are a reasonable data-handling choice.

4. **Coherent, focused experimental design.** The two-photon-energy choice via an inner-potential argument, the choice of temperature (T_N < T < T₀), and the comparison to 3D calculated band structures are all handled competently.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The central conclusion is overclaimed relative to the data.** The paper's main claim—"SDW-QCP is more appropriate than local quantum criticality"—rests on the *absence* of an FS reconstruction across x = 0.07. But there are two problems with the logic as written:

   - In the LQC picture of Fig. 1, the c–f Kondo/FS crossover is expected at a temperature **below T₀**, whereas the data were taken at ~20 K ≈ T₀ and above T_N. Above T₀ the LQC model also predicts a large, 4f-participating FS. Hence a measurement *above* T₀ showing persistence of a large FS is **not** able to distinguish SDW from LQC; only a low-temperature (below T₀, and below the ordering temperatures) series would be expected to show the LQC crossover. The authors' own closing sentence ("the crossover below the magnetic ordering temperature should be examined…") concedes this. Therefore, as presented, the data are *consistent with* SDW but do not exclude LQC. The word "can be claimed that the SDW-QCP model is suitable" reads as stronger than warranted. This needs to be either stated more cautiously or backed by low-T data.

   - **Only two Ce compositions are measured (x = 0 and 0.18) bracketing x_c = 0.07.** "No reconstruction across the critical composition" is an inference from a coarse 0→0.18 interpolation. A pronounced but x_c-localized reconstruction could plausibly be missed. At minimum the text should make explicit that the absence of a change is established only at this coarse composition resolution, and ideally at least one intermediate composition (e.g., x ≈ 0.07–0.12) should be discussed or shown.

2. **Interpretation is partly circular with respect to the reference calculations.** The 3D FS labeling and the "delocalized 4f" conclusion are anchored to the calculations of Denlinger et al. and Yano et al. (Refs. 21, 22), which presuppose the itinerant picture the paper is trying to support. The authors are honest about residual ambiguity (e.g., the "not conclusive" off-resonance absence of the band-4 hole branch; the note that both the Ce FS and the La FS are Ru-4d dominated at the BZ boundary), but the review should weigh this: much of the quantitative support for band assignment and the volume comparison is inherited from prior calculations rather than measured independently.

3. **The vanishing of the large Z-point FS on resonance is hand-waved.** The claim that the large, circular off-resonance FS "becomes invisible" on resonance "due to the weakness of the resonance enhancement near the X point" is important, because a 4f-dominant surface would be *expected to grow* on resonance. Explaining its disappearance as a pure Ru-4d origin is physically sensible but is only stated, not demonstrated. Given that *which* surface carries the 4f weight is the crux of the paper, this merits a clearer argument (e.g., k_z dependence of the resonance ratio, or line-shape analysis), not a one-sentence attribution.

## Minor points

- Energy resolution (120–160 meV) is modest for cleanly resolving the additional on-resonance bands; a brief statement on systematic uncertainties from the derivative processing and from the inner-potential assumption would strengthen reproducibility.
- The schematic phase diagram (Fig. 1) is borrowed from Ref. 1; it would help to make explicit which of its features are model assumptions rather than measured.
- Several claims of "remarkably similar" dispersions (CeRu₂Si₂ vs. x = 0.18) would benefit from even one quantified metric (e.g., a Fermi-wavevector shift) rather than purely visual comparison.
- The title promises a study "near the QCP," but the two compositions are 0 and 0.18, i.e., one well below and one well above x_c = 0.07; the title overstates how close to the critical point the data lie.

## Recommendation

The paper reports a solid, technically accomplished resonant-ARPES measurement with a genuinely interesting on-resonance observation of a 4f-hybridized heavy-quasiparticle surface. However, the headline conclusion—preferring SDW quantum criticality over LQC—does not follow from the present data: the measurements are taken at T ≈ T₀ (above the temperature where LQC predicts the FS crossover) and at only two compositions spanning, rather than sampling, the critical composition. The SDW-vs-LQC distinction the paper set out to address is therefore not actually resolved; it is left as "consistent with SDW."

For a top-tier venue the argument must be either (i) strengthened with low-temperature, below-T₀ measurements and additional compositions near x_c, or (ii) rewritten so that the claim is the more defensible "4f electrons remain itinerant and form a heavy FS on both sides of x_c at T ≈ T₀," with the SDW/LQC inference stated explicitly as a consistent-but-not-exclusive interpretation. With that framing tightened, the work is a competent, positive contribution to the heavy-fermion criticality literature.
