## [1804.08503v2-general-review] General Review


I read the full paper (Battaglia–Prato–Zaffran, "Hirzebruch surfaces in a one‑parameter family", arXiv:1804.08503v2). Here is my review.

## What the paper does

The central idea is to extend the Hirzebruch family $\mathbb F_n$ (indexed by positive integers $n$) to a one‑parameter family $\mathbb F_a$ indexed by positive **real** numbers $a$, and to view each $\mathbb F_a$ from three complementary angles:

1. **Kähler toric quasifold** — via the Delzant (symplectic) and [BP1] (complex) constructions applied to the non‑rational smooth trapezoid $P_a$ with vertices $(0,0),(0,1),(a+1,1),(1,0)$ and the quasilattice $Q_a=\mathbb Z\times(\mathbb Z+a\mathbb Z)$. For $a=n\in\mathbb Z$ this recovers $\mathbb F_n$; for rational non‑integer $a=p/q$ it is a Kähler toric orbifold with two order‑$q$ singular divisors; for irrational $a$ it is a genuine quasifold.
2. **Leaf space of a compact foliated manifold** — a generalized LVM datum $(\Lambda,\mathcal T^*)$ with a fifth "ghost vector" $(0,-a)$, producing $(N_a,\mathcal F_a)$ of complex dimension 3 with leaves $(S^1)^2$ (rational) or $S^1\times\mathbb R$ (irrational), whose closures give the correct irrational behaviour, and whose leaf space is exactly $\mathbb F_a$.
3. **Arbitrary toric cut of $\mathbb C\times S^2$** — the trapezoid $P_a$ is obtained by cutting the strip $[0,\infty)\times[0,1]$ with the *irrationally* sloped line $x=ay+1$, which is impossible in the classical Lerman cut; the authors' non‑rational cutting machinery [BP2] gives $\mathbb F_a$ as that cut, and the paper then shows the cut decomposes as $S^2/\Gamma_a$ (compact) plus a dense open subset of $\mathbb C\times S^2/\Gamma_a$.

## Strengths

- **Good unifying narrative.** Tying together non‑rational Delzant/quasifold constructions, foliated (LVM) models, and symplectic cuts around a single, concrete and well‑known family is exactly the kind of "two perspectives on one object" story that makes hard machinery legible. For readers who know only the integer Hirzebruch surfaces, it concretely *shows* what goes wrong at irrational slope (the cutting line has no normal vector in $\mathbb Z^2$) and how the quasifield framework repairs it.
- **A genuinely useful observation.** The claim (citing [BZ2 Theorem 2.1]) that the *complex leaf space* does not depend on the choice of Gale dual, while the *leaf complex structure* does, is a clean and non‑trivial statement: infinitely many foliated manifolds $(\Lambda,\mathcal T^*)\to N/\mathcal F$ sit over the *same* leaf space $\mathbb F_a$. The diagram on p. 10 is one of the clearest places in the paper.
- **The Section 3 decomposition** — $\mathbb F_a$ as a disjoint union of $S^2/\Gamma_a$ and a dense open subset of $(\mathbb C\times S^2)/\Gamma_a$, with the orbifold quotient map for $a=p/q$ giving $\Gamma_a=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$ — is a concrete takeaway that is not just formal.
- The presymplectic side is handled correctly and the standard transverse‑Kähler result ([M] Thm 7) is invoked appropriately to show $\Psi_a^{-1}(0)/S^1$ is presymplectic with tautological foliation sent diffeomorphically to $\mathcal F_a$.

## Weaknesses and problems

1. **No theorem statements.** The paper's two headline claims — "the leaf space is *exactly* $\mathbb F_a$" (abstract, Intro, §2.2) and "$\mathbb F_a$ is the toric cut of $\mathbb C\times S^2$ in arbitrary direction" — are asserted in the body and pushed into prior work ([BZ2], [BP2]) rather than stated, proved, or at least formulated as theorems. For a paper whose abstract says "we introduce a family", the central results should carry theorem numbering with the verification steps that *are* in the paper (e.g., the moment map computation and the two open-set descriptions in §3) tied to them.
2. **The "quasifold" is a singular leaf space, but no regularity is discussed.** A leaf space of a non‑closed foliation need not be Hausdorff or even locally Euclidean. The paper freely writes "leaf space is exactly $\mathbb F_a$" and then treats $\mathbb F_a$ as the non‑rational toric quasifold, but never says in which category the identification lives, nor whether $\mathbb F_a$ is Hausdorff. The irrational case — the mathematically interesting new content — is exactly where this matters most, and it is left implicit.
3. **Figures/captions are sloppy.**
   - Figure 1 (p. 1) is captioned "The fan $\Delta_2$" but the surrounding sentence reads "for $n=2$ the slanting ray passes through $(-1,n)$" — the fan is actually drawn for a general/irrelevant value, and the caption should say so or the figure should show $\Delta_2$.
   - Figure 4 is captioned "The fan $\Delta_{\sqrt2}$" (an irrational slope) and is the correct illustration of the non‑rational phenomenon — good — but the body on p. 4 still refers to Figure 1 as the $n=2$ example in a context that should be the general one.
   - In Figure 7, the point labelled "1=4" and the "chamber" are hard to decode without the construction in hand; a one‑line legend would help.
4. **Reduction to the integer case is left implicit.** The statement "$a=n$ gives the standard Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb F_n$" appears in §1.2, but a short theorem/remark spelling out that each of the three models specializes correctly (quasifold $=\!$ smooth toric variety, leaf space $=\!$ the classical foliation of a known 3‑manifold, cut $=\!$ the classical Lerman cut recovering $\mathbb F_n$) would remove all doubt about the extension being faithful.
5. **Minor: notation collision.** $\mathcal F_a$ is used both for the holomorphic foliation on $N_a$ (§2.2), its conjugate $\overline{\mathcal F}_a$, and — in the §2.3 diagram — as the tautological presymplectic foliation on $\Psi_a^{-1}(0)/S^1$. The paper says the latter is "sent diffeomorphically to $\mathcal F_a$", so the identification is legitimate, but a distinct symbol for the presymplectic one until the diffeomorphism is established would avoid confusion on a first read.
6. **Scope is narrow.** With one example family and results delegated heavily to the second/third authors' earlier papers, this is closer to a focused case study with a survey flavor than a source of many new facts. That is fine for the contribution's intended size, but it limits impact: the reader who already has [P], [BP1], [BP2], [BZ1], [BZ2] gains mainly the consolidation, and the reader who does not has to take a lot of machinery on faith.
7. **The "infinitely many foliated manifolds, one leaf space" statement** would be sharper with a sentence noting *what* varies (the choice of Gale dual / chamber $\mathcal T^*$ among the infinite triangulated vector configurations encoding the same triple $(\Delta_a,\{X_i\},Q_a)$) and *why* (the leaf space depends only on the triple, the leaf complex structure on the Gale dual) — both ingredients are in the text but not synthesized into one pointed claim.

## Verdict

A technically correct, clearly motivated, well‑written short paper extending a classical family to non‑rational parameters using the authors' own non‑rational toric machinery, with the nice integrative payoff of three equivalent viewpoints and a clean statement about the independence of the leaf space from Gale‑dual choice. Its main shortcomings are presentational (no theorem statements, missing Hausdorff/leaf‑space regularity discussion, a couple of figure/caption slips) and the modesty of the new content — it primarily applies and consolidates existing theorems to one concrete family rather than proving new structural results. Appropriate for a venue matching its length and scope; a revised version with explicit theorems, a short specialization‑to‑$\mathbb F_n$ statement, and the figure fixes would be noticeably stronger.

### Overview

The paper extends the classical one-parameter family of Hirzebruch surfaces 𝔽ₙ (parametrized by positive integers) to a family 𝔽ₐ parametrized by arbitrary positive real numbers a. Each 𝔽ₐ is realized as a **Kähler toric quasifold**, and the authors give it from two complementary perspectives that were unavailable to them in the classical (rational) setting:

1. **As a complex leaf space** of a compact foliated manifold (Nₐ,𝒜ₐ), following their prior machinery of vector/point configurations, Gale duality, and "chambers" (BZ1); the presymplectic-foliated variant (BZ2) is treated in §2.3.
2. **As a symplectic cut** of the noncompact toric manifold ℂ×S² in a direction that is rationally sloped only when a is rational, using the "arbitrary-direction" cutting procedure of BP2 (§3). They also give an alternative realization via a toric blow-up of a weighted projective plane.

### Strengths

- **Clean, pedagogical unification.** The real virtue of the paper is that it takes one familiar object and renders it in the three languages of the authors' program (quasifolds, non-LVM foliations, nonrational symplectic cuts). This makes it a genuinely useful worked model / testing ground for the nonrational toric geometry machinery. The "diagram of choices" in §2.2, distinguishing where the leaf-space *complex structure* depends on the Gale dual from where the *underlying smooth type* does not, is well worth the paper.
- **Crucial distinction on the leaf.** The behavior of the generic leaf (torus (S¹)² for rational a, S¹×ℝ for irrational a, with closure (S¹)² vs. (S¹)³) and the q-sheeted wrapping analysis over the rational leaf are the kind of concrete, checkable predictions that make the theory tangible.
- **Correct and careful.** I don't spot errors; the singular sets, the quasigroup Γₐ and its relation to ℤ₂, and the moment maps carry through consistently. The figures are genuinely helpful (nonrational fan, trapezoid, cut diagrams).
- **Honest about scope.** The authors are explicit that the topology of 𝔽ₐ changes on rationality and that this is *not* a deformation — a point of potential confusion handled upfront, correctly invoking Hirzebruch's parity result on the two diffeomorphism types.

- **Originality is real but modest.** Nearly every technical ingredient (quasifolds, nonrational Delzant, nonrational cutting, vector/point configurations, the LVM generalization) is cited from the authors' own preceding works (P, BP1, BP2, BZ1, BZ2, BZ3). The paper is best described as an elegant *application and synthesis* of existing machinery to a classical family rather than the introduction of a new technique. Nothing here would be hard for a reader fluent in BZ2 to reconstruct.

There is no computation of invariants (e.g., cohomology ring, equivariant cohomology, a quasifold moment polytope invariant) that the nonrational framework would predict new answers for, no comparison with other nonrational families, and no application that a non-expert would find striking. The "infinitely many presymplectic foliated models for each 𝔽ₐ" remark is interesting but developed only as a pointer (BZ3, in preparation).

### Verdict

A well-executed, correctly written paper whose main contribution is the coherent, multi-perspective package it provides for the nonrational continuation of a celebrated family. It is not a result that changes the field and introduces essentially no new machinery beyond what the authors have already published; its value is expository-unifying and it will be most useful to people working in nonrational toric geometry / holomorphic foliations, for whom it is a clean canonical example. I would regard publication as justified but not high-impact.

I checked the computational pieces and they hold up:

- **Cutting moment maps (Section 3).** The cutting S¹ action (u,[v:z])↦(e^{−2πit}u,[e^{2πiat}v:z]) with moment Φ_Y = −|u|² + a(z+1)/2 is consistent: cutting at level −1 gives |u|² = 1 + a·m_v in moment coordinates (x=|u|², y=(z+1)/2 for the unit-height strip), i.e. exactly the slicing line x = 1 + ay of the trapezoid P_a through (1,0) and (a+1,1). The "factor ½" concern cancels once one notes the strip's width convention [0,∞)×[0,1] is consistent with the same normalisation in the trapezoid.

## [1804.08503v2-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


This is Battaglia–Prato–Zaffran, "Hirzebruch surfaces in a one-parameter family" (arXiv:1804.08503). The abstract already gives the two claims, and I've read the introduction plus the construction setup.

## The paper's stated contribution, in my own words

The classical Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb{F}_n$ only makes sense for positive integers $n$: it is the projectivization of $\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(n)$ over $\mathbb{CP}^1$, a smooth compact toric variety, equivalently (via a Delzant polytope) a right trapezoid $P_n$ whose normal fan is rational. The authors ask whether this family can be continuously extended so that $n$ becomes an arbitrary positive *real* $a$.

To that end they build, for every real $a>0$, a space $\tilde{\mathbb F}_a$ which specializes to the genuine Hirzebruch surface when $a$ is an integer, and present two geometric realizations. First (following their earlier work, BZ1) a complex model $N_a$ carrying a holomorphic, transversely Kähler foliation $\mathcal{F}_a$ whose leaf space is exactly $\tilde{\mathbb F}_a$; here the leaf at rational points is $(S^1)^2$ (orbifold) and the generic leaf is $S^1\times\mathbb R$ (nonsel) at irrational points. Second (extending their earlier "Lerman-cut" work, BP2) a symplectic realization of $\tilde{\mathbb F}_a$ as the quotient of a strip in $\mathbb C\times S^2$, generalizing the cut so that the cutting line may be irrationally sloped.

## What is actually new, and what is not

**Genuinely new:**
- The *family* idea itself: framing the trapezoid/fan with a continuous real parameter and packaging it into a uniform definition of $\tilde{\mathbb F}_a$ for all $a>0$. That framing is the paper's real, if modest, contribution.
- The *irrational* Lerman cut of $\mathbb C\times S^2$: the classical Lerman cut (L) requires a rationally sloped line, so allowing an irrationally sloped strip-cut is a technical extension beyond the cited prior work; it is one of the few places the paper pushes past its references.
- An explicit *non-defining* description of $N_a$ as a dense subset with a foliation, rather than only as an orbifold.

**Not new (explicitly acknowledged and merely reused):**
- All of the machinery. Quasilattices (P1), toric quasifolds (P), the complex/presymplectic-quotient theorems (BP1), the foliation construction (BZ1), and the rational/integer special case are all imported wholesale from the second author's and the co-authors' own earlier papers; the integer case and the $a\in\mathbb Q$ orbifold case are essentially restatements of BP1/BZ1.
- The $a=n$ integer case is the ordinary toric Hirzebruch surface, long-known (H, F, D).
- The general non-Kähler complex model $N_a$ (with its $(S^1)^2$/$S^1\times\mathbb R$ leaves and nonglobalizable Kähler class) sits in the Meer–van Vlj / Lopez de Medrano–Vervojovsky–Meersseman world of LVM surfaces and non-Kähler surfaces, which the paper cites.

**Overall novelty assessment.** This is an extension/consolidation paper: a known special case ($a\in\mathbb Z$, $a\in\mathbb Q$) plus a modest technical generalization (the irrational cut) re-packaged into a clean one-parameter family. The intellectual novelty is low; none of the core constructions are original to this paper, and the "one-parameter" framing mostly re-states results from the authors' prior work.

## [1804.08503v2-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


# Evaluation: Battaglia–Prato–Zafran, *Hirzebruch surfaces in a one-parameter family* (arXiv:1804.08503v2)

## Important framing note

This is a **pure-mathematics construction/exposition paper, not an empirical study**. It has no experiment in the lab sense. What the reader can "reproduce" is a *recipe*: for any positive real $a$, run one of three named constructions and obtain the object denoted $\mathbb F_a$. I therefore restate the methodology as a reproducible construction procedure and flag the places where "one can verify / by [reference]" substitutes for a proof.

---

## The object being constructed

The classical Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb F_n$ ($n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$) is the projectivization of $\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(n)$ over $\mathbb{CP}^1$; it is smooth and toric. Concretely:

- **Fan** $\Delta_n$ (the *smooth fan*) is the normal fan of the **right trapezoid** $P_n = \operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(0,1),(n+1,1),(1,0)\}$. Its four rays are $\langle(1,0)\rangle,\langle(0,1)\rangle,\langle(0,-1)\rangle,\langle(-1,n)\rangle$; the primitive generators $\{(1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(-1,n)\}$ form a (lattice) basis per maximal cone, which is what makes the fan rational, smooth and simplicial.
- $P_n\subset(\mathbb R^2)^*$ gives a Kähler structure on $\mathbb F_n$ via Delzant.

The single generalization: **allow the fourth ray to be $(-1,a)$ for any $a\in\mathbb R_{>0}$**, and the trapezoid to $P_a=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(0,1),(a+1,1),(1,0)\}$. For non-integer $a$ the fan is no longer rational in $\mathbb Z^2$ (the slanting ray misses the lattice), so the "methodology" is precisely a set of ways to build $\mathbb F_a$ in the **non-rational / quasifold / foliation** setting, following the second author's prior programme. A shared datum for all three constructions is the **quasilattice**

$$Q_a=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{(1,0),(0,1),(-1,a)\}=\mathbb Z\times(\mathbb Z+a\mathbb Z)\supseteq\mathbb Z^2,$$

i.e. the natural replacement for $\mathbb Z^2$: replace the lattice by the $\mathbb Z$-span of the ray generators.

---

## Reproducible recipe A — the quasifold construction (§1.2)

Input: $a>0$.

1. Form the simple 2-polytope $P_a$ with vertices above and the ray-generator set $\{X_1=\!(1,0),X_2\!=\!(0,1),X_3\!=\!(0,-1),X_4\!=\!(-1,a)\}$, and $Q_a$ as above. This is the Delzant-type triple $(P_a,\{X_i\},Q_a)$.
2. By the quasifold version of Delzant (Delzant for the symplectic side; Pratt–Prato–[BP1, Thm 2.2] for the complex side) the triple yields a 4-dim **symplectic toric quasifold** and a 2-dim **complex (Kähler) toric quasifold** $X_{P_a}$, equiviariantly diffeomorphic with Kähler form. Explicitly (asserted as "one can verify"):
   - Symplectic: $\{\zeta\in\mathbb C^4:\,|\zeta_1|^2+a|\zeta_3|^2+|\zeta_4|^2=1+a,\ |\zeta_2|^2+|\zeta_3|^2=1\}\,/\,(\mathbb R^2/Q_a)$.
   - Complex: $\{\zeta:\zeta_3\zeta_4\ne0\}\cup\{\zeta:\zeta_1\zeta_3\zeta_4\ne0\}\cup\{\zeta:\zeta_1\zeta_2\zeta_3\ne0\}\cup\{\zeta:\zeta_2\zeta_4\ne0\}\,/\,(D_a^4)_\mathbb C$, with $(D_a^4)_\mathbb C=\mathbb C^2/Q_a\cong\mathbb C^*\times(\mathbb C^*/\Gamma_a)$.
3. Call this quasifold $\mathbb F_a$. If $a=n\in\mathbb Z$ this is exactly $\mathbb F_n$. If $a=p/q\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb F_a$ is a Kähler toric **orbifold** with two disjoint order-$q$ singular divisors $z_2=0$ and $z_3=0$ (the two trapezoid bases).

## Reproducible recipe B — the complex/presymplectic foliation construction (§2)

1. Build the **triangulated vector configuration** $(V_a,\mathcal T)$ encoding $(\Delta_a,\{X_i\},Q_a)$: $V_a=((1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(-1,a),(0,-a))$ — the fifth entry is an explicit **ghost vector** — and triangulation $\mathcal T=\{\{1,2\},\{2,4\},\{3,4\},\{1,3\},\{2\},\{3\},\{4\},\varnothing\}$. (For $n$ the analogous config uses ghost $(0,-n)$.)
2. Apply **Gale duality** to the integer-relations in $\mathbb R^5$, interpreting each column as a complex number. The paper uses a basis whose relation matrix (rows) is
$$\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&1&1\\0&1&1&0&0\\1&0&a&1&0\end{pmatrix}$$
 which yields the ordered **point configuration** $\Lambda=(i,\,1,\,1+ia,\,i,\,0)\subset\mathbb C^a$ (affine $\mathbb C$) and the **virtual chamber** $\mathcal T^*=\{\{3,4,5\},\{1,3,5\},\{1,2,5\},\{2,4,5\}\}$, whose four triangles have non-empty intersection (equivalently $\mathcal F$ is polytopal, Shephard's condition).
3. This is an **LVM datum** $(\Lambda,\mathcal T^*)$. The $\mathbb C_\Lambda$-action on the chamber-open set $U(T^*)\subset\mathbb{CP}^4$ is
$$t.[z_1:z_2:z_3:z_4:z_5]\mapsto[e^{-2\pi i t}z_1:z_2z_2:e^{2\pi i t(1+ia)}z_3:e^{-2\pi i t}z_4:z_5].$$
 Its quotient $N_a:=U(T^*)/\mathbb C_\Lambda$ is a compact complex manifold (a generalized LVM / non-compactified toric variety in the Pratt–Bosio line).
4. Use the **conjugate point configuration** $\overline{\Lambda}$; $\mathbb C_{\overline{\Lambda}}$ commutes with $\mathbb C_\Lambda$ and gives a dense $(S^1)^4$-orbit on $N_a$, hence the holomorphic **transversely Kähler foliation** $\mathcal F_a$.
5. The holomorphic leaf space is $\mathbb F_a$, via the map
$$[z_1:z_2:z_3:z_4:z_5]\mapsto[z_1z_5^{-1}:z_2z_5^{-1}:z_3z_5^{-1}:z_4z_5^{-1}].$$
6. (Presymplectic version, §2.3.) Add the extra half-plane datum $-ay\ge -2a$ and the associated connected subgroup of $(S^1)^5$ $\exp(\operatorname{Rel}(V_a))$ acting Hamiltonianly on $\mathbb C^5$ with moment map
$$\Psi_a(z)=\big(|z|^2-2(a+1),\ |z_1|^2+a|z_3|^2+|z_4|^2-1+a,\ |z_2|^2+|z_3|^2-1\big).$$
 Then $\Psi_a^{-1}(0)/S^1\cong N_a$ and the same $\mathbb F_a$ is the leaf space.

**Topological outputs** (stated, from BZ1): generic leaf $\mathcal F_{[\mathbb Z]}\simeq(S^1)^2$ if $a\in\mathbb Q$, else $S^1\times\mathbb R$; leaf closure $\overline{\mathcal F_{[\mathbb Z]}}\simeq(S^1)^2$ (rational) / $(S^1)^3$ (irrational). For integer $a=n$ the leaf space is exactly $\mathbb F_n$; for $a=p/q$ rational-integer the leaf is a $q$-sheeted cover of the canonical one.

## Reproducible recipe C — the "arbitrary-direction" symplectic cut of $\mathbb C\times S^2$ (§3)

1. Recall the classical route: $\mathbb F_n$ equals the symplectic toric blow-up of the singular point of weighted $\mathbb{CP}^2_{(1,n,1)}$; in toric terms $\mathbb{CP}^2_{(1,n,1)}$ corresponds to triangle $T$ with vertices $(0,-1/n),(0,1),(n+1,1)$ and the singular point to the vertex $(0,-1/n)$. A $1/n$ blow-up = cut off the corner = trapezoid $P_n$ (Guillemin / Delzant-cutting).
2. **Cut alternative:** cut the pointed polyhedron (half-strip) $[0,+\infty)\times[0,1]$ with the line $x=ny+1$ to produce $P_n$. Symplectically $[0,+\infty)\times[0,1]\leftrightarrow\mathbb C\times S^2$ with its standard torus action.
3. **Irrational extension:** to cut $[0,+\infty)\times[0,1]$ by the line $x=ay+1$ for irrational $a$, the standard Lerman cut fails because the line's normal is not in $\mathbb Z^2$. Fix by replacing $\mathbb Z^2$ with $Q_a=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z^2\cup\{(-1,a)\})$, so one cuts the *quasifield* $\mathbb C\times(S^2/\Gamma_a)$ with $\Gamma_a=Q_a/\mathbb Z^2\approx\{e^{2\pi iat}|t\in\mathbb Z\}$ acting by rotations of $S^2$.
4. Realise the $S^1$-Hamiltonian on $\mathbb C\times(S^2/\Gamma_a)$: $e^{2\pi it}\cdot(u,[v:z])=(e^{-2\pi i t}u,[e^{2\pi i a t}v:z])$, with moment map $\Phi_Y=-|u|^2+\tfrac{a(z+1)}2$.
5. Apply the weight $-1$ cut on $\mathbb C\times(S^2/\Gamma_a)\times\mathbb C$ (last factor): $
u_-(u,[v:z],w)=-|u|^2+\tfrac{a(z+1)}2-|w|^2$, and form the cut space $\nu_-^{-1}(-1)/S^1$. The paper asserts (by BP2 Thm 2.3) that this **coincides with symplectic quasifold $\mathbb F_a$**. The cut is explicitly the disjoint union of the quotient $\Phi_Y^{-1}(-1)/S^1$ and the open dense set $\{(u,[v:z]):|u|^2-\tfrac{a(z+1)}2<1\}\,/\Gamma_a$.
6. (Bonus decomposition) $\mathbb F_a$ also comes from triangle $T_a=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,-1/a),(0,1),(a+1,1)\}$ blown up at the point mapping to vertex $(0,-1/a)$ by amount $1/a$.

## Assumptions the methodology papers over

The paper's value is unifying three *already-published* recipes, so it deliberately leans on its own prior programme. A reproducing reader is blocked, or must take on faith, the following:

1. **Entire dependence on un-proved external theorems.** The construction *is* BP1 (non-rational toric varieties), BP2 (non-rational symplectic toric cuts), BZ1/BZ2 (simplical toric varieties as complex/presymplectic foliated manifolds), P (quasilattices), and Delzant/D (polytope→toric manifold). None is proved; "reproducing" means trusting or re-deriving all five.
2. **"One can verify" realizations (p. 7).** The explicit symplectic set $\{...\}/\mathbb R^2/Q_a$ and the complex union-of-sets presentation of $(D_a^4)_\mathbb C$ are asserted for the triple $(P_a,\{X_i\},Q_a)$, not derived; so are the moment maps $\Phi_Y$ and $\nu_-$ in the cut.
3. **Uniqueness / choice-dependence of the Gale-dual datum.** The paper itself says infinitely many $(V,\mathcal T)$ and correspondingly infinitely many $(\Lambda,\mathcal T^*)$ exist; it picks one (with a particular ghost vector and a particular relation basis). It asserts, without proof, that the complex structure of the leaf space does not depend on this choice.
4. **The "balanced and odd" conditions** on a triangulated vector configuration are imposed as requirements rather than verified; their role in guaranteeing a well-defined foliated manifold is delegated to BZ1.
5. **Ghost-vector convention.** The fifth vector $(0,-a)$ in $V_a$ is added only to make the configuration "odd"; the choice is presented as a convention, not justified uniquely.
6. **Topological identifications.** The leaf / leaf-closure types ($(S^1)^2$ vs $S^1\times\mathbb R$; $(S^1)^3$) and the "$q$-sheeted cover" claims for $a=p/q$ are attributed to BZ1 / MV / CLMV ("measure of rationality of $V$"), not proved here.
7. **The rational-non-integer orbifold claim.** For $a=p/q\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathbb Z$, the assertion that $\mathbb F_a$ is a Kähler toric orbifold with exactly two disjoint order-$q$ singular divisors at the trapezoid bases is stated, not shown.
8. **Domain restriction $a>0$.** Boundary cases ($a=0$, which would be $\mathbb{CP}^1\times\mathbb{CP}^1$ or a degenerate case, and negative $a$) are silently excluded; the trapezoid is only simple/concave in the stated form for $a>0$.
9. **Parity / non-deformability caveat.** The paper notes $\mathbb F_n$ splits into (odd, even) by $n$'s parity and cannot be connected by deformation, yet presents the family as if a "one-parameter family" — the jump in diffeomorphism type as $a$ crosses integer values is acknowledged only in passing, not analysed.
10. **The cut's validity for irrational slope.** The step "add $(-1,a)$ to the lattice and the cut proceeds" is asserted to fix the missing normal-vector problem; that the resulting quasifold cut really has $P_a$ as moment polytope and coincides with Recipe A's $\mathbb F_a$ is attributed to BP2 Theorem 2.3.
11. **The half-plane extra datum $-ay\ge-2a$ in the presymplectic foliation** is introduced as "with the additional datum of the half-plane", not derived from $(P_a,\{X_i\},Q_a)$.
12. **Equivalence of the three recipes.** That Recipies A, B and C all give the *same* $\mathbb F_a$ is the paper's implicit thesis, but the identifications are each one-way (quasifold $\cong$ leaf space $\cong$ cut space) citing the source papers; no self-contained argument closes the loop.

---

## Bottom line

A reader who already has BP1, BP2, BZ1, BZ2, P and Delzant *can* reproduce the construction and verify each "one can verify" line by consulting those — the three recipes are spelled out concretely enough (explicit polytopes, vector configs, $\mathbb C^k$-presentations and moment maps) to implement. But as a self-contained methodology it is thin: it is a well-organised unification of prior results whose load-bearing theorems, non-uniqueness caveats, and domain/parity edge cases are taken as black boxes.

## [1804.08503v2-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


## The paper's logical argument, as I read it

**The question.** Hirzebruch surfaces $\mathbb{F}_n$ form a one-parameter family indexed by *positive integers* $n$. The authors ask whether this can be extended so that the parameter $a$ is any *positive real number*.

**Why the integer restriction is artificial.** In the convex-geometric picture each $\mathbb{F}_n$ is a smooth projective toric surface whose normal fan $\Delta_n$ is simple, smooth, and rational, with a moment polytope that is the right trapezoid $P_n$ with vertices $(0,0),(0,1),(n+1,1),(1,0)$. If one allows $a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, the same recipe still produces a simplicial polytopeal fan $\Delta_a$ and trapezoid $P_a$, but for irrational $a$ neither is rational in $\mathbb{Z}^2$ — this is exactly the obstruction: classical toric geometry cannot build a variety from a nonrational fan.

**The fix.** Replace the lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$ by the *quasilattice* $Q_a=\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}+a\mathbb{Z})$, the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of the primitive generators of the rays of $\Delta_a$. The machinery of *quasifolds* (Prato) then produces, for each $a$, a Kähler toric quasifold $\mathbb{F}_a$; for rational $a$ an orbifold; and for integer $a=n$ the ordinary Hirzebruch surface. The paper then supplies **three realizations** of this same space:

1. **Foliations** (BZ1): a compact complex manifold $N_a$ with a holomorphic, transversely Kähler foliation $\mathcal{F}_a$ whose leaf space is $\mathbb{F}_a$. The generic leaf is $(S^1)^2$ when $a\in\mathbb{Q}$ and $S^1\times\mathbb{R}$ otherwise — so the topology genuinely jumps at the rational/irrational boundary. There are also presymplectic counterparts, and non-unique choices of Gale dual give infinitely many models with the *same* leaf space.

2. **Symplectic cuts of $\mathbb{C}\times S^2$** (Lerman, generalized by BP2 for arbitrary directions): the trapezoid $P_a$ is cut from the strip $[0,\infty)\times[0,1]$ by the line $x=ay+1$. For irrational $a$ the cut line has no normal vector in the lattice, so the classical Lerman cut is inapplicable; using the quasifield cut one obtains $\mathbb{F}_a$ as a cut of $\mathbb{C}\times(S^2/\Gamma_a)$ plus an open dense piece.

3. **Blow-up** of weighted projective space $\mathbb{CP}^2_{(1,n,1)}$ (the integer/rational analogue of the classical construction).

The through-line is that all three viewpoints agree when $a$ is an integer and jointly extend the Hirzebruch family to a smooth one-parameter family of (quasi)surfaces.

## Feedback

**What works.** The framing is clean and the motivation is honest: the question "can $n$ be made real?" is natural, the authors pinpoint exactly where the integer restriction becomes merely a rationality assumption, and the choice of $Q_a$ as the quasilattice is forced by the ray generators rather than arbitrary. Presenting the same object through three independent constructions (foliation, cut, blow-up) is the paper's real strength — it gives the nonrational $\mathbb{F}_a$ several simultaneous interpretations and confirms coherence across them. The exposition is careful, the figures are genuinely clarifying, and the rational/irrational leaf-topology dichotomy is a nice concrete marker that something new (not just a reparametrization) happens.

**Main weaknesses.**

1. **It is a unification note, not a thesis-bearing paper.** Almost nothing is proved in this document. Key statements are deferred to the authors' previous work — "one can verify," "as in [BP2] Theorem 2.3," "following [BZ1] Section 2.2," "well-known result [M] Theorem 7." The logical argument is *faithful* but *thin*: the paper's substance is an assembly of results from [P], [BP1], [BP2], [BZ1], [BZ2] with the Hirzebruch family threaded through as a running example. There are no new theorems, no new estimates, no result that is not already the stated consequence of a cited paper.

2. **Originality is limited to the combination.** The generalization scheme (lattice → quasilattice → quasifold) is the second author's prior work; Lerman cuts, Delzant-type constructions, and LVM/MV foliations are all standard or preexisting. What is new is the *question* and the *three-way agreement* for the Hirzebruch case. That is a legitimate but modest contribution — it reads more like a long, well-done example / expository article than a research paper advancing a technique.

3. **No indication of consequence beyond the example.** Beyond recovering the family and noting the leaf-topology jump, the paper does not extract any new invariants, classification, or application from having a *continuous* (rather than discrete) Hirzebruch family. A reader who is not already invested in quasifolds gets little to take away; a reader who is already working in this area gets a tidy survey of their own toolkit.

4. **Minor:** the "three constructions" are announced in the introduction but the third (blow-up of weighted projective space) is underdeveloped and partly overlaps the cut story. The claim that infinitely many foliated models $(N_a,\mathcal{F}_a)$ map to one leaf space is stated but its geometric meaning (why non-uniqueness of Gale dual matters, what varies) is left to [BZ2].

**Soundness.** As far as the document shows, the argument is internally consistent and the recalcitrant case (irrational $a$) is handled by the genuinely applicable nonrational tools rather than glossed over. I find no logical gap; the caveat is that the paper rests heavily on cited results I am taking on faith as stated.

**Net assessment.** A coherent, clearly written unification that poses a natural question and answers it by three consistent constructions, but its contribution is largely expository and derivative of the authors' earlier work, with no new theorems and limited reach beyond the specialist audience.

## [1804.08503v2-quantitative-claims] Audit of quantitative claims


I've read the paper in full (Battaglia–Prato–Zaffran, "Hirzebruch Surfaces in a One-Parameter Family," arXiv 1804.08503v2, 15 pp., math.SG). Here is the assessment.

## The strongest quantitative claim

The paper contains no empirical results at all — its central (and strongest) claim is a **theorem of the kind the experimental version of the question is aimed at, transposed into pure math**:

> For *every* positive real number a, there exists a compact Kähler toric quasifold F_a, with quasilattice Q_a = Z × (Z + aZ) and moment polytope the trapezoid P_a = conv{(0,0),(0,1),(a+1,1),(1,0)}, satisfying (i) an explicit model as {z ∈ C⁴ : |z₁|² + a|z₃|² + |z₄|² = 1+a, |z₂|² + |z₃|² = 1}, and (ii) **agreement of two independent constructions**: F_a is simultaneously the complex leaf space of a compact foliated manifold (N_a, 𝔽_a) built via the LVM/Gale-duality machinery [BZ1], *and* the symplectic cut ν₋¹(−1)/S¹ of C × (S²/Γ_a) cut in the (possibly irrational) direction x = ay + 1 [BP2]; for integer a = n this recovers the classical Hirzebruch surface F_n.

## Audit of the evidence

**Is the comparison fair?** The question's "comparison" framing doesn't quite apply — the two constructions are not competitors to be benchmarked. They are two routes derived from the *same* convex datum (P_a, {(1,0),(0,1),(0,−1),(−1,a)}, Q_a), and the claimed agreement is a coherence property: [BZ1] gives leaf space ≅ F_a, [BP2, Thm 2.3] gives the cut ≅ F_a. Since both inherit from the identical input triple, there is no tuning or asymmetry to make an unfair advantage possible, and the paper does a reasonable amount of direct verification (completeness/simpliciality of Δ_a, the explicit S¹-action and moment maps Φ_Y, ν₋, the decomposition of the cut into S²/Γ_a plus an open dense set of C×S²/Γ_a). One caveat: the "we can pass from one Hirzebruch surface to another, but not via a deformation" remark is asserted by appeal to the parity diffeomorphism fact [H] rather than argued, which is fine but leaves a small gap if a reader wants the argument internal.

**Are baselines current?** Yes, within the domain and as of writing (2018). The classical anchors (Hirzebruch [H], LVM [LV, M], Meersseman–Verjovsky [MV], Lerman cut [L], Delzant [D], Guillemin [Gu]) are correctly positioned, and genuinely contemporary work is included: Lin–Sjamaar [LS] and Nguyen–Ratiu [NR] (2017 preprints), and the authors' own 2018 papers [BP2], [BP3]. The main structural caveat is that [BP2], [BP3] and [BZ1–3] are the authors' own program — so the "baselines" for the two nontrivial results are substantially the authors' own prior theorems. This is standard for a research-program paper, but it means the strongest claim rests essentially entirely on the truth of those cited theorems; the present paper proves almost nothing new and is expository/illustrative by design.

**Error bars / statistical significance?** Not applicable — the paper has no experiments, no randomness, no samples. The mathematical counterpart is proof strength, and that is where the audit is most telling: every load-bearing fact is delegated to a citation (compactness of N_a [BZ1 §2.2.4]; Kählerness [BP1 Thm 3.2]; the cut identification [BP2 Thms 1.1, 2.3]; leaf-topology formulas [BZ1 §2.1.1]; the "all complex tori by varying Gale dual" claim [MV Thm G]). There are no hidden empirical claims to inflate, and I found no internal inconsistencies (e.g., the generic-leaf topology formula S¹×R vs (S¹)² matches between §2.2 and the introduction), only surface typos ("Hirzebuch," a duplicated "25" tag in the chamber diagram).

**Verdict:** the claim is sound but not newly established here — the paper is a cleanly written illustration of pre-existing machinery, applying it to one well-chosen toy family. A reader must accept the prior theorems on faith; nothing in this paper independently substantiates the central identification.

## [1804.08503v2-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Evaluation: *Hirzebruch surfaces in a one-parameter family* (Battaglia–Prato–Zaffran, arXiv:1804.08503v2)

## The single weakest link

**The step in §2 (and the first sentence of the Abstract) where (Nₐ, 𝓕ₐ) is asserted to have leaf space "exactly ℝ̃ₐ" — i.e., the identification of the foliated model's leaf space with the specific Kähler toric quasifold ℳ_{𝒫ₐ} (= ℝ̃ₐ) constructed in §1.2. This is the step where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data.**

## Exactly why

**What the paper's own data actually establish.** Section 2.2 supplies the explicit datum: a vector configuration Vₐ = ((1,0),(0,1),(0,−1),(−1,a),(0,−a)), a triangulation 𝒯, its Gale dual point configuration Λ = (i, 1, 1+ia, i, 0) with chamber 𝒯* = {{3,4,5},{1,3,5},{1,2,5},{2,4,5}}, and an explicitly written ℂₐ × 𝒰(𝒯*)-action. From these, applying the BZ1 recipe, the paper obtains: (i) Nₐ := 𝒰(𝒯*)/ℂ_Λ a compact complex 3-fold; (ii) the induced ℂ*-action giving a holomorphic 1-dimensional foliation 𝓕ₐ; (iii) the local leaf-space structure 𝓕̃_[z], which the paper computes *piecewise* (ℂ/(ℤ⊕iqℤ) vs. ℂ/(ℤ⊕iℤ) for a = p/q; 𝔂* vs. a compact torus for irrational a).

**Here is the internal tension that breaks the entailment.** On the very pages making the "exactly ℝ̃ₐ" claim, the paper states: *"the complex structure of the leaves does vary upon the choice of Gale dual,"* and, passing to noncommutative/LVM data, *"By varying our choice of Gale dual we obtain all possible two-dimensional compact complex tori."* The data on the table therefore determine a **family** of leaf and leaf-space complex structures parametrized by the (infinite) choice of Gale dual — not one of them. What §1.2 has defined, however, is a **specific** object: the Kähler toric quasifold ℳ_{𝒫ₐ} with its canonical Kähler form. The gap between "for each Gale dual, a smooth foliated manifold with a leaf space of *some* complex structure of the right type" and "the leaf space is *exactly* ℝ̃ₐ (with *the* Kähler structure)" is filled by no computation in the paper — only by the diagram on p. 10 and the label "BZ2 Theorem 2.1 / Theorem 2.4" (uniqueness of the leaf space).

**Why the cited bridge is not a verification here, specifically.** The uniqueness theorems being invoked require that the chosen triangulated vector configuration (Vₐ, 𝒯), in the strong sense of the companion papers, encode exactly the triple (Δₐ, {(1,0),(0,1),(0,−1),(−1,a)}, 𝒬ₐ). The paper's data make that encoding suspect in ways it never checks:

1. **Vₐ is never regular for any a > 0.** The ghost vector v₅ = (0,−a) is collinear with v₃ = (0,−1) for *every* a (and *identical* to it when a = 1, so the "5-vector" configuration of F₁ is literally a duplicate-vector configuration). The balance (Σvᵢ = 0) and oddness (5 − 2 = 3) conditions are satisfied and are checked; non-degeneracy of the configuration — the condition that matters for applying the cited existence/uniqueness machinery to this specific datum — is not.
2. **The chamber 𝒯* contains two identical triangles.** Since v₁ and v₄ map to the *same* point of the Gale dual (Λ₁ = Λ₄ = i), the triangles {3,4,5} and {1,3,5}, and {1,2,5} and {2,4,5}, coincide as geometric triangles. The polytopal condition (nonempty total intersection) does hold — one can check the four triangle intersections are nonempty for all a > 0 — but the chamber is degenerate in this duplication, and the paper's statement "In our case, we obtain 𝒯* = {…}" with four listed (really two distinct) triangles is used to count the four standard charts 𝒰{zⱼzₖzₗzₘ ≠ 0} without addressing the coincidence.
3. **Kählerness is never transferred.** Even granting BZ2's uniqueness of the *complex* leaf space, the abstract's first promise is that ℝ̃ₐ is the leaf space of a foliation with *transversely Kähler* structure **matching** the Kähler toric quasifold of §1.2. The explicit projection [z₁:…:z₅] ↦ [z₁z₅⁻¹:z₂z₅⁻¹:z₃z₅⁻¹:z₄z₅⁻¹] is a *map*; nothing on the page identifies the pulled-back transverse (or leaf) complex structure with the one ℳ_{𝒫ₐ} carries — and the paper itself has just declared the leaf-side complex structure is choice-dependent. So the "one object, two perspectives" claim of the Abstract — the paper's thesis — is exactly the assertion not supported by the paper's own data.

**Contrast with the rest of the chain (which is fine).** The convex-geometric step (§1.2) is a clean application of cited Delzant/PB theorems to verifiable data (Δₐ simple, 𝒬ₐ = ℤ×(ℤ+aℤ), Pₐ rational iff a is). Notably, I re-checked the **cut** computation of §3, which *is* entailed by the data there: the νₐ-level −1 cut of ℂ×(S²/Γₐ) has moment image {0 ≤ Y ≤ a, 0 ≤ X ≤ 1+Y} with (X, Y) = (|u|², a(z+1)/2), which is precisely the trapezoid 𝒫ₐ = conv{(0,0),(0,1),(a+1,1),(1,0)} in (x, ay) coordinates. So the §3 geometry is checked; its only remaining leap ("moment image 𝒫ₐ ⇒ cut ≅ ℳ_{𝒫ₐ}", p. 16, "BP2 Theorem 2.3") is a uniqueness-of-Delzant statement — external, but one whose hypotheses *are* transparently met. The §2 leap, by contrast, is undercut by the paper's own recorded choice-dependence. Hence §2's "exactly ℝ̃ₐ" is the *single* weakest link, not the cut side.

## Minor additional defects (not the main link)

- p. 7: for a = p/q, "two disjoint singular **divisors** of order q given by z₂ = 0 and z₃ = 0, which correspond to the bases of the trapezoid" — the order-q singularities of a 2-dimensional toric orbifold live at the two *vertices* (cones ⟨(0,1),(−q,p)⟩ and ⟨(0,−1),(−q,p)⟩, both of index q; the other two cones have index 1), i.e., at the *ends* of those divisors. The divisors themselves are smooth away from those points. Terminology error that slightly misplaces the singularity.
- The construction's fate at a = 1 (v₅ = v₃, identical rays) is never mentioned, in the family that claims to recover *all* Fₙ.

## Bottom line

The paper is a correct, cleanly written expository bridge that packages three of the authors' preprint-level results (BZ1, BZ2, BP2) around a natural one-parameter deformation of the Hirzebruch family, and its elementary computations (𝒬ₐ, the chamber, the cut's moment image) check out. ## [1804.08503v2-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


This is Battaglia–Prato–Zaffran, *Hirzebruch surfaces in a one‑parameter family* (arXiv:1804.08503v2). The paper works through the generalized Hirzebruch trapezoid \(P_a\) for \(a\in\mathbb R_{>0}\), presenting \(\mathbb F_a\) via (i) a compact complex foliated manifold whose leaf space is \(\mathbb F_a\), and (ii) a nonrational symplectic cut of \(\mathbb C\times S^2\). ## Sentences that overclaim

1. **The title.** "Hirzebruch *surfaces* in a one‑parameter family" implies a smooth (indeed algebraic) one‑parameter family of surfaces. Only the integer members are surfaces in any classical sense. The title advertises a smooth algebraic family that does not exist.

2. **Abstract: "We introduce a family of spaces … that includes all of the Hirzebruch surfaces."** "Includes all the Hirzebruch surfaces" is vacuous — \(\mathbb F_n=\mathbb F_a\) at \(a=n\) is an identity, not a construction. The genuinely new content is at irrational \(a\), and the abstract never tells the reader (a) that the rational members are pre‑existing toric orbifolds or (b) that the irrational members are quasifolds, not manifolds. So the promise ("a family including all Hirzebruch surfaces") overstates, while the actual novelty (continuation to nonrational \(a\) in the quasifold category) is left implicit.

3. **Introduction (p.3): "In this article, we present two alternative constructions of \(\mathbb F_a\)."** Both "constructions" are explicitly attributed elsewhere in the text to the authors' *own prior papers* — Section 2 opens "we apply a construction developed in [BZ1]"; Section 2.3 says the presymplectic picture "can be viewed in a symplectic setting by applying a simple variant of the generalized Delzant construction [BP2] introduced in [BZ2]. The object is also *not* new independently: p.3 itself says "According to [P, BP1], we may … construct, for each positive real number \(a\), a Kähler toric quasifold \(\mathbb F_a\)." The hedge word does work the authors didn't justify — a classic framing overclaim.

6. **Section 2.3 (p.11): "In particular, in the former, symplectic quasifolds are also viewed as leaf spaces of presymplectic manifolds."** "In particular" asserts an implication that the surrounding text (citing [LS] and [NR]) does not actually derive; those papers are described as context, not as proving this consequence. The connective overstates the logical link.

Supporting, well‑hedged, and (unlike the above) not overclaims: the "infinitely many foliated manifolds, but only one complex leaf space" claim (p.10) and "N can have any dimension ≥ 3 with leaf space \(\mathbb F_a\)" (p.4) are both explicitly grounded in [BZ2, Thm 2.1] and in the ability to add ghost vectors, so they land where the evidence is.
