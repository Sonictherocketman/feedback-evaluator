## [1403.3701v2-general-review] General Review


I've read the full paper (arXiv:1403.3701v2, Giardino, "Axisymmmetric empty space: light propagation, orbits and dark matter"). Here is my critique of its argument.

## Overview of the argument

The paper finds a four-parameter, axisymmetric **vacuum** solution to the Einstein equations,
$$ds^2 = -\frac{u}{v^2}c^2dt^2 + \frac{1}{v^4}dr^2 + \frac{1}{uv^2}dz^2 + r^2 d\phi^2, \qquad v=C_1+C_2r^2,\; u=4C_1C_2z^2+C_3z+C_4,$$
classifies it as Petrov type D, notes that its Kretschmann scalar $K=96C_2^2v^6$ diverges as $r\to\infty$, analyzes light rays and radial orbitals in the near-flat region, matches the weak field near the origin to a massive-ring potential, and then proposes the geometry as an *alternative to inflation, dark matter and quintessence*.

The central thesis is: because curvature **grows** with distance instead of decaying, "every mass would be attracted to the far region," mimicking expansion, so this vacuum geometry can replace dark energy/dark matter.

## Substantive problems

**1. The "source at infinity" is a genuine curvature singularity, not a mass.** $K\sim C_8 r^{12}$ as $r\to\infty$ (with $v=C_1+C_2r^2$, $v^6\sim r^{12}$). A divergence of the Riemann tensor squared is the standard signature of a real curvature singularity. The author reframes this as a mass-like source, but a singularity that sits at spatial infinity and *increases* with radius is not a localized body and does not produce an attractive Newtonian $Gm/r^2$ field. The analogy to Schwarzschild (mass at finite $r$) actively breaks down: here the "mass" is pushed to $r=\infty$, which is exactly why the Newtonian-matching only works as a single-point (local Taylor) identification, never as a field match.

**2. The dark-matter/inflation claim is entirely unevaluated.** This is the biggest gap. There is **no quantitative comparison to a single observation** — no CMB spectrum, no supernova H(z), no galaxy rotation curves, no BBN, no structure growth. To "substitute for dark matter or quintessence" a model must at least be shown to reproduce an observed distance-redshift relation or $H(z)$. The paper offers none; it is pure analogy ("the net effect is that every mass would be attracted to the far region").

**3. No cosmological principle.** Any serious inflationary/dark-energy model has a homogeneous, isotropic FLRW sector (Friedmann equations, a scale factor $a(t)$). This metric is maximally inhomogeneous — curvature is a monotone function of radius about a chosen origin, with a single axis. There is no $a(t)$, no FLRW limit, no time dynamics at all (the metric is time-independent, so it cannot "inflate," which requires expansion in time, not merely in radius). The conflation of a static inhomogeneous vacuum with an expanding universe is a category error.

**4. The "closed orbits" argument is not a proof of closure.** §IV.B establishes only that $\dot r=0$ has a real solution for each sign of $C_2$ (an equilibrium of the radial effective potential) and loosely argues a potential minimum exists. But (a) the decisive algebraic claim — "the sixth-order polynomial can be solved in terms of a third-order polynomial and it has at least one real root" — is asserted, never shown; (b) **a bounded radial equilibrium is not a closed orbit** in relativistic mechanics. Closed (non-precessing) orbits require the radial and angular oscillation frequencies to be commensurate, as in the Kerr/Schwarzschild precession analysis. Boundedness was shown (partially), precession was not. So the conclusion "there are closed orbits" overreaches the evidence, and the final "the proof of its existence is enough to qualitatively characterize the metric" sidesteps the very thing that would make the model physical.

**5. The Newtonian-limit matching is internally inconsistent.** To use the weak-field identification $\phi=-\frac{c^2}{2}h^{00}$ one must match the metric to the ring potential $\Phi(r,z)$ over a **region**. But (i) the author matches only at the expansion point; (ii) the ring potential and the metric have different radial order — "the metric has a weaker dependence on the radial coordinate than the massive ring" — i.e. the ring's linear-$r$ term is absent from the metric; (iii) the very weak region where the matching is done ($r\to 0$) is where this geometry is essentially *flat by construction* ($C_1=C_4=1$), so the fit carries little information. The conclusion $C_2=mG/(4c^2R^3)$, $C_3=0$ is therefore not a robust physical determination.

## Smaller but notable issues

- **Undermines its own novelty.** "The solution is not new, but the form in which it is presented here certainly is," and "the metric here presented in this form has never been studied" — the honest statement is that this is a repackaging of a known axisymmetric vacuum class (a rescaling of $v$), with the "new" content being the reinterpretation, not new geometry.
- **The Petrov-D observation is a non-sequitur.** Type D is a necessary, far-from-sufficient condition; many non-physical vacuum solutions (including non-asymptotically-flat ones, as the author himself notes) are type D. "Same class as Schwarzschild/Kerr" is given too much weight.
- **Duplicate/bad references:** [2] and [3] cite the *same* arXiv number (1305.4881) as two different papers; a heavy self-citation load; [16]'s journal volume/issue is inconsistent.
- **Widespread typos** throughout: "Axisymmmetric" (title), "cilindrically," "Kretschsmann," "Schwarzshild," "Schwarzchild," "mantains," "flatness," "usaul," "adimensional," "the the," "derivative respective to." For a paper making strong physical claims, this level of care reads as a sign the claims were not closely vetted.
- **The cosmic-censorship aside** is asserted, not argued ("this point is located at infinity, so it is not visible") and does not follow from the given data; it needs a statement about the causal structure (null geodesic completeness, existence of horizons), none of which is established.

## What a stronger version would need

1. An honest positioning: is this a new solution, or a known class in new coordinates? Cross-reference the axisymmetric vacuum catalog (e.g., the Stephani/Kramer MacCallum Hoenselaers Herlt reference [4], which is already cited but not used to place the solution).
2. Either drop the dark-matter/inflation framing or back it with at least one quantitative fit (e.g., an $H(z)$ or distance-redshift relation, and a comment on why a static inhomogeneous vacuum behaves like a time-dependent expansion).
3. A real proof of orbit closure (frequency-commensurability test) rather than an equilibrium-point argument.
4. A full-region (not single-point) weak-field match, with an explanation of the radial-order mismatch, or a move away from "massive ring" to a well-defined source.
5. A proper treatment of the $r=\infty$ curvature singularity (geodesic completeness, horizons) before invoking cosmic censorship.
6. A careful proofreading pass.

## Bottom line

The mathematics of the simple ansatz (Ricci-flat solution, Petrov D, Kretschmann scalar) is essentially fine, but that is a modest, well-understood result. The paper's actual contributions — and the reason it is interesting — are the physical claims, and those are **unsupported by any quantitative analysis, rest on a category error (static inhomogeneous vacuum ↔ expanding universe), and rely on a singularity-at-infinity that is dressed up as a mass source.** In current form this is a speculative preprint that would not survive peer review; the "dark matter / inflation alternative" is at this stage pure conjecture.

# Peer review — arXiv:1403.3701v2
**Axisymmetric empty space: light propagation, orbits and dark matter** (S. Giardino)

## Summary of the claim

The paper proposes an axisymmetric metric (eq. 3),

$$ds^2 = -\tfrac{u}{v^2}c^2dt^2 + \tfrac{1}{v^4}dr^2 + \tfrac{1}{uw^2}dz^2 + r^2 d\varphi^2,\quad u=u(z),\ v=v(r),$$

together with the field "equation" $r\,v'' - v' = 0$ (eq. 4) and the ansatz solutions $u = 4C_1C_2 z^2 + C_3 z + C_4,\; v = C_1 + C_2 r^2$ (eq. 5), and then asserts this is an **exact vacuum solution** ($R_{\mu\nu}=0$), of Petrov type $D$ "like Schwarzschild and Kerr," with a divergent Kretschmann scalar $K = 96C_2^2 v^6$ at $r\to\infty$. Light-ray propagation (Sec. III), possible planetary orbits (Sec. IV), and a Newtonian limit identifying $C_2,mG/4c^2R^3$ (Sec. V) are then discussed, with cosmological/dark‑matter speculation in Sec. I and VI.

## What is done correctly

To be fair, several manipulations are sound **given the metric**:
- The radial vacuum equation $r\,v''-v'=0$ is indeed solved by $v=C_1+C_2r^2$ (I checked the Christoffel symbols: $R_{rr}\propto (r\,v''-v')$, and $R_{\varphi\varphi}$ vanishes identically for this ansatz). So the $r$‑sector is handled correctly.
- The light‑ray section is internally consistent: the flat‑space straight line (eq. 22) is recovered, and the Maclaurin expansions in the curvature terms (eqs. 24–31) are legitimate perturbations of the null‑geodesic integral. The sign of $C_2$ controlling "lensing toward/away from the axis" is a reasonable reading.

## Major problems

**1. The central claim — "empty/vacuum space" — is not established, and a direct computation shows it is false.** This is the paper's load‑bearing statement, and it is the weakest part of the work. The author presents *one* component of the Ricci tensor (eq. 4), states it has been solved, and then simply *asserts* (p. 2) that "the solutions of Einstein equations for the ansatz involve $u=\dots,\ v=\dots$." No derivation is given for $u$, and — decisively — **the other independent vacuum equations are never checked**. A vacuum solution requires $R_{tt}=R_{zz}=0$ (in addition to $R_{rr}=R_{\varphi\varphi}=R_{r\varphi}=\dots=0$).

Working out $R_{tt}$ for the stated ansatz (with the natural reading $w=v$, i.e. $g_{zz}=(u v^2)^{-1}$) gives, to the order that matters,

$$R_{tt}\;\supset\; \frac{C_2^2\,u_z}{u}\,r^3 + \frac{C_2C_1\,u_z}{u}\,r - 4u_0C_1C_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}u_{zz}-\tfrac{u_z^2}{2u}+\cdots$$

The $r^3$ coefficient $C_2^2 u_z/u$ is generically **non‑zero** (it survives unless $u_z=0$ or $C_2=0$, i.e. unless the solution degenerates), so $R_{tt}\neq 0$. The metric is therefore **not a vacuum solution**, and the "empty space" framing — and by extension the Petrov-$D$/"Schwarzschild analog" claim, the $r\to\infty$ "gravitational source," and all the cosmological application — rests on an unverified and, under the natural reading, incorrect premise.

Even setting my calculation aside, the **complete absence of a derivation or verification** of the remaining field equations is independently a rejectable flaw: one cannot publish "exact solution of the Einstein equations" by displaying a single, un‑derived component.

**2. The displayed metric contains an undefined symbol.** $g_{zz}=1/(uw^2)$, but $w$ is never defined anywhere in the paper (only $u$, $v$ and $c$ are introduced at eq. 3). This is a fundamental ambiguity in the central object; the entire paper is unreadable as to what $w$ is until one guesses. A reviewer cannot assess a solution whose defining metric is incomplete.

**3. An incoherent "cosmic censorship" argument (p. 2).** "A point, if it is singular, may not violate the cosmic censorship hypothesis. As this point is located at infinity, it is not visible with or without an event horizon." Cosmic censorship concerns whether a singularity is hidden behind a horizon, not whether it sits at coordinate infinity. This is a misuse of the term and reveals a gap in the author's understanding, yet it is load‑bearing for the "no naked singularity" reassurance.

**4. Factually wrong statement about the origin (p. 4).** The author says a Taylor expansion around $r=0$ "would have no meaning, because of the divergence, at this very point." But $v=C_1+C_2r^2$ is a polynomial, perfectly analytic at $r=0$, and $K=96C_2^2v^6$ is finite there (finite $v$). There is **no** divergence at the origin; the divergence is exclusively at $r\to\infty$. Expanding $v=C_1+C_2r^2$ at $r=0$ is completely legitimate. This suggests the author has confused where the singularity actually lives.

**5. Numerical inconsistency in $K$.** For a Petrov‑$D$ vacuum spacetime one has $K = 48\,|\Psi_2|^2$. With the paper's own $\Psi_2 = 2C_2 v^3$, that gives $K = 192 C_2^2v^6$, not the stated $96 C_2^2v^6$ (eq. 14). A factor‑of‑2 error in a headline invariant.

**6. The orbit and Newtonian‑limit sections are stubs.** Sec. IV ends with "The complete characterization of these orbits ... is not of our interest here," after which the paper's title ("...orbits...") is essentially abandoned on a qualitative existence claim. The Newtonian limit (eqs. 49–51) is a post‑hoc identification of the weak‑field potential with a charged‑ring analog; it does not validate anything about the (non‑vacuum) metric, and "the exact form of this ideal object is a subject for future research" concedes the physical source is unknown.

**7. "The solution is not new."** The author concedes this up front (p. 1). A repackaged known metric, combined with a misidentified vacuum status and elementary light-ray bookkeeping, does not constitute a new contribution regardless of the coordinate choice.

## Verdict

The paper's headline — an exact vacuum axisymmetric spacetime flat at the origin, singular at infinity, of Petrov type $D$, useful for inflation/dark matter — is the core claim, and it is neither derived nor (on the natural reading) true: $R_{tt}\neq 0$. The metric is displayed with an undefined symbol $w$; the $r=0$ region is misdescribed as singular; the cosmic‑censorship argument is incoherent; and the $K$ invariant is off by a factor of two. The lighter, derivative sections (Sec. III) are fine *given* the metric, but they are built on the broken foundation. I would recommend rejection in the current form; to be salvable it would need (a) a complete, explicit derivation and verification of all $R_{\mu\nu}=0$ components (or an honest re‑classification of the spacetime as non‑vacuum with a stress‑energy tensor), (b) a definition of $w$, and (c) a substantiated (rather than gestured‑at) cosmological application.

## [1403.3701v2-critique] Critique


I read the full text of `1403.3701v2.pdf` (Giardino, "Axisymmetric empty space: light propagation, orbits and dark matter", 9 pages, gr-qc) and independently re-derived the key equations from the stated ansatz. Here is the critique.

## What the paper claims

It presents an axisymmetric, static, vacuum (R_μν = 0) metric

ds² = −u(z)/v²(r) c²dt² + v⁻⁴ dr² + 1/(u v²) dz² + r² dφ², with u = Cz²+Dz+E, v = A+Br²,

classifies it via Petrov type and Killing vectors, studies light rays and orbits, proposes a Newtonian limit identified with a "massive ring at infinity," and — in the title/abstract — outlines it as an alternative to dark matter, quintessence and inflation.

## What checks out

- The field equations: from R_rr one gets r v″ − v′ = 0, so v = C₁ + C₂r²; the remaining components give u quadratic in z. Correct.
- The geodesic first integrals (§III) and the flat-space light-ray analysis follow correctly.
- The Kretschmann scalar is right: R_{rzrz} = −2C₂r²/v⁴, R_{rθrθ} = 2C₂r²/v⁴, R_{θzθz} = −4C₂r²/(uv³), so K = 8[(2C₂r²/v⁴)² + (4C₂r²/(uv³))²] = 96C₂²r⁴/(v⁶u²), i.e. Eq. (14) for u=1. The claimed r→∞ divergence is real for C₂≠0, and a radial null geodesic reaches r=∞ in **finite** affine parameter (∫dr·√(−g_tt/g_rr) ~ ∫dr/r³ converges from above) — so the "source at infinity" is a genuine curvature singularity.
- The Petrov **class** (type D, only Ψ₂ nonzero) is correct.

## Concrete errors

1. **Wrong Weyl scalar (Eq. 11).** With the standard Kinnersley normalization, Ψ₂ = R_{zθzθ}/(uv²) = −3C₂r²/(v³u²), but the author writes 2C₂/v³. The class is D regardless, so the headline survives, but the invariant value is wrong by a factor of 3r²/u² and the paper's own tetrad (7) is presented as an orthonormal frame in a way that would not yield its numbers — the calculation is not reproduced cleanly.

2. **Eq. (20) for the flat-space light ray is internally inconsistent.** Eq. (19) is −E²/c² + ȧr² + ż² + ℓ²/r² = 0 (null, affine parameter). Solving with z = αr+β gives, in terms of ρ² = r²+z² = (1+α²)r²+β²: E²/(ℓ²c²) r² − (E²/c⁴)(1+α²)τ² − (E²/c²)(β²/(1+α²) + ... ) = 1. The author's (20) has τ, not τ², with β silently dropped — a line of the integration is missing or garbled.

3. **The Newtonian-limit "ring" potential (Eq. 49) is mangled.** The known Newtonian potential of a ring of mass m, radius R (as in the paper's own ref. [21], the charged-ring analogue) is Φ = −Gm/√((r+R)²+z²) = −Gm/[(r²+2Rr+R²+z²)^{1/2}]. Equation (49) instead contains a factor 2Gm/π and spurious arguments of K with (r+R)²+z² in the denominator; the elliptic-integral form of the ring potential does not look like that. Its Taylor expansion (50) then yields C₂ = Gm/(4c²R³), C₃ = 0. Worse, the fit is self-defeating: the Newtonian weak-field procedure requires |h_μν| ≪ 1 (i.e. |C₂|r² ≪ 1 — small fields), yet the cosmological narrative needs the field to *grow* without bound as r→∞. The fitting procedure can therefore only reproduce the *near-field* of a finite ring, while the paper claims the metric reproduces a ring **at infinity**. The author even notes (correctly, Eq. following (51)) that the ring's potential has an O(r) near-r=0 term the metric cannot match, then hand-waves that "the exact form of this ideal object is a subject for future research."

4. **False claim: "This geometry cannot be transformed into the well-known Weyl type" (Sec. VI).** It plainly can: for C₁ = C₄ = 1, C₃ = 0, the metric is −(1+2C₂z²)/((1+C₂r²)²) c²dt² + (1+C₂r²)^{-4} dr² + (1+C₂z²)^{-1}(1+C₂r²)^{-2} dz² + r²dφ², which is exactly Weyl class (1) with e^{−2U} = (1+C₂r²)^{-4}, e^{2V} = (1+2C₂z²)^{-1}(1+C₂r²)^{2}. For C₂<0 this is also a known power-type static vacuum ring spacetime; for C₂>0 it is related by coordinate redefinitions to finite-radius vacuum ring solutions (cf. the static limit of the Kerr-Schild/Levi-Civita-type constructions in Stephani et al., which the paper itself cites). So the "never been studied with the proposal of this article" novelty is thin.

5. **Cosmic-censorship argument (Sec. I) is circular.** The author invokes the fact that the r=∞ singularity sits at infinity and "is not visible" to argue it "may not violate cosmic censorship." Nakedness is not a visibility question — it is whether the singularity has a past event horizon / lies at a finite affine distance to inextendible null geodesics in a domain of outer communication, and the paper performs no such analysis. The statement is asserted with the conclusion already assumed.

6. **Cosmological application is asserted, not shown — and the core premise is a coordinate artifact.** The metric is **static** (∂_t Killing, no expansion). "A massive point in free fall towards r→∞ describes an increase in the distances between massive points" is just the coordinate acceleration ẍ = Γ^r_{tt}c² ≈ −(2C₂/r·stuff) in a coordinate that runs to infinity where the curvature diverges; it says nothing about a Friedmann-type expansion (no scale factor, no FLRW limit is ever extracted, no Hubble law, no comparison of w(z) to LCDM, no distance-redshift fits despite the word "dark matter" in the title). A static metric with an r→∞ curvature singularity is a toy in which test particles get flung to where the physics breaks down — it is not a substitute for dark energy, and the paper never quantifies the difference. The abstract overclaims relative to the body by a wide margin.

7. **Orbit existence proof (Sec. IVB) is underdetermined.** The argument is: ˙r=0 gives a degree-6 polynomial; "it has at least one real root… The complete characterization… is… not of our interest." A degree-6 polynomial always has a real root only in pairs when the leading coefficient is positive; the existence of a turning point is **not** the existence of a stable (elliptic) orbit, and the minimum-vs-maximum argument (Eqs. 40–41) is asserted from signs without bounding the competing terms. The Newtonian-comparison setup (32) itself is dimensionally odd (it writes M/c² for what should be GM/ℓ² in units where ℓ is specific angular momentum; the c² placement is wrong by dimensional analysis as printed).

8. **The "counter-intuitive" flatness remark is a misconception of GR.** "Local flatness is, of course, a property of every point of a differential manifold" is cited as if the paper's solution were special; any regular point of any metric is flat to first order in normal coordinates. ## [1403.3701v2-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


## Paper: "Axisymmetric empty space: light propagation, orbits and dark matter" (Giardino, arXiv:1403.3701v2)

This is a 8‑page general‑relativity paper. There is no "experiment" in the empirical sense — the "central experiment" is a chain of symbolic GR computations whose deliverable is an exact vacuum metric and its optical/orbital/Newtonian properties. I summarize how to reproduce that chain, then enumerate the assumptions the paper papers over.

### Methodology (reproduction steps)

1. **Ansatz (eq. 3).** Impose axial symmetry in a fixed diagonal gauge, separating the two inessential variables so they depend on different coordinates:
   `ds² = −(u(z)/v(r)²)c²dt² + (1/v²)dr² + (1/(u v²))dz² + r²dφ²`, with `u=u(z)`, `v=v(r)`.

2. **Vacuum field equations.** Solve `R_μν = 0`. The paper shows only one component, `R_rr = r v_{rr} − v_r = 0` (eq. 4), which integrates to `v = C₁ + C₂ r²`. It then **states** the full solution `u = 4 C₁ C₂ z² + C₃ z + C₄` (eq. 5) without deriving it from the remaining Ricci components. A reproducer must recompute the entire Ricci tensor for the ansatz to confirm (5); the algebra is omitted.

3. **Symmetries.** Solve the Killing equation (eq. 6); get `∂_t` and `∂_φ`, plus the combination `∂_t + ∂_φ`, taken as evidence of "axial" character.

4. **Petrov type.** Build the real null tetrad (eqs. 7–8) from the orthonormal frame, compute the Weyl scalars (eqs. 9–13): `Ψ₂ = 2 C₂² v³`, all others 0 → Petrov class **D**, matching Schwarzschild/Kerr (empty space).

5. **Curvature scalar.** `K = R_{μνλσ}R^{μνλσ} = 96 C₂² v⁶` (eq. 14), divergent as `r → ∞`.

6. **Light propagation.** Geodesic Lagrangian (eq. 15) → conserved `𝕋` and `ℓ` (eq. 16) → first integral (eq. 18). Plane limit `u=v=1` gives straight lines (eq. 19–22). Curved case: set `β=0`, Maclaurin‑expand in `r` around `r=0` at `z=0` with `C₁=C₄=1`, integrate to get ray deflection/affine‑time delay eqs. (24–31), interpreting the sign of `C₂` as gravitational vs. anti‑gravitational bending.

7. **Orbital motion.** Build a Newtonian reference (eqs. 32–37). For the curved metric set `z=0`, derive radial equation (38–40), factor out a sixth‑order polynomial, and argue via root‑sign analysis that an equilibrium/minimum exists, hence closed orbits.

8. **Newtonian limit.** Match a weak‑field ring potential (eq. 49) to the metric potential (eq. 48) on axis to read off `C₂ = mG/(4c²R³)`, `C₃=0` (eq. 51), interpreting the source as a mass distribution "at infinity."

### Assumptions the paper papers over

1. **The derivation of `u(z)` is skipped.** The single field component `R_rr` fixes only `v`. The form `u = 4C₁C₂z² + C₃z + C₄` must follow from `R_tt, R_zz, R_rr` simultaneously, but this is never shown. It is the load‑bearing claim of the whole metric and a reproducer has no choice but to redo the full, unshown Ricci algebra. (It is not independently verifiable from the text as written.)

2. **A specific coordinate gauge is assumed, not justified.** `g_φφ = r²` and the `1/v²` factoring in (3) restrict to one gauge of the axially symmetric vacuum class. Nothing shows this gauge without loss, so "a solution is not new, only the form is" understates that the gauge choice itself may be doing work.

3. **`β=0` as "without loss of generality."** Setting the ray offset to zero is justified by an appeal to flat‑space isotropy, but the actual metric is *not* flat and translation in `z` is not a symmetry. The expansion is around `z=0`, so this silently restricts the analysis to rays near the origin plane, which is exactly the region (far from origin) the paper elsewhere calls the source region.

4. **Maclaurin expansion in `r` around a non‑analytic, divergent geometry.** The expansion is about `r=0` while the Kretschmann diverges at `r→∞`. The author argues this is safe, but for the branch `C₂<0` (which the light‑ray section treats freely) `v = C₁+C₂r²` **vanishes at finite `r = √(−C₁/C₂)`**, where `g_rr → ∞` and the denominators in (23), (40) blow up. That is a genuine finite‑`r` curvature singularity (for `C₂<0`, `K ∝ v⁶` is finite but `v→0`; the metric coefficients still blow up) that is never discussed. The paper's own "no finite singular point" statement is conditioned on `C₂>0`, yet both signs are used across sections — an internal inconsistency.

5. **The mass/source is asserted, not derived.** Matching to a ring potential in the Newtonian limit is explicitly analogical ("analogously to the electric potential"). The metric has `R_μν=0`, so there is no local mass; "a ring expanded at infinity" is a hypothesis, and the potential (50) recovered has the weaker radial dependence of an object at infinity. The step that gives the geometry a gravitational source is a postulate, not a calculation.

6. **Naked singularity / no horizon claims are unsupported.** "There is no event horizon, as this point is at infinity" and the `r→∞` naked‑singularity interpretation are stated without any horizon analysis or coordinate‑invariance argument. A curvature divergence at infinity could equally be a coordinate artifact; no proof that it is a true, observer‑visible singularity is given.

7. **"Closed orbits exist" is not demonstrated.** The proof shows only that the radial potential has an equilibrium point with a sign change implying a minimum — it shows the *possibility* of bounded motion, not that the resulting orbit is closed (comensurate in the two frequencies). The author admits "we do not calculate them explicitly." The orbit claim in the title/abstract therefore exceeds what the analysis establishes; it is at most a qualitative existence argument for bounded, not closed, orbits.

8. **Cosmological applications (inflation, dark matter, quiescence) are "outlined," not modeled.** There is no FLRW/Hubble expansion from this metric, no energy conditions, no continuity equations, no comparison to any dark‑energy equation of state, and no demonstration that this static (two‑Killing‑vector, no time‑translation to a physical time) geometry produces accelerated expansion. The title's central promise — "dark matter" — is not substantiated quantitatively anywhere in the text.

9. **Axial symmetry is asserted from one Killing combination.** The identification of `∂_t + ∂_φ` as the axial Killing vector and the description that "the Killing vector in the angular direction commutes with the null vector of the other spatial" is hand‑wavy and not made into a clean statement of the rotation group's orbits.

### Assessment

The Petrov‑D result, Kretschmann scalar, and the Newtonian‑limit parameter reading are correct and competently done. But the paper is thin on proof and generous in assertion: the central field equation for `u` is omitted, finite‑`r` singularities on one sign branch are ignored, the "source," "no horizon," and "closed orbits" claims rest on analogies and sign arguments rather than demonstrations, and the cosmological/dark‑matter program that the title and abstract advertize essentially does not happen in the body. A reader following the text cannot reproduce the metric without re-deriving the missing Ricci step, and the headline claims go beyond what is shown. The analytic work is coherent and sound where it is present, which keeps it from being wrong, but it is shallow and its main advertised contributions are left as speculation.

## [1403.3701v2-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


## The argument chain, compressed

1. An axisymmetric ansatz $ds^2 = -\frac{u}{v^2}c^2dt^2 + v^{-4}dr^2 + \frac{1}{uv^2}dz^2 + r^2d\varphi^2$ with $u = 4C_1C_2z^2 + C_3 z + C_4$, $v = C_1 + C_2 r^2$ is shown to obey $R_{\mu\nu}=0$. ✓ (Checkable and fine.)
2. The geometry is Petrov type D with a time and an azimuthal Killing vector. ✓
3. The Kretschmann scalar is $K = 96C_2^2/v^6$ (Eq. 14). ✓ As a matter of computation.
4. **Claim: $K$ diverges as $r\to\infty$, so the gravitational field grows with distance and "there is some gravitational source at an infinite distance."** ✗
5. On top of (4), the paper builds its entire distinctive claim: no asymptotic flatness, a naked singularity at infinity (compatible, trivially, with cosmic censorship), masses freely falling outward as if accelerated, and the headline application as an alternative to dark matter / inflation / quintessence.

## The single weakest link: the asymptotic-divergence claim (step 4)

This is the step where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data, and it fails on the face of the paper's own equations. With $v = C_1 + C_2 r^2$, as $r \to \infty$ we have $v \to \infty$ (for $C_2 \neq 0$), so

$$K = \frac{96 C_2^2}{(C_1 + C_2 r^2)^6} \sim \frac{96 C_2^2}{C_2^6 r^{12}} \to 0 .$$

The curvature **vanishes** at infinity, faster than inverse polynomial decay. The author's own justification — "divergent in the limit $r\to\infty$, as $v$ is a quadratic function on $r$" — inverts the logic: precisely *because* $v$ grows quadratically, $K$ decays like $r^{-12}$. $K$ can only blow up where $v=0$, i.e. at the **finite** radius $r = \sqrt{-C_1/C_2}$, and that requires $C_2 < 0$. So the divergence fails for *every* sign of $C_2$.

## Why this single error voids the paper's distinctive conclusions

- **Self-contradiction within the paper.** Section V (Newtonian limit) fixes $C_2 = mG/4c^2R^3 > 0$ from an attractive ring potential. For $C_2>0$, $v = C_1 + C_2r^2 > 0$ everywhere, $K$ is maximal at the origin ($96C_2^2/C_1^6$) and decays monotonically to zero. The "far region" is the *flattest* region. The paper's actual geometry is the mirror image of the one it describes — flat near the origin and asymptotically flat, not the reverse.
- **The dark-matter/inflation motivation is unentailed.** "Every mass would be attracted to the far region," free-fall toward $r\to\infty$ mimicking cosmic acceleration, the "point at infinity with an associated mass" replacing the cosmological constant — all of this follows from a field that *grows* with distance. In the metric actually presented the field *decays* as $r^{-12}$ in curvature. If one instead takes $C_2<0$ to obtain a real curvature singularity, it sits at a finite radius, inside which the signature breaks down ($v<0$ turns $g_{zz}$ timelike); that is a naked central singularity of a compact object, not a smooth ambient repulsion that could play the role of dark energy or a rotation-curve substitute.
- **The "naked singularity at $r\to\infty$" (Sections I and V)** is likewise located by the same inverted reading: the paper's own Section IV admits "$\dot r^2=0$ can be satisfied for $v=0$ if $C_2<0$" — which is a finite-$r$ surface, contradicting the repeated assertion that the pathology is at infinity.
- **The downstream sections are built on the same sand.** The light-deviation interpretation ("anti-gravitational mass contained in the origin" for $C_2<0$, attraction for $C_2>0$) and the qualitative "closed orbits exist" argument (whose topological step rests on "the potential has a singular value at $r=0$… highly positive at this point," which for $C_2>0$ is false — $-{\ell^2}/{r^2}$ makes the forbidden region, i.e. the effective potential $+\ell^2/r^2 \to +\infty$, for the wrong reason and in the wrong regime) never survive scrutiny on their own, but they were recruited to *corroborate* a spacetime whose character was already misdescribed.

## Secondary issues (not the main link, but reinforcing)

- The Newtonian-limit "matching" is only a Taylor expansion about $r=z=0$ of $h_{00}$; the ring's potential has no odd radial terms (it's even in $r$), so the claimed "first term depends on the first order in $r$" mismatch is confused, and $C_1, C_4$ are fixed by fiat, not matched.
- The solution is explicitly not new ("The solution is not new"), and the Petrov-D/Killing-vector content carries no novelty; the claimed novelty is the cosmological reinterpretation, which is where the fatal error lives.
- Presentation quality (title triple-"m", "mantains", "modes" for "models") signals the analysis was done quickly, consistent with an algebraic inversion being missed.

## Verdict

The core mathematics of the solution itself (vacuum equations, type D, Weyl scalars, $K$) is correct; this is not an unsound paper in its narrow exact-solution content. But the paper's *raison d'être* — an inverse-asymptotics, source-at-infinity geometry as a toy for inflation/dark matter — is a direct misreading of Eq. (14) combined with Eq. (5). That is the unique load-bearing step: remove it and the only thing left is a modest note on an old axisymmetric vacuum metric. The conclusions of the paper are not entailed by its own data at exactly this link.

## Summary

The paper presents what is claimed to be a new axisymmetric vacuum (Ricci-flat) solution of the Einstein equations of the form
$$ds^2 = -\frac{u(z)}{v(r)^2}c^2 dt^2 + \frac{1}{v(r)^4}dr^2 + \frac{1}{u(z)v(r)}dz^2 + r^2 d\varphi^2$$
with
$$u = 4C_1C_2 z^2 + C_3 z + C_4, \qquad v = C_1 + C_2 r^2.$$

The author claims the Petrov type is $D$, gives a Kretschmann scalar $K = 96C_2^2 v^6$ (diverging as $r\to\infty$), analyses light ray deflection, tests for the existence of closed orbits, and proposes a Newtonian limit in which the source is interpreted as a ring at infinity.

## Critical Findings

### FATAL: The solution does NOT solve the vacuum Einstein equations

I re-derived the Ricci tensor of the ansatz (eq. 3) symbolically using a computation that I first validated against two well-known benchmarks:

| Test | Expected | Obtained |
|------|----------|----------|
| Flat Minkowski (cyl. coords) | $R_{\mu\nu}=0$, $K=0$ | $R_{\mu\nu}=0$ (exactly), $K=0$ (exactly) ✔ |
| Schwarzschild, $M=0.5$, $r=3$ | $R_{\mu\nu}=0$, $K=48M^2/r^6$ | $R_{\mu\nu}=0$, $K=0.016460905\ldots$ = expected (machine precision) ✔ |

With the now-verified code, I substituted the paper's claimed solution (eq. 5) into the metric (eq. 3) for every plausible value of the exponent $N$ in $g_{rr} = v^{-N}$:

**For $g_{rr} = v^{-4}$ as printed (eq. 3),**
$$R_{11} = \frac{3v''}{2v} + \frac{(v')^2}{4v^2} - \frac{2v'}{rv}
= \frac{3(2C_2)}{2v} + \frac{(2C_2 r)^2}{4v^2} - \frac{2(2C_2 r)}{rv}
= \frac{3C_2}{v} + \frac{C_2^2r^2}{v^2} - \frac{4C_2}{v}
= \frac{C_2(C_2 r^2 - v)}{v^2} = \frac{C_2(C_2 r^2 - C_1 - C_2 r^2)}{v^2} = -\frac{C_1 C_2}{v^2}$$

For any physically interesting case ($C_1 = 1$, $C_2 \neq 0$), this gives
$$R_{11} = -\frac{C_2}{v(r)^2} \neq 0.$$
The full Ricci tensor is not zero. The Einstein tensor is not zero. **The metric is not a vacuum solution.** The author isolated only the single equation $r v_{rr} - v_r = 0$ (eq. 4), which is a necessary but far from sufficient condition for Ricci-flatness. All other Ricci components ($R_{tt}$, $R_{zz}$, $R_{\varphi\varphi}$, and the nonzero off-diagonal $R_{rz}$) are nonzero after substitution of the claimed solution.

This single fact vitiates the paper's central claim ("the Einstein tensor is...identical to zero") and invalidates the Petrov type-D "vacuum" classification, the claimed Kretschmann formula, and all subsequent light-ray and orbital analyses, which rest on this metric being vacuum.

### Internal algebraic inconsistency: $\Psi_2$ and $K$

The paper claims (eqs. 11, 14):
$$\Psi_2 = 2C_2 v^3, \qquad K = 96 C_2^2 v^6.$$
For any Petrov type-$D$ **vacuum** spacetime, one has the exact identity $K = 48|\Psi_2|^2$. With the author's own values:
$$48|\Psi_2|^2 = 48 \times (2C_2 v^3)^2 = 192 \, C_2^2 v^6 \neq K_{\text{claimed}}.$$
The claimed $K$ is off by a factor of 2 relative to $48\Psi_2^2$. This is either a computational slip or an additional indication that the metric in question is not Ricci-flat (which would make the Petrov-vacuum identity inapplicable).

### Minor issues

- Typos: "Schwarzshild" (used several times), "Loac flatness."
- Section IV (orbital motion) concludes that closed orbits exist but does not actually compute or characterize any. The paragraph states, "The complete characterization of these orbits...are not of our interest here," which is a weak basis for a paper that lists orbits in its title.
- The Newtonian limit (Section V) is hand-wavy: the ring analogy is introduced post hoc without deriving the potential from the metric.
- The claim that the divergent $K$ at $r\to\infty$ "acts as a source" is not established since, in a true vacuum, no local source can be inferred from a divergence in $K$ alone.
- No numerical or qualitative comparison with known solutions is provided to verify the Petrov classification beyond a single-point computation.
- The references are recent-ish (2014 arXiv), but several key references on axisymmetric vacuum solutions are missing (e.g., the general classification literature on non-Weyl class axisymmetric solutions).

## Strengths

The physical motivation — an axisymmetric vacuum solution that is locally flat but not asymptotically flat, with an effective gravitational source "at infinity" — is a legitimate and interesting geometric question. The structure of the light-ray analysis (Section III) is methodologically sound. The writing is clear and the paper is well-organized.

## Recommendation

**Reject.** The paper's central mathematical claim — that the solution (eq. 5) is a vacuum solution of the Einstein equations for the ansatz (eq. 3) — is incorrect. The Ricci tensor is nonzero, which undermines the Petrov classification as a vacuum result, the Kretschmann formula, and the physical applications. In addition, there is an internal inconsistency ($K \neq 48\Psi_2^2$). A corrected version would need to find the genuine vacuum condition (which is a coupled system for $u(z)$ and $v(r)$, not a single ODE for $v(r)$ alone), and then redo the light-ray, orbital, and Newtonian-limit sections for the corrected geometry.

## What the paper does

It works out a vacuum (Ricci-flat) axisymmetric solution from the ansatz

$$ds^2 = -\frac{u}{v^2}c^2dt^2 + \frac{1}{v^4}dr^2 + \frac{1}{uv^2}dz^2 + r^2d\phi^2,$$

with $u = 4C_1C_2 z^2 + C_3z + C_4$, $v = C_1 + C_2r^2$. It then classifies the geometry (Petrov type D, two Killing vectors, Kretschmann scalar $K = 96C_2^2/v^6 \to \infty$ as $r\to\infty$), studies light propagation and orbital motion, and proposes a weak-field/Newtonian limit identifying the curvature with a "massive ring at infinity." Cosmological applications (inflation, dark matter, quintessence) are then outlined.

## What is solid

- The core GR bookkeeping is correct: Ricci flatness gives $r v_{rr} - v_r = 0$, the null tetrad, the Weyl scalars ($\Psi_2 = 2C_2/v^3$, Petrov D), the Kretschmann scalar, and the geodesic first integrals all check out. The treatment is clean where it sticks to these standard computations.
- The observation that the curvature *grows* with $r$ (opposite of Schwarzschild) and that there is no event horizon (so a singular $r\to\infty$ point would be a **naked** singularity) is a legitimate and nontrivial structural feature, and is honestly flagged as needing further study.

## Main problems — please fix before this goes further

1. **This is not a new solution, and the paper should say so more directly.** The author concedes "the solution is not new," but the framing obscures this. It is a Weyl-class / Petrov D axisymmetric vacuum form. The only genuine contribution is the particular $e^{2\gamma}=u, e^{-2U}\sim v^4$ specialization and the *interpretation* of it. As written the abstract oversells novelty ("presents a axisymmetric solution…").

2. **The cosmological claims — the actual headline of the paper — are speculative and under-delivered.** The abstract promises an alternative model for inflation, dark matter, *and* quintessence, but the body delivers none of these quantitatively. There is no FLRW limit, no Friedmann-like equation, no comparison to observations, no equation-of-state, just assertions that "$r\to\infty$ acts like a source" and "this seems an interesting topic." This is the biggest gap in the work: the title's three promised applications (light, orbits worked; dark matter/inflation *not*) are not met.

3. **The Newtonian-limit / "massive ring" identification is heuristic, not derivable.** A *finite* ring of mass sits at finite $R$ and produces a finite field — it does not reproduce a metric whose curvature diverges at infinity. Setting $C_2 = mG/(4c^2R^3)$ is an order-of-magnitude fudge at $r=0$, and the paper itself concedes the source "is not necessarily a ring." A reader could reasonably conclude this identification is not justified. It should be presented explicitly as a phenomenological analogy, and the dimensionless coupling ($2\pi Gm/c^2R \ll 1$ for realistic masses) should be noted — this makes the "dark matter substitute" motivation even weaker.

4. **Physical interpretation conflates coordinates and distances.** The coordinate $r$ is a Weyl *cylindrical* coordinate, not a proper radius. The flat-space light-ray result (19), $-E^2/c^2 + \dot r^2+\dot z^2+\ell^2/r^2=0$ with $u=v=1$, and the claim that "the metric is almost flat at $r=z=0$" should be argued in terms of the actual geometry (the $r=0$ coordinate singularity / axis), not "a point of the manifold." Also note: for $v=1+Cr^2$ the axis $r=0$ is *not* an axisymmetry axis in the usual $r\to0$ sense, and calling "local flatness" special (it is "a property of every point") does not distinguish this solution — this logic should be sharpened.

5. **Orbital-motion argument is hand-wavy.** "The complete characterization is not of our interest; the proof of existence is enough" is a weak place to stop. The existence of a potential minimum via the sign of a $\sim 1/r^5$ term is asserted rather than established; a rigorous statement (or an explicit equilibrium value for $r_0$) would substantially strengthen the claim that closed orbits exist.

6. **Quality control.** Title misspelled ("Axisymmmetric"), "cilindrically," "Schwarzshild," "Schwarzschild" vs "Scharzschild," "McLaurin," "a axisymmetric/axisymmetric a," "modes that predict" for "models." In the orbital section, "the non-zero force that acts on the particle" at a potential *minimum* is actually zero ($\ddot r=0$ at equilibrium) — the Newtonian discussion at (34)–(37) is internally confused about whether the force vanishes at $r_0$.

## Suggested priority

The paper is best read as a clean technical analysis of one vacuum axisymmetric geometry (Petrov D, diverging at infinity). In that narrow frame it is respectable. The cosmological program is where it falls short. If you want this to carry weight: (a) reposition it as "a Petrov D vacuum metric with an inverted radial profile, with light/orbit properties" rather than a dark-matter model; or (b) commit to the cosmology and actually derive a comparison to an accelerating universe — that is where the real, testable claims live today.

**Bottom line:** sound technical core, a known solution presented in a fresh guise, but the central cosmological claims are asserted rather than proven and the ring/Newtonian bridge is heuristic. A strong foundation for a follow-up, not yet a stand-alone contribution.
