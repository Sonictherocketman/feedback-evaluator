## [1610.08635v1-general-review] General Review


I've read the full paper (Bednarek, "High energy neutrinos produced in the accretion disks by neutrons from nuclei disintegrated in the AGN jets"). Here is my review of its argument.

## Summary of the argument

The paper proposes a three-step neutrino-production chain in AGN:
1. Nuclei (taken as He) are accelerated to high Lorentz factors in re‑connection regions of the inner jet or at the jet/cocoon boundary, at distances where the seed disk photons can photodisintegrate them.
2. Disintegration liberates **relativistic neutrons** that, being neutral, escape the magnetically confined jet and propagate toward the dense, optically‑thick accretion disk.
3. In the disk the neutrons hadronic‑scatter with the matter, producing mesons that decay to neutrinos. The claim is that because the neutron‑production threshold is lower than that for photo‑pion (p–γ) production, and the neutrons inherit the parent power‑law spectrum, the resulting ν spectrum extends as a flat power law down to ~10 TeV and up to PeV — matching the IceCube extragalactic neutrino (ENB) spectrum, which pure p–γ models flatten below PeV.

## What is genuinely good

- **The core physical insight is sound and reasonably original.** Charged products (p, π±) from jet collisions are trapped in the jet's magnetic field and, in the dilute jet plasma, are more likely to radiatively lose energy or decay before reaching dense matter. A neutral secondaries that can be aimed at the disk is a legitimate way around the "where do the target photons/target matter meet the hadrons" tension that limits many p–γ jet models. Framing neutrons as an energy‑carry channel from jet to disk is a real idea, not a rehash of Nellen/Mücke.
- The **threshold argument** (nuclei‑disintegration energy ∝ A m_B E/T vs the p–γ pion threshold) is a credible reason the neutron channel could populate lower energies and keep the spectrum harder at the low end.
- It at least **attempts a falsifiable check** (M87: predicts ≲ ~0.7 ν/yr, i.e. non‑detection), which is better than many purely cosmogenic fits.

## Main weaknesses and the critique an author should address

1. **It is a chain of order‑of‑magnitude estimates, not a calculation.** Eqs. (1)–(21) are scaling laws threaded through free parameters (ξ, η, B_j, α₀, γ‑Lorentz, χ, and the lumped factor A_N). A_N is itself the product of five or more uncertain factors (AGN duty cycle, jet/accretion efficiency, acceleration efficiency, fraction of neutrons reaching the disk, disk fraction active), and the paper concedes the last two are "the most uncertain." Because of this degeneracy, the apparent agreement with IceCube in Fig. 3 is a *consistency* of two ~2σ, large‑error‑bar data points, not an *explanation*. There is no likelihood ratio, no Δχ², and — critically — no comparison against the natural competitors (p–γ in the jet, p–p in the disk, or a stacked BL Lac population). "The best description is at δ≈2.2, χ≈0.1" is a statement about where the curve fits the data, not evidence the mechanism is right.

2. **The neutron transport step is assumed, not derived, and the arithmetic looks inconsistent.** The crux of the whole model is that neutrons reach the disk before decaying over ~8×10¹⁸ cm. The paper states this requires only γ_n ≳ 8×10², and quotes the characteristic travel distance as X_N = c·t_n·γ_n ≳ 8×10¹⁸ cm. Taken literally, c·t_n ≈ 2.6×10¹³ cm, so c·t_n·γ_n ≥ 8×10¹⁸ cm demands γ_n ≳ 3×10⁵ — **about three orders of magnitude above the stated 8×10² threshold**. Either the decay‑length estimate is off or the neutron energies at the relevant (low) end of the spectrum simply cannot make the trip, in which case the low‑energy extension of the ν spectrum (the feature this model exists to explain) is in trouble. This deserves a careful, energy‑dependent calculation of the neutrons' angular and energy distribution at birth, the solid angle subtended by the disk, and the survival fraction — not the single threshold, and not the hand‑waived "(2–6)% of neutrons reach the disk" that sets the normalization.

3. **The normalization is tuned to the data.** To reproduce Fig. 3 the author uses AGN fraction ≈5%, χ≈0.01–0.1, 10% of jet power into nuclei, 10% of accretion into the jet, and a specific "active" disk fraction. None of these is independently constrained by the model; several are pulled to be consistent with the iceCube point cloud. A useful critique is that the model has enough free parameters to fit essentially any reasonable flat spectrum, so its "fit to IceCube" has low statistical power.

4. **Internal tension on the spectral index.** The paper invokes re‑connection acceleration (citing Larrabee et al. 2003) which is expected to give a power law with index near 1 (very flat), yet the "best description" needs δ≈2.2. So the natural spectral index of the cited accelerator is *not* the one required; the required index is assumed rather than shown to emerge. This should be flagged as a fine‑tuning of the parent spectrum.

5. **The competing channels are not modeled, so the neutron advantage is asserted, not demonstrated.** The paper argues the neutron channel beats p–γ because of its lower threshold, but never puts a p–γ term (or a p–p‑in‑disk term, which in an optically thick dense disk can be significant) into the same cascade. The disk "hadronic cascade proceeds efficiently" is assumed. Without a simultaneous multi‑channel calculation, the distinctive claim — that neutrons *dominate* the low‑energy part — is not established.

6. **The M87 "test" does not discriminate.** Predicting non‑detection from the nearest AGN is true trivially for essentially every ENB‑normalization model (M87 at z≈0.004 carries negligible volume contribution). It cannot distinguish the neutron model from p–γ/p–p cosmogenic scenarios, so it does not advance the case for this mechanism specifically. A more informative prediction would be, e.g., a distinctive correlation with disk vs. jet emission, or a different neutrino–γ‑ray energy ratio, neither of which is developed.

7. **Minor issues.** (a) "Larabee et al. 2003" should be **Larrache et al. 2003** (ApJ 586, 72). (b) Eq. 6 cites "Cyrbut et al. 2003" (apparently a typo for **Cyburt et al. 2003**, the nuclear‑data compilation in the reference list), which is a mismatched citation. (c) The accreted composition is taken as exactly 75% H / 25% He (n̄_A = 6×10²³) and He is the only disintegrating species; real AGN ISM has CNO and the "He‑rich because of star formation" motivation in the intro is not carried quantitatively into the calculations. (d) The redshift is truncated at z_max = 2 with "negligible" above; fine, but worth stating the assumed evolution in SMBH/accretion rate explicitly.

## Bottom line

The central idea — using photodisintegration‑produced neutrons as neutral messengers that carry jet‑accelerated nuclear energy into the dense disk, thereby sidestepping magnetic confinement and the p–γ low‑energy threshold — is physically sensible and a genuinely fresh angle on the IceCube ENB problem. But in its present form the paper is a string of scaling arguments with several degenerate, data‑fitted free parameters, a quantitative step (neutron decay length / fraction reaching the disk) that is under‑derived — and appears numerically inconsistent — and a single‑object prediction that cannot discriminate the model. It reads as a credible *exploratory* proposal, not yet a quantitatively convincing mechanism. The author should (i) fix/derive the neutron transport survival fraction, (ii) compute the ENB with competing channels in the same framework, (iii) show a statistical comparison to IceCube rather than "agreement," and (iv) produce a genuinely distinguishing, positive prediction.

# Peer Review

**Paper:** *High energy neutrinos produced in the accretion disks by neutrons from nuclei disintegrated in the AGN jets* (W. Bednarek, 2016)

## Summary

The author proposes a hadronic neutrino channel in active galaxies: heavy nuclei (chiefly He) are accelerated to modest relative energies in the jet's reconnection regions and/or at the jet–cocoon boundary; this soft radiation from the inner accreton disk photonuclearly disintegrates the accelerated nuclei into relativistic neutrons; the neutrons — which decay more slowly than the equivalent proton-induced channels would allow — reach the dense, optically-thick inner disk and there produce pions and hence neutrinos. The neutrino spectra are then integrated over the SMBH population to build the extragalactic neutrino background (ENB) and compared to IceCube's 2013–2015 measurements, and an event-rate estimate is given for M87.

## What the paper does well

- **A genuinely interesting mechanism.** Pivoting on *neutron extraction via photonuclear spallation of nuclei* rather than the canonical $p\gamma$ or $pp$ is a real conceptual shift. The qualitative point that the disintegration cross section is $\sim$an order of magnitude larger, and the threshold lower, than pion production, so the neutron spectra can extend above $\sim$10 TeV where the $p\gamma$ component flattens, is a legitimate and usefully stated advantage. This is a reasonable answer to the otherwise-motivating "low-energy upturn" argument against standard photo-hadronic models.
- **Internal consistency of the scaling chain.** Eqs. (3), (6)–(8) (disintegration faster than crossing; the Lorentz-factor bound from reconnection acceleration) and Eqs. (10)–(11) (acceleration length vs. disintegration length) are applied coherently. The order-of-magnitude conclusion that a few-pc inner-jet region satisfies both conditions for $10^{6}–10^{9}\,M_\odot$ BHs (with the conditions getting "easier" for more massive BHs, Eq. 8) is self-consistent and the sign of the scaling is correct.
- **Honest hedges.** The author repeatedly flags $\sim$10–20% parameter uncertainty and explicitly treats the M87 $\sim$0.7 event/yr rate as an *upper limit* (since the disk/accretion rate there is low, making the SS model an upper bound). That restraint keeps the claims within defensible bounds.
- **A falsifiable nearby-object prediction** (M87 detectability) is provided, which is good practice.

## Main weaknesses

1. **Entirely order-of-magnitude; no numerical verification.** The whole chain — nuclear spallation yields, neutron transport and decay lengths, the "cascade in the dense disk," radiative/cooling feedback — is a series of scaling estimates. There is no Monte-Carlo transport, no particle-level cascade, no back-reaction on the jet plasma. This is a *plausibility* model, and the word "cascade" in the abstract is used to describe steady-state fluxes that are assumed rather than derived. A reviewer in 2016 (and certainly now) would expect at least a transport-code cross-check of the neutron → pion → neutrino yield and the cooling lengths in the magnetized disk before the ENB is claimed.

2. **A very large, effectively single effective free parameter.** The normalization $A_N \simeq 2\times10^{-6}$ is, as the author admits, a product of an AGN fraction ($\sim$5%), an accretion efficiency ($\chi=0.01$–0.1), a jet-power fraction (~10%), an acceleration efficiency (~10%), a neutral fraction, and a "few coefficients." These are nearly degenerate; $A_N$ is effectively tuned, not derived. Fitting $\sim5$ degenerate, order-of-magnitude factors to a background spectrum whose own uncertainty is a factor of $\sim$2 (as the paper correctly states) does not amount to *explaining* the IceCube ENB. Several independent AGN/hadronic models can do the same fit. The "explanation" claim in the abstract overstates what the calculation supports.

3. **Geometry is loose.** Nuclei are accelerated along a collimated jet that tilts toward the disk, yet the neutrons are described as "directed to the disk" and later "isotropic" in the cocoon frame, and the resulting neutrinos "mildly beamed in the direction perpendicular to the disk." How a collimated jet deposits neutrons into a face-on disk, and how that yields a non-strongly-beamed, all-flavour background over the population, is not worked out quantitatively. Since the ENB comparison depends on the beaming/covering fraction, this vagueness propagates directly into the flux normalization.

4. **The efficiency arguments are comparative, not quantitative.** "Nuclear disintegration is $\sim$an order of magnitude more efficient than $p\gamma$ pion production" is the load-bearing claim, but it is never computed as a ratio of yields; the actual neutron energy spectrum from spallation (and its fraction of the parent nuclear energy, claimed to be $8^{-1}$ to $2^{-1}$) is assumed. This is the single most important number in the paper, and it is not computed.

5. **Context of the data (2016).** The 2015 IceCube HESE sample (~27–29 events, rising-spectrum "feature") is the comparison target. With the systematics the author acknowledges, the spectral index (they require $\delta\approx2.2$, "clearly flatter than 2... taking into account large error bars") and the cut-off energy are not tightly discriminating between this model and the alternatives. The ENB point and its fit are therefore weak as a discriminator.

## Verdict (as a reviewer)

This is a **major revision** in tone. The physical idea is sound, original enough to be interesting, and internally consistent at the level it is presented; it is not wrong. But the rigor is low: the central efficiency ratio and the neutron yield are assumed rather than computed, the geometry is hand-waved, and the "we can explain the IceCube ENB" conclusion exceeds the quantitative support given five near-degenerate, order-of-magnitude parameters and data with factor-2 uncertainties. The paper would become substantially stronger with (i) a transport-code calculation of the neutron spallation yield and the pion/neutrino cascade and cooling in the disk, (ii) a systematic (and honestly reported) parameter scan rather than a single tuned $A_N$, and (iii) a statistical (likelihood) fit to the IceCube data making the comparison explicit that the model is *consistent with*, not uniquely favored over, simpler hadronic AGN models.

It is a respectable, thoughtfully-hedged theoretical note that contributes a concrete mechanism and a nearby-object prediction to the AGN-neutrino literature, rather than a definitive result.

# Critique

**Paper:** W. Bednarik, *High energy neutrinos produced in the accretion disks by neutrons from nuclei disintegrated in the AGN jets* (arXiv:1610.08635, astro-ph.HE, 2016).

## Core idea

Nuclei (chiefly ⁴He) accelerated in reconnection regions / the jet–cocoon boundary layer of an AGN jet are photodisintegrated by the soft disk radiation, liberating relativistic neutrons. These neutrons propagate toward the dense, optically‑thick accretion disk, where (the author asserts) they trigger a hadronic cascade producing the neutrinos. The motivation — using a lower‑threshold, larger‑cross‑section channel than p–γ photopion production to obtain neutrino spectra extending to the PeV with index ≈ 2–2.2, thereby reproducing the IceCube extragalactic neutrino background (ENB) — is sound and well stated.

## Strengths

- **Novel mechanism.** Nuclear photodisintegration as the *dominant* neutron source, with neutrons injected into the disk, is a genuinely fresh combination and a defensible route to a harder ENB spectrum than pure jet p–γ models.
- **Sound motivation.** Correctly identifies that photopion models flatten below the PeV (threshold) and needs a flatter index (~2) to match data — a real weakness of the leading models at the time.
- **Clear, well‑organized order‑of‑magnitude derivations**, with useful scaling relations for the disintegration mean free path, max Lorentz factors, and disk column density; a useful schematic (Fig. 1).
- **Honest about non‑detection** — correctly predicts M87 would be unobservable (0.7 ν/yr), treating it as an upper limit.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The decisive step is asserted, not calculated.** The actual neutrino production — a hadronic/neutron cascade inside an optically‑thick disk — is never computed. It is simply stated that neutrons "initiate the cascade" and that the resulting spectrum follows the injection (nuclear) index. In a dense medium the neutrino yield per neutron, the pion decay‑vs‑absorption balance, and the spectral‑index shift through the cascade are the hard physics, and none is quantified. The neutrino index comes out whatever δ (nuclear index) you put in, so it is an *input*, not a prediction.

2. **The data "match" is a normalization fit.** The ENB is reproduced by tuning the free factor A_N ≈ 2·10⁻⁶ and picking δ≈2.2 to land inside the *factor‑of‑2 error bars* of a ~few‑event dataset (Aartsen et al.). Because the data uncertainty is itself ~2× across the band, essentially any plausible index 2–2.4 is "consistent." This is a fit to sparse data, not a confirmation.

3. **Unmanaged optical‑depth tension / internal inconsistencies.** The disk must be dense enough to make pions interact, yet optically shallow enough that the ~10¹² cm pion mean free path (only a factor of 10 under the ~10¹³ cm scale height — not "much shorter" as claimed) lets pions decay in flight and neutrinos escape. "Optically thick" (caption) and "density is low" coexist without resolution. The beaming story is also muddled: in the cocon frame nuclei are ~isotropic, yet the paper needs well‑defined directionality to send ~2–6% of the neutrons onto the disk, an ad‑hoc attenuation factor that is not derived.

4. **Missing constraints on the hadronic/nuclear jet power fraction.** The model requires ~10% of jet power in accelerated nuclei and ~1–5% in escaping neutrons. Existing γ‑ray / IC / cosmic‑ray and ENB constraints on the hadronic/jet‑power fraction and on the EM counterpart of the jet are not cited or checked — a large claim that prior observations would likely constrain.

5. **Single‑author / scaling‑only** treatment throughout (β=1, ν=0.1, γ₀=10, etc. are chosen, not derived); neutron yield per nucleus and energy partition into neutrons (1/8–1/2) are asserted rather than taken from nuclear‑reaction kinematics. The two acceleration sites (reconnection vs. boundary) are both kept without deciding which is responsible.

## Verdict

A creative, physically motivated, and clearly written hypothesis with a legitimate motivation, but one whose central quantitative claims rest on an uncalculated cascade step, a fit to a very small dataset, hand‑waved geometry/attenuation factors, and (crucially) no check against EM / jet‑composition constraints. It is not unsound, but it is an underdeveloped, under‑constrained idea that reproduces sparse data by tuning rather than by prediction.

## [1610.08635v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


# Bednarek 2016 — arXiv:1610.08635 (astro-ph.HE)

## What the paper claims, in my own words

**Stated contribution.** The author proposes a neutrino-production chain for AGNs: heavy nuclei (chiefly helium, plus heavier species from stellar burning in the host galaxy) are accelerated to high Lorentz factors in the *inner* part of the relativistic jet — either in magnetic reconnection regions in the jet/cocoon system or at the boundary layer between the fast inner jet and its slow cocoon. Because the jet is aimed close to the black hole, the accelerated nuclei plough into the dilute but hot radiation field of the dense inner accretion disk, where they suffer **photodisintegration**: they break up into individual nucleons. The liberated **neutrons**, carrying a sizable fraction (1/8 or more) of the nuclei's energy, fly on (being neutral) into the optically thick Shakura–Sunyaev disk, where their hadronic collisions with disk matter and radiation initiate a **cascade** that yields multi-TeV neutrinos. A power-law-neutron spectrum with exponential cutoff is then integrated over the whole super-massive black-hole (SMBH) population (Li et al. 2011 luminosity function, up to z=2), and the resulting extragalactic neutrino background (ENB) is matched to the 2015 IceCube all-flavour measurement (Aartsen et al. 2015a). The fit requires δ≈2.2, η_acc≈0.1, AGN duty cycle ≈5%, and only ≈5% of the nuclear energy in the form of neutrons reaching the disk. As an observable corollary, the model predicts a rate of only about 0.7 events yr⁻¹ from M 87 — i.e., no source identification is expected.

## Judgement against the cited prior work

**What is genuinely new:**

1. **The accelerated hadrons are *nuclei*, not protons.** All the photo-hadronic jet models the author cites as the main prior art (Nellen, Mannheim & Biermann 1993; Mannheim 1995; Halzen & Zäs 1997; Bednarek & Protheroe 1999; Atoyan & Dermer 2001; Mücke & Protheroe 2001; Mücke et al. 2003; Murase et al. 2014; Padoani et al. 2015) are proton-driven. Replacing the primary with bound nuclei and exploiting *nuclear* physics is the paper's real innovation.
2. **The specific mechanism of photodisintegration and neutron extraction** — using that σ(γ,A) ≈ σ(γ,p)+O(1)× higher while the disintegration threshold E_min≈2.4×10¹⁵ AHe/T₄.₅ eV is *lower* than the photopion threshold pγ. The author quantifies an attractive consequence of this: extracted neutrons sit one to two decades *below* the primary energy, so a hadronic cascade in the disk can yield neutrinos at multi-TeV and higher *without* the photopion threshold suppression that flattens sub-PeV spectra in the pure pγ models (the point the author makes against Mücke et al. 2003, Padoani et al. 2015), or the column-density problem in the p–p models (Becker & Tjus 2014). The energetic argument that neutron extraction (~1/8, or up to ~1/2 for AHe-dominated matter) dominates over Bethe–Heitler e⁺e⁻ losses (a ratio of ~3 orders of magnitude) is also a new quantification.
3. **Neutrino production displaced from the jet into the disk**: in the cited pγ models, neutrinos are largely produced *in the jet* (or at the jet–disk boundary) from π⁰ decay. Here, the neutron acts as a "courier": it is produced in the tenuous outer layer of the disk, decays, and triggers a *hadronic* cascade in the dense inner disk where the pions/muons decay after cooling. The geometry (neutrons as neutral messengers of jet power into the disk) is the cleanest novelty.
4. **The claim that a sub-PeV power-law without photopion threshold is explainable** is a distinct physical claim that, to the extent I can see in the citations, is not made in the cited proton-based jet models.

**What is not new (borrowed wholesale):**

- **Acceleration sites.** Reconnection-region acceleration in jets (Siroi & Spitkovsky 2014; Sironi, Petropoulou & Giannios 2015; Giannios 2010/2013; Kagan et al. 2015; Uzdensky 2016) and jet/cocoon-boundary-layer acceleration (Ostrowski 1990, 1998, 2000; Berezko & Krymski 1981, 1983; Bismozvati-Kogan & Loeb 1995; Rieger & Duffy 2004, 2006) are the standard machinery, just applied to a different particle.
- **The whole cosmological-integration pipeline** — Schechter (1976) function, the Li et al. 2011 luminosity function of SMBHs in AGN, z_max≈2, Ω_m=0.3 Ω_Λ=0.7, H₀=70 — is standard in the IceCube-era ENB literature (Murase 2015 review, Padoani et al. 2015, Kimura, Murase & Toma 2015).
- **The form of the analysis** — power law × exponential cutoff, free normalization factor A_N≈2×10⁻⁶ read off as the product of AGN duty, accretion efficiency, acceleration efficiency, and neutron extraction — is essentially the same structure the author used in the pre-2013 pγ models (citations of Bednarek & Protheroe 1999, Bednarek & Protheroe 2001, etc.), just re-parameterized around neutron fraction rather than pion-production efficiency.
- **The M 87 check** is a single-object estimate that re-uses Walsh et al. 2013 and the IceCube detection efficiency from Aartsen et al. 2015b; the conclusion "M 87 not detectable" is itself not a strong claim — many competing models at the time also predicted sub-1/yr rates.
- **The physics of the disk** — the Shakura–Sunyaev density Σ≈4.6α̇⁻¹ m⁻¹/² r⁻³/² and z(r) formulas are taken directly from Eq. 2.8/2.9 of Shakura & Sunyaev 1973 with η=0.1, ṁ=0.1, m=10⁶; the B≈370 T₄.₅ G field is simply the energy-density equivalence of the soft radiation. None of this is new.

## Soundness and quality notes (why this is incremental rather than a landmark)

The argumentation is order-of-magnitude and *hand-waving* in a few places, and the paper is a single-author arXiv preprint (v1, 27 October 2016) with no peer review. Specific soft spots:

- **Neutron transport to the dense inner disk** is asserted, not derived: the author shows Xₙ≈σγₙη_γ>~10¹⁸ cm and claims this is "clearly larger" than d≈0.01 z≈10¹² cm, so neutrons reach the disk; but there is no calculation of the angular distribution in the co-moving frame nor of the fraction that actually survives to *hit* the dense inner disk rather than the outer disk or cocoon. The 5% neutron-reaching-disk is essentially a free parameter that's fit to IceCube, not a prediction.
- **Disintegration and acceleration co-location** is asserted via the R_disc≈40Γ₂² R_S/ε ≈ the inner-jet scale (Giannios 2013), but the paper does not model how *accelerated* nuclei with γ_He~10⁴–10⁷ (Eq. 5/12) actually survive to R_disc: the reconnection region at ξR_in~10⁴⁴ cm has L_rec and a distance to the disk that is comparable, and there is no time-budget analysis between the maximum-energy condition (Eq. 12) and travel time to the disintegration radius.
- **The "1/8 to 1/2" neutron energy fraction** (for AHe or nucleons-dominated matter) is presented without a numerical photodisintegration calculation; the paper leans on Cyburst et al. 2003 appendix rather than computing fragmentation spectra itself. So the claim that neutron extraction dominates over e⁺e⁻ (3 orders) is a cross-section argument, not a rate argument.
- **The disk cascade** is treated as a single collision step; there is no iterative cascade, no check that the p–p cross section (σ_np≈3×10⁻²⁶ cm²) with n≈2.3×10¹³ cm⁻³ gives λ≈1.5×10¹² cm < z, and no treatment of pion decay vs. reinteraction in the disk for muons. ## Understanding of the paper's logical argument

The paper (Bednarek, 2016) proposes a mechanism for producing the TeV–PeV neutrinos seen by IceCube, and for building up the diffuse Extragalactic Neutrino Background (ENB), by *coupling AGN jets to their accretion disks*.

2. **Nuclei in the jet.** Accretion matter onto a SMBH is helium-rich (~25% He by number) because of stellar nucleosynthesis. These heavy nuclei (dominantly used as an example, He) are accelerated to relativistic energies in reconnection regions inside the jet, or at the boundary between the fast jet and its slow cocoon (Ostrowski-type shocks).

3. **Nuclear disintegration ("spallation into nucleons").** Relativistic nuclei colliding with the soft (few 10⁴ K) accretion-disk blackbody radiation undergo photodisintegration. The photodisintegration cross section is much larger than for pion production *and* the threshold is far lower, so neutrons are produced below ~PeV where pγ/pπ spectra would flatten.

The key geometric claim (Sec. 2.1) is that the travel distance to the disk, ~γₙλ_des ≳ 8×10¹⁸ cm (for high γₙ), can exceed the reconnection-region size, so a fraction of the neutrons actually strike the dense, optically-thick inner disk before decaying.

5. **Neutrino production in the disk.** Inside the disk, neutrons hadronically scatter on the matter, producing charged pions → muons/neutrinos. The paper then estimates the disk density, thickness, magnetic field, and shows that for a typical inner temperature Larmor radii of the produced protons are within the disk thickness, so neutrinos are emitted quasi-isotropically from the inner disk. ## [1610.08635v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Evaluation of Bednarek (2016): "High energy neutrinos produced in the accretion disks by neutrons from nuclei disintegrated in the AGN jets"

## Overall Assessment

This paper proposes a creative mechanism for explaining IceCube's extragalactic neutrino background (ENB): heavy nuclei (He) accelerated in AGN jet reconnection regions undergo photo-dissociation by the disk radiation field, releasing relativistic neutrons that then plow into the dense accretion disk and produce neutrinos via nuclear interactions. The central motivation is that this mechanism avoids the pion-production threshold suppression that flattens the spectrum in standard p–γ models below the PeV, naturally producing a flat spectrum (δ ≈ 2) matching the data.

## The Single Weakest Link: The Normalization Step (A_N ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁶)

The step where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data is the transition from "with A_N = 2 × 10⁻⁶, the model reproduces the IceCube flux" to "therefore, this mechanism explains the ENB." Here is why this is the break:

**1. A_N is a composite free parameter with no first-principles prediction.**

The paper itself defines A_N as the product of six astrophysical factors: the Eddington-scaled accretion rate χ, the jet power fraction (½), the efficiency of transferring jet power into nuclei (10%), the fraction of nuclear energy in neutrons (1/8 to 1/2), the AGN duty cycle (5–10%), and the fraction of neutrons that survive to the disk (2–6%). The model contains no mechanism to predict what A_N *will* be rather than what it *must* be to fit the flux. The paper then argues each factor is "reasonable" based on independent order-of-magnitude estimates — but this is a post-hoc plausibility check, not a derivation.

**2. The required value sits at the optimistic edge of the parameter space.**

The product is most compatible with A_N ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁶ only when several factors simultaneously take favorable values:
- χ = 0.1 (10% Eddington) — at the high end for the general radio-loud AGN population (Wu & Liu 2004 quote ~0.01 for closer radio-loud AGN; the paper acknowledges this tension explicitly)
- f_reach = 5% (neutron survival) — a purely geometric assumption with no detailed transport calculation
- f_neutron = 1/4 or higher (energy fraction in neutrons)

If χ were the more conservative 0.03, or if the neutron survival fraction were at the low end (2%), the predicted flux would fall a factor of 3–10 below the data. The individual factors each carry uncertainties of factor 2–3, and their product carries a cumulative uncertainty of at least an order of magnitude, making the match to the data a narrow coincidence rather than a robust prediction.

**3. The match is model-independent in the normalization.**

Every competing model of the ENB (p–γ in jets, p–p in lobe collisions, etc.) can be normalized to match the IceCube flux by adjusting its own effective luminosity or duty cycle. The normalization alone does not discriminate between models. What would discriminate is a *predicted* flux independent of any fit, and the model does not provide one.

**4. The "spectral shape" argument partially masks the normalization gap.**

The paper's strongest physical claim is that the nuclear mechanism naturally produces a flat (δ ≈ 2) spectrum extending to multi-TeV energies. But the flatness is inherited directly from the assumed input spectrum of the accelerated nuclei — the photodissociation cascade and the subsequent disk interaction preserve the spectral shape with only a small kinematic correction (the neutrino index is δ + ~0.2 ≈ 2.4 from pion-decay kinematics). The "novel physics" determines *where* neutrinos are produced (dense disk vs. dilute jet) and thus allows the low-energy extension, but it does not determine *why* the index is 2. The index is set by the acceleration process, which the paper takes as an input (δ ≈ 2, the generic Fermi-acceleration result).

In other words: the data entail that a source with δ ≈ 2 and A_N ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁶ would be consistent with the ENB. They do not entail that the specific physical mechanism of nuclear disintegration + neutron disk interactions *produces* this combination of parameters. The paper's "explanation" is really a consistency check: the mechanism is physically plausible, and its predicted spectral shape is compatible with the data, but the absolute flux normalization is fitted rather than predicted.

## Summary of Minor Additional Weaknesses

- The paper does not explicitly compute the nuclear interaction rates or pion multiplicities in the disk; the neutrino spectra in Fig. 2 are parametric constructions with the total power set to a fraction of the accretion luminosity.
- The geometry of "neutrons directed toward the disk" from a jet/cocoon system is qualitatively described (Fig. 1) but the 2–6% survival fraction is a hand-waving geometric factor.
- The M87 predictability check (0.7 events/yr) uses the global ENB normalization (A_N = 2 × 10⁻⁶) as a free input for an individual source with uncertain local parameters, making the "non-detectability" argument circular.

## Scoring

The idea is genuinely creative — using nuclear photodissociation and a neutron-mediated transport to bypass the p–γ threshold — and the physical picture is largely sound. However, the central claim is undercut at the normalization step and the "explanation" is closer to a parameter-constrained consistency story than a predictive model.

## 1. Where the framing overclaims relative to the content

**Abstract, "It is shown that the relativistic nuclei can efficiently fragment onto specific nucleons in collisions with the disk radiation."**
Nothing has been *shown* in the sense of a demonstration; Sections 2.1–2.3 derive chains of order-of-magnitude inequalities (Eqs. 3, 6–11) under assumed inputs (η = 0.1, B_in = 100 B₂ G, α_o ~ 10, T_D from a fiducial Eddington fraction). The word "efficiently" is a conclusion the paper never quantifies into a single survival/fragmentation probability; it is a conjunction of ~pc-scale free paths vs. reconnection lengths, each with an unassigned error. "It is shown" should be "we estimate, under fiducial parameters, that…".

**Abstract, "We propose that the neutrinos produced in such scenario from the whole population of super-massive black holes in active galaxies can explain the extragalactic neutrino background recently measured by the IceCube, provided that 5% fraction of galaxies is AGN and a few percent of neutrons reach the accretion disk."**
This is the single biggest overclaim. The "explanation" actually consists of a *fitted normalization*. In Sec. 5 the author writes that the model matches the ENB "provided that the normalization factor of the spectrum of nuclei is equal to A_N ~2×10⁻⁶," where A_N is defined textually as the product of the jet-power fraction (2⁻¹), neutron energy fraction (~8⁻¹ to 2⁻¹), acceleration efficiency (10%), AGN duty cycle (5%), the fraction of neutrons that reach the disk (1.6–6.4%), and the Eddington ratio. That is six stacked assumptions, each known to at best a factor of a few, whose product is one number tuned until the curve passes through the 2013–14 IceCube diffuse-flux points, which carried order-unity systematic uncertainty and a sample of a few dozen events. Any background model can be normalized to that data; the "explanation" therefore carries essentially the information of the data itself. The conditional clause at the end ("provided that 5% … and a few percent …") quietly reveals that the two most load-bearing numbers are *inputs*, not predictions.

**Abstract, "It is predicted that the neutrino signals in the present neutrino detectors, produced in terms of such a model, are not expected to be detectable even from the nearby radio galaxies similar to M87."**
This contradicts the paper's own number. Sec. 5 estimates **~0.7 ν yr⁻¹** from M87 using the ENB-fitted A_N. That is not a null detection — it is a ~half-per-year Poisson process, i.e. ~40–50% odds of at least one event in a decade of exposure, exactly the regime in which a discovery claim becomes natural. The author later concedes the 0.7/yr "should be considered as the upper limit" because M87's ṁ ≈ 1.6×10⁻³ is far below the ṁ = 0.1 of the fiducial model — which is the honest statement. "Not expected to be detectable even from the nearby radio galaxies" is the opposite of a model that predicts ~0.7/yr from its nearest prototype target; as written it is also a double hedge ("It is predicted that… are not expected to be…").

**Sec. 1, "Therefore, neutrons from nuclei can easily produce multi-TeV neutrinos in a consistency with the IceCube observations."**
Two problems. "Easily" is qualitative, and "in a consistency with the IceCube observations" (also a grammar slip) is stated in the introduction but first actually tested — weakly — in Sec. 5, one way (normalization) only.

**Sec. 4 (bottom of p. 8), "Such SMBHs might be responsible for the PeV neutrino events recently detected by the IceCube telescope."**
The hedge "might" is appropriate, but the sentence sits directly under the sentence "the neutrino spectra clearly extend up to the PeV energies for the efficiency of acceleration close to χ = 0.1" — the choice of χ = 0.1 is *the top of the radio-loud range* the paper itself quotes (0.01–0.1), which makes "close to χ = 0.1" read as a tuned corner of parameter space rather than a property.

"Consistent with" is defensible for a normalization that was fitted; "very consistent with" in the conclusion is not — no statistic, no error bars on the model curve, no comparison of δ = 2.2 vs δ = 2.4 curves to the data, no χ². The "best description" (δ = 2.2, χ = 0.1) is identified by eye against points with ~factor-2 bars.

**Sec. 5, "We also have checked that the contribution to the ENB from SMBHs at larger redshifts z > 2 can be safely neglected."**
"Safely" is unsupported by any displayed integral, luminosity function value at z > 2, or figure. Given the Schechter function and the (1+z) weighting in the volume element, this is the kind of claim that should be on a page, not in a sentence.

**Conclusion, "The Lorentz factors of neutrons extracted from nuclei are clearly lower than those produced in pure proton-photon collisions (by one/two orders of magnitudes). This is the framing of the paper's central novelty — and it is garbled and self-contradictory as written. The *threshold of nuclear photodisintegration* is *higher* than the pion-production threshold (that is stated elsewhere as the reason neutrons from nuclei carry *more* energy, higher Lorentz factors). Here it is invoked as the reason the p→γ spectrum *flattens*. The comparison being argued (neutrons from nuclei have γ_n ~ 2–3 orders of magnitude above the p–γ pion-decay neutrons, hence the spectrum extends to higher energy rather than flattening below the PeV) is the interesting physics, but the sentence as printed does not state it; a careful reader has to reconstruct the argument.

**Sec. 2.2, "In such a case, they are expected to spend half of their residence time in the cocoon."**
"Half of their residence time" appears without derivation — it is carried as a factor in the neutron-capture budget.

**Sec. 2.1, "This matter is likely to be more abundant in heavy nuclei, in respect to primordial matter containing ~25% of helium, due to the efficiency of the central star formation occurring in the parent galaxies."**
The 25% helium figure is a *mass* fraction of pristine baryons; invoking "the efficiency of central star formation" (unspecified) to raise the heavy-element content is a hand-wave that should at least cite a metallicity/AGN-nucleus abundance estimate. The whole neutron-extraction efficiency range (~8⁻¹ to ~2⁻¹) is only weakly grounded by this sentence.

# Peer review — arXiv:1610.08635v1
**Title:** High energy neutrinos produced in the accretion disks by neutrons from nuclei disintegrated in the AGN jets
**Author:** W. Bednarek (Univ. of Łódź, 2016)

---

## Summary and scope

The paper proposes **neutron spallation from heavy nuclei** (mainly He-4, possibly heavier nuclei) as the dominant neutrino production channel in AGN. Nuclei are assumed to be accelerated to high Lorentz factors (γ_He ~ 10^9) in reconnection regions within the inner relativistic jet and/or at the jet-cocoon boundary, where they then interact with the soft thermal radiation of the inner accretion disk. The resulting neutrons — having a shorter disintegration timescale than photo-pion production — fly ballistically across ~pc scales and reach the optically-thick inner disk, where they undergo hadronic collisions (n + gas → pions) and their associated neutrinos escape. The paper tracks the whole cascade from acceleration → disintegration → neutron transport → hadronic collisions in the disk → neutrino spectra, then integrates over the cosmic SMBH population (to z=2) to produce an extragalactic all-flavour neutrino background (ENB) and compares with the then-current IceCube 2015 all-flavour flux. The paper also estimates the event rate expected from M87 as a nearby-source test.

---

## Strengths

1. **A genuinely different neutrino-production channel.** The core physical observation is reasonable: the inelasticity coefficient for nuclear spallation is ~10^3 times larger than for e± pair production, and the energy threshold for spallation is lower than for photo-pion production (p+γ → n+π). This gives the model a natural low-energy extension — neutrino spectra that do not flatten below ~10 TeV — which qualitatively matches the shape of the observed IceCube flux. The mechanism is a legitimate candidate that should be taken seriously.

2. **Self-consistent cascade.** The paper follows the full chain: acceleration → disintegration → neutron flight → hadronic interactions in the disk. All the key scales (L_acc, λ_He, neutron flight distance, disk thickness) are identified and compared, and the paper shows (order-of-magnitude) that the relevant regions are self-consistent for M_BH ~ 10^9 M☉ with reasonable jet parameters (χ = 0.1, δ ≈ 2).

3. **Quantitative ENB estimate.** The paper derives an analytic expression for the ENB parameter, A_N ≈ 2×10⁻⁶, and discusses the sensitivity to the spectral index δ and the accretion efficiency χ. The resulting fit to the IceCube data (Fig. 3) is reasonable for an order-of-magnitude model.

4. **Testable predictions.** The paper attempts to be falsifiable: the M87 event rate prediction (~0.7 yr⁻¹) is a concrete (albeit weak) observable; the claim that no nearby AGN is detectable is a constraint in itself.

---

## Major weaknesses

1. **No comparison to standard p-γ or p-p models.** This is the most serious gap. The paper claims that the standard p-γ models fail because they "flatten below PeV" and therefore cannot match the IceCube power law. Yet p-γ models produce neutrinos at *all* energies — they are just flatter at lower E. The paper does not quantify how much of the observed flux the flat component produces, nor does it present a side-by-side fit of the combined p-γ + spallation component versus the p-γ-alone model. Without this, the claim that the spallation component is required is not established.

2. **Unconstrained free parameters.** The overall ENB normalization A_N = (0.8–3.2)×10⁻³ (for the product of all model coefficients) is effectively fitted to the observed data. Two of the largest uncertainties — the AGN duty cycle and the fraction of neutrons that actually reach the disk — are treated as free parameters within wide ranges. The paper should be more explicit about how well-constrained these are, and ideally present a parameter space in which the model is over-constrained (or degenerate).

3. **No gamma-ray cross-check.** High-energy neutrino production via hadronic interactions necessarily produces a comparable flux of high-energy γ-rays (from π⁰ decay). The ENB model implies a specific TeV–PeV γ-ray flux from the AGN population. The paper does not compare with the Fermi-LAT / HESS / CTA upper limits on the AGN gamma-ray background. Above ~100 TeV, γ-rays are heavily attenuated by EBL pair production, so the model can evade the most stringent constraints — but the 10 TeV – 100 TeV range (where neutrinos are most abundant per the model) is a window where γ-rays should be observable and the model should be checked against observed limits. This omission is a significant gap for a paper in a prestigious journal.

4. **Neutrino flavor content not discussed.** Since the production mechanism is still pion/kaon decay, the flavour ratio is the standard (1:1:1) at Earth. The paper does not discuss any new flavour signature that could distinguish this model from the standard p-γ model, nor does it compare the expected event rate among flavours in IceCube's three detectors. This weakens the claim that the model produces a distinct observable signature.

5. **Nuclear cross-section uncertainty.** The paper assumes He-4 disintegration cross-sections in the TeV-per-nucleon range (E_N ~ 10^14–10^15 eV), which is far above lab-measured thresholds. The paper cites Cyburty 2003 for the maximum cross-section σ_He^des ~ 2×10⁻²⁷ cm², but it does not discuss the uncertainty in the cross-section's energy dependence at these extreme energies. Since the neutron production rate scales directly with this cross-section, the uncertainty could be a factor of a few or more. This should be discussed explicitly.

6. **Acceleration of heavy nuclei is assumed, not justified.** The paper states that "essential part of the jet power" can be transferred to relativistic nuclei, but does not estimate how much of the jet energy actually couples to heavy nuclei vs. protons and electrons. Reconnection acceleration (Giacnios 2010; Uzdensky 2016) has been primarily demonstrated for electrons in blazar jets. The maximum Lorentz factors needed (γ_He ~ 10^9) for disintegration at ~PeV require δ ≈ 2 and the largest possible B_in. This is a strong constraint and the paper should quantify, for realistic reconnection acceleration efficiency η_1 = 0.1–0.2, whether γ_He ~ 10^9 is achievable. If only a small fraction (e.g., 25% by mass in primordial He) is accelerated to PeV nuclei, the absolute flux may be significantly below IceCube sensitivity.

7. **M87 prediction is too weak to be a useful discrimination.** The predicted rate (~0.7 yr⁻¹) is an "upper limit" that is not meaningfully distinguishable from zero over a 4-year IceCube observation window. The paper says M87 is a "typical contributor" to the ENB, but its low Eddington ratio (~10⁻³) makes the disk under-luminous and the model predicts very weakly. This is not a testable prediction of the model — it is a non-favourable non-detection. A stronger prediction (or clearer statement of what *would* disprove the model) would strengthen the paper.

---

## Minor weaknesses

- **Terminology inconsistency:** "disintegration" and "spallation" are used interchangeably. In nuclear physics these have distinct meanings; the paper should pick one or explicitly define the relationship.
- **Schematic diagram (Fig. 1) is not to scale** and should be annotated more clearly — the jet, cocoon and disk geometries are not quantitatively described in the figure.
- **The paper is somewhat repetitive** — the reconnection acceleration mechanism is described in both Section 2.1 and in Section 2.2 (jet boundary), with some overlap in the physical arguments. Tighter editing would help.
- **Equation 16** ("χ L_Edd = 1.3 × 10⁴⁶ ... erg s⁻¹") mixes the Eddington luminosity at face value with the accretion efficiency χ; it would be clearer to define L_acc = η A L_Edd separately.
- **The claim that only 5% of galaxies are AGN** is based on visual morphology (Dressler & Shectman 1985). Modern X-ray and radio surveys give different numbers for different luminosity ranges. The paper should discuss the sensitivity to this choice more explicitly.
- **z_smax = 2** truncation of the ENB integral: the paper states that the contribution from z > 2 is "negligible," but does not show a quantitative check of the z-dependence.
- **No simulation or MC treatment** of the neutron propagation and hadronic interactions — the collision probabilities are treated analytically. For a precision comparison with IceCube data, this level of approximation is marginal.

---

## Overall assessment

The paper proposes a plausible, physical mechanism — neutron spallation from accelerated heavy nuclei in AGN jets — as an alternative neutrino production channel to p-γ and p-p, with the specific advantage of extending the neutrino spectrum down to ~10 TeV without a low-energy turnover. The physics is broadly sound, and the author makes a fair attempt to track the full phenomenological chain and compare with the then-current IceCube background data. However, the paper in its current form is an **order-of-magnitude, first-pass phenomenological proposal** with several important gaps: no quantitative comparison with competing models, no gamma-ray cross-check, no flavour analysis, and critical parameters left effectively free. For a prestigious journal, the paper needs at minimum: (i) a quantitative side-by-side fit of the ENB in the spallation model vs. the standard p-γ model; (ii) a check against γ-ray flux constraints in the 10 TeV–100 TeV range; and (iii) a more rigorous treatment of the cross-section uncertainty at the relevant energies. With these additions, the paper could be a valuable contribution to the AGN-neutrino literature; as it stands, it is a solid conference-proceedings / journal-of-record (A&A, MNRAS) paper that would benefit substantially from a major revision before publication in a leading venue.

**Recommendation: Major revision.**

## What you did — overall impression

The paper proposes a concrete hadronic channel for the IceCube diffuse flux: nuclei (mainly He, plus a heavy-nuclei admixture) accelerated in the inner-jet reconnection regions and at the jet/cocon boundary are photo-disintegrated by the accretion-disk radiation; the extracted relativistic neutrons travel into the dense disk where their nN collisions produce pions and neutrinos. Because photo-disintegration has a per-nucleon threshold an order or two below pγ pion production on the same photon field, the resulting ν spectrum extends to multi-TeV, and you fit the extragalactic background (ENB) population-integrated to z = 2 against the 2015 IceCube diffuse flux, plus an honest negative prediction for M87.

This is a well-organized, self-contained semi-analytical paper. Every claim in the text is tied to a numbered order-of-magnitude estimate, the schematic (Fig. 1) is helpful, the parameter dependence is explored (Fig. 2), and the conclusion is admirably honest (M87 not detectable in this model). The core physical premise is sound — the photonuclear giant-resonance cross section is larger than σ(pγ→nπ+) and its threshold is lower, so the "low threshold" advantage is real. That said, in places the paper claims more than the equations support. Here is what needs attention, roughly in priority order.

## Major issues

**1. The "down to TeV" selling point is over- and under-stated at the same time.**
- Your Eq. (21) gives E_n^min ≈ 1.4×10⁶ M_9^½ GeV ≈ **1.4 PeV** for a 10⁹ M☉ hole, and larger for 10¹⁰ M☉. Since pions take only a fraction of the neutron energy, the *lowest* neutrino energies are ~100s of TeV for the very black holes that are supposed to explain the PeV events. The multi-TeV extension really only comes from lower-mass (10⁷ M☉) systems, where E_n^min ∝ M_BH^½ is smaller. You should make this mass dependence explicit rather than presenting a uniform "extends down to the TeV range" (abstract, p. 1, conclusion). The plots (Figs. 2–3) in fact only start at ~10–30 TeV — say so in words.
- Equally, you should state *against which* pγ competition the low-threshold argument is made. In the mildly relativistic inner-jet/disk-frame context you assume (Γ ~ 2), your claim is solid: on a 3×10⁴ K blackbody, pγ→nπ+ needs E_p,thr ~ few×10² GeV per proton, while Eq. (6) needs ~GeV–PeV per nucleon — a factor ~100–1000, i.e. your "one to two orders of magnitude" is defensible. But the standard comoving-frame pγ jet models (e.g. Nellen et al. 1993, Murase 2014 with high δ) do *not* flatten below PeV at all, so the introduction's blanket statement that "most of these models predict … spectra which flatten below PeV energies" caricatures the field. Be explicit about the class of competing models you are competing with, or a referee will attack the motivation.

**2. The ENB "explanation" is really an order-of-magnitude consistency check — say so in the abstract and conclusion.**
The normalization A_N ~ 2×10⁻⁶ is a product of f_AGN ~ 5%, χ ~ 0.1, ~10% of jet power into nuclei, ~2% neutron reach fraction, etc. You yourself put the uncertainty on the product of the "well-constrained" coefficients at (0.8–3.2)×10⁻³ — roughly **two orders of magnitude above** the value the fit requires. The model does not uniquely predict the IceCube flux; it is compatible with it for a wide band of plausible assumed efficiencies. The abstract's phrasing "can explain the extragalactic neutrino background" overstates the body; "consistent with, at the order-of-magnitude level, is honest and still strong.

**3. The key free number — the fraction of neutrons reaching the disk (1.6–6.4%) — is asserted, not calculated.**
You state "We assume that a part of neutrons liberated from the nuclei impinge onto the accretion disk" and later derive the 1.6–6.4% window backward from A_N. Instead, compute it forward: (a) the solid angle of the disk subtended at the reconnection region, for height h ~ 0.1–1 R_in; (b) the flight time vs. the decay length. The check is easy and would actually help you: a PeV neutron has γ ~ 10⁶, so cτ_γ ~ 10¹⁵ cm ≈ 100+ pc, far exceeding the ~10¹³ cm distance to the disk; the geometric solid angle for a disk at ~0.1–1 R_in is comfortably at the percent–tens-of-percent level. Showing that your assumed 2% lies inside both allowed ranges turns your weakest free parameter into a derived one.

**4. No quantitative comparison with the competing channels.**
The entire motivation is that *this* channel beats pp and pγ models at producing the steep TeV–PeV flux. But you never show them side by side. Add a panel (or a figure) where, for the same SMBH luminosity budget and energy allocation, a pγ (disk-frame) model and/or pp model produces a flatter spectrum that fails the data while yours succeeds. Without that, "this model is better motivated" is just an assertion.

**5. Inconsistent disk-temperature bookkeeping and notation.**
Eq. (3) uses T_D = 3×10⁴ T_4.5 K, while Eq. (17) gives T_D ≈ 5.5×10⁴ χ_1^{1/4} M_9^{−1/2} K. These are different scalings; explain which is which (or unify) and define the units of M_BH in Eqs. (20)–(21) and the χ vs. χ_1 convention once and for all. Readers will stumble here; a referee will definitely check Eqs. (20)–(21) numerically given Issue 1.

**6. M87 numbers.**
You write "estimated accretion rate ≈ 1.6×10³ (di Matteo et al. 2003)" — as written this is a super-Eddington rate and contradicts M87's known ~10⁻³–10⁻⁴ Eddington ratio; I believe you mean ṁ/ṁ_Edd ≈ 1.6×10⁻³. Re-check the 0.7 events/yr upper limit with the corrected value, and state explicitly that the rate scales with χ² so the low accretion rate is the main reason the prediction is so low.
