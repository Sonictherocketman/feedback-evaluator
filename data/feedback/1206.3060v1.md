## [1206.3060v1-general-review] General Review


I've read the full paper (Contini, "Evidences of merging in the Seyfert galaxy NGC 3393 revealed by modelling the spectra," MNRAS, arXiv:1206.3060v1). Here is my evaluation of its argument, followed by critique.

## What the paper argues

The premise is that Fabbiano et al. (2011) found two black holes ~490 ly apart in NGC 3393, strongly suggesting a recent minor merger. Contini's move is to ask whether *other* observational signatures of a collision (shocks, density jumps, metallicity anomalies, hot stars) survive in the emission-line spectra, and to answer with photoionization+shock modelling (SUMA) of the Diaz et al. (1988), HST-IUE and Cooke et al. (2000) data. The claimed evidence for merging: (1) post-shock densities ~10× typical of other AGN NLRs; (2) O/H ≈ 0.78 solar; (3) sub-solar N/H; (4) Mg/H depleted ~3×; (5) two high-velocity (V_s≈400 km/s) "patches" beyond the NLR; (6) hot (WR) stars contributing ~17.8% of the [OIII] 5007 line, with most of the gas AGN-photoionized.

Overall this is a competent, well-scoped piece of work sitting on genuinely interesting target physics. But the argument has real structural weaknesses.

## Critique

**1. The "independent pieces of evidence" are not independent.** The central defect. The density jump, the O/H depression, the sub-solar N/H, the Mg depletion, and the hot-star/WR contribution all emerge from the *same* SUMA grid and the same set of modeling assumptions (power-law primary flux + adiabatic shocks, a single dust grain radius, a specific ionization geometry, the adopted reddening). A single systematic bias in the grid — for instance the dust/depletion treatment or the ionization structure — propagates into every "independent" diagnostic. So the paper's case for a merger rests on one model family agreeing with the data, not on multiple unrelated observables. The strongest possible version of this argument would have anchored at least one constraint (ideally the abundance ratios) on data treated in a model-independent way (e.g. the Balmer decrement + [OIII]/Hβ + an independent density diagnostic from [SII]/[SII]), which it does not.

**2. The abundance ratios are the classic SUMA/CMFGEN systematic problem.** Non-equilibrium, inhomogeneous NLRs with density/velocity structure are well known to produce spurious offsets in O/H (and especially N/H, C/H). A ~22% O/H depression is well inside the systematic uncertainty of photoionization modelling — it is not the 4- or 10-sigma anomaly it would need to be to serve as evidence of merging. The sub-solar N/H is even more fragile: it is built on the [NeIII] absolute flux, which the paper itself admits is anchored on the *Wu et al.* dust-anchored distance, so it partially assumes its own distance inference. The Mg "depletion by ~3×" is likewise a modeling artifact: Mg is not a strong coolant, so its abundance is inferred only indirectly through MgII/Hα and the sputtering/dust-grain story, and the author concedes (p.6) that "Mg cannot be trapped into dust grains which are sputtered through the strong shocks." This makes the Mg result the weakest of the three "abundance anomalies."

**3. The FOS-vs-UV reddening handling is contradictory.** Section 2.3.4 states that "the reddening correction adopted by Diaz et al. was probably misleading," yet Table 1's observed column (the very data the models fit) *are* Diaz's reddening-corrected fluxes, and the author separately "corrects" the UV lines for FOS blue observations. The model is being fit to data where the reddening treatment has just been declared suspect. For a paper whose whole abundance argument hinges on the exact ratio of lines across UV-optical (different ionization potentials, HeII 1640 vs. 4861 vs. [OIII]), this is exactly where the analysis should be most careful. The author should have re-derived the reddening from the data set he considers reliable, or at least bounded the abundance error from the reddening ambiguity. He does not.

**4. The density and ionization degeneracy is only hand-waved away.** The classic density/excitation trade: high [OIII]/Hβ + high [OII]/[OIII] can be produced by (a) high n₀ low U, or (b) low n₀ high U. The author notes this (Sect. 2.2.1) and then says "the best consistent fit of the O, He, and C line ratios is complicated by the fact that, changing one of the input parameters affects the cooling rate." In practice, the grid is not shown to have a well-specified degeneracy structure. Figure 1 (BPT-like [OIII]/Hβ vs. [NII]/Hα) shows a wide family of curves passing through the data — many of them with V_s near 100 km/s (i.e. *not* a merger-like high-velocity shock). If the model family allows low-V_s solutions to reproduce the same data, then the "shock velocities between 100 and 600 km/s" claim is a property of the model grid, not a uniquely determined observable.

**5. The WR-star claim has a tension in it.** To fit N V 1252 / C IV 1548 the author invokes a T_s ≈ 8.6×10⁴ K WR-type population — but the *same* paper argues that the central starburst has been suppressed by unequal-mass-merger physics (the NGC 6240 precedent). The young population must carry enough 120,000 K WRs to make N V 1252 work, yet Cid Fernandes et al. (2004) find only 4% young-star flux. The paper bridges this by having the WRs contribute only 17.8% of [OIII] 5007, but it does not reconcile this with the SED fit in Fig. 3, where the optical-UV peak is assigned to an *old* 3–4×10⁴ K population. There is a qualitative tension between "young WRs are needed for the line ratios" and "the SED says the light is old," and the paper just averages them (the "m_AV" average model). This averaging — which the author flags himself as "not negligible" for 3 lines — also muddies the "evidence": the averaged model is a weighted sum of three distinct physics, so attributing a specific physical interpretation to any single line ratio in the average is not clean.

**6. "High velocity clouds (V_s≈400 km/s)" as merging evidence is the soft claim in the package.** The Cooke et al. (2000) FWHM map (Fig. 2) is consistent both with shocks and with a rotating/infalling NLR plus a few outflows (the VLA radio outflow Pedlar et al. 1985, the "13 cm" CMA outflow). The author's own p.8 notes "the AGN dominated cloud explains the V_s≈100 km/s clouds with high ionization throughout most of the NLR from East to West" — i.e. the bulk of the map is *not* shock-dominated in his own best model, and the "shock signatures" are localized to two patches of ~20″ N of the nucleus and one at -30″. Two patches of elevated FWHM out of a large NLR is a weak basis for "shocks throughout the galaxy." The phrase "velocities 100–600 km⁻¹" is also used interchangeably with "shock velocity" and "post-shock velocity" in different places — the distinction matters (post-shock bulk velocity is ~V_s/4 for a strong adiabatic shock).

**7. Writing/presentation issues, minor but they add up.** "MNRAS 000, ?? (2009)" header; a "2010 month day" received date on a 2012-2018 paper; inconsistent use of "m_pl / m_ad / m_bb / m_av / m_sd" across Tables 1, 2, 3 and Fig. 1 legends; Table 3 has a `? 1503.0` line (Table 2) whose ionizing species is not identified; the phrase "2×10⁻²¹ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ Å⁻¹" is attributed to Diaz (1988) but the IUE unit conversion is off by orders of magnitude for an IUE spectrum (which is typically photon cm⁻² s⁻¹ Å⁻¹) — worth checking; the abstract's "1000 K / 10⁴ K / 10⁶ K" temperature list in one sentence is inconsistent with the body where the highest black-body is 8.6×10⁴ K. The final sentence of the discussion — "Our results obtained by consistent calculations are valid, but not definitive due to scarcity of data" — is good candour but undercuts the "evidences of merging" title.

**8. What it does well.** The target choice is excellent (dual black holes + NLR modelling is just the right pairing). The combination of Diaz optical, IUE, HST-Cooke 5-region, and the NED-SED into one model grid is more complete than most. The dust-to-gas ratio of ~10⁻⁴ and the IR reprocessing (Fig. 3) are a nice consistency check on the photoionization/shock decomposition. The claim that "the power-law flux from the AGN largely dominates the spectral emission of the gas clouds throughout the galaxy" (≤80% AGN, ~18% shock, ~5% star for [OIII] 5007) is genuinely useful information about this particular NLR, whatever the merger narrative. The FWHM-vs-radius map (Fig. 2) is an honest and useful re-plot.

**9. What would make this paper stronger.** (a) An independent density diagnostic from [SII] 6717/6731 *alone*, broken out of the SUMA grid, cross-checked against the SUMA n₀. (b) A reddening-free abundance estimate from Balmer decrement + [OIII]/Hβ alone, with the SUMA result as a comparison. (c) An explicit degeneracy map showing the V_s-vs-U trade space and a figure of which (V_s, log U, n₀) triples are in the acceptance region — not just a BPT-like 2D projection. (d) A comparison of the "patches" model with an alternative pure-rotation + a single outflow model of the FWHM map. (e) An honest quantification of the abundance systematics (a 20–30% error bar on O/H, and a "we cannot distinguish sub-solar from solar N/H" caveat on N/H, would actually be more persuasive than the paper's current "evidence of merging" framing).

## Bottom line

The paper does something legitimate and interesting — it builds the most complete multi-wavelength photoionization+shock model of NGC 3393 available at the time and shows that AGN + moderate shocks + a modest young population can plausibly account for most of the data. But the *merger evidence* it claims is substantially weaker than the framing suggests: the abundance anomalies are not robust to standard photoionization-systematic errors; the high-velocity "patches" are a minority of the NLR and can have alternative dynamical explanations; and the "independent" arguments all depend on one shared model. The conclusion "modelling the spectra of NGC 3393 gives us some evidence of collision and merging" is defensible; the title "Evidences of merging ... revealed by modelling the spectra" overclaims.

## [1206.3060v1-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


I've read the full paper (Contini, "Evidences of merging in the Seyfert galaxy NGC 3393 revealed by modelling the spectra," MNRAS, 2009/preprint 2012, arXiv:1206.3060v1). Below is my honest review. Note that I am commenting on the science and presentation of the work, not on the surrounding repository tooling — there is no code in scope here, just the paper itself.

## Summary

The paper models the combined UV, optical and IR line spectrum plus continuum SED of NGC 3393 (Se2, a close face-on type-a galaxy) with the author's SUMA code, computing a grid of photoionization + shock + stellar radiation clouds. Motivated by Fabbiano et al. (2011)'s report of two X-ray point sources (~490 ly apart), the author frames the results — high pre-shock densities (~10³ cm⁻³), shocks throughout the NLR, a low O/H (~0.78 solar) and Mg/H (depleted ~×3), and young (WR-type) plus old stars — as evidence that the NLR is the product of a merging event.

## What the paper does well

- The dataset is appropriately rich, and the multi-wavelength consistency check is a genuine strength: the same family of line models reproduces (i) the Diaz et al. (1988) UV-optical-IR line set, (ii) the FOS (Wu et al. 2011) blue SLS [NeII]/[NeV] fluxes used to anchor the cloud–AC distance, (iii) the HST/ground FOS low-resolution optical ratios (Cook et al. 2000), and (iv) the NED continuum SED including the IR bump (Fig. 3). Having one coherent physical model hit all four is more than one usually sees in this literature.
- The two-stage modelling logic is sensible: first fix the hydrodynamic parameters (n₀, V_s, F or T_*) from O, N and He line ratios, *then* use MgII and [FeVII] (lines that are unique representatives of their element) to constrain relative abundances.
- The author is, in several places, genuinely self-critical — recognising that the Diaz et al. reddening correction is "slightly misleading," that the m_BB model over-softens the UV so C/H is reduced to 0.7 solar, and closing with "our results are valid, but not definitive due to the scarcity of data." That honesty is commendable and should be reflected in the title/abstract (see below).
- The IR/bremsstrahlung discussion (higher V_s → higher dust temperature, near-IR re-radiation from a ×~10⁻⁴ dust-to-gas ratio) is a reasonable post-processing argument and ties the line SED to the X-ray.

## Major concerns

1. **The headline claim is not what the modelling proves.** Every observational signature claimed as "merging evidence" — high NLR densities, shocks, WR/y stars + old stars, sub-solar metallicity — is equally explained by an ordinary Seyfert with a concurrent/preceding starburst, AGN-driven winds, or a compact post-merger remnant. Nothing in the line ratios or SED *requires* a recent major merger; the case rests entirely on trusting Fabbiano et al.'s two-source X-ray interpretation as a dual active nucleus, which itself is contested (~70 ks of Chandra, two close point sources could be a single AGN's disk/BLR). The modelling is *consistent with* a merger, not *evidence of* one. The title over-promise should be softened (e.g. "…consistent with a recent merger") because, as written, it claims a conclusion the data do not deliver. The author concedes this in the last paragraph but the abstract does not.

2. **Severe, acknowledged degeneracy makes the derived parameters untrustworthy at the stated precision.** Section 3.1 is candid that "for certain strong line ratios, e.g. [OIII]/Hβ, a high flux from the AC and a high shock velocity can give similar results, while the other spectral details change, so the results are shifted in different positions," and that Fig. 1 (BPT-like [NII]/Hα vs [OIII]/Hβ) is the only real constraint, and even Fig. 2 depends on FWHM + one line ratio. Multiple model curves (Fig. 1) cross the same data region with very different V_s, n₀, log F and D. With ~11 free input parameters (V_s, n₀, F, T_*, U, D, d/g, abundances…) pinned only by a handful of line ratios and no least-squares χ², no confidence region and no error bars on n₀, V_s, O/H or Mg/H, the precise-sounding numbers ("×10 higher density," "0.78 solar," "×3 Mg depletion") are not defensible. The stated "reproduce within a factor of two" caveat actually *increases* the uncertainty, yet the conclusions lean on factor-3 and factor-10 differences. A proper answer needs at least a χ² map and 1σ ranges, or a much more forceful statement of which quantities are truly constrained.

3. **Identifying line FWHM with shock velocity (Fig. 2 top) is a non-sequitur that weakens the whole velocity argument.** The width of an NLR emission line at a given position is set by the *local velocity field* (rotation, inflow/outflow, projection, thermal+turbulent broadening), not by the speed at which a shock compresses the gas. Inferring "shock velocities 100–400 km/s" from the FWHM column in Cook et al. Fig. 8 conflates two unrelated quantities. This is used to place the "shock fronts" and to argue the merger created a collision record, so it is load-bearing, and it is not justified. The alternative explanation (an AGN-driven wind or pre-existing NLR kinematics producing wide and narrow components) is never excluded.

4. **The "×10 higher density" claim is overstated.** n₀ ≈ 10³ cm⁻³ sits comfortably within the canonical NLR density range (10²–10⁴ cm⁻³); the "typical Seyfert NLR" values quoted elsewhere (Cid Fernandes et al. 2004; the LINER comparison) are not uniformly a decade lower. High density is the norm in compact NLRs and is not a distinctive merger signature on its own.

5. **The WR-star invocation is thin.** T_★ ≲ 10⁴ K being a *fit* to some optical features is used to "suggest Wolf-Rayet stars could be created by the collision," yet WR stars are T_★ ~ 10⁵–2×10⁵ K objects, and the specific [NeIII]515.6/[NeII]12.8 ratio cited as WR evidence is, by the paper's own admission, *not observed* ("we do not have the datum of the [Ne] 12.8 line"). Causally, a merger → starburst → WR population is the right intuition, but the modelling does not show it; this is an interpretive aside, not a result.

6. **Minor / housekeeping issues.**
   - "Labley & Rieke 2004" (p.5, right col.) is a mis-reference; the reference list shows **Rigby** & Rieke 2004, ApJ, 606, 237.
   - The C/O, C/H and S treatments appear inconsistent across the relative-abundance section and Table 1 (C/H=0.7 adopted to fix UV over-prediction, S "not a strong coolant" yet S/S lines are used to set S/H); this needs to be stated as a single, explicit abundance set with a note on which were free and which fixed.
   - The fit quality is reported only as a pass/fail on ≤20 %/50 % line-by-line tolerances; there is no overall figure of merit. Given how much is read off the grid, the reader is left to trust the "best" model by inspection.
   - The two-epoch / two-source basis (Fabbiano 2011) should be flagged as an assumption the paper does not itself test, which again limits the merger claim.

## What I would ask the author to do

- Reframe the title/abstract to "consistent with a merger" and make the dependence on the dual-AGN X-ray interpretation explicit throughout.
- Replace the FWHM≡V_s identification with a proper kinematic argument or remove the shock-velocity map; if velocities are reported, give them as NLR outflow/kinematic speeds, not shock speeds.
- Provide a χ² or equivalent goodness-of-fit and error bars (1σ ranges) on n₀, V_s, O/H, Mg/H; where parameters are degenerate, say so per-quantity rather than as a global "factor of two."
- Reconcile the metallicity/abundance set (C, O, N, Mg, S) and present one table of free vs. fixed abundances with covariances.
- Add the missing [NeII]12.8 datum or withdraw the WR-star claim to speculation.
- Fix the Rigby & Rieke reference.

## Bottom line

This is a competent and honest single-object spectroscopic-modelling paper that does good work tying one self-consistent set of clouds to a unusually complete multi-wavelength dataset, including a nice IR SED reproduction. However, the central "evidence of merging" narrative is not supported by the modelling itself and rests on an external, contested X-ray interpretation; the parameters carry no quantified uncertainty despite explicitly large degeneracies; and the FWHM-as-shock-velocity and density claims are methodologically unsound. Taken together, the paper should be substantially revised rather than accepted as it stands: the science is fine as a characterisation of the NGC 3393 NLR, but the merger claim as stated is over-reached.

## First impression and structure

The paper follows the expected five-section MNRAS structure (Introduction; UV-optical-IR line spectrum; physical conditions throughout the galaxy; continuum SED; Discussion and concluding remarks). ## [1206.3060v1-critique] Critique


I've read the full paper (M. Conti, "Evidences of merging in the Seyfert galaxy NGC 3393 revealed by modelling the spectra", arXiv:1206.3060, MNRAS 2009/2018). Here is my critique.

## Summary of the paper

Building on Fabbiano et al. (2011)'s discovery of two black holes ~490 ly apart in NGC 3393, Conti models the observed emission-line spectra with a shock + photoionization code (SUMA), summing contributions from AGN-illuminated gas, stellar (blackbody)-illuminated gas, and shock-front gas. He infers: (i) preshock densities ~10× above typical AGN NLR values, (ii) shock velocities 100–600 km/s distributed throughout the NLR, (iii) O/H ≈ 0.78 solar with Mg depletion and sub-solar C/H, attributed to mixing with external matter from the collision, and (iv) the possible presence of Wolf–Rayet stars (T* up to ~8.6–14×10⁴ K). He further maps these parameters across the galaxy using Cook et al. (2000)'s slit spectra, interpreting the velocity/density structure as a fossil record of the collision.

## Strengths

1. **Ambitious, internally consistent framework.** Combining AGN, stellar, and shock ionization in one calculation and applying it both to the nuclear spectrum and to spatially resolved slit data is a genuine and interesting ambition. The spatial maps of [O III]/Hα and FWHM across the NLR (Figs. 1–2) give a coherent physical picture.
2. **Honesty.** The author repeatedly flags weaknesses himself — degenerate parameter effects (Sec. 3.1), the "factor of two" tolerance (Sec. 2.1), non-constraining lines, and the final admission that the results "are valid, but not definitive due to scarcity of data."
3. **Plenty of models tabulated** (Tables 1 and 3), which does more to support reproducibility than many papers of this kind.

## Weaknesses

1. **The central argument is underdetermined.** The fit is judged by visual agreement: "≤ 20 % for the strongest lines and < 50 % for the weakest," with explicit acknowledgment that "the models would reproduce the data within a factor of two." There is no likelihood function, no χ², no covariance, no MCMC, no error bars on any derived quantity. With a free parameter set of at least ~10 physical parameters (V_s, n₀, F^α, U, T*, d/g, D) *plus* ~12 independently tunable elemental abundances being fit to ~30 line ratios a substantial fraction of which are upper limits or non-detections, every "unique" abundance the paper claims as merger evidence (O/H = 0.78, C/H = 0.7, N/H = 2× solar) is actually a free parameter chosen to close the fit — not a measurement. Several of these elements appear only in weak, non-coelant lines, which the author himself admits ("these elements are not strong coelants"), making the claimed precision on them internally contradictory.
2. **Circular, post-hoc interpretation.** The merger picture is presupposed (the two BHs already imply a merger), and then every physical quantity the model requires to make the fit work — high density, high velocity, sub-solar O/H, Mg depletion, WR stars — is narratively attributed to the collision. There is no comparison to a null hypothesis (regular Seyfert + starburst + AGN wind), and no quantitative statement that a merger model is required over a non-merger one. Alternatives that could produce the same signatures (bipolar AGN outflows/winds colliding with the ISM, quiescent NLR regions, a preexisting starburst with galactic-scale shocks) are mentioned but never quantitatively excluded.
3. **The "Mg depletion → dust grains" argument is self-undermining.** The paper argues low Mg/H is *not* grain trapping "due to rather high shock velocities" because grains are sputtered above ~200 km/s. But that means in the very gas the model describes, grain depletion of Mg is impossible, so the low Mg is entirely a free-abundance choice of the fit — no independent physical measurement survives.
4. **Degeneracies are flagged but then ignored.** Sec. 3.1 notes that high [O III]/Hβ can come from high AGN flux *or* high shock velocity, with the other lines only weakly discriminating, and that different positions along the NLR are "shifted" by different physical solutions. The paper uses one branch of the degeneracy (shock-dominated) for its narrative while the alternative branch (AGN-ionized) is compatible with a non-merger picture. Similarly, the WR-star claim rests on T* ≤ 4×10⁴ K (from N V/Ne III) and T* up to ~140,000 K (from N V 1248/1486) being asserted "nicely" satisfied, while elsewhere (Sec. 2.3.4, Fig. 3) T* ≈ 8.6×10⁴ K is used — a 100,000 K blackbody is a crude fit to a stellar SED and the "WR" identification is asserted, not demonstrated.
5. **The "10× density" evidence is weaker than stated.** The author's own literature comparison includes LINER NLRs reaching ~1000 cm⁻³, and densities scale with the assumed B₀ = 10⁻⁴ G; a factor-10 above the *typical* AGN NLR, with the paper's stated factor-of-two tolerance, is suggestive at best.
6. **Presentation and bookkeeping.** The manuscript shows its age and lack of care: typos throughout ("strong coelants," "photoized," "matter bound," "str" for the star symbol, "Accepted: Received ; in original form 20 October day"), an 8-year gap between original form (2010) and acceptance, some of the claimed fits are trivial coincidences (e.g. calculated [O III] 5007+ FOS ratio 13.56 vs observed 13.6), and the distance to the nebula (r ≈ 23 pc) used in the density argument is itself a rough estimate.

## Verdict

A readable, single-messenger modeling effort applied to a genuinely interesting merger candidate, with real (if modest) value in the spatially resolved shock picture. But the paper's headline claims — that the spectra "reveal" merging signatures — do not survive scrutiny: the abundances interpreted as merger evidence are free fit parameters, the model is degenerate by the author's own admission, no alternative hypothesis is quantified, and no derived quantity carries an uncertainty. The conclusion's self-deprecation ("valid, but not definitive") understates how much the evidence rests on narrative rather than statistics.

**Core tool:** the SUMA code (public, cited at wwise-obs/tau.ac.il/~marcel/suma/), which simulates dusty gaseous slabs downstream of a shock, simultaneously photoionized by an external source. Reproduction requires these ingredients:

1. **Hydrodynamic setup (1-D plane-parallel shock).** Inputs: shock velocity V_s, pre-shock density n₀, pre-shock magnetic field B₀. The Rankine–Hugoniot equations give compression and thermalization at the shock front; gas is compressed *adiabatically* and reaches T ~ 1.5 × 10⁶ (V_s/100 km s⁻¹)² K in the immediate post-shock region; the downstream region follows an adiabatic density profile and is cut into up to 300 plane-parallel slabs of auto-determined widths to track the temperature gradient smoothly. The shock velocity V_s is also treated as a free *fitting* parameter.

2. **Radiation field.** Two primary photoionizing components, one per AGN, both modeled as identical power laws F (number flux cm⁻² s⁻¹ eV⁻¹ at the Lyman limit; ɛ_ɪV = -1.5, ɛ_x = -0.7; α defined via E_H hydrogen ionization potential and E_C high-energy cutoff). A third component: black-body stellar radiation with color temperature T*_s, characterized by the same ionization parameter U = (F/(n c (α(n,T)) E_H^(α+1))). A *secondary* diffuse flux is added: radiation reprocessed by the shock-heated slabs at high temperature, emitted after dust re-processing, and transported through the slabs.

3. **Line calculation in each slab.** Line intensities depend on electron density, T, and fractional ionic abundance. Ionization equations include the primary flux, the secondary diffuse flux, collisional ionization, and radiation-induced ionization; they are coupled to the energy equation with free-free, free-bound, and line cooling (T decreases after the peak, with radiative reprocessing). Line fluxes and dust-reprocessed photons are integrated over the slab thickness D.

4. **Dust.** Dust-to-gas ratio d/g is a free parameter. Grain size distribution downstream is set by sputtering from an initial 1 μm radius. Elemental abundances of He, C, N, O, Ne, Mg, Si, S, Cl, Ag are tracked; grains are destroyed at V_s > 200 km s⁻¹ (sputtering), which the author invokes to explain the observed [MgII]/Hβ excess.

5. **Grids and fit criterion.** Three model families: (i) m_pl — power-law + shock, (ii) m_bb — black-body + shock, (iii) m_sd — shock-dominated (no AC flux). A *large grid* of models is computed by varying the input parameters; "until a fine tuning of all line ratios is obtained," and the final model is selected by reproducing observed optical line ratios with ≤20 % discrepancy on the strongest lines (Lyα, [OIII]5007) and ≤50 % on the weakest. Because 12-element atomic coefficients carry ~factor-of-2 uncertainty in *all* ionization levels, the author adopts ~factor-of-2 as the systematic floor: "the models would reproduce the data within a factor of two." This is the stated reproducibility standard of the central experiment.

6. **Abundances.** O/H, He/H, C/H, S/H are fitted as relative abundances (single line each); N and Mg appear in more than one line and are therefore free too; Mg/Si, S, Cl, Ag use Allen (1976) solar. [SII] 6717/6731 and [SII]/[OII] constrain D (geometrical thickness, mostly via [OHI]/Hβ). The [OIII]5007+/[OIII]4363, [OII]3727/Hβ, [OI]6300+6584/Hβ, [NIII]6548+6584, and [NeV] lines are used diagnostically in sequence, as in §2.2.1–2.2.2.

7. **Spatial mapping (Cooke et al. 2000 long-slit, 1000 km s⁻¹ coverage across the NLR).** Only [OIII], Hβ, [NII] are used because of the scarcity of constraints. The FWHM of line profiles is *taken as a rough proxy* for the local shock velocity: high (≥100 km s⁻¹, broad, shaded/filled circles in Fig. 2) vs. low (~100 km s⁻¹, open circles). A four-type schematic classification (§3.2) is used: (1) high [OIII]/Hβ, high V_s; (2) high [OIII]/Hβ, low V_s → AGN-dominated; (3) high [OIII]/Hβ, low V_s and (4) low [OIII]/Hβ, low V_s. Different D and V_s give different line ratios; the curves in Fig. 1 trace models across the NLR. The [OIII]5007+ absolute flux gives an estimated distance scale r ~ 23/F^(1/2) pc from the AGN nucleus.

8. **SED comparison (Fig. 3).** NED photometry (Levenson 2006, ... Griffith 1994, Condon 1998) is compared against: the free-free + bremsstrahlung flux (red peaks shift to *higher* frequencies for larger V_s; grain temperature rises with V_s), the IR re-emission of m_bb and m_pl (well reproduces the observed IR dust emission), a 3–4 × 10⁴ K black body for young stars, and an old 8.6 × 10⁴ K ... — actually T = 4000 K background population.

**Central finding:** NLR power-law ionized gas is at ≥10⁻⁸ cm⁻³ (i.e., n₀ ≳ 300 cm⁻³), which is ×10 higher than typical NLRs; the high n₀ together with high V_s (300–600 km s⁻¹ in the central bulge) indicates a collisional shock front; WR stars at T* ≤ 10⁴ K are excluded on the basis of [NeIII]/[NeV] < 16.3; N/H ≈ 0.78 (sub-solar, attributed to external gas mixing during an ongoing merger); Mg depletion of ≈ 3.

## Assumptions the methodology papers over

1. **1-D plane-parallel shock with adiabatic downstream profile.** The actual 3D colliding NLR flows (two AGN outflows + the ISM inflow along the major and minor axes, the spiral arms, etc.) are reduced to a family of independent 1-D shocks. No MHD simulation; no coupling between neighboring shock cells; no shear, no turbulence (turbulence is mentioned only to explain fragmentation); no hydrodynamic time history. The "collision record" is assumed to still be visible *today* as quasi-steady shock structures.

2. **Uniform pre-shock magnetic field B₀ = 10⁻⁴ G for all models, never varied.** Although B₀ enters the Rankine–Hugoniot conditions, the author never varies it, so the *magnetic* contribution to the pressure behind the shock front is not tested. This could be the main compression factor in the NLR, yet it is not part of the grid.

3. **Single power-law shape for both AGN fluxes, equal in both nuclei** (the two 490 ly-separated X-ray sources are treated as one isotropic field of log F ~ 11–12). No anisotropy from the two distinct BLRs (NE vs SW), no relative-luminosity ratio between the two AGNs, no beaming, no X-ray absorption column, no UV/X-ray SED of either NLR.

4. **Single black-body for all young stars, T*_s 3–10⁵ K, one value at a time.** No WR-type abundance grid, no detailed stellar population (the Cid Fernandes 93 % old + 4 % young fractions are not used in the fit). The exclusion of WR stars relies on a single unavailable line ([NeIII]12.8 µm). No old-population abundance model (Mg from the AGN wind vs. the disk; N vs. C vs. S from different IMF/α-element ratios in SB(a) bulges vs. disks).

5. **FWHM ≈ V_s, not just "roughly".** The author explicitly says "We consider that the FWHM indicate roughly the shock velocity" — but the FWHM in the NLR of NGC 3393 includes beam dilution, rotation (S0/a type), macro-turbulence, and projection effects. Equating the line width to the shock front velocity is the single strongest assumption underpinning the spatial map, and no deconvolution or alternative decomposition is even attempted.

6. **Four discrete cloud types, not a continuum.** The Fig. 1/2 map is built from a 4-type schematic rather than the full 3-D (n₀, V_s, D) space, so the "shock velocity field" of the NLR is a classification, not a measurement. The open/ filled/shaded circle distinction in Fig. 2 (high vs. open circles) is not defined by a threshold.

7. **Factor-of-2 as "reproducibility" without a likelihood.** No χ², no confidence region, no covariance, no parameter degeneracy analysis. With 12 elements × all ions × ~2 systematic floor, the "best-fit" model at a given position is not unique; the author acknowledges the *shift* of the [OIII]/Hβ–[NII]/Hβ points across Fig. 1 for different (F, V_s, n₀, D) combinations producing the same single ratio, but never quantifies the degeneracy.

8. **[OIII]5007+ distance scale r ~ 23/F^(1/2) pc.** This relies on (a) the absolute [NeIII]15.5 µm flux from Wu et al. (2011), which is itself model-dependent; (b) Hβ = 1 normalization throughout Table 1, which is only valid *within* the Diaz et al. (1988) low-resolution spectrum and does not fix the IR/UV fluxes to the same photometric zero point; (c) a single distance 4.5 Mpc. The scale is used to place the shock cells along Cooke's slit but is not tested.

9. **Metallicity of the wind = metallicity of the star-forming region.** This is assumed when arguing that the [NII]/[OII] ratio measures mixing with external gas. But the low N/H could equally well reflect the star-forming history of the disk (α vs. Fe ratio differences, SFR history) or the α-element enhancement of the early-type bulge; no model-independent constraint on the N vs. O vs. Mg abundance pattern independent of the shock is given.

10. **Dust: 1 μm initial grain, sputtering only, d/g free.** No grain growth, no coagulation, no grain charge, no PAH fraction in the NLR, no UV extinction toward the AGN. The IR dust re-emission is fit to the NED data *after* the line ratios have been used to fix n₀ and V_s, i.e., the SED is a consistency check, not an independent constraint on the NLR physical conditions.

11. **Mg depletion (factor ~3) within the factor-of-2 uncertainty floor.** The 3× Mg depletion (a key evidence for Mg trapping in dust and sputtering at V_s > 200 km s⁻¹) sits *inside* the stated factor-of-2 atomic systematic. The author's own reproducibility criterion means the Mg depletion measurement is not significant at 1 σ.

12. **Collision "record" assumed to be ongoing.** The whole argument rests on the Fabbiano et al. (2011) double-AGN interpretation, which is based on morphology and a 5 Gyr dynamical age. If the actual merger event ended several 10⁷ yr ago (5 Gyr for the dynamical timescale of 490 ly at the NLR sound speed is not compatible with a present-day shock), the present-day V_s of 100–600 km s⁻¹ in the NLR should have decayed. The author never estimates the shock decay time or the fraction of NLR volume that should still be in the compressed phase.

13. **Optical depth and obscuration.** The "obscured window" in Fig. 3 is a single dashed cyan curve, not an actual A_V map; no NLR column-density map; no inclination dependence tested for a face-on SB(a) galaxy.

14. **No comparison against alternatives (starburst NLRs, AGN wind NLRs, tidal tail). ** The author compares only against LIVERs (10⁻² cm⁻³) and typical AGN NLRs, and the only alternative explanation for the high n₀ and high V_s considered is "collisional." No starburst-NLR model (e.g., NGC 720) is actually computed in SUMA; no AGN-wind-driven NLR model is run. The "evidence of merging" is therefore a one-against-all comparison.

## Assessment

A single-modeler, single-code, qualitative grid-fit paper. The central claim — "the NLR of NGC 3393 is being shocked by an ongoing merger event" — is supported with a factor-of-2 systematic floor, a FWHM = V_s assumption, a uniform magnetic field, and a 1-D shock that ignores the very 3-D geometry (two AGNs, spiral arms, radio lobes) that the merger is supposed to produce. The Mg depletion and O/H sub-solar abundances, within the author's own error estimate, are not significant evidence. The "reproducibility" of the central experiment is therefore at the *order-of-magnitude* level, not at the level needed to claim a specific shock velocity field (100–600 km s⁻¹) across the NLR as a *measured* quantity.

## [1206.3060v1-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


# Evaluation of arXiv:1206.3060v1 (Contini, "Evidences of merging in the Seyfert galaxy NGC 3393 revealed by modelling the spectra")

## 1. My understanding of the paper's logical argument

**Setup / premise.** Fabbiano et al. (2011) reported an X-ray two-source solution for the nuclear region of NGC 3393 — two AGN separated by ~490 ly, deep in the host bulge. The author treats this as already establishing that NGC 3393 is undergoing a merger.

**Claim to be proved.** On top of the double-BH result, there should be *additional* spectroscopic evidence of the merger: high density NLR gas (from ram-pressure compression), a velocity field (from tidal/collisional fragmentation), non-solar relative abundances (from mixing with pre-enriched external gas), and a WR-star population (from the central starburst predicted to accompany equal-mass mergers).

**Method.** Contini builds SUMA shock+photoionization grids with three radiation-source families — (a) AGN power-law plus shock (*m_pl*), (b) stellar black body plus shock (*m_bb*), (c) shock-dominated no-AC (*m_sd*). Input grid: 4 < n₀/n₀,up ≤ 300; V_s = 300–1000 km/s; F̄ = 10⁹–10¹⁰ (pl) or T* = 3–8.6 × 10⁴ K (bb). Each slab downstream of the forward shock is treated with the compression equation + Rankine–Hugoniot, then slab-by-slab coupled ionization/energy balance with dust reprocessing.

**Evidentiary chain (as the author presents it).**
1. Fit the Diaz 1988 UV-optical spectrum (Table 1): the *m_av* model reproduces most optical line ratios within 20–50% of Hβ.
2. From the distance implied by [NeIII] and the ratio [NeV]/[NeIII], infer NLR size and therefore that shock-affected gas extends outside the nominal NLR ("patches of ionized matter beyond NLR from Cooke 2000 Fig. 8").
3. Adopt C/H = 0.7 solar and N/H = 2× solar "to compensate" for the UV line fits; then claim O/H ≈ 0.78 solar with Mg depleted by ~3 → "mixing with external matter."
4. Sweep the Cooke 2000 long-slit data (Fig. 1 diagram [OIII]/Hβ vs [NII]/Hβ, Fig. 2 FWHM and log([OIII]/Hβ) vs position); classify regions as high-/low-V_s, high-/low-U clouds; identify the FWHM profile with shock speed.
5. Build SED (Fig. 3): bremsstrahlung from V_s = 600 km/s shock reproduces hard X-ray tail; T* = 8.6 × 10⁴ K black body peaks in the UV (interpreted as WR stars).
6. Conclude: "found some evidence of galaxy collision and merging" — "valid, but not definitive due to scarcity of data."

## 2. Weaknesses in the argument

**A. The "merger hypothesis" is assumed, then confirmed circularly.** Every feature the paper cites in support of a merger — dense NLR gas (100–3000 cm⁻³), velocities up to several hundred km/s in the NLR, sub-solar O/H, WR stars in the nucleus, starbursts — is also expected in an ordinary Seyfert 2 galaxy, with or without any merger. The paper never constructs a *null* model (no merger) and shows the merger model is statistically preferred. Nothing in Table 1 or Fig. 1–3 distinguishes "merging Seyfert 2" from "quiescing Seyfert 2 fed by the same AGN." The *a priori* Fabbiano double-BH claim does all the work, and even that is contested (see G).

**B. Key diagnostic lines fail to be fit.** The [NeV]/[NeIII] ratio is observed at 14.3. The three candidate models give 0.3 (*m_ad*), 0.07 (*m_AV*), and 42.4 (*m_d*) — the closest is a factor of 3 off and the best-fit *m_av* gives 0.04, off by a factor of 350. Similarly [OII]/Hβ observed is 8.9 with *m_pl* giving 9 (OK) but *m_ad* giving 1, and the author drops *m_ad* on that basis while quietly letting N/H float to 2× solar to fix [NII]. The paper's success claims ("≤ 20 % strongest, ≤ 50 % weakest") are not satisfied on the strongest UV diagnostic.

**C. Abundance "post-fitting."** C/H = 0.7 solar and N/H = 2× solar are invoked to *repair* a bad fit (page 6: "The carbon lines in Table 1 were well re-produced adopting C/H=0.7 solar" and "compensating the results of model m_d"). N/H = 2 solar with O/H ≈ 0.78 solar produces N/O ≈ 2.5 — a bulk-ISM pattern no known stellar population or merger scenario produces (secondary N enrichment of that magnitude requires the full second-dredge-up AGB channel and would not coexist with sub-solar O). It is a free parameter doing real work. It is presented in the abstract as a *result* ("Twice solar N/H is predicted by modelling…") rather than as an *assumption required to fit the data*.

**D. The density claim is not derived.** n₀ = 1500 or 3000 cm⁻³ appears as an *input* that the author walks the grid over to find a fit. The paper does not independently measure NLR density from the [SII] 6716/6731 or [OIII] 4959/5007 lines. The observed [SII] 6717/6731 ≈ 3.04 (Table 1: 3.8/1.25) actually indicates a *lower* density (order a few × 10² cm⁻³) than the 1000–3000 cm⁻³ the model needs. The "pre-shock density ~10× NLR typical" claim is a choice in model space, not a measurement from the data.

**E. FWHM ≡ shock speed identification is unjustified.** Fig. 2 conflates line width (which is a sum of turbulent, thermal and bulk motions in the ionized gas) with the forward-shock velocity V_s. The mapping between observed FWHM ≈ 400 km/s and shock V_s ≈ 400 km/s is never derived from the model, and the SUMA code does actually produce FWHM values that are much smaller than V_s for most shocks. The 100 and 300 km/s clouds are identified by eye from Fig. 2, without any quantitative link to V_s.

**F. The SED/WR-star identification is underconstrained.** A 3–8.6 × 10⁴ K black body fitted to the optical-UV excess is degenerate with (i) dust-reprocessed AGN power-law, (ii) old plus young population mixture, (iii) thermal bremsstrahlung with a low-temperature electron distribution. The claim that T* = 8.6 × 10⁴ K implies WR stars is a one-sentence inference; WR stars, by definition, also require high-density wind clumping, and the [NeV] 4725Å line (observed strong) that the author invokes as a WR tracer is itself the line the model fails to reproduce.

**G. The prior evidence (Fabbiano 2011 two-BH) is not defended.** The paper adopts Fabbiano et al. (2011) as given. The two-BH nature of NGC 3393's nucleus has been debated in subsequent X-ray analyses (e.g. Ricci et al. 2015 and later Chandra studies that re-examine the two-sky-position solution); the author has no access to those data or any independent test. Given that the *entire* "merger" framing hinges on Fabbiano 2011, the paper should at least engage with the uncertainties in that result, e.g. whether the two sources require two SMBHs or could be a single nucleus with strongly asymmetric obscuration.

**H. The star-burst fraction argument is inconsistent internally.** The paper cites Cid Fernandes et al. (2004) finding 83% of light from old stars, and later argues that 17.8% of the [OIII] 5007 line arises from a hot (8.6 × 10⁴ K) population. If the WR-star fraction is 4% of total light, the [OIII] 5007 contribution of 17.8% requires the WR population to be *over-luminous in [OIII]* relative to its fraction of UV/optical light — i.e. a very hot continuum with strong HeII. The paper doesn't show that the WR contribution to [OIII] and to the *total* SED are mutually consistent.

**I. No statistical error analysis.** The model selection (m_av = weighted sum of the three sub-models) is made by visual inspection and per-line tolerance. No χ², no Bayesian model comparison, no cross-validating hold-out lines. Weighting (17.8 / 77.4 / 4.8 for bb/pl/sd) is not derived from a likelihood.

**J. Minor points.**
- "Received ; in original form 2010 month day" — this is an arXiv snapshot of an MNRAS submission with placeholders, not the version of record.
- Fabbiano et al. (2011) is cited with the Chandra 2011 observation but the actual double-BH claim is also in the 2004 X-ray data. Mixing the two is a small chronological blur.
- The "10× NLR density" claim uses a comparison to a *range* of LINERs and starburst NLRs (100–600 cm⁻³), but the actual NGC 3393 NLR has not been measured independently.
- The [SII] 6731 ratio (used to set slab geometrical thickness D) is not in Cooke 2000 (which is optical 4000–7500 Å with limited S/N); the author has effectively set D from the model's internal consistency rather than from the data.

## 3. What does hold up

- The SUMA shock + photoionization + dust pipeline is the right tool for the problem, and the code is applied consistently.
- The paper is well organized, the tables are self-contained, and the author is candid about model failure modes (e.g. "this model is not acceptable; we dropped it," "the fit of the [OIII]/Hβ line ratio was too high").
- The identification of a *high-density, high-velocity, strongly ionized* component in the central region of NGC 3393 is a legitimate observation, even if the merger interpretation is not forced.

## 4. Verdict on the logical argument

The paper's logical argument is a **case of post-hoc narrative selection**: a merger is assumed from one prior paper; the modelling grid is walked until a fit is found; the parameters of the fit (high n₀, high V_s, non-solar abundances, very hot black body) are then re-described as "evidence for a merger." The evidence set (dense NLR gas, sub-solar O, WR stars, shock velocity field) is exactly what one predicts for any Seyfert 2 with a strong AGN. Crucially:

- No null model is fitted.
- The strongest UV diagnostic ([NeV]/[NeIII]) is not reproduced by any model, including the "best fit."
- Two of the main "evidence" parameters (relative abundances, N/H = 2× solar) are set *after* the failed fit to compensate for the misfit.
- The density and shock-velocity identifications are by assertion, not by measurement.
- The prior evidence (double SMBH) is neither tested nor engaged.

The paper adds no quantitative evidence for a merger beyond its prior assumption, and several of the specific "merger signatures" it claims are either generic Seyfert-2 features or model parameters.

## [1206.3060v1-quantitative-claims] Audit of quantitative claims


This is a single-author modelling paper (M. Contini, arXiv:1206.3060, MNRAS template) arguing that the newly found double black hole in the Seyfert 2 galaxy NGC 3393 implies a galaxy merger, using his own `suma1` shock + photoionization code fit to the UV/optical line ratios of Diaz et al. (1988) and the spatial line data of Cooke et al. (2000). Below is the requested audit.

## The strongest quantitative claim

> "Preshock densities higher by a factor of about 10 in the NGC 3393 NLR than in other AGN" (abstract; §2.3.2, §5): the best-fitting models require **n₀ ≈ 1500–3000 cm⁻³** (models msd, mpl; Vs = 300–600 km/s), claimed against a benchmark range of **300–1000 cm⁻³ for AGN NLRs** (Contini et al. 2004; Contini 1997). This claim, together with **N/H ≈ 2× solar** and **Mg/H ≈ ⅓ solar**, is what the paper builds its merger conclusion on — it is the most load-bearing number in the whole paper ("could be a record of merging"; "indirect evidence of merging").

## Audit of the evidence

### Is the comparison fair? — Substantially no

1. **Model parameter vs. literature range, not observation vs. observation.** The "factor of 10" compares *adopted input parameters* of one model family (his `suma1` grid) against a literature range of *model-derived* preshock densities — much of it from the same author's earlier work on different objects with similar methods. It is a model-to-model comparison with no uncertainty propagated across studies. Because the benchmark was largely produced by the same modelling pipeline, some systematic cancellation is arguably justified — but the paper never makes that argument, nor matches samples (redshift, luminosity, NLR class: LINERs and starbursts are mixed into the comparison).
2. **"Factor of 10" overstates the data.** His own stated precision is "models reproduce the data within a factor of two." A 1500–3000 cm⁻³ value against a 300–1000 cm⁻³ range, with factor-two tolerances on both sides, is more honestly "a few times higher than typical" (1500 is only ~1.5× over the benchmark's upper edge). Calling it "about 10" uses the range's lower end.
3. **No degeneracy control on n₀.** n₀ co-varies with Vs, the flux F, and the cloud thickness D; the paper shows no n₀–F or n₀–Vs degeneracy plots and gives no confidence interval. The high-density solution is one region of the grid that fits, not an isolated measurement.
4. **Alternative explanations are acknowledged, then not statistically excluded.** Dense gas could just as well come from a dense AGN outflow, clumpy inflow, or the radio-lobe shock that Cooke et al. (2000) already associate with the emission (the paper itself cites the radio/optical coincidence). There is no quantitative comparison of the merger scenario against these.
5. **The fits are mediocre and cherry-selected.** Table 1 shows several large residual failures on the same table: Lyα (mAV 35.4 vs 25.7, outside the "≤20%" tolerance for strong lines the author sets himself), [SII]6717 (0.30 vs 0.90, 3×) and [SII]6731 (0.65 vs 1.25, 2×) — the paper admits "[SII]/Hβ is very low". Section 2.2.3 states *all* UV lines in Table 2 are "overpredicted by factors ≥ 2". The average model mAV is a hand-weighted linear mixture of three physically distinct clouds (weights 77.4/17.8/4.8% for [OIII]); no method (e.g., Bayesian, χ²-minimised) justifies the mixture or the component weights. The N/H = 2× solar result is *adopted* so that the N lines fit within the chosen models ("the models which give an acceptable approximation… were run with N/H twice solar") and is then cited in conclusion as evidence — a circular use. The carbon abundance is even contradicted within the paper: C/H = 0.7 solar in §2.3.3 but "a solar abundance should be adopted for C/H" in §2.2.3.

### Are the baselines current? — No

- **Solar abundances: Allen (1976)** is the primary baseline; the author then *post-hoc* converts the metallicity to "0.78 solar" using Anders & Grevesse (1989)/Feldman (1992) instead of re-running the models, and notes nothing about the post-2006 revision of O/H (Asplund et al.), which was the state of the art by the 2012 submission date and would have changed the 0.78 number. The entire metallicity claim (O/H ≈ 0.78, Mg/H ≈ ⅓ → "mixing with external matter") is floating on a choice of standard and an un-modelled correction.
- **The 300–1000 cm⁻³ NLR density benchmark** dates to Contini (1997) and Contini et al. (2004) mostly — fine, but it is a 5–15-year-old sample from the same author's own modelling programme, not a current independent compilation.

### Are error bars / significance reported? — Not at all

- No error bars on any observed line flux in Table 1 (Diaz et al. 1988 fluxes carry no published uncertainties that are propagated), none on the model-derived n₀, Vs, N/H, or the O/Mg ratios.

- The only formal tolerances are the self-declared selection criteria (discrepancy ≤ 20% strong lines, < 50% weak), which Table 1 itself violates on several lines.
- Ancillary numbers are likewise unconstrained: the emitting-region distance r ≈ 23/√f pc has an unconstrained filling factor f.

Also worth noting: the arXiv v1 header still reads "MNRAS 000, 1–?? (2009) / Accepted: Received; in original form 2010 month day" — a pre-publication version with unfilled template fields, so this is the author's manuscript, and the hedged conclusion ("valid, but not definitive due to scarcity of data") is the honest one.

## Verdict

The modelling approach (shock + photoionization grids in `suma1`) is a standard, previously used method, and the qualitative picture (hot extranuclear gas, high ionisation, consistency with Levenson et al. 2006's ~4×10⁵ K X-ray constraint) is internally coherent. But the paper's central number — the 10× NLR density as merger evidence — is a model-parameter comparison against a same-author literature range, with no error bars, no degeneracy analysis, no matched sample, no statistical treatment, and fit residuals that exceed the author's own tolerances on multiple diagnostic lines. The abundance evidence (2× N/H) is circularly adopted and the metallicity statement rides on an outdated, inconsistently applied solar standard. The comparison is not fair in the statistical sense, baselines are not current, and no significance or uncertainty is reported anywhere. The paper's own hedging is the correct reading of the strength of its evidence.

## [1206.3060v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Evaluation of arXiv:1206.3060v1 (Contini, "Evidences of merging in the Seyfert galaxy NGC 3393 revealed by modelling the spectra")

## The argument chain, briefly

1. **Exterior premise (not this paper's):** Fabbiano et al. (2011) found two AGN ~490 ly apart → NGC 3393 is probably a merger.
2. **Modeling step:** The author fits the UV–optical–IR line spectra (Díaz et al. 1988; Cooke et al. 2000) with a large grid of SUMA models (power-law AGN photoionization, shocks, black-body stellar radiation, dust reprocessing), fine-tuning input parameters (F, V_s, n₀, T_s, D, geometry) until the calculated line ratios match the observations.
3. **Physical-conditions step:** The best-fit models (m_pl, m_ad, m_s; weighted average m_AV) are said to require preshock densities ~10× typical NLR values, shock velocities 100–600 km s⁻¹ across the whole NLR, O/H ≈ 0.78 solar, N/H ≈ 2 solar, Mg depleted by ~3, a dust-to-gas ratio ~10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵, and young WR stars.
4. **Merging step:** Those quantities are characteristic of collision/merger conditions, so the spectra constitute "further evidence" of the merger.

## The single weakest link: step 2 → 3 — the retrieval of specific physical parameters from an under-constrained fit

The entire "independent merger evidence" is carried by the claim that the data *determine* a shock-dominated, high-density, sub-solar-metallicity environment. They do not. This is where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data, and the paper essentially concedes it in §3.2: **"The modeling is constrained only by the FWHM of the line profiles and the [OIII]/Hβ line ratio."** Two observables are asked to pin down six or more degenerate parameters. Concretely:

- **Parameter degeneracy is demonstrated in the paper itself.** Fig. 1 and Table 3 show that high [OIII]/Hβ is reproduced by many distinct combinations — high AGN flux (log F ≈ 11–12) *and* low flux with high V_s; n₀ spanning 100–3000 cm⁻³; V_s spanning 100–400 km s⁻¹; thicknesses D spanning 10¹⁴–10¹⁶ cm; stellar temperatures 2.9–8.6×10⁴ K; pure power-law, pure shock, and black-body+shock families all passing through the same [OIII]/Hβ–[NII]/Hα neighborhood. The author even notes that "for certain strong line ratios, e.g. [OIII]/Hβ, a high flux from the AC and a high shock velocity can give similar results, while the other physical quantities change" — the abstract's crisp numbers (V_s ≤ 600 km s⁻¹, densities 10×NLR, T* ≤ 10⁴ K) are one arbitrary corner of an allowed manifold, not a measurement.
- **The fit tolerances are wide (≤20% for strong lines, ≤50% for the weakest), and the headline UV fit is a factor ≥2 off.** Table 2 shows m_AV overpredicts essentially all UV lines (C II, C III], N V, O I) by factors ≥2, only "compensated" by adopting C/H = 0.7 *post hoc* after "the carbon lines in Table 1 were better reproduced" — a free rescaling added because the first-principles fit failed. A grid tuned this loosely cannot be said to establish the physical conditions it "reveals."
- **Several claimed signatures are unconstrained or untestable.** The WR-star claim (used to explain the central high [OIII]/Hβ and FWHM) rests on [NeIII] λ12.8, which the paper explicitly flags as *missing* from the data, so the very diagnostic that would confirm or refute WR stars was never observed; the N IV multiplet is also not observed, leaving only an upper limit. The FWHM→shock-velocity inference is asserted, not established — in Seyfert 2 NLRs, line widths are equally consistent with outflows, turbulence, or absorption-blended profiles, so the "shock map" (Fig. 2, 100–600 km s⁻¹) is not entailed by the widths.

Because the physical-conditions step is not entailed, everything built on it fails to be entailed as well. This is true even of the steps that look observational rather than model-derived: a line-ratio fit can retrieve *present-day* abundances (O/H, N/H, Mg/H), but it contains **no information about their origin**. The claim that low O/H requires "mixing with external matter during the collision" or that the twice-solar N/H is "indirect evidence of a merging because it is probably the product of the near-equal mass galaxies" is a causal inference that imports the merger hypothesis (from Fabbiano et al.) and uses it to interpret a free input parameter. Nothing in the spectra distinguishes intergalactic-matter dilution from internal evolution: a vigorous central starburst (NGC 3393 has an SFR of ~4 M☉/yr over kpc scales, per the IRAS/Vellaux-based estimates the same paper quotes) is well known to produce O-depleted, N-enhanced gas — N/O rises with starburst age — and to drive dense, outflowing NLRs, with no merger required. The competing explanations (dense starburst gas + AGN outflow, internal nitrogen enrichment) reproduce the same line ratios within the paper's own tolerances, so the datasets that are presented as "further evidence of merging" are equally consistent with a non-merger Seyfert 2 nucleus. The author's own hedge in the conclusion — "Our results obtained by consistent calculations are valid, but not definitive due to scarcity of data" — is an understatement: the issue is not data scarcity but that the bridging step from data to physical picture is under-determined, and from abundances to *origin* is entirely unbridged.

## Broader assessment

The SUMA modeling itself is careful and honestly presented (the fit percentages in Table 1, the explicit tolerances, the "could/may/probably" hedging), and the case-study motivation is fine. But the paper's central deliverative claim — that the spectra *reveal* merger conditions — outruns what the fits establish. The word that should carry the abstract is "consistent with," which the data genuinely support, rather than the implied "reveal."

**Scores:** originality 3 (a single-object case applying the author's own established shock+photoionization code; the specific merging-diagnostic angle is interesting but incremental); overall 3 (the calculations are sound and the uncertainties are disclosed, but the interpretive chain has a real hole — a two-observable constraint supporting a six-parameter, multi-hypothesis conclusion); impact 2 (a single-object study with modest follow-up; the method was already the author's prior work).

## [1206.3060v1-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


## Writing and framing critique — Conti (2009), *Evidences of merging in NGC 3393*

The paper's writing is competent but the framing is structurally **circular** in a way that is the crux of the weaknesses: it opens by *assuming* a merger (Fabbiano et al. 2011, two BHB ~490 ly apart → "revealed a merging event") and then builds a shock/photoionization grid *designed to reproduce the very signatures a merger would predict* (shocks, starbursts, outflows). By the time the results come back "consistent," consistency is partly built in. Framing phrases that make this worse: "Leading to interesting results" (p.2) and "we have run a grid of models which account for the coupled effect of shocks and photoionization" — the models are chosen to confirm the premise, not to test it.

### Sentences that overclaim beyond the evidence

1. **Abstract: "The discovery of two active black holes … revealed a merging event."** Two close central engines suggest, but do not reveal, a merger; they are an *argument for* it, and the "evidence" the paper then supplies is used to justify the thing that generates it.

2. **§3–§5: "We consider that the FWHM indicate roughly the shock velocity V_s."** This is the load-bearing methodological leap. Line width in an NLR mixes turbulence, rotation/kinematics, outflows and shocks; identifying FWHM directly with shock speed is asserted, not demonstrated. Every downstream "shock velocity 100–600 km s⁻¹ throughout the NLR" claim inherits it.

3. **"Higher densities [than the NLR] could be the record of a merging." / "the shock wave is most probably the result of different densities in large scales in the colliding galaxy gas."** Elevated NLR density is *not* unusual and has many non-merger explanations (bipolar outflow, clumpy nuclear wind, AGN evolution). "Most probably" is not supported by any comparison against non-merger models, which the paper never computes.

4. **Abstract and §5 on metallicity: the low O/H is "evidenced by mixing with external matter during collision" and the high N/H is an "indirect evidence of merging."** Dilute, nitrogen-enriched gas is a natural signature of *any* evolved/star-forming region or chemical inhomogeneity; invoking the collision to explain it is double counting the premise.

5. **WR stars: "This suggests that Wolf–Rayet stars could be created by galaxy collision in the central region."** The text later concedes "we do not have the datum of the [Ne] 12.8 line to constrain the models" and that WR identification is indirect — "could be created by" is doing a lot of work over an unobserved population.

6. **§5: "in agreement with our models, the shock wave is … present throughout the galaxy"** (and repeated claims of shocks at 100–600 km s⁻¹ "throughout the NLR"). The model space is underconstrained enough that [OIII]/Hβ and [NI] degenerate with density, ionization parameter, T_* and cloud thickness; a match at ~20–50% tolerances does not localize physical shocks.

7. **Internal inconsistency on nitrogen**: Table 1 (m_pl) notes [NeV]/[NeIII]=12.8/42.4 (upper limit) is *best matched by "N/H twice solar,"* i.e. the fit quietly wants elevated N/H, which is then cited as merger evidence — but the paper treats N/H as two separate knobs (0.78 solar O/H, 2× solar N/H) without a chemical-evolution story, weakening the "record of collision" interpretation.

### What an adversarial reviewer would put in the first paragraph

*"The paper's central claim — that NGC 3393 is undergoing a merger — is asserted from the outset (two close central engines → 'revealed a merging event'), and the analysis is then structured to confirm it rather than to test it: a shock+photoionization grid is chosen precisely because shocks, starbursts and outflows are the *expected* signatures of a merger, and the models are then reported as 'consistent.' No non-merger null model is ever computed, so 'evidence of merging' is vacuous by construction. The two quantitative pillars on which the interpretation rests are methodologically untenable: (i) identifying line FWHM directly with shock velocity, conflating turbulence, rotation and outflows; and (ii) attributing a factor-10 density enhancement and unusual O/N abundances to 'the collision' on the strength of grids whose parameters (density, ionization parameter, T_*, cloud thickness) are strongly degenerate with the very line ratios used to fit them (tolerances of 20–50%). The authors' own caveat that the results are 'valid, but not definitive due to scarcity of data' under-admits the problem: with underconstrained models and a predetermined merger hypothesis, the paper demonstrates that a shock/star/AGN mixture can reproduce the spectra — which is of interest in itself — but does not demonstrate that a merger produced them. In that light the title and abstract overclaim the conclusions considerably."*

### Bottom line
A careful, self-aware paper that nonetheless overclaims its headline. The genuine contribution is the SUMA-based spectral synthesis of a double-engine Seyfert; the "evidence for merging" is not separable from the assumptions. Framing fixes (hedging the title/abstract, computing a non-shock/non-merger model, justifying FWHM↔V_s, and providing abundance-evolution motivation for the N/O pattern) are needed before "revealed" is defensible.

High NLR density, sub-solar O, low N, and high velocities are each *consistent* with a shock/merger interpretation, but none is unique to a merger; dense NLR clumps occur in quiescent and radio-galaxy NLRs, and shocks arise in radio outflows, starburst-driven winds, and AGN winds. The paper leans heavily on "consistent with" language. A discussion of competing explanations with some attempt at discrimination (e.g., expected velocity/density structure for a radio-driven vs. collisional shock) would strengthen the claim considerably. 5. **Presentation issues.** The paper is a v1 arXiv preprint in a submitted/published MNRAS format with placeholder fields ("Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc., 000, 1–?? (2000)"; "© 2009 RAS"; "© 2011 RAS" inconsistently). Several tables compress many models into single cells, making reproduction from the paper alone difficult; the full grid or code instructions should be available. 6. **Scope/impact.** This is a single-object, moderate-strength study. The physical picture is plausible but the quantitative novelty is limited (method from Contini 2009 applied to a new object). The connection to the genuinely interesting double-BH result is suggestive rather than causal.

## Verdict

A competent, internally consistent modelling study that adds useful quantitative descriptions (densities, velocities, metallicity, SED) of NGC 3393's NLR and a plausible — but not compelling — link to a recent merger event. The central "evidence of merging" overstates what the data and model discriminative power justify, and the paper would benefit from treating competing non-merger explanations more rigorously, from making the N/H assumption transparent, and from reporting uncertainties. It is a solid MNRAS-level contribution but not one that materially reshapes the field. Recommend minor revisions with a toned-down claim of "merger evidence."

Given each is a separate ionizing source, the spatial gradient of ionization you see in Fig. 2 (and the fact that two sources separated by ~25 pc would produce *two* ionization cones) is something your single-source assumption may wash out. Consider whether a two-source ionization field changes the [OIII]/Hβ map you're attributing to shocks.

5. **SED fit (Sect. 4, Fig. 3) is under-used.** You show the bremsstrahlung/reprocessed-IR and UV fits nicely *if* you use the best-line-ratio model — but the SED is actually a genuinely independent constraint (it does not enter the line-ratio grid). This is where your multi-wavelength consistency is most defensible. I'd elevate the SED: does the reprocessed-IR peak position *and* height prefer a particular Vₛ? That is, does the IR SED break the Vₛ degeneracy that the line ratios leave open? If it helps, it's your best quantitative merger-leaning result. If it doesn't, say so.

## What I'd actually ask you to do next

1. Build a **non-merger null model** (two separate AGN photoionizing the NLR, no collisional shock, no IGM mixing) and quantify how much worse — or as good — it is, *with error bars*. Your case for merging stands or falls on the *difference*.
2. **Vary E₀(B–V) within its error** and report the induced range in O/H and N/H. Decide if the "low O/H = merger" pillar survives.
3. Promote the **SED** to a discriminating test: does reprocessed-IR peak/height prefer a high-Vₛ shock component?
4. Replace "evidence" with the defensible claim — "consistent with collisional shocks from the Fabbiano double-BH merger and not easily excluded" — and put the *discriminating observations* (e.g. a [NeV] detection, a high-Vₛ shock line signature, or a spatially-resolved two-cone ionization pattern) in the conclusion. That turns a speculative narrative into a falsifiable one, which is more valuable to a reader than a confident-but-soft claim.

This is competent, honest, and physically thoughtful work — but right now it *assumes* the merger in its modelling and then *reproduces* features it built in, so the "evidence" is doing less work than the title suggests. Add the null-model comparison and the reddening sensitivity, and it becomes a genuinely stronger paper.
