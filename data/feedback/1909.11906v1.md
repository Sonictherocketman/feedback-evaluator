## [1909.11906v1-general-review] General Review


# Review of "χc1 decays into a pseudoscalar meson and a vector-vector molecule"
(Ikeno, Dias, Liang, Oset — arXiv:1909.11906v1, 27 Sep 2019)

## Argument of the paper

The paper studies the decays χc1 → η(η′,K⁻) + R, where R is one of the f₀(1370), f₀(1710), f₂(1270), f′₂(1525), K₂*⁰(1430). The methodological ingredients are:

1. **Production structure.** The χc1 (a c c̄, taken as an SU(3) singlet analogous to s s̄ being an isospin singlet) first produces a VVP (or admixed ⟨VV)⟨P⟩) configuration via SU(3) contractions of the vector-meson matrix V and pseudoscalar-meson matrix P. Explicit production weights h_i^(η,η′,K⁻) are derived (Eqs. 3–12), and the small admixture ⟨VV)⟨P⟩ enters through a single free parameter β (Eq. 13).

2. **Final-state interaction.** The two vectors interact in S-wave; a P-wave is required by parity at the production vertex (Eq. 16) which makes the rate ∝ p_η². The resonances are *inputs* to the FSI as vector-vector molecules described in refs. [16, 17]; their couplings g_i and loop functions G_i are taken from those references.

3. **Result.** The only experimentally accessible ratio is R₁ = B(χc1→η f₂(1270))/B(χc1→η′ f′₂(1525)) = 3.7–26 (PDG, huge error bar). The pure ⟨VVP⟩ hypothesis (β=0) gives R₁^th = 1.1±0.3, which misses the centroid (7.4). Fitting β = −0.28 recovers R₁^th = 7.1, and with that β the authors report six "predictions" R₂…R₇ (Eq. 27) plus an independent estimate of β from ratios of production weights (β ≈ −0.267), which is the headline of the work.

## Critical assessment

**Overall the physics is internally consistent and the bookkeeping is clean**, but the argument has several real weak spots that the paper does not fully defend.

### 1. The "predictions" are circular because of a single-ratio fit

The headline is "prediction of six more ratios." In practice the fit uses **only one parameter β and one measured ratio R₁ — which carries a factor ~7 experimental uncertainty (3.7–26 vs. centroid 7.4)**. The recovered β = −0.28 is therefore determined mostly by the (very wide) error bar of R₁. The authors acknowledge this explicitly ("With the caveat of large uncertainties…"). Consequently R₂…R₇ are essentially *interpolations* between the endpoints of the R₁ band, not independent predictions. R₁ is by construction reproduced once β is fixed, so there is no non-trivial success in the abstract's claim that the model "describes the experimental ratio." An honest re-statement would be: the model predicts *correlations* among the seven ratios once a single β is fixed, and R₃, R₄, R₆, R₇ are the *least* β-sensitive — those are the meaningful tests, and that is what should be highlighted as the experimental target rather than "reproduced R₁."

### 2. The β ≈ −0.3 is "estimated" from one observation, not predicted

The estimate of β ≈ −0.267 comes from the observation that f₂(1270) is mostly ρρ and f′₂(1525) is mostly K* K̄*, so the ratio of production weights has an extra factor of (1+β)/(1+(8/3)β) (Eq. 26). This is a post-hoc rationalisation of the same observation that motivated the ⟨VV)⟨P⟩ term, i.e. it re-uses the same input. A genuinely *a priori* estimate of ⟨VV)⟨P⟩ is missing; the paper should discuss whether SU(3) or chiral symmetry gives any reason for β~−1/4 in magnitude or sign, or whether β should be a function of the energy.

### 3. Couplings are taken from references whose method the authors' own footnote disputes

Footnote 1 cites refs. [18, 19] (Gülmez–Meißner–Oller 2017; Lu et al. 2018) who argue the methods of [16, 17] "cannot be extrapolated to the energies where the tensor states appear," and only then invokes [20, 21] as a fix. But the *numerical input* (all g_i, G_i in Table I) still comes from [16, 17]. The authors should be more explicit about which values differ between [16/17] and [20/21], and ideally show that R₁…R₇ are not sensitive to that choice. As it stands, the parametric uncertainty of β (or rather, of g_i) is folded into a flat 30 % error on every ratio, which is too coarse a statement given the very different relative errors (4 % on ρρ couplings for f₂(1270) vs. 12 % for K*K̄* in f′₂(1525)).

### 4. f₀(1370) is treated as a clean resonance near f₂(1270)

The ranges of g_i for f₀(1370) (e.g. (7913.62, −1048.34) MeV) already signal that the solution is not robust; f₀(1370) is a broad state strongly coupled to ππ and to the a₀(980) region. The model treats it as a single narrow resonance. In η f₀(1370) decays of the χc1, phase-space and ππ–ηη crosstalk in the loop function can matter, and a one-channel treatment is optimistic here. The paper should at least propagate the two solution branches separately instead of averaging (it does report ranges for the g_i in Table I, but R₃, R₄, R₇ are reported as single numbers "0.060/10.9" without the implied branch spread).

### 5. η–η′ mixing-angle and strong-phase systematics are not propagated

All h_i^(η,η′) weights depend on the η–η′ mixing angle (ref. [26], a 1992 measurement). The mixing angle has a non-negligible uncertainty (the "ideal mixing angle" is ≈ 39.7° or so), and R₁ is precisely the ratio of η to η′ rates — i.e. the mixing angle enters linearly into each side. The error analysis in the paper varies only g_i and G_i; the mixing angle and the relative strong phases between the S- and P-wave loops are not included. Given that R₁ is the single anchor ratio, this is a non-negligible blind spot.

### 6. Spin-structure shortcut

The paper justifies dropping the spin-0/spin-2 production factors by noting that ratios are taken between states of *the same* spin pair (f₀(1370) vs. f₀(1710), f₂(1270) vs. f′₂(1525)). This is fine **only** if, for a given V V pair, the spin-0 and spin-2 production amplitudes are exactly equal. They are not — the VVP amplitude in the SU(3) singlet is purely one structure (S-wave in V V, P-wave in V P), whereas the spin-2 amplitude from the same SU(3) contraction is generically different. The shortcut is more of a hope than a symmetry, and the paper should either (a) compute the spin-2 weights explicitly, or (b) bound the ratio |A(J=2)/A(J=0)| to show the ratios R₂…R₇ are robust. The R₁ discrepancy could plausibly be a large part of this neglected spin effect, in which case the need for a fit to ⟨VV)⟨P⟩ is partly an artefact of the spin approximation.

### 7. What the paper does get right

- The clean observation that h_{K̄*K*}^{(η)} = 0 for the pure ⟨VVP⟩ structure, while h_{K̄*K*}^{(η′)} ≠ 0, is a useful SU(3) counting result. The consequence — that η f₀(1710) and η f′₂(1525) should be very small if the f₀(1710)/f′₂(1525) are predominantly K*K̄* molecules, while η f₂(1270) should be large given the ρρ dominance of f₂(1270) — is the most *interesting and falsifiable* content of the paper, and it deserves to be promoted to the abstract. The currently reported null limits (η f₀(1710), η f′₂(1525) not seen in PDG) are consistent with this, but the paper should quantify "consistent": what upper bound does one need to rule out the molecular hypothesis for these specific states?
- Identifying six specific experimental ratios with predicted magnitudes (R₃ ≈ 0.06, R₄ ≈ 0.06, R₅ ≈ 4.6·10⁻⁴, R₆ ≈ 4.2–10.3, R₇ ≈ 1.1–10.9) is exactly the kind of concrete experimental target that makes a charmonium-molecule paper useful, and the authors do this well.

### 8. Minor issues

- Eq. (10): the symmetry factors from producing identical particles are folded into h_ρρ; the paper should verify that the 1/2 factor for η f₂(1270) (two ρ in the resonance) is accounted for consistently when comparing to [16, 17].
- Eq. (22) reports a PDG centroid of 7.4 from numbers 3.7–26; this is the *arithmetic* mean (not a probability-weighted mean), and the authors never note the difference.
- The figure 2 caption is missing the error band: "sensitive to the value of β, in particular for values around ∼−0.3" — the curves in Fig. 2 already show this but the paper does not put a band on them from the 30 % error.

## Bottom line

A competent, incremental paper in the established Oset–Molina–Geng charmonium-molecule programme. The SU(3) counting is clean, the motivation for measuring R₃…R₇ at experiment is genuine. But the central claim — "we reproduce R₁ and predict six more ratios" — overstates what the data can support: a single ratio with a factor-7 error bar fits a single free parameter, and the resulting "predictions" are largely interpolations in β. The spin-structure shortcut (Section 6) is the least-defended step in the argument and most likely to be challenged by a referee. The most valuable content of the paper — the *null* prediction for η f₀(1710) and η f′₂(1525) from pure ⟨VVP⟩ — is under-foregrounded.

## [1909.11906v1-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


I've read the full paper. Here is my assessment.

## Summary of the work

Ikemoto, Dias, Liang, and Oset study χ_c1 → η(η′, K̄⁻) + {f₀(1370), f₀(1710), f₂(1270), f₂′(1525), K̄₂*(1430)}, treating these resonances as vector–vector molecular states dynamically generated by V V final-state interaction (the hidden-local-gauge program of refs. 15–17). Assuming χ_c1 is an SU(3) singlet, they build the primary VVP production vertex from the SU(3) matrices, propagate large errors from the input couplings (g_i, G_i) in Table I, and—because only one ratio is measured—introduce a small admixture of the ⟨VV⟩⟨P⟩ structure (parameter β) to match the single data point R₁, then quote six "predictions."

## Strengths

- **Clean SU(3) bookkeeping.** Eqs. (3)–(12), with the symmetry/weight factors and the P-wave angular-momentum factor (16), are worked out carefully and consistently. The observation that h_{K⁺K̄⁻}^{(η)} = 0 in the ⟨VVP⟩ structure—and therefore f₀(1710)/f₂′(1525) are naturally suppressed in the η channel while η′ still produces them—gives a physically sensible explanation for the (non-)observation pattern.
- **Transparency about uncertainty.** The Monte Carlo propagation of the g_i, G_i errors, the explicit statement of the ~30% error on the ratios, and the honest framing that the model's predictive power is currently limited by the data are all to their credit.
- **Consistency check.** The independent estimate of β from Eq. (26) (β = −0.267) reproducing the fitted β = −0.28 is a nice self-check.
- The mechanism in Fig. 1 is correctly implemented, and the global constant C cancelling in the ratios is properly noted.

## Concerns (the core of my critique)

1. **The "prediction" framing is overstated.** The abstract claims "no free parameter" for R₁ at β = 0, but the authors immediately need β = −0.28, *fitted to that one ratio*, to reproduce R₁. So six of the seven quoted numbers are driven by a single parameter tied to a single (very imprecise) measurement. This is interpolation, not prediction. The abstract and conclusions would be more honest if they said so explicitly.

2. **The validation is weak: one ratio with a huge error bar.** R₁^data = 3.7–26 (centroid 7.4). The pure ⟨VVP⟩ value (1.1 ± 0.3) is below the band, but a value in [3.7, 26] is so wide that it is hard to regard the need for the admixture as a decisive discrimination. The β → −0.28 step reads a little like post-hoc tuning to sit inside a very forgiving window.

3. **The input couplings are the fragile link, and the error estimate may be optimistic.** Table I is the real worry. For f₀(1710), g(ρρ) = (−1029.91, 1086.85) MeV *crosses zero*, and the K⁺K̄* and φφ couplings are likewise sign-flipping double ranges; f₀(1370) has comparable spread; G_i for f₂(1270) φφ has >200% error. Any ratio involving f₀(1710) (R₃, R₄, R₇) is then barely determined by the inputs, and a blanket "~30%" on the final ratios looks small relative to inputs that span an order of magnitude and flip sign. These two f₀ states need a much larger individualized uncertainty, or their predicted ratios should be flagged as essentially uncontrolled.

4. **β fixes only the *magnitude* of one specific ⟨VV⟩⟨P⟩ flavor structure.** Eq. (11) assumes a particular flavor contraction for the admixture term (h′ ratios in Eq. 12: h′_ωω = 4 h_ωω = 4 h_ωω, etc.). But the product ⟨VV⟩⟨P⟩ is not forced to have that single decomposition; there is room for additional independent flavor structures. Presenting "one free parameter β" as the only knob is therefore an implicit assumption that deserves an explicit statement (and, ideally, a sensitivity test of the predicted ratios to alternative ⟨VV⟩⟨P⟩ structures).

5. **Scope: competing production pictures are not confronted.** No discussion of intermediate D D̄* or tetraquark mechanisms, nor of the tension noted in footnote 1 (refs. 18, 19 question the tensor-state method of 16, 17; resolved per 20–21). For the scalar f₀ resonances the molecular interpretation is itself contested, so the whole chain of "predictions" is downstream of two debatable claims (VV molecular nature + validity of 16,17 extraction). The authors should be more explicit that the predictions are conditional on the whole molecular picture being correct.

6. **Minor:** With the spin-0 vs spin-2 production factors deliberately dropped (rates grouped by same-spin VV), the model cannot connect the 0⁺ and 2⁺ families; that is acceptable for the within-family ratios chosen, but it should be emphasized that no cross-spin statement is being made.

## Bottom line

This is a competent, well-contained, internally consistent phenomenological study that carefully extends the authors' established hidden-local-gauge/virtual-V V framework to new χ_c1 channels. There are no errors I can flag in the formalism. Its scientific value is real but modest: it shows a qualitative SU(3) pattern for the (non-)production of the f₀/f₂ states and, with one fitted parameter, yields a handful of future-measurable ratios. Its main weakness is that the "predictions" rest on (i) a parameter fitted to a single, very uncertain ratio and (ii) input couplings—especially for f₀(1710)—so poorly constrained that a uniform 30% error is optimistic. If the authors (i) recalibrate the uncertainties on the f₀(1710)/f₀(1370) ratios, (ii) soften the "prediction"/"no free parameter" language, and (iii) state the assumption behind the particular ⟨VV⟩⟨P⟩ flavor structure, the paper would be considerably stronger. As it stands, it is a solid, sound contribution that should be accepted with these clarifications, not with them left to the reader.

## [1909.11906v1-grammar-review] Grammar Review


# Editorial review

I have read the paper as an editor, focusing on grammar, prose, and the clarity of the argument (set apart from its physics). The manuscript is a routine, competently structured phenology-paper: Introduction → Formalism → Results → Conclusions, with a single loop-coupling figure (Fig. 1) and one plotting figure (Fig. 2). For its class it reads well, but there are real rough patches that a copy-edit pass should clean up.

## What works
- The structure is conventional and the logical thread is easy to follow: build the SU(3) tensors, write the production weights, insert the loop, and read off the ratios. The reader is not lost.
- The abstract is tight and states the deliverables (one tested ratio, six predictions) clearly.
- Numbering of equations and cross-references are, as far as I can see, consistent, and the table (Table I) is well laid out.

## Grammatical and phrase-level problems
These are genuine errors, not style preferences, and should be corrected before submission:

1. **Run-on / fused sentence (p. 2).** "A mixture of the SU(3) trace ⟨VVP⟩ and ⟨V⟩⟨PP⟩ was used and it was shown to correspond to the same structures developed in [3] and [8] and **there it was used to describe successfully** the BESIII data…" — three coordinated "and"-clauses with an odd second "it was used"; restructure.
2. **Garbled subject (p. 8).** "**The fact that in the PDG we do not find branching ratios** for ηf₀(1710), ηf₂(1525) **would then find a natural explanation** in the dominance of the ⟨VVP⟩ structure." A fact cannot "find" an explanation. It should read, e.g., "The absence in the PDG of branching ratios for … would then be naturally explained by …"
3. **"renders … to" (p. 9).** "By using the values … we find the extra factor …, **which for β = −0.267 renders R₁ᵗʰ to the value of 7.1**." "Render to" is not English; use "gives R₁ᵗʰ = 7.1" or "yields the value 7.1."
4. **Adverb placement (p. 8).** "The K*K̄ pair is **not primary produced**" → "is not produced primarily."
5. **Number agreement (p. 8).** "…hence the f₀(1710) and f₂(1525) **resonance** would not be produced." Two resonances → "resonances."
6. **Fused/comma-spliced sentence (p. 9).** "We also find that β is different for different ratios **,** some increase with β and others decrease." The second clause is not grammatically joined to the first; rewrite as "…for some ratios the quantities increase with β while for others they decrease."
7. **Dangling "which" (p. 8).** "…the f₀(1710) and f₂(1525) can be produced, **which are indeed observed**." The relative pronoun ambiguously attaches to "the f₀…f₂" but the sentence reads as if it attaches to "can be produced"; split the sentence.
8. **Word order (p. 8).** "has no free parameter for this ratio **if we take first β = 0**" → "if we take β = 0 first."
9. **Wrong preposition (p. 8).** "**To** this evaluation it is also important to consider the theoretical errors" → "For this evaluation …"

## Style and consistency
- **Overuse of "indeed" / "A good laboratory to see …"** — the prose leans on loose, slightly colloquial openers ("A good laboratory to see such states…"). Tighten.
- **Repeated long sentences in the Introduction** that embed three or more clauses before the main verb (pp. 2–3); a copy-edit should break these.
- **Notation drift:** the paper alternates between *K*K̄, K̄K*, and K̄K*−bar for the same channel; settle on one. Likewise "vector-vector molecular states of [16, 17]" is a stray citation mid-list that reads like a leftover.
- **Article misuse (Acknowledgments):** "the hospitality of **the** Guangxi Normal University" — drop "the" before a proper name.
- **Missing commas after introductory clauses** recur ("in order to take into account the symmetry factor n!,…", "Before we proceed to include the contribution of the new term, …").

## Argument (as presented)
Set aside the physics, the *narrative* argument is coherent but thin. The paper hangs on one effectively-fitted parameter, β, which is adjusted to match the single measured ratio R₁. This is acknowledged honestly, but the writing could make the logical dependency clearer: several predicted ratios are "stable under the changes within the experimental uncertainties of R₁," yet Fig. 2 shows that *all* of R₁–R₇ swing strongly as β varies, which somewhat undercuts the "stability" claim as it is phrased. An editor would want the "stable/unstable" distinction spelled out in the text, not left to the reader of the figure.

## Bottom line
A readable, field-standard submission with a sound argument skeleton, but it carries a handful of genuine grammatical errors (items 1–7 above) and a set of style/consistency issues that would embarrass it in a polished journal. Recommend a round of professional copy-editing before publication: fix the broken sentences, settle the K*K̄ notation, and reconcile the "stable ratios" claim with Fig. 2.

## [1909.11906v1-critique] Critique


## Evaluation of Ikeno, Dias, Liang, Oset — *"χ_c1 decays into a pseudoscalar meson and a vector‑vector molecule"* (arXiv:1909.11906v1)

### Summary of the work
The authors extend the long‑standing "hidden‑gauge / dynamically generated hadronic molecule" program of the Valencia group to one unexplored branch: χ_c1 decays where a pseudoscalar P (η, η′, K⁻) is emitted as a **spectator** while a vector–vector (VV) pair is produced first and subsequently rescatters in the S‑wave to form a molecule R (f₂(1270), f₂′(1525), f₀(1370), f₀(1710), K₂*⁰(1430)). Treating χ_c1 as an SU(3) singlet, they build ⟨VVP⟩ invariants, derive the flavor production weights h_i^{(η,η′,K⁻)} (Eqs. 3–12), add a small admixture β⟨VV⟩⟨P⟩, and compute ratios of branching fractions. One ratio, R₁ = B(χ_c1→η f₂(1270))/B(χ_c1→η′ f₂′(1525)), is compared to PDG, and six further ratios are "predicted."

### What the paper does well
- **Clean, correct SU(3) bookkeeping.** The derivation of h_i^{(R)} with the 1/√n! symmetry factors (Eq. 10) and the relation between the singlet structure and the isospin states of [16,17] is done carefully, and the cancellation of the overall loop function G and of the unknown normalization C **in ratios** is a smart way to strip out two sources of uncertainty.
- **A genuinely qualitative success.** Because h_{K*K*}^{(η)} = 0 (the K*K* pair is not primary‑produced on the η side, Eq. 10), the model *predicts the absence* of χ_c1→η f₀(1710) and η f₂′(1525) — both indeed missing from the PDG — while η′ f₀(1710) and η′ f₂′(1525), with h_{K*K*}^{(η′)} = −√3/2, are allowed. This is a nice, testable, non‑trivial consequence of the spectator‑P structure (p. 8).
- **Reasonable error treatment** in principle: they propagate the (large) g_i, G_i input errors via Monte‑Carlo (Eq. 22→23) rather than just quoting a nominal value.

### Main weaknesses

1. **The method is a one‑parameter fit, and the "predictions" are not as robust as presented.** At β=0 (no admixture) the model gives R¹ᵗʰ = 1.1 ± 0.3, well below the experimental centroid of 7.4 over the range [3.7, 26]. They then *choose* β ≈ −0.28 to match 7.1. Every "prediction" R₂–R₇ therefore inherits the value of β fixed by a single, very poorly determined observable. The paper states that R₂–R₇ "are not so sensitive" to β and attaches a **flat ~30% error** to all of them (p. 9). This is not supported by their own Fig. 2, where R₁ moves by roughly a factor of ten across β ∈ [−0.3, 0] and several of R₂–R₇ vary by tens of percent over even the narrow β‑window near −0.3. The 30% error is an underestimate; assigning the same number to a strongly β‑dependent ratio and a weakly β‑dependent one is not defensible.

2. **The "estimate" of β (Eq. 26) is circular.** The analytical factor (1+β)/(1+(8/3)β)², tuned to reproduce the same R₁ = 7.1 (giving β = −0.267 ≃ −0.28), is a post‑hoc rationalization of the fit rather than an independent constraint. It confirms self‑consistency but adds no predictive power.

3. **The prediction chain rests on exactly the inputs the authors themselves flag as disputed.** Footnote 1 concedes that the f₂/f₂′ molecular couplings taken from [16,17] have been shown (in [18,19] and their own [20,21]) not to extrapolate reliably to the tensor‑state energies. Table I is blunt about the consequence: for f₀(1370) the coupling g_i spans (7913.62, −1048.34) MeV — i.e. its **sign is unconstrained** — and G_i errors reach 220%. Ratios built on f₀(1370) (R₃, R₄, R₇) are therefore essentially arbitrary, yet they are reported with the same nominal 30% error. This is the most serious internal inconsistency: the quantitative "predictions" in Eqs. 27 should be far more heavily caveated, or downgraded to order‑of‑magnitude guides.

4. **The one experimental test is statistically weak.** [3.7, 26] is a factor‑≈7 band. "Describing" it (abstract) overstates the achievement; the comparison neither discriminates the model nor pins down β. The paper is, in effect, a roadmap awaiting data the authors acknowledge as absent.

5. **Simplifications that deserve sharper justification.**
   - The claim that spin‑factors (VV spin 0 vs. 2) "are unnecessary" (p. 6) because only same‑spin ratios are compared is plausible but is asserted; it implicitly assumes the production rate for a spin‑0 and spin‑2 VV pair from the same vertex is identical after the C normalization cancels — this should be stated/proven, not waved away.
   - Only the g_i coupling is used for the f₂/f₂′ (tensor) channels; the additional b, d tensor couplings available in [16,17] are dropped without comment, a possible source of systematic error.
   - Phase/symmetrization conventions in Eq. 9 (−ρ⁺, ρ⁰, ρ⁻; −K*⁰, −K*⁻) are a little ad hoc; the "unit normalization" claim deserves an explicit line.

6. **No access to absolute rates.** Because C and G cancel, the framework can be tested only against *ratios*. If the absolute magnitudes of the measured branching ratios (Eqs. 19–21, all ~10⁻⁵) ever come in at orders different from the model's expectation, there is no knob to absorb it. This is a structural limitation, not just an experimental one.

### Overall judgment
This is a **competent, clearly written, internally consistent but low‑novelty phenomenology note**. It completes the last branch (P‑as‑spectator → VV molecule) of a well‑established machinery the collaboration has already applied in [15, 22, 23, 24]. The methodology is sound and free of fatal error, and the qualitative prediction of the *missing* η decays is a real (if modest) success. But the quantitative predictions are fragile: they hinge on a single free parameter β badly constrained by the data, on f₂/f₂′/f₀ couplings the authors themselves consider questionable, and on an error estimate (30%) that their own Fig. 2 contradicts. The impact is limited to "motivational" for future χ_c1 → P+R measurements, and those predictions will only be meaningful once β is independently pinned by additional data — which is precisely the loop the paper does not close.

In short: a correct continuation of a known program, with one nice qualitative result, whose quantitative claims are overstated relative to the actual uncertainties.

## [1909.11906v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Stated contribution (in my own words)

The authors study the two-body-plus-molecule decays of the χ_c1 (J^PC = 1++) into a light pseudoscalar P = η, η′, or K⁻ plus one of the "vector–vector molecular" resonances f₀(1370), f₀(1710), f₂(1270), f₂′(1525), K₂\*⁰(1430). The mechanism is: χ_c1 first produces a primary VVP configuration (in which P is a spectator, built from SU(3) vector and pseudoscalar meson matrices using the assumption that c̄c is an SU(3) singlet, analogous to s̄s being an isospin singlet); the two vectors V_i V_i′ then undergo final-state interaction via the previously established coupled-channel machinery, which dynamically generates the resonance. A subleading ⟨VV⟩⟨P⟩ vertex with strength β is added as an admixture. The amplitudes are t_R^(P) = Σ_i h_i^(P) G_i(M_R) g_{R,ii′}, where the SU(3) Clebsch weights h_i^(P) are derived fresh for this channel, while the resonance couplings g_i and loop functions G_i are imported from the prior VV-molecule papers (refs. [16, 17]). The result is seven branching-ratio predictions: they fit the ratio R₁ = B(χ_c1→η f₂(1270))/B(χ_c1→η′ f₂′(1525)) = [3.7–26] (PDG) by choosing β ≈ −0.28 (the pure ⟨VVP⟩ limit, β=0, gives only 1.1), and then predict six further ratios R₂–R₇ for measurement at BESIII/Belle-type experiments. ## Understanding of the Paper's Logical Argument

The paper tackles $\chi_{c1}$ decays into a pseudoscalar meson ($\eta$, $\eta'$, or $K^-$) plus one of several exotic resonances ($f_2(1270)$, $f_2'(1525)$, $f_0(1370)$, $f_0(1710)$, $K_2^{*0}(1430)$). The logical chain proceeds as follows:

**1. SU(3) Singlet Production Vertex.**
The $\chi_{c1}$ ($\bar{c}c$ state) is assumed to be an SU(3) flavor singlet — exactly analogous to the $\bar{s}s$ being an isospin singlet. Therefore the production vertex must be an SU(3) invariant built from the vector ($V$) and pseudoscalar ($P$) meson matrices. The dominant structure (established in prior work on $J/\psi$ and $\chi_{c1}$ processes) is $\langle VVP \rangle$ — a product of three SU(3) matrices contracted into a singlet.

**2. Molecular Resonance Assumption.**
The tensor and scalar resonances in the final state are assumed to be *dynamically generated* from vector-vector ($VV$) final-state interactions (refs. [16, 17]). Their couplings $g_{Ri}$ to each $VV$ channel ($\rho\rho$, $\omega\omega$, $\phi\omega$, $K^*\bar{K}^*$) and the loop functions $G_i(M_R)$ are taken as inputs from those prior studies (Table I).

**3. Amplitude and P-Wave.**
The decay amplitude for channel $\eta$ (and similarly $\eta'$, $K^-$) is:
$$t_R^{(\eta)} = \sum_i h_i^{(\eta)} G_i(M_R)\, g_{Ri}$$
where $h_i^{(\eta)}$ are Clebsch-Gordan production weights from the SU(3) algebra. Since the $\chi_{c1}$ has $J^{PC} = 1^{++}$, the pseudoscalar must be emitted in a **P-wave**, contributing a $|\vec{p}_\eta|$ factor. The decay width picks up a $p_\eta^2$ (P-wave phase space).

**4. SU(3) Selection Rule → Physical Prediction.**
A central result is the observation that $h_{K^*\bar{K}^*}^{(\eta)} = 0$: the $\eta$ cannot be produced with a $K^*\bar{K}^*$ pair from the pure $\langle VVP \rangle$ structure. Since $f_0(1710)$ and $f_2'(1525)$ couple dominantly to $K^*\bar{K}^*$, the decays $\chi_{c1} \to \eta\, f_0(1710)$ and $\chi_{c1} \to \eta\, f_2'(1525)$ are **forbidden** at leading order. This provides a natural SU(3) explanation for the absence of these channels in the PDG.

**5. One Free Parameter $\beta$ and Matching to Data.**
A small admixture of the $\langle VV\rangle\langle P\rangle$ structure (with coefficient $\beta$) is introduced to allow some $K^*\bar{K}^*$-related $\eta$ production and to improve the one data point that exists:
$$R_1 = \frac{\mathcal{B}(\chi_{c1}\to\eta f_2(1270))}{\mathcal{B}(\chi_{c1}\to\eta' f_2'(1525))}$$
- With $\beta = 0$: $R_1^{th} = 1.1 \pm 0.3$
- PDG: $R_1 = [3.7,\; 26]$ (centroid $\approx 7.4$)
- With $\beta = -0.28$: $R_1^{th} = 7.1$ (matches the centroid)

**6. Predictions for Six New Ratios.**
Fixing $\beta = -0.28$, the paper predicts six additional ratios ($R_2$–$R_7$) that have no experimental data yet, along with an estimated $\sim 30\%$ theoretical uncertainty.

## Feedback

### Strengths

1. **Clean physical insight from the SU(3) algebra.** The vanishing of $h_{K^*\bar{K}^*}^{(\eta)}$ giving a natural explanation for the absence of $\eta\,f_0(1710)$ and $\eta\,f_2'(1525)$ in the PDG is a genuinely satisfying piece of structure. It connects a group-theoretic selection rule to an experimental null result without any adjustable parameter.

2. **Ratio-based methodology eliminates the unknown normalization.** By working exclusively in ratios of branching fractions, the overall vertex strength $C$ cancels. This is the right strategy when the absolute scale of the production vertex is unknown.

3. **P-wave treatment is well motivated.** The argument that the $1^{++}\to 0^{-+} + R$ transition requires the pseudoscalar to carry the orbital angular momentum ($L=1$), and that this introduces channel-dependent $p_\eta^2$ factors, is correct and important for distinguishing $\eta$ and $\eta'$ rates.

4. **Well-written and clearly organized.** The formalism is compact, the logical flow from algebra to amplitudes to physical predictions is easy to follow, and the one new ingredient ($\beta$) is cleanly separated from the established framework.

### Weaknesses and Concerns

1. **The "validation" is extremely thin.** The single datum used to fix $\beta$—$R_1$—has an experimental range of $[3.7, 26]$, roughly a factor of 7. Over that full range, $\beta$ could vary from close to 0 (at the low end, since $R_1^{th}(\beta=0) \approx 1.1$ which is actually *below* 3.7) to roughly $-0.3$ or beyond. The paper picks $\beta = -0.28$ to match the centroid, but does **not** propagate the experimental uncertainty on $R_1$ into an uncertainty on $\beta$ or onto the six predicted ratios. Given the shape of Fig. 2 (ratios vary by factors of 2–10 over $\beta \in [-0.30, 0]$), this is a significant omission. The "predictions" should really be given as *ranges* over the allowed $\beta$ interval.

2. **The spin-factor simplification is under-discussed.** The paper states that the $VV$ pair can be produced in spin 0 or 2, but then says this is "unnecessary" to include because "we only compare rates for $VV'$ states with the same spin." This is only partially correct. While comparing $f_0(1370)$ (0⁺) to $f_0(1710)$ (0⁺) and $f_2(1270)$ (2⁻) to $f_2'(1525)$ (2⁻) does cancel *overall* spin-0 vs. spin-2 factors, within a *single* resonance the relative weights of the different $VV$ channels depend on whether the $VV$ pair is in $J=0$ or $J=2$ configuration. The loop functions $G_i$ also differ for the two angular-momentum configurations. A more careful treatment would decompose the amplitude into spin channels and show that the result is insensitive to the relative spin-0/spin-2 content. The paper should at least argue this more explicitly, or provide an estimate of the effect.

3. **Coupling uncertainties in Table I are large.** For $f_0(1370)$ and $f_0(1710)$, the couplings $g_i$ have ranges of several hundred to over 1000 MeV, and the loop functions carry 42% errors in some channels. A more honest error analysis would use the same Monte-Carlo random-number procedure the authors apply to $R_1$.

4. **The $\beta$ determination is essentially a one-parameter fit to one (very uncertain) number.** There is no independent constraint on $\beta$. The $\langle VV\rangle\langle P\rangle$ admixture was also studied in refs. [3, 8] for $J/\psi$ decays, where values of $\beta$ (or equivalent) were found to be small. The paper does not connect the $\beta = -0.28$ it finds here to those earlier determinations, which would significantly strengthen the result. A consistency check against the known $\beta$ from $J/\psi \to \phi(\omega) f_0 f_0$ or $\chi_{c1} \to \phi h_1(1380)$ processes is missing.

5. **Limited discrimination power.** Several of the predicted ratios ($R_2 = 0.16$, $R_3 = 0.060$, $R_5 = 4.6 \times 10^{-4}$) are small, meaning they correspond to absolute branching fractions of order $10^{-5}$ or less. Measuring these will be experimentally challenging at current and near-future B-factory facilities (BESIII, SuperKEKB). The paper is strongest on $R_6$ and $R_7$ (order 10), but these involve $K^*$-related channels that may be harder to reconstruct. The practical experimental prospects should be discussed more carefully.

6. **The assumption of pure molecular nature is not stress-tested.** All five resonances are assumed to be *entirely* vector-vector molecules. If, say, the $f_0(1710)$ has a partial $q\bar{q}$ or tetraquark component (which would alter its $K^*\bar{K}^*$ coupling relative to the molecular estimate), the predictions would shift. The paper acknowledges the molecular picture in the conclusions ("the underlying hypothesis that the resonances considered are indeed dynamically generated from the $VV$ interaction") but does not quantify the sensitivity.

### Minor Points

- Footnote 1 provides good context about the ongoing debate on tensor-state generation (Gümeß/Meißner/Oller vs. Geng/Molina/Oset), but this caveat should be repeated in the discussion section where the $f_2$ and $f_2'$ results are interpreted, since the conclusions rest heavily on those specific molecular structures.
- The phase convention for the isospin states (Eq. 9) uses $(-\rho^+, \rho^0, \rho^-)$, which is non-standard (typically $(\rho^+, \rho^0, -\rho^-)$ or with a CG-sign convention). This does not affect physics but could confuse readers cross-checking the algebra.
- The paper could benefit from a brief discussion of the $\chi_{c1} \to K^- K_2^{*+}(1430)$ mode's feasibility, since the $K^-$ is soft and the reconstruction of $K_2^*$ via $K\pi$ might be challenging.

### Summary

This is a well-motivated, cleanly written application of the authors' established SU(3) + molecular-resonance framework to a new set of $\chi_{c1}$ decays. The SU(3) selection rule $h_{K^*\bar{K}^*}^{(\eta)} = 0$ and its connection to observed null results is the paper's most satisfying element. However, the quantitative content is limited: one very-uncertain data point fixes the single free parameter, spin factors are dropped with hand-waving, coupling uncertainties are somewhat understated, and the predictions lack error bars that properly reflect the $\beta$-induced sensitivity shown in Fig. 2. The work is competent and the predictions are reasonable, but neither the theoretical apparatus nor the experimental constraint is strong enough for the paper to make a decisive statement about the vector-vector molecular nature of these states.

4. **Reliance on the VV-molecule hypothesis and on Refs. [16, 17].** The whole enterprise presupposes that f₀(1370), f₀(1710), f₂(1270), f₂′(1525), K₂*⁺⁰(1430) are VV molecules. This is an assumption, not a conclusion, and the footnoted debate about the production of the tensor states [18–21] shows it is nontrivial. The model is only as good as this structural hypothesis, and the conclusions do not clearly separate "test of the production vertex" from "test of the molecular interpretation."

5. **Scope and novelty.** The formalism is an extension of a well-established program (Refs. [3–22]); the new ingredient (VV → vector-vector molecule with P as spectator) is a natural variant on the authors' own earlier work [15, 22]. It is a solid incremental addition to the program rather than a conceptual advance.

6. Minor: the ηη′ mixing convention and the treatment of the C-parity of the χc1 could be stated with a bit more care; the global constant C being "cancelled in the ratios" is stated but the fact that different ratios share different subsets of C (so the ratio is genuinely C-free only because C multiplies every amplitude identically) deserves one explicit sentence.

## Overall judgment

A careful, technically sound but incremental paper. It extends the authors' established hidden-gauge molecular framework to a new but closely related decay channel, and its principal value is the set of ratios it predicts and the qualitative prediction of suppressed ηf₀(1710)/ηf₂′(1525) modes. Its principal limitations are that (i) the one available data point is fit rather than predicted, (ii) the six "predictions" are dominated by the unconcerted uncertainty in β, and (iii) it rests entirely on the (unproven) VV-molecular interpretation of the scalar/tensor resonances. It is well written and would be of interest to the hadron-spectroscopy community, but it is not a strong standalone contribution and of limited impact beyond the field.

**Recommendation:** Accept in minor form, provided the authors temper the "no free parameter"/"test of the formalism" language and propagate the β-uncertainty into the R₂–R₇ predictions. Originality is modest (incremental extension of existing work); overall soundness is good; impact is limited.

## What the paper actually does

- Models χc1 → P (P = η, η′, K⁻) + R where R is one of the vector–vector molecular states f0(1370), f0(1710), f2(1270), f2′(1525), K̄2*0(1430).

## The genuine strengths

1. **The ratios cancel the unknown strong-decay normalisation** — this is the right thing to do and it is stated clearly.
2. **A real physical insight: the natural suppression of ηf0(1710) and ηf2′(1525).** Because h_{K̄K*}^{(η)} = 0 in the pure `<VVP>` structure (Eq. 10) while the f0(1710)/f2′(1525) couple dominantly to K̄K*, the `<VVP>` structure *predicts* these should be absent, whereas η′f0(1710), η′f2′(1525) survive. 3. Careful bookkeeping of the η–η′ mixing, the isospin decomposition, the P-wave requirement from angular momentum/parity, and the phase-space factor |p̄_η|².
4. Honest error analysis — you propagate the input uncertainties instead of hiding them.

## The weaknesses I would not let you gloss over

**1. You are fitting one parameter to one number and calling the rest "predictions."** This is the central methodological problem. The entire result depends on β, and β = −0.28 is *chosen to reproduce the single available ratio R1*, which itself has an experimental range of **[3.7, 26] — a factor of ~7**. The conclusion that these are predictions "that can be tested" is over-stated given that the theory is pinned down by one badly-measured datum. You should say this plainly in the abstract/conclusion rather than the current soft "the experimental situation should improve" phrasing.

**2. The "small admixture" is not small.** In §II you introduce `<VV><P>` as a "small admixture" of the dominant `<VVP>` term, but the value needed is β ≈ −0.3, i.e. a **~30% admixture with opposite sign**. That is a substantial correction, not a perturbation. Either relax the "small" language or justify why a 30% subleading structure is acceptable. Note the negative sign is also not qualitatively obvious — it is essentially a fitting freedom.

**3. The whole thing stands on contested input.** Table I's g_i and G_i are taken from [16, 17] (Molina–Meißner–Oller; Geng–Oset). Your own footnote 1 concedes that the very method used to *generate the tensor states* (f2, f2′) has been questioned by an independent group [18, 19], and you point at your own rebuttals [20, 21]. I understand the internal logic, but as a reviewer I note: **your predictions are downstream of a disputed framework.** The wide ranges on the f0(1370)/f0(1710) couplings in Table I (e.g. g_{ρρ}(f0(1370)) = (7913, −1048) MeV — note the sign ambiguity and an order-of-magnitude range) show how little is actually pinned down. **4. The SU(3)-singlet assumption for a *hidden-charm* state needs defending.** You write that "a c c̄ state can be considered as an SU(3) singlet, in the same way that an s s̄ state is an isospin singlet." The s s̄ → isospin-singlet analogy is itself loose, and for χc1 the strong decay to three light mesons goes through c c̄ annihilation (gluons), so you are really assuming the effective annihilation vertex has a pure flavour-SU(3)-singlet (trace) structure. The paper's own value is therefore somewhat speculative. I would temper "we hope to test the predictions in the future" — realistically the near-term testable subset may be much smaller than six.
