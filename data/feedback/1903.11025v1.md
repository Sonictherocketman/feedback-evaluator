## [1903.11025v1-general-review] General Review


# Review: "Fully spin-polarized nodal loop semimetals in alkaline-metal monochalcogenide monolayers" (arXiv:1903.11025v1)

## Summary

The paper proposes a family of two-dimensional ferromagnetic alkaline-metal monochalcogenide monolayers (MX; M = Li–Cs; X = S, Se, Te) and, with CsS as the prototype, predicts a **nodal-loop half-metal (NLHM)** in which the two crossing bands at E_F carry the *same* spin. The nodal loop is protected by the horizontal mirror ℳ_z and, unlike non-magnetic 2D NLSs, survives SOC when the magnetization is out-of-plane. Rotating the spin converts the ground state into a Chern-number-±1 QAH insulator (intermediate angles) or a "Weyl-like" two-node semimetal (in-plane magnetization). QAH can alternatively be reached by one-hole doping or by isoelectronic substitution (CsP, CsAs), and quantized Kerr/Faraday responses are proposed as detection handles. Methods: VASP DFT+SOC, DFPT phonons, MD, Wannier-projected TKNN/AHC calculations, a fitted 3-band k·p-like lattice model, and thin-film optics.

## Strengths

1. **Genuinely nice protection mechanism.** Because spin points along z, ℳ_z remains an exact magnetic symmetry *even with SOC*, and opposite ℳ_z parities of the crossing p_{x,y}/p_z bands forbid hybridization. Same-spin (fully spin-polarized) crossings with a ~3.8 eV gap in the minority channel make this an attractive spin-filtering platform in principle.
2. **Clean symmetry bookkeeping.** The magnetic point-group table (Table 1) and the mapping θ → NLHM / QAH / Weyl-like / trivial is organized, and the argument that the trivial insulating phases have 𝒯ℳ-symmetries forcing C = 0 is correct and nicely distinguishes their system from the LaCl-type QAH proposal of Liu et al.
3. **Effort beyond a bare band plot.** Formation energies across the whole 15-member family, phonon spectra, MD, phonon checks for CsP/CsAs, a minimal SOC-inclusive lattice model that reproduces the DFT bands, and quantized AHC on a 3000×3000 mesh are all done properly. The MO Kerr/Faraday quantization (θ_K ≈ −π/2, θ_F ≈ Cα) is a useful, concrete experimental handle.
4. **Honesty about concurrency.** The "Note added" on MnN and PtCl3 NLHMs is appreciated, though it deserves more than two sentences.

## Major problems

1. **The flagship phase is not the ground state, per the authors' own data — and this is never confronted.** The MAE fit MAE(θ) = 1.32·cos²θ − 0.04·cos⁴θ meV is positive for all out-of-plane θ, i.e. **CsS has an in-plane (x-axis) easy axis**, as the paper itself states ("The positive value of MAE indicates a preferred magnetization along the x-axis"). Consequently the nodal-loop half-metal, the subject of the abstract, only exists when the spin is pinned or field-aligned out-of-plane; the *equilibrium* phase of CsS at θ = 90° is the Weyl-like semimetal. Anisotropy-field estimates are absent, but they are easy: B ≈ ΔE/μ_B ≈ 1.28 meV/μ_B ≈ 20 T for CsS, and using the family's MAE values (LiSe 5.9 meV/cell, LiTe 8.38 meV/cell) the NLHM candidates would need **~100–145 T**. Tens of Tesla are not a realistic operating regime, so the "excellent materials platform" claim for the NLHM cannot stand as written. Either reframe the NLHM as a field-stabilized/metastable phase (and say so in the abstract), or reconsider whether the sign convention in the MAE table was swapped. The paper sidesteps this by citing that "the magnetization direction can be tuned by an external field" (Fe3GeTe2 example), with no numbers.
2. **No estimate of the magnetic ordering temperature.** The Mermin–Wagner theorem is cited to motivate the question, but never quantified: no spin-wave/Sansov/DMFT Tc estimate for a 1 μB/cell monolayer with ~1 meV anisotries. For CsS the easy axis is *in-plane*, which maximizes fluctuations (XY-like) and plausibly lowers T_C further. Classical MD tests only structural, not magnetic, stability, so the paper cannot support its spintronics motivation.
3. **Internal inconsistency in the MD/thermal-stability claim.** The main text says the honeycomb lattice "maintains up to 100 K," the SI caption (Fig. S1) says the runs are at **50 K and 100 K**, but the actual plot labels in Fig. S1 read **"T = 500K" and "T = 1000K."** At least two of these are wrong, a basic reproducibility problem in a feasibility claim.
4. **The protection is environment-fragile and this is unaddressed.** The nodal loop is protected by the *horizontal* mirror (the monolayer plane itself) — exactly the symmetry broken by a substrate, an overlayer, or a second layer, and (unlike in their FM case) by SOC in any slab asymmetry. Real growth will be freestanding-on-something; no discussion of substrate effects, interlayer coupling, or strain/disorder appears anywhere. The QAH routes (doping, CsP/CsAs) are less fragile and arguably should be the headline for that reason.
5. **Family claim overstated relative to Fig. S2.** Only 5 of 15 MX monolayers (LiS, LiSe, LiTe, NaSe, NaTe) host the crossing at E_F; the K/Rb/Cs rows of the figure mostly do not, even though the introduction presents the whole family as the platform. The sign of the MAE also flips across the family (CsAs −9.07 meV, strong out-of-plane easy axis — consistent *with* its intrinsic-QAH claim, but making the family's phase diagram more complicated than the text suggests). An explicit tabulation of "candidate → equilibrium phase (from MAE sign) → phase at forced out-of-plane spin" would clarify what is real.

## Minor problems

- **"Weyl-like" is overloaded**: Fig. 2(c) calls the nodal *loop* "Weyl-like (twofold-degenerate)," while the in-plane nodal *points* are the "Weyl-like semimetal" of the title claim. Distinguish the terminology.
- **Group labels**: the text says the in-plane nodal-point phase has "the group m′m/2 at θ = nπ/3," but Table 1 lists m′m′2′ at φ = 60°. Reconcile.
- **Eq. (4)**: "ℏω is the phonon energy" should be *photon* energy.
- **Reference list numbering is corrupted**: after (60) the list jumps to "(9) Maciejko …" and "(8) Tse …" before resuming at (63).
- **QAH temperature scale never stated**: 86 meV ≈ 1 K (158 meV ≈ 2 K) gaps; the paper should compare against CrI3, MnBi2Te4, Cr2Ge2Te6 QAH gaps explicitly when making the device case.
- **"In terminoloy of Ref. [10]"** typo; Fig. 3(c)/Fig. 5(a) gap labels are hard to parse (49 meV + 86 vs. 158 meV) — define which gap is the QAH gap in the caption.
- The one-hole-per-cell doping (3.52×10¹⁴ cm⁻²) is asserted "achievable by current experimental techniques" — a sentence on how (electrostatic gating vs. chemical substitution; spatial uniformity; disorder) would be needed, since impurities could pin the Fermi level inside the 49–86 meV scale.
- The concurrency note should be expanded into a comparison (MnN/PtCl3: easy axis, protecting symmetry, predicted Tc, gaps) since novelty is partly a function of that distinction.
- Suggest reporting NLHM transport signatures (chiral hinge/Fermi-arc currents in the finite-flake limit) — currently the "half-metal" has no predicted measurable current signature beyond AHC of the gapped variants.

## Verdict

Well-executed computations organized around a clever symmetry argument, but the central conclusion is undermined by the authors' own magnetization-anisotropy result: in none of the tabulated NLHM candidates does the equilibrium magnetization point out of plane, and forcing it there would cost tens to a hundred tesla of field. Combined with the missing Tc estimate, the MD-temperature inconsistency, and the unaddressed substrate fragility of the horizontal-mirror protection, the "excellent materials platform" claim is ahead of the evidence. The paper would be substantially stronger (and more honest) reframed around (i) the in-plane Weyl-like ground state, (ii) the field-stabilized NLHM with explicit anisotropy-field numbers, and (iii) the more robust intrinsic-QAH route (CsP/CsAs, doped CsS) as the practical channel, plus a rough Tc estimate.

**Scores:** Originality — solid combination of known ingredients (same-spin nodal loop, SOCsustained by ℳ_z, field-tunable NLHM/QAH/Weyl-like diagram, MO-quantized detection) in a newly proposed ionic-material family, partly shadowed by concurrent 2D NLHM work → 3. Overall — sound methodology and internally mostly consistent, but a headline claim contradicted by the paper's own data going undiscussed, plus the inconsistencies noted above → 3. Impact — interesting but currently low practical leverage (unsynthesized chemically exotic 2D ionic sheets, high-field requirement, unestimated Tc, ~1 K QAH scale) → 2.

## [1903.11025v1-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


# Review of "Fully spin-polarized nodal loop semimetals in alkaline-metal monochalcogenide monolayers" (Zhou et al., arXiv:1903.11025v1)

## Summary
Using PBE-GGA DFT plus a 3-orbital effective lattice model, the authors screen the 15 alkaline–metal monochalcogenide monolayers $MX$ ($M=$ Li–Cs, $X=$ S, Se, Te, planar honeycomb) for topological phases. The central result: with out-of-plane magnetization the two lowest spin-down $p$-derived bands cross along a mirror-protected nodal loop that *survives SOC*, because $\mathcal{M}_z$ remains a good symmetry of the ferromagnet with $M\parallel z$. Rotating the magnetization maps the system to a Chern-$\pm1$ QAH phase (also obtainable by hole doping / substituting S→P,As) and to a Weyl-like point-node phase with in-plane $M$. QAH is characterized electrically (Kubo AHC) and optically (quantized Kerr $=-\pi/2$, Faraday $=C\alpha$). A family of candidates is identified as LiS, LiSe, LiTe, NaSe, NaTe.

## Strengths

1. **The core symmetry insight is real and is the paper's actual novelty.** The non-trivial step is that in a *ferromagnet* with out-of-plane magnetization $\mathcal{M}_z$ is preserved even with SOC (unlike the non-magnetic SOC-killed nodal loops), and the opposite $\mathcal{M}_z$ parity of the two crossing bands blocks $p_{x,y}$–$p_z$ hybridization. This is stated clearly, supported by Wannier parities (Fig. 2d), and is the mechanism that makes the nodal loop a "half-metallic" nodal loop rather than just an accidental crossing. The effective model (Eqs. 15–20) then reproduces DFT and independently yields the same three-way phase classification — a genuinely useful cross-check, not decoration.

2. **Systematic screening.** Going across the full $5\times3$ family with formation energies, phonon spectra, MAE fits ($K_1,K_2$), and relativistic band structures (Fig. S2, Table S1) is the right level of systematic work for a materials-proposal paper, and it distinguishes the NLHM candidates from the rest of the table honestly (only 5 of 15 qualify).

3. **The magnetization-tunable phase diagram is a nice, compact result.** The ISOTROPY-based magnetic-point-group table (Table 1) plus the Chern-vs-$\theta$ map (Fig. 3b) give a clean, reproducible logic: NLHM at $\theta=\pi$, Weyl-like (mixed Weyl nodes of type-(i)) at $\theta=\pi/2$ under $\mathcal{M}_x$, and a gapped $\mathcal{TC}_{2y}$-broken QAH insulator in between. The connection to prior work (Liu et al. QAH; Niu/Mokrousov mixed Weyl) is made appropriately.

4. **An optical probe is proposed**, not just a transport one (Kerr/Faraday, Fig. 5), which is a sensible addition since Kerr/Faraday microscopy is a standard tool for 2D magnetic materials.

## Weaknesses / points needing attention

1. **The NLHM is a metastable state, and this is underplayed.** By their own MAE fit, $\mathrm{MAE}(\theta)=1.32\cos^2\theta-0.04\cos^4\theta$ meV is *maximal* along $z$ and *zero* in-plane; i.e. the natural ground state is **in-plane**, and the nodal-loop phase they build the paper around requires pinning the moment out-of-plane. They note the direction can be tuned by field (à la Fe$_3$GeTe$_2$) but never quantify the required field or compare its cost to $T_F$. A reader cannot tell whether "out-of-plane" is a few-T field or an impractical one. This should be front-and-center, buried only implicitly.

2. **Small anisotropy ⇒ the practical claims are unproven.** The max MAE (1.28 meV/cell for CsS) is below CrI$_3$ (1.37) and Fe$_3$GeTe$_2$ (2.76), and Table S1 shows *several* members (LiTe, KTe, RbTe, CsTe, CsAs, CsP) have **negative** MAE (in-plane preferred). A 2D ferromagnet with ~1 meV anisotropy has no reason to order above low temperature; no Curie-temperature estimate (e.g. via $k$-MC or the mean-field $T_C\propto J$) is given, so "excellent platform for spintronics" is aspirational rather than demonstrated.

3. **Thermal-stability evidence is weak.** The MD (Fig. S1c,d) is run only to **100 K**. "The planar lattice maintains up to 100 K" is a low bar — 100 K is below the temperature the ferromagnet is even expected to exist, so structural and magnetic stability are conflated. A room-temperature run would substantially strengthen (or falsify) the feasibility story.

4. **Functional dependence of every topological number is unaddressed.** These are PBE-GGA results. The QAH gap is quoted as **86 meV** after single-hole doping — PBE underestimates gaps, so this is likely *underestimated*, but the *existence/size* of the gap and hence the robustness of $C=\pm1$ are functional- and k-mesh-dependent and should be at least partially checked (HSE06 or RPA, or a $k$-mesh convergence statement). The large 3.79 eV spin gap is PBE-typical but again untested. No self-interaction / $d$/$p$-localization concern is discussed for the 1 $\mu_B$-per-cell moment.

5. **The "formation energy" is not a stability criterion.** $E_f=0.27$ eV/atom is just the energy of the 2D layer above its bulk — normal for any exfoliated layer and not evidence of *synthesizability*. The comparison to "ZnO 0.19, silicene 0.76" is apples-to-oranges (silicene's large value is relative to its bulk, and it needed a Si substrate to form). A relative formation energy with respect to the constituent elements / competing phases, or a phonon check of *all* candidates (not only CsS, CsP, CsAs), would be the honest test. Right now the NLHM candidates are shown to be phonon-stable only for CsS.

6. **Minor points.** (a) The Faraday signal $C\alpha\approx2.3^\circ$ (for $C=1$) is very small — the "quantized" Faraday platform may be below experimental noise; the authors should acknowledge detection sensitivity. (b) Fig. S2's identification of LiS/LiSe/LiTe/NaSe/NaTe as NLHMs by "eye-balling" band crossings is not backed by explicit $\mathcal{M}$-parity / Berry-curvature checks for each — the full family deserves at least the parity check done for CsS. (c) Extracting a planar monolayer by relaxing the (111) slab of a *buckled* bulk is a somewhat constructed structure; a direct 2D global-structure search (e.g. whether a buckled ground state is lower) would reinforce the case.

## Verdict

This is a solid, well-executed prediction paper whose **central physics (SOC-stable, fully spin-polarized, mirror-protected nodal loop; magnetization-direction-controlled NLHM/QAH/Weyl phase diagram in a screened family) is credible and clearly argued**, and it is well positioned within the 2018–19 wave of 2D topological-ferromagnet proposals. The effective-model cross-validation and the proposed electrical + optical probes are genuine added value. However, the **practical claims — synthesizability, thermal stability, and that the NLHM/QAH are actually reachable at usable temperature and field — are not demonstrated**: NLHM sits in the metastable out-of-plane direction, anisotropy is small, MD stops at 100 K, all topological numbers are PBE-only, and only a subset of candidates is phonon-checked. As a *materials platform* the case is therefore "candidate, pending verification," not "excellent platform" as the abstract claims. It is a respectable advance rather than a field-shaping result, and should be revised to move the metastability/field/functional-dependence caveats from footnote-level to first-class.

## [1903.11025v1-critique] Critique


I've read the full main text (13 pages plus the figure set). Below is my critical evaluation.

# Critique: "Fully spin-polarized nodal loop semimetals in alkaline-metal monochalcogenide monolayers" (arXiv:1903.11025v1)

## What the paper does

Using PBE-level DFT plus a 3-orbital tight-binding model, the authors study a 15-member family of hypothetical alkaline-metal monochalcogenide (MX, M = Li–Cs; X = S, Se, Te) monolayers and claim: (i) a ferromagnetic half-metal in which a nodal loop at E_F is formed *entirely by one spin channel* ("nodal loop half-metal," NLHM); (ii) SOC-robustness of the loop via the M_z mirror (with out-of-plane magnetization); (iii) a magnetization-direction-dependent topological phase diagram — QAH (C = ±1) at oblique polarizations, "Weyl-like" two-fold nodes for in-plane magnetization (via m'm2' mirror planes), and a trivial gap otherwise; (iv) magneto-optical (Kerr/Faraday) signatures; and (v) CsP/CsAs monolayers as putative *intrinsic* QAH insulators and doped CsS as an engineering route, with proposed doping concentration 3.52 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻².

## Strengths

1. **The "nodal loop half-metal" concept is a genuinely fresh twist.** Prior 2D ferromagnetic nodal-line proposals (which they cite) had band crossings between opposite-spin states. A nodal loop confined to a single spin sector is a cleaner fit for spin-polarized-transport applications, and the paper is (to my knowledge) the first systematic construction of one.
2. **The symmetry argument is actually solid.** For out-of-plane magnetization, M_z remains an exact symmetry even with SOC (since it commutes with a spin polarized along z, spin_z stays a good quantum number — so the "fully spin-polarized" character of the loop survives SOC, which is a consistent and non-trivial point). Opposite mirror eigenvalues of the crossing bands + a small tight-binding model that reproduces the SOC-on DFT bands (Fig. 3a) make the protection claim convincing.
3. **The phase diagram as a function of magnetization direction is handled systematically** — magnetic space groups tabulated for the full (θ, φ) sphere, AHC computed on a k-grid, Chern numbers extracted, and the quantization σ_xy = e²/h at θ = 45° consistent with the chiral-mirror (TC₂ᵧ/mirror-odd) argument that allows C = 1 rather than forcing a mirror-Chern = ±½ quantum spin Hall state. The comparison with Liu et al.'s LaCl proposal (absence of TM_z in their system) shows good command of the literature.
4. **Good diagnostic breadth for the era**: phonon spectra, formation energies, Fermi-level orbital decomposition, model fitting, AHC, and Kerr/Faraday spectra as a *proposed experiment*. The magneto-optical proposal (quantized low-frequency limit from magneto-electric θ) is a reasonable, non-invasive detection route that is better motivated than in many similar papers.

## Weaknesses

**Major.**

1. **No Curie-temperature estimate at all — the central omission for a spintronics paper.** The entire application story is a 2D ferromagnet hosting spin-polarized topological phases, yet there is no mean-field, spin-wave (Holstein-Primakoff), or Monte Carlo estimate of T_C. The moments are small (1 µ_B/cell), the in-plane MAE is *zero* (the xy-fit in Fig. 1d is MAE(φ) = 0, an XY ferromagnet whose ordering temperature is capped near 0.7 J/k_B), and the only comparison given is the out-of-plane MAE (1.28 meV/cell) against CrI₃ and Fe₃GeTe₂ — an incomplete comparison since the T_C of an XY 2D ferromagnet is exchange-limited, not anisotropy-limited. The Mermin-Wagner discussion is likewise dispatched in one sentence. The material's practical relevance as stated ("excellent platform for spintronics") is essentially unquantified.
2. **Thermal-stability support is weak: MD only up to 100 K.** For a "practical feasibility" section, 100 K is well below the standard 300 K (or better) benchmark, and it is striking that a paper *touting* large (86–158 meV) QAH gaps as room-temperature-relevant simultaneously concedes only 100 K structural stability. The 0.27 eV/atom formation energy of CsS is also merely "competitive" (they compare to ZnO and silicene), and the monolayer must be exfoliated from a {111} surface — no synthesis route beyond "extraction from bulk," no discussion of surface relaxation, Cs adatom migration, or substrate-induced distortion that would break M_z.
3. **All electronic structure is GGA-level; the QAH-gap magnitudes are unvalidated.** 86 meV (doped CsS) and ~158 meV (CsS/CsP/CsAs) gaps, and the 3.79 eV spin-up half-metal gap, all come from a single functional, with no HSE/hybrid +U cross-check and no discussion of known GGA band-gap biases (typically an underestimate of the *topological* gap, but here the "large gap" is a headline claim). The "hole doping" is a supercell-size change, so the quoted 3.52 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻² and the gap at that doping carry finite-size caveats that the paper doesn't mention. The intrinsic-QAH status of CsP/CsAs rests on band structures + a quantized-AHC plot; a Berry-covariant-derivative/Wannier topological invariant computation would have made this much more defensible.

**Minor.**

4. **The "Weyl-like" nodes (in-plane magnetization) are identified only by twofold degeneracies in two band cuts and the mirror-plane argument.** No chirality/Berry-flux calculation is provided, so the statement that these are protected Weyl points (rather than accidental crossings that a small perturbation would gap) is asserted via symmetry but not demonstrated quantitatively.
5. **Robustness windows are not quantified.** The nodal loop survives SOC but is gapped out by rotating the magnetization; "how far from the z axis can the spin stray before the loop dies?" is never answered numerically (e.g., a critical tilt angle vs. anisotropy). This matters precisely because the material is an *easy-plane* ferromagnet — at realistic temperatures the spin is not pinned to z, so the NLHM state and the trivial/gapped states compete thermally.
6. **Abstract imprecision.** "The QAH state and Wey-like semimetal can be achieved by rotating the spin from the out-of-plane to the in-plane direction" conflates two different mechanisms (C = 1 occurs at *oblique* angles in 2'; the in-plane limit *m'm2'* gives the nodal points, not the QAH). The body text is careful; the abstract is not.
7. The tight-binding model's fidelity is demonstrated only for the band edges (Figs. 2d, 3a); its AHC/topological output for the tilted-magnetization phases is not independently shown.
8. No discussion of dielectric environment, strain, or layer stacking on Mₙ protection or on the AHC — a single-sentence sensitivity analysis would have strengthened the "platform" framing.

## Verdict

This is a competent, well-executed first-principles prediction paper: the core symmetry-protected, SOC-robust, *fully spin-polarized* nodal loop mechanism is correctly and convincingly argued, the magnetization-direction phase diagram is a nice systematic contribution, and the magneto-optical detection suggestion is a reasonable forward link. Its main liability is the gap between its application-driven framing and its evidence: a spintronics proposal with no ordering-temperature estimate, a stability section that stops at 100 K MD, and single-functional DFT backing the headline "large QAH gap" claims. As a materials-prediction paper it is sound and above average; as a *feasibility* argument it is incomplete.

## My understanding of the paper's logical argument

The paper (Zhou et al., arXiv:1903.11025) makes the following chain of claims:

1. **Motivation.** For spintronics, one wants 2D nodal loop semimetals (NLSs) whose conducting channels are *fully* spin-polarized (i.e., inside a half-metal), so that a generated transport current carries pure spin. Earlier 2D ferromagnetic NLS proposals had crossings between bands of opposite or mixed spin character, so full spin polarization was not achieved.

2. **Candidate identification.** Take the (111) planes of CsCl-type alkaline-metal monochalcogenides MX (M = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs; X = S, Se, Te) as monolayers (MX MLs). Establish practical feasibility: formation energies of 0.27–0.59 eV/atom (comparable to experimentally synthesized ZnO ML at 0.19 eV/atom), gapless phonon spectra (CsS, CsP, CsAs), and MD runs showing structural integrity up to 100 K. Establish magnetism: CsS ML is an FM half-metal (1 μB per cell, dominated by S p orbitals) with FM ground state 7.06 meV/cell below AFM and 139.77 meV/cell below NM.

3. **The central physical claim.** With magnetization out of plane (along z), the spin-down channel has a nodal loop at E_F (crossings along Γ–K and Γ–M, formed by p_z and p_{x,y} S orbitals) while the spin-up channel is gapped by ~3.79 eV → a *fully spin-polarized* nodal loop half-metal (NLHM). The protection works because the out-of-plane magnetization leaves the mirror *M_z* intact as a *magnetic* symmetry, and — crucially — *M_z* remains a good symmetry even when spin–orbit coupling is included, since the two crossing bands carry opposite *M_z* mirror eigenvalues that forbids p_{x,y}–p_z hybridization. This is the twist that distinguishes it from nonmagnetic NLSs where mirror-protected loops are more fragile. An effective lattice model (block-diagonal in {p_{x,y}}, p_z by the mirror) reproduces the DFT bands and confirms the mechanism.

4. **Phase diagram by rotating the spin.** Using magnetic point groups (computed with ISOTROPY) and a SOC Hamiltonian with a general spin direction:
   - Out of plane (θ = nπ): NLHM survives; the SOC gap regions near Γ host quantized AHC (C = +1, −1), so hole-doping by one hole (3.52×10¹⁴ cm⁻²) turns CsS into a QAH insulator with an 86 meV gap.
   - In plane along the mirror lines (φ = nπ/3 in the xy plane): *M* symmetry shrinks the loop to a pair of "mixed Weyl points of type-(i)" in the mirror plane — a Weyl-like 2D semimetal with C = 0.
   - In plane off the mirror lines: all crossings gap out; the surviving TC₂ᵧ symmetry allows a finite Berry curvature and the system is a QAH insulator with C = +1 (σ_xy = e²/h at θ = 45°).
   - The xy-plane phase pattern has period π/3, and intermediate phases are topologically trivial (TM_z symmetry forces C = 0).

5. **Detection.** Electric (quantized AHC) and optical (Kerr angle quantized to −π/2, Faraday angle to Cα) probes in CsS, and intrinsically in CsP and CsAs MLs, which the authors claim are QAH insulators in their native state without doping.

## Feedback

**Strengths.**

- The core idea is physically well-motivated and correctly executed: horizontal mirror + out-of-plane magnetization + SOC is a clean combination, and the symmetry bookkeeping (opposite *M_z* parities, block-diagonal lattice model, magnetic point group table, ISOTROPY) is done carefully and consistently. The Berry-curvature parity arguments for when σ_xy must vanish (in-plane mirror, TM_z phases) and when it need not (TC₂ᵧ generic phases) are sound.
- The methodology is comprehensive for a materials proposal: DFT (phonons, MAE, spin channels), Wannier/lattice model, Chern numbers and AHC on a 3000×3000 k-mesh, magneto-optical spectra, and a 5×3 materials screening.
- Good scholarly practice: acknowledging two concurrent NLHM proposals (MnN, PtCl₃) in a note added.

**Substantive weaknesses.**

1. **The headline state is a metastable magnetic configuration, and the paper does not confront this.** The MAE fit MAE(θ) = 1.32 cos²θ − 0.04 cos⁴θ meV means the in-plane (x) direction is the easy axis: the NLHM requires out-of-plane magnetization, which costs +1.28 meV/cell in CsS (and +5.9/+8.4 meV/cell for LiSe/LiTe, Table S1). A spin of 1 μB feels ~0.06 meV/T, so stabilizing the out-of-plane state would require fields of tens to ~100 T. No substrate, strain, or stacking strategy is offered; the paper even presents the *small* MAE as a virtue, confusing magnitude with sign (for the NLHM phase one would want the *out-of-plane* easy axis). Meanwhile the actual ground-state manifold is in-plane (a Weyl-like semimetal at φ = nπ/3 and a trivial insulator otherwise, since the in-plane MAE is isotropic), so the "fully spin-polarized nodal loop" is not the equilibrium phase of the material.

2. **No Curie-temperature estimate, despite the spintronics framing.** The introduction invokes Mermin–Wagner, but the paper then gives no estimate of T_C at all — no exchange constants extracted (e.g., from the supercell spin configurations or spin-wave stiffness), no Ising/Heisenberg Monte Carlo. With one μB per cell, an isotropic "XY ferromagnet" in-plane MAE, and an FM–AFM splitting of only 7 meV/cell, T_C could plausibly be far below any practical regime (and a strict XY anisotropy with truly zero in-plane MAE would forbid long-range order at any T in 2D by Mermin–Wagner itself). The MD "stability up to 100 K" is a *structural* check at a very low temperature and does not speak to magnetic ordering. For a paper pitching these materials as "an excellent platform for advanced spintronics," this is the most important missing calculation.

3. **Internal inconsistencies in which compounds actually host the NLHM.** The text claims "all NLHM candidates ... including LiS, LiSe, LiTe, NaSe, and NaTe" — but omits CsS, which is the prototypical compound whose nodal loop is established throughout the paper (Fig. 3a: loop at E_F survives SOC). The abstract and title, by contrast, present "MX (M = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs; X = S, Se, Te) monolayers" as the host family, as if all 15 qualify. Only a handful of the 15 actually qualify, and the reader should be told exactly which ones do (with and without SOC) in a single unambiguous statement/figure.

4. **A misleading mechanism statement.** "Considering the SOC effect, the mirror reflection symmetry cannot protect the nodal loops in nonmagnetic materials" is not accurate: in nonmagnetic 2D materials with a preserved mirror (horizontal or vertical), mirror-protected nodal lines survive SOC just as well (mirror Chern number protection is SOC-robust there; indeed many predicted 2D NLSs rely exactly on this). The electron counts (6 for CsP, 8 for CsAs, both even vs. 7 for CsS) are consistent with them being gapped, and the quantized AHC of Fig. 5(b) supports QAH, but the justification should say one extra electron / one hole relative to CsS. Also, the QAH in CsP and CsAs presumably requires the out-of-plane magnetic orientation (where the M_z-protected gaps sit); Table S1 shows CsAs actually prefers out-of-plane (MAE = −9.07 meV/cell, favorable), while CsP is only weakly favorable (−0.35) — worth stating explicitly since the magnetic orientation dependence developed elsewhere in the paper is central.

6. **Methodological caveats left unexamined.** (i) Everything is PBE-GGA without +U/HSE; the 7 meV/cell FM over AFM stability and the near-degeneracy of in-plane directions are functional-sensitive. (ii) No study of disorder/defect robustness of the half-metallic crossing or the doped QAH state, and no discussion of how the 3.79 eV spin-up gap and the 86 meV spin-down gap would behave beyond the ideal lattice. (iii) The doping figure 3.52×10¹⁴ cm⁻² corresponds to one hole per primitive cell, which is fine, but "achievable by current techniques" is asserted by citation without any discussion of whether this heavy, soft Cs-based monolayer can even be grown on a substrate without reconstruction.

**Minor issues.**

- The SI gives the lattice model space group as "P6̄m2 (No. 187)"; P6̄m2 is No. 186 (No. 187 is P6̄2m). One of the symbol or the number is a typo (the reference to a σ_h mirror plane matches 186).

- The QAH Chern-number figure (Fig. 3b) only plots θ; an equivalent plot or statement that the C pattern repeats with the in-plane angle convention of Table 1 would make the phase diagram self-consistent as presented.

**Overall assessment.** The central result — a fully spin-polarized, SOC-robust nodal loop in a 2D ferromagnetic half-metal, protected by a horizontal mirror, and the spin-rotation phase diagram connecting NLHM ↔ QAH ↔ Weyl-like semimetal — is a clean, well-argued, and genuinely useful symmetry insight, demonstrated with more than the usual depth (model + topology + optics). However, the flagship state sits above the magnetic ground state by 1–8 meV/cell with no stabilization route, there is no T_C estimate anywhere despite the spintronics framing, the compound-by-compound NLHM picture is internally muddled, and a few statements (the nonmagnetic-mirror claim, the CsAs isoelectronic argument, the space-group number) are wrong or misleading as written. It is a solid but over-claimed materials-proposal paper within an extremely crowded 2018–19 niche of predicted 2D topological ferromagnets; its practical value (and I am aware of no experimental follow-up on these soft, Cs-containing monolayers) remains limited.

## [1903.11025v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Evaluation of 1903.11025v1: "Fully spin-polarized nodal loop semimetals in alkaline-metal monochalcogenide monolayers"

## The Single Weakest Link: The Ground-State Contradiction

The conclusion of this paper — that MX monolayers constitute "a new 2D material platform hosting the exotic NLHM and QAH states" — rests on a nodal loop half-metal (NLHM) that **only exists in the out-of-plane (z-axis) magnetized configuration**. But the paper's own data shows this configuration is **not the thermodynamic ground state**, and there is no quantitative argument that it is practically accessible.

### Tracing the argument chain

1. **Premise (DFT + symmetry):** With magnetization along *z*, the spin-down bands cross at E_F to form a nodal loop protected by the 𝓜_z mirror plane. This is well-supported by the DFT band structures, orbital projections (Fig. 2), parity analysis, and the effective lattice model.

2. **Premise (SOC robustness):** Because 𝓜_z commutes with SOC for out-of-plane spin, the nodal loop survives. Also well-supported.

3. **MAE calculation (Fig. 1c):** The paper *explicitly* fits MAE = K₁cos²θ + K₂cos⁴θ with K₁ = 1.32 meV, K₂ = −0.04 meV, and states: *"The positive value of MAE indicates a preferred magnetization along the x-axis rather than along the z-axis."* The in-plane (XY) ferromagnetic state is the ground state, and the out-of-plane state — the one hosting the NLHM — sits **1.28 meV/cell above it**.

4. **The missing step:** The paper then says *"the magnetization direction in 2D ferromagnets can be effectively tuned by applying an external magnetic field"* (citing Fe₃GeTe₂) and moves on, **never quantifying the field required**.

### Why this breaks the entailment

With μ = 1 μ_B per cell and an anisotropy barrier of ΔE = 1.28 meV, the Zeeman field needed to flip the moment from *x̂* to *ẑ* is:

$$B_{\text{crit}} \approx \frac{\Delta E}{\mu_B} \approx \frac{1.28 \text{ meV}}{0.1 \text{ meV/T}} \approx 13 \text{ T}$$

Thirteen tesla is not the "effectively tunable" regime that Fe₃GeTe₂ represents (where in-plane→out-of-plane switching occurs under ~1 T). Under that field:
- The paper never checks whether the half-metallic gap (3.79 eV in spin-up) or the nodal-loop topology survives.
- Thermal fluctuations at any realistic operating temperature (k_B·300 K ≈ 26 meV ≫ 1.28 meV) will randomize the out-of-plane alignment instantaneously.
- The 1.28 meV anisotropy, while numerically modest, is *smaller* than the kJ/mol of typical van der Waals interlayer binding, so the out-of-plane state is essentially a non-ground-state computational artifact that requires pinning beyond any practical device scenario.

### The precise gap

The data establishes: *"If you forcibly constrain the spin to point along *ẑ*, the spin-down channel exhibits a symmetry-protected nodal loop at E_F."*

The conclusion claims: *"MX monolayers are nodal loop half-metals … offering an excellent materials platform for spintronics."*

The step from "conditional band structure" to "this material platform" requires that the out-of-plane configuration be the equilibrium (or at least easily accessible metastable) state. The MAE data **refutes** this. The ground state of the monolayer is the XY ferromagnet, which hosts either a Weyl-like pair of two-fold nodes (at θ = π/3) or a topologically trivial insulator — **not** the NLHM. The NLHM is a state the data does not select as the material's actual state, and the paper provides no quantitative pathway (external field, substrate engineering, strain) to reach it in practice.

Everything else in the calculation (phonon stability, formation energy, FM vs. AFM energy differences, the lattice model) is internally consistent. The one point where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data is this: **the paper's own anisotropy energy proves the NLHM is metastable, not the ground state, and the field required to stabilize it (~13 T) is not discussed, never computed, and incompatible with the "spintronics platform" framing the paper sells.**

---

On the broader assessment: the paper's idea — that a mirror symmetry that coexists with SOC in a ferromagnet can protect a nodal loop from SOC-induced gapping — is genuinely interesting as a symmetry argument. The family of 15 candidate materials is broad. But the core material claim is undermined by the authors' own MAE result, and the "platform" language overstates what the data supports. The work is a solid computational screening that identifies a symmetry-protected topological state in a constrained magnetic configuration, but not a demonstrated material platform.

# Peer Review

**Title:** Fully spin-polarized nodal loop semimetals in alkaline-metal monochalcogenide monolayers

## Summary

The authors use first-principles (DFT) calculations together with an effective tight-binding lattice model to propose a new family of "nodal-loop half-metals" (NLHMs) in ferromagnetic alkaline-metal monochalcogenide *MX* (*M* = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs; *X* = S, Se, Te) monolayers. The central claim is that for out-of-plane magnetization the Fermi energy contains a closed nodal loop formed by two *same-spin* (spin-down) band crossings, protected by a *ℳ*_z mirror symmetry that survives spin–orbit coupling (SOC) because the magnetization preserves the mirror plane. By rotating the magnetization the authors report a transition to a quantized quantum-anomalous-Hall (QAH) insulator (C = ±1) and, for in-plane magnetization, a Weyl-like semimetal phase with nodal points in a mirror plane. They further argue that CsP and CsAs monolayers are *intrinsic* QAH insulators and that the QAH state in CsS can be reached by hole doping (~3.5×10¹⁴ cm⁻²). The authors assess practical feasibility via formation energy, phonon stability, molecular-dynamics thermal stability, and magnetic exchange. The work is presented as a candidate 2D spintronics platform, with magnetooptical Kerr/Faraday signatures proposed for the QAH state.

## Strengths

1. **Clear and internally coherent central idea.** The distinction from prior 2D ferromagnetic NLHMs — here the nodal loop is *fully spin-polarized* (both crossing bands of the same spin channel) — is well motivated and well articulated. The symmetry argument that *ℳ*_z protects the loop *despite* SOC for out-of-plane magnetization is the paper's strongest conceptual contribution and is developed carefully (Figs. 3, discussion of the *ℳ*_z parity of the crossing bands preventing p_{x,y}–p_z hybridization).

2. **Consistent symmetry bookkeeping across the magnetic-point-group table (Table 1).** The use of ISO78/isotropy analysis to tabulate the magnetic point groups as a function of polar/azimuthal angles, and the mapping of the resulting symmetry constraints on Ω_xy and the sign of the Chern number, is a tidy and reproducible framework. The period-π (in-plane) and period-π/3 (in-plane, azimuthal) structure of the phase diagram is clearly stated.

3. **Breadth of the materials family.** Scanning the full 5×3 = 15 composition grid and identifying specific NLHM candidates (LiS, LiSe, LiTe, NaSe, NaTe) plus the dopant/analogue route to intrinsic QAH (CsP, CsAs) makes the work more than a single-structure prediction.

4. **Practical feasibility is addressed.** Formation energy, absence of imaginary phonon frequencies, MD up to 100 K, and magnetic ground-state energy ordering (FM lower than AFM by 7.06 meV/cell and NM by 139.77 meV/cell) are reported. This is a reasonable, if limited, stability/feasibility package.

5. **Proposed observables.** The Kerr and Faraday spectra with the expected quantized low-frequency plateaus (Fig. 5c,d), together with doping- and substitution-based routes to the QAH state, are useful and experimentally oriented.

## Weaknesses and concerns

1. **No experimental validation, and the materials are predicted-only.** The entire claim rests on DFT. There is no discussion of whether the 2D monolayer is experimentally isolable from the bulk (the authors take the (111) slab of CsCl-type bulk and flatten a noncoplanar 3D layer into a planar honeycomb — a significant structural reconstruction). How far the relaxed 2D energy is from the isolated-bulk limit, and the sensitivity of the nodal-loop crossing to strain and substrate, is not assessed. This is the single largest gap for a "high-impact" claim.

2. **Robustness of the topological state is only partially addressed.** Spin–orbit coupling is included for the main claims and the SOC-robustness of the *ℳ*_z-protected loop for out-of-plane magnetization is a genuine strength. However, the QAH and Weyl-like phases live at *tilted* magnetization angles where the *ℳ*_z protection is lost; those states are protected only by the (weaker, angle-dependent) magnetic point-group constraints. The authors should report whether the QAH gaps (86 meV reported for doped CsS) and the Weyl nodes survive disorder/structural perturbations, and whether the exchange field (1 meV-scale MAE) is strong enough against any competing AFM interactions or thermal spin canting.

3. **Magnetic stability is arguably underdeveloped for 2D spintronics.** A MAE of ~1.28 meV/cell is below that stated for CrI₃ and Fe₃GeTe₂, and the in-plane MAE is isotropic (XY ferromagnet). Curie temperature is *never* estimated (e.g., via Monte Carlo of the fitted Heisenberg model, which the authors already construct). Without a T_C estimate the "spintronics platform" application claim is not quantified. The energy difference of only 7.06 meV/cell between FM and AFM in a doubled supercell suggests long-range coupling is weak, reinforcing the need for a T_C estimate.

4. **Doping-based QAH is a single-point claim.** The "dope one hole" argument (one hole per cell → 3.52×10¹⁴ cm⁻²) is plausible but the paper does not show that the Fermi level actually lands inside the nontrivial gap I for a *finite*, robust doping, nor that the band reconstruction under real p-type doping does not close the gap. The analogous CsP/CsAs claim of "intrinsic QAH" is likewise asserted; a Chern-number calculation on the neutral, relaxed structure (with SOC) would be expected as the load-bearing evidence for Fig. 5.

5. **Effective model validation depth.** Fig. 2d/3a show the three-orbital tight-binding model reproduces DFT near the Fermi level. This is good, but the model parameters (Table S2) are not scrutinized for how well they reproduce the *gap* magnitude (not just the crossing) or how sensitive the predicted Chern-number changes are to the SOC/hopping parameters. A parameter-sensitivity statement would strengthen the topological conclusions.

6. **Presentation issues.**
   - The abstract states the QAH and Weyl states are achieved "by rotating the spin from the out-of-plane to the in-plane direction," which is a slightly loose gloss over the more nuanced phase diagram in Sections 3–4.
   - Some figures rely heavily on color (Fig. 2a green/blue spin channels), a minor accessibility concern.
   - The "Note added" acknowledging MnN and PtCl₅ 2D NLHM predictions is appropriate but the paper does not critically compare its materials to those (e.g., magnetic strength, MAE, stability), so the novelty/advantage over the contemporaneous work is only implicit.

7. **Minor technical point.** The claim "*ℳ*_z is a good symmetry regardless of SOC for out-of-plane magnetization" (p. 1) is the pillar of the work; it would be strengthened by explicitly verifying the *ℳ*_z parity product of the two crossing bands in the *with*-SOC calculation (the paper shows the parity argument for the no-SOC case in Fig. 2d but the SOC case relies on the argument rather than a direct parity table).

## Minor/specific questions for the authors

- Can you provide a Curie-temperature estimate from the fitted Heisenberg exchange parameters?
- Can you report Ω_xy / Chern number for the *in-plane* Weyl-like phase and confirm the nodes are truly point-nodes (vs. residual lines) under SOC?
- For CsP/CsAs, please tabulate the SOC band gap at the Γ point and confirm the nontrivial Chern numbers at the *natural* Fermi level.
- What is the energy cost / lattice relaxation of extracting the planar (111) monolayer from the bulk, and is the nodal loop sensitive to the choice of (100)/(110)/(111) slab?
- Did you include van der Waals corrections in the layer stability analysis?

## Overall assessment

This is a competent, well-structured, and internally consistent DFT + lattice-model study. Its clear contribution is the identification of a *fully spin-polarized* nodal-loop half-metal whose loop is protected by a SOC-robust mirror symmetry for out-of-plane magnetization, plus a symmetry-based phase diagram (NLHM → QAH → Weyl-like) under magnetization-rotation, and a plausible route to intrinsic QAH via substitution (CsP, CsAs). The symmetry analysis (magnetic point groups, Ω_xy argument) is done carefully. The material feasibility study is reasonable for a 2019 prediction paper.

The work falls short of the top of a prestigeous-journal standard primarily because: (i) it is entirely theoretical with a single-structure stability demonstration that does not address substrate/strain robustness of the key crossings; (ii) no Curie-temperature estimate, despite claiming a spintronics platform; (iii) the QAH claims rest partly on a single-hole doping assumption and an asserted (not fully computed, in the main text) neutral CsP/CsAs Chern number; and (iv) only a brief nod to contemporaneous competing candidates. These gaps temper what is otherwise a solid contribution.

**Recommendation:** Major revision. The core physics is sound and interesting; acceptance would hinge on adding T_C estimates, explicit SOC-parity tables for the protected loop, a Chern-number confirmation of the neutral CsP/CsAs QAH state, and a robustness discussion (substrate/strain/disorder) for the nodal loop and QAH gaps.
