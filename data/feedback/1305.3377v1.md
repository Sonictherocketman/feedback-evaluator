## [1305.3377v1-general-review] General Review


# Evaluation of arXiv:1305.3377v1 — "Microscopic and self-consistent description for neutron halo in deformed nuclei" (Li, Meng, Ring, Zhao, Zhou)

## Summary of the argument

The paper reports a deformed relativistic Hartree–Bogoliubov framework in the continuum (DefRHBC/DefRCHB): Dirac–Bogoliubov quasiparticles expanded in a spherical Woods–Saxon basis (to handle large asymptotic ranges and discretize the continuum), axially deformed mean fields expanded in Legendre polynomials, and a density-dependent zero-range pairing force with a quasiparticle-energy cutoff. The headline physical claim is that in the weakly bound prolate nucleus ⁴⁴Mg (β₂ = 0.32, S₂ₙ = 0.44 MeV with NL3) the neutron halo density extends preferentially perpendicular to the symmetry axis — i.e., a prolate core with a slightly oblate halo — and that the halo shape is set by the Λ-component content of the dominant near-threshold orbitals (Λ = 1 p-wave in ⁴⁴Mg vs. Λ = 0 p-wave in the prolate-halo case ³⁶Ne). This is interpreted as a "decoupling" of halo deformation from core deformation, with suggested experimental signatures (double-hump longitudinal momentum distributions, new dipole modes, effects on sub-barrier capture).

## Strengths

- **Physically coherent and interesting central observation.** A halo whose deformation sign differs from that of the core is a genuinely non-trivial consequence of continuum structure, and connecting it to the Λ-mixing of the near-threshold orbitals is a reasonable microscopic explanation.
- **Appropriate tool choice.** Deformed + pairing + continuum are all required for this physics, and DefRHBC is arguably the most self-consistent framework available at the time; the Woods–Saxon-basis route gives an explicit, controlled discretization of the continuum rather than coordinate-space integration.
- The paper is honest about which numbers come from which parameter set and shows that the mechanism produces both halo shapes (oblate in ⁴⁴Mg, prolate in ³⁶Ne), which makes the Λ-component argument more credible than a single-nucleus observation.

## Critique

### 1. Positioning and originality: this is a progress report, and it shows

Most of the results are explicitly reprinted from refs. [21–23] ("This figure is originally published in Ref. [21]" — for *both* figures). The ⁴⁴Mg halo decoupling result itself is the PR C 82, 011301(R) (2010) paper, i.e., the main novelty predates this work. What is genuinely new here is essentially the ³⁶Ne comparison and a short discussion paragraph. If the intent is a conference summary, that is fine, but the abstract oversells it: it promises that "the generic conditions for the existence of halos in deformed nuclei and for the occurrence of this decoupling effect are discussed." In fact, no generic conditions are derived — the "discussion" collapses to two sentences in the Summary ("the existence and the deformation of a possible neutron halo depends essentially on the quantum numbers of the main components of the single particle orbits in the vicinity of the Fermi surface"). No systematic study over isotopes, no classification of shell configurations, no formulas relating deformation signs to orbital content. A table of (N, dominant near-Fermi orbital, Λ content, predicted core/halo deformation sign) across the Mg and Ne isotopes would have made the claimed "generic conditions" real; instead it is an assertion from two data points.

### 2. The core/halo decomposition is never defined

The central claim rests on Fig. 1(b)/(c) — splitting the neutron density into "core" and "halo" contributions — but the paper never states *how* this decomposition is performed (e.g., subtraction of a closed-core calculation? grouping by orbital? by radial occupation?). Such a split is somewhat ill-defined: the mean field is self-consistent, so the whole density is one self-adjusting object, and the sign of the halo quadrupole can plausibly depend on the subtraction scheme. "Slightly oblate" is also never quantified — no β₂ of the halo component, no numerical quadrupole ratio. A reader cannot judge how robust the decoupling claim is, because "slightly oblate" is asserted from a contour plot with a "dotted circle for guiding the eye."

### 3. No quantitative results at all

The Results section contains exactly two density figures. There is no table of two-neutron separation energies, rms radii, quadrupole deformations, or binding energies for the studied isotopes, and no comparison against the (then-available) experimental data for the bound members of the Mg and Ne chains (⁴⁰Mg, ⁴²Mg, etc.), even for quantities that need no exotic-beam measurement. Given that S₂ₙ for ⁴⁴Mg is 0.44 MeV — where small changes in the interaction move the nucleus across the drip line — a comparison of S₂ₙ(n) trends for the experimentally known isotopes would be the minimum benchmark needed to trust predictions at the drip line. Its absence weakens every downstream halo claim, which is exactly where the benchmark would most help.

### 4. The NL3-vs-PK1 discrepancy is noticed but not exploited

The paper notes that NL3 binds Mg through ⁴⁶Mg while PK1 binds only through ⁴²Mg. That is a *large* model uncertainty (a two-neutron difference in locating the drip line, i.e., a difference in the very existence of ⁴⁴/⁴⁶Mg) that should condition all halo claims — yet it is mentioned and dropped. It would be more honest and more informative to state whether the specific decoupling phenomenon in ⁴⁴Mg survives with PK1 (or why ⁴⁴Mg is even predicted with PK1 at all), and to discuss which parameter set is preferred on nuclear-matter/experimental grounds.

### 5. Numerical control is asserted, not shown

- **Box size:** R_max = 20 fm with S₂ₙ ≈ 0.44 MeV implies an asymptotic neutron wavelength of order several fm; whether the 2⁰–10⁻⁶ tail level and the inferred halo quadrupole converge with R_max is exactly the kind of check halo papers are judged on, and none is reported.
- **Basis truncations:** E^cut_qp = 60 MeV for the quasiparticle space and 100 MeV for the Woods–Saxon basis, plus the no-sea approximation — all standard, but a single basis-convergence statement (e.g., S₂ₙ and β₂ under E^cut variation) would be cheap and reassuring, especially for the pairing field, which is truncated in the same space.
- **Pairing calibration:** V₀ = 380 MeV·fm³ is fixed by reproducing the *proton* pairing energy of a *spherical* nucleus (²⁰Mg) as obtained with a *non-relativistic* force (Gogny D1S). Using a proton, non-relativistic anchor to calibrate neutron pairing in very neutron-rich deformed nuclei is a weak calibration point; no sensitivity of the ⁴⁴Mg halo to V₀ (or to the density dependence) is discussed. Given the paper's dependence on the *presence* and shape of halo pairing, this is a real gap.

### 6. The proposed experimental probes are not computed

The double-hump longitudinal momentum distribution (à la Sakharuk & Zelevinsky [30]) is named as the key observable that would confirm decoupling, but no such calculation is performed. A single-panel comparison — LMD for ⁴⁴Mg with vs. without decoupling, or ³⁶Ne vs. ⁴⁴Mg — would have turned the paper from "model makes an interesting picture" into "this is what an experiment should see." As written, the experimental section is a list of possibilities with no numbers.

### 7. Minor issues

- Reference [1] (Bulgac) is labeled "(1980)" though it is the arXiv:nucl-th/9907088 preprint — an obvious date error for a citation that sits as the first HFB reference.
- Fig. 1(a) caption ("proton density (for x < 0) and neutron density (for x > 0)") is confusing: the two halves look like unrelated plots in a single frame.
- Grammar/typo errors throughout ("One the other hand," "may also has some influence," "its cores is prolately deformed") — minor, but inconsistent with a polished proceedings submission.
- Reference [32] is "to be published," leaving an odd-A claim unsupported.

## Net assessment

The physics is sound and within the standard, well-validated practice of the relativistic mean-field community, and the core observation (sign mismatch between halo and core deformation, tied to Λ-mixing of near-threshold orbitals) is the paper's genuine value. But as published the paper is thin: it is a re-presentation of prior results plus one new nucleus, with no quantitative tables, no benchmarks against available experimental data, no definition or robustness discussion of the decomposition the conclusion rests on, no statement of the (large) parameter-set uncertainty, and no calculation of the very observable it proposes as a test. The "generic conditions" advertised in the abstract are asserted, not established. It would have become a much stronger paper with: (i) an isotope-by-isotope table of deformations and S₂ₙ for Mg/Ne compared to the experimental drip-line systematics; (ii) an explicit, tested core/halo decomposition with a numerical halo quadrupole; (iii) one concrete knockout-observable calculation; and (iv) a basis/box/pairing-strength convergence statement. On the published merits: competent, clear, incremental.

## Summary of the work

The paper reports a **deformed relativistic Hartree–Bogoliubov theory in the continuum (DefRHBC)** built on the Dirac Woods–Saxon basis, and uses it to study neutron halos in deformed weakly bound nuclei, focusing on **⁴⁴Mg** and **³⁶Ne**. The headline result: in ⁴⁴Mg the core is prolate (β₂≈0.32) but the halo is *slightly oblate*, i.e. a **decoupling of the halo's deformation from the core's**, attributed to the Λ (p‑wave) character of the near‑threshold orbitals. The contrast case ³⁶Ne (β₂≈0.52) has a prolate halo matching the core.

## What is good

- **A genuinely useful capability is being described.** Combining deformation, pairing, and the continuum *self‑consistently and microscopically* was a real gap; the WS‑basis DefRHBC is a sound, computationally tractable way to get it. This is a legitimate and non‑trivial piece of computational infrastructure for medium‑mass exotic nuclei.
- **The core/halo decomposition is clean and visual.** Separating each isoscalar/isovector density into bound‑core and weakly‑bound‑halo pieces (Figs. 1–2) makes the shape argument easy to read and is a reasonable way to present a "deformed halo."
- **The physical conclusion is interesting and plausible.** A halo whose deformation is set by the intrinsic parity/Λ structure of the orbitals near the Fermi surface, rather than adiabatically following the core, is a subtle and somewhat surprising effect. The ⁴⁴Mg vs ³⁶Ne comparison (Λ=1 vs Λ=0 p‑wave dominance flipping the halo sign) is a nice, specific mechanism‑level explanation.
- **Good numerical hygiene is disclosed** (box 20 fm, j<10.5ℏ, +/− energy cutoffs, no‑sea, density‑dependent zero‑range pairing).

## Concerns / weaknesses

- **This is a summary/proceedings‑style paper, not a primary contribution.** The format (keywords, PACS, "This figure is originally published in Ref. [21]") and the content make it clear that the substance is the group's own 2010–2012 papers (Refs. 21–23). The novelty relative to *those* is modest; as a standalone piece you can't tell what's new here. That is a real handicap for the reader and lowers its value.
- **"Halo" separation is not methodologically defined.** The core/halo split looks like a partial‑wave or occupancy projection, but the criterion is not stated. Because the effect is subtle (a *slightly* oblate halo), it is essential to demonstrate the result is robust to *how* you decompose the density rather than an artifact of the projection. That analysis is absent.
- **No comparison to experiment or to other theories.** At the time ⁴⁴Mg and ³⁶Ne were at/beyond the drip line and not measured. There is no benchmark against R‑matrix, SKM, UNEDF, or any data; no RCHB‑sphere convergence check cited; the two‑neutron separation energies are quoted (0.44 MeV) but not judged against alternative models. The "generic conditions for halos in deformed nuclei" claim is made on the basis of **two nuclei** — far too thin to support a general statement.
- **Parameter consistency is a soft spot.** The neutron pairing strength V₀ is fixed to reproduce the *proton* pairing energy of spherical ²⁰Mg from a **different** force (Gogny D1S). Mixing an RMF interaction (NL3/PK1) for the mean field with a pairing calibrated from a different phenomenological force, and doing so through a *different nucleon's* pairing, is a non‑trivial assumption for halo structure (which is exquisitely sensitive to the last‑particle binding). No sensitivity study of V₀ or ρ_sat on the halo shape is presented.
- **Convergence is not reported here.** For continuum physics, convergence in box size, basis truncation, and the positive‑energy cutoff is the single biggest source of systematic error. These are named but not varied, and the effect (oblate vs prolate halo) is precisely the kind of delicate feature that can be cut‑off‑dependent.
- **Predictions are made but not quantified.** The double‑hump longitudinal momentum distribution, new dipole modes, and the sub‑barrier capture effect are proposed as observable signatures but not computed even approximately. As it stands these are invitations, not results.
- Minor: only NL3 and PK1; no odd‑A quantitative results despite the blocking extension being advertised; occasional grammar slips ("may also has some influence," "44Mg and its cores is prolately deformed").

## Bottom line

The paper presents a **sound and practically important framework** and an **interesting, plausible physical effect** (shape decoupling of a deformed neutron halo), but as a standalone document it reads as an **extended summary of the authors' prior work**, with a thin physical sample (two nuclei), no experimental or cross‑theoretical validation, and missing details on the halo‑density decomposition and convergence that would be required to defend a delicate sign‑of‑deformation claim. I would want: a defined halo‑separation scheme with a robustness check, V₀/cutoff convergence, a broader isotope scan (systematic Ne/Mg/Si, and a deformed odd‑A member), and at least one concrete momentum‑distribution or excitation calculation to anchor the "observable" claims.

## [1305.3377v1-critique] Critique


I've read the paper in full. Here is my critique.

## Overview

Li, Meng, Ring, Zhao & Zhou present a summary of their work on deformed relativistic Hartree–Bogoliubov theory in continuum (DefRHBC): a deformed Woods–Saxon-basis implementation of the RHB equations (with a no-sea approximation, density-dependent zero-range pairing, and Legendre-expanded mean fields), applied to the weakly bound, deformed nuclei ⁴⁴Mg and ³⁶Ne. The headline physical claim — that in ⁴⁴Mg the prolate core coexists with a *slightly oblate* neutron halo, i.e. a decoupling of halo and core shapes, governed by the Λ components of the near-threshold orbitals — is presented together with the generic conditions for halo formation and decoupling in deformed systems.

## Strengths

1. **Clear, self-contained exposition of the framework.** The chain of approximations — continuum via Woods–Saxon basis, axial deformed blocks in (m, π), density-dependent pairing, no-sea cutoff, blocking extension — is laid out compactly and accurately. The Woods–Saxon basis as a "reconciler" between oscillator-space matrix problems and coordinate-space integration is well motivated and the choice of box (R = 20 fm) and cutoffs (j < 21/2, E_cut = 100 MeV) is stated, which is more than many nuclear-structure papers give.

2. **Physically interesting and largely correct insight.** The statement that the *shape* of a halo is determined by the angular-momentum decomposition of the weakly bound orbitals (low Λ, low centrifugal barrier → extension in the equatorial plane or along the symmetry axis) is the paper's real conceptual contribution. The idea that the halo need not follow the core deformation is nontrivial and, to my knowledge, correct: a p-wave with Λ = 1 (m = 1) feels a small barrier in the equatorial direction precisely where the prolate core is thin, so the diffuse density can relax toward oblateness. This observation did seed follow-on discussion of "deformed halos."

3. **Self-consistency claim is legitimately made.** Because the mean field, continuum, pairing, and deformation are solved in one self-consistent cycle, the halo-core shape relationship is not an artifact of a fixed-potential model — this is a meaningful advantage over cluster/ Nilsson-type pictures, and the authors are right to emphasize it.

## Weaknesses

1. **This paper contains essentially no new physics.** Both figures are explicitly reprinted from Ref. [21]; every quantitative result (β₂ = 0.32, S₂ₙ = 0.44 MeV for ⁴⁴Mg; β₂ = 0.52 for ³⁶Ne) is deferred to Refs. [21–23]. It functions as a conference-proceedings review of the authors' own recent papers, and the incremental step over [23] is thin. A paper whose entire "results" section is "we found X in [21] and Y in [23]" should be judged as a summary, and it is a competent but unremarkable one.

2. **No quantitative measure of the halo deformation.** The oblateness of the ⁴⁴Mg halo rests on a visual density decomposition. The paper never states (i) how the core/halo separation is defined (a fixed radius at some density? a model-dependent split?), nor (ii) a numerical quadrupole moment of halo and core separately. Since the halo density spans 10⁻¹…10⁻⁶ fm⁻³ — i.e. the *tail* of the density dominates the "halo" figure — its apparent oblateness is extremely sensitive to the decomposition convention. A claim of *sign* of a deformation on a diffusely decomposed tail needs a β₂^halo number and an error bar; without one, "slightly oblate" is not established, let alone robust.

3. **Large, unquantified model dependence.** The authors note — and then move on past — the fact that with PK1, ⁴²Mg (not ⁴⁶Mg) is the last bound Mg isotope, i.e. under PK1 the very existence of ⁴⁴Mg (and thus of its oblate halo) is doubtful since its neutron Fermi surface would be above threshold. One of the two parameterizations in their own study denies the existence of the system the paper's headline result refers to. This is a serious vulnerability for a "generic conditions" discussion, and the paper contains no attempt to address it (no cross-parameter-set halo shape comparison, no statement of which feature survives).

4. **No validation against experiment and no predicted observables.** For a theory paper making a structural claim, there is no comparison to anything measurable: no matter/charge radii, no breakup-cross-section or longitudinal-momentum-distribution *calculation*, no binding-energy table against data for the Mg/Ne chains. The "experimental observables" paragraph (double-hump momentum distributions, dipole modes, sub-barrier capture) is purely suggestive, with no numbers. One could at least test the framework against the well-established ⁶⁴–⁷²Ni halo region or compare S₂ₙ for the heavier Mg isotopes to the RIKEN data.

5. **Minor consistency and presentation issues.** The density-dependent zero-range pairing strength is calibrated to the *proton* pairing gap of ²⁰Mg computed with a nonrelativistic Gogny force (D1S) — a pragmatic but hybrid choice whose transferability to the deep-neutron halo sector is not discussed; "S₂ₙ" vs "2n" notation is inconsistent; Λ = 1 is loosely called "p wave" although the component is a *prolate* deformed p-wave (Λ, m = 1, π = −); reference [32] is "to be published"; the claim "the halo is formed by several orbitals close to the threshold" (summary) is in some tension with the repeated emphasis on one "essential level."

6. **Missing methodological controls.** No basis-convergence test (j < 21/2, E_cut, number of Dirac-sea states) is quoted despite the tail density — the physical observable — being exponentially sensitive to all three, and no check against direct r-space DefRHBC results for the halo density tail is reported, even though such comparisons were the selling point of the Woods–Saxon basis in Ref. [15].

## Verdict

A well-written, pedagogically useful summary of a coherent body of work, resting on one genuinely interesting — and defensible — physical insight (halo/core shape decoupling in ⁴⁴Mg, with a clear orbital-mechanism explanation). But the claim itself is carried by figures and reasoning from other papers, is presented without the quantitative halo-deformation measure that would make it secure, and the underlying existence of ⁴⁴Mg is contested by one of the two force sets the authors themselves use. It advances the field mainly by consolidating and communicating a result, not by establishing it.

## Stated contribution, in my own words

The authors present a **microscopic, self-consistent framework for describing neutron halos in deformed (prolate or oblate) nuclei**: a deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in the continuum (their "DefRHB C" theory), built on scalar/vector meson fields plus a density-dependent zero-range pairing force, with all quantities expanded in a deformed Woods–Saxon single-particle basis so that the continuum, pairing, and deformation are treated simultaneously without a large-box discretization. This combination applied specifically to *deformed* halos is a new suggestion, if speculative.

**Verdict.** The novel content is ~15–20% integrative synthesis plus two speculative experimental predictions; the framework and all presented results are drawn from the cited (largely the group's own 2003–2012) literature, with two figures reprinted outright. There is a mild tension between the abstract's claim that a theory "has been developed" here and the text's attribution of every ingredient elsewhere, and the results section adds no new nucleus, parameter set, or observable beyond [21]. It reads as a mini-review of the group's own program — a useful and technically sound snapshot, but an incremental contribution.

## [1305.3377v1-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


# Evaluation of arXiv:1305.3377v1 — *Microscopic and self-consistent description for neutron halo in deformed nuclei*

**Li, Meng, Ring, Zhao, Zhou (2013)**

## 1. What the "experiment" actually is

This is not a laboratory paper — there is no beam, target or detector. The "central experiment" is a **numerical mean-field calculation** of the ground-state shapes and density distributions of two deformed, weakly bound nuclei, **⁴⁴Mg** (n=32, p=12) and **³⁶Ne** (n=24, p=10), done in the **Deformed relativistic Hartree–Bogoliubov theory in continuum (DefRHB C)**. Reproducibility therefore means: reproduce the code, the basis, the forces, the cutoffs and the self-consistency iteration.

## 2. Methodology, in detail

**(a) RHB equations of motion.** The mean field is the relativistic one with scalar σ, vector ω, isovector ρ mesons, and a one-photon Coulomb term:

- Dirac Hamiltonian `ℏ_D = α·p + V(r) + β(M + S(r))`, with `S = g_σ σ`, `V = g_ω ω⁰ + g_ρ τ₃ ρ⁰ + e(1−τ₃)A⁰/2` (eqs. 2–4).
- Poisson-like meson equations with source densities built from `U_k, V_k` quasiparticle amplitudes (eqs. 5–6).
- Pairing in the pp channel: **density-dependent zero-range force (DDZRF)**
  `V^pp = V_0 (1/2)(1−P^σ) δ(r_1−r_2) (1 − ρ(r_1)/ρ_sat)` (eq. 7),
  so the local pairing gap is `Δ(r) = V_0 (1 − ρ(r)/ρ_sat) κ(r)` with `κ(r)=Σ V_k†(r) U_k(r)`.

**(b) Continuum / large-r handling.** Instead of a harmonic-oscillator box or large-box coordinate-space grid, the authors use a **spherical Woods–Saxon (WS) single-particle basis** (Ref. [15]), generated for a spherical WS potential with `R_max = 20 fm`, mesh `Δr = 0.1 fm`, kept down to `j < 21/2 ħ`. WS functions fall off more realistically at large `r`, which is the core reason the method is claimed to be suitable for `S_2n ≈ 0.44 MeV` weak binding.

**(c) Deformation.** For **axially deformed** nuclei with **spatial-reflection symmetry**, densities and mean fields are Legendre-expanded
`f(r) = Σ_λ f_λ(r) P_λ(cos θ)` with **λ ∈ {0, 2, 4, …} only** (even parity, eq. 10). Quasiparticle amplitudes are expanded in the *deformed* WS basis [15] with `m^π`-labelled blocks.

**(d) Discretization.** In each `m^π` block, the RHB equation becomes a standard BdG matrix diagonalisation (eq. 13) with
- `𝒜 = ⟨nκm|ℏ_D|n′κ′,m⟩`, `𝓑 = ⟨nκm|Δ|n′κ′,m⟩`.
- **No-sea approximation** for the sum over `k>0` (only above-Fermi quasiparticle states contribute to vector/isovector/charge densities; the Dirac sea is truncated — number of kept negative-energy states per `(ℓ, j)` block is matched to the number of positive-energy states in that block).

**(e) Forces and pairing parameters.**
- Two relativistic-meson parameter sets are used: **NL3** [27] and **PK1** [28].
- DDZRF parameters `ρ_sat = 0.152 fm⁻³`, `V_0 = 380 MeV·fm³` (standard DDZRF values, Ref. [16]).
- The **proton pairing** in ²⁰Mg is instead **calibrated externally from a spherical RHB run with the non-relativistic Gogny D1S** force — a different Hamiltonian than the one used in the deformed neutron-rich runs.

**(f) Output and the "central" result.**
1. Solve the BdG eigenvalue problem in every `m^π` block, iterate the mean fields self-consistently (chemical potential chosen to fix N, Z).
2. Build the total proton and neutron densities from `U_k, V_k`; plot in log scale (Figs. 1, 2).
3. Project the *neutron* density onto "core" and "halo" parts (a post-hoc orbital decomposition; the halo is attributed to the orbit(s) at/above the Fermi surface, the core to the orbits below). This is where the paper's physical claim lives: for ⁴⁴Mg, the halo carries a large **Λ=1 (p-wave)** component → oblate tail on a prolate core (`β₂=0.32`, `S_2n=0.44 MeV`); for ³⁶Ne, the halo carries **Λ=0 (p-wave)** → prolate tail on a prolate core (`β₂=0.52`). "Decoupling" refers to the fact that the partial-wave projection of the halo has the *opposite* intrinsic quadrupole sign to that of the core.

2. **Dirac-sea truncation rule (positive=negative per `(ℓ,j)` block)** is a heuristic chosen to keep the matrix size manageable; no sensitivity analysis is reported.

**1. The central claim rests on a convention, not a measurement.**
> "...the density distribution of this weakly bound nucleus has a very long tail in the direction perpendicular to the symmetry axis which **indicates** the prolate nucleus ⁴⁴Mg has an oblate halo and **there is a decoupling** between the deformations of the core and the halo." (Results, p. 5)

The whole headline result — "oblate halo + prolate core → decoupling" — is read off a post‑hoc split of the neutron density into a "core" and a "halo" (Figs. 1b/1c, 2b/2c). That split is a **choice of decomposition**, not an observable. A density can be partitioned by energy, by radial extent, or by binding, and the sign of the "halo" quadrupole depends on the cut. The paper never varies the definition, gives no halo radius or r.m.s.-radius separation (i.e., no quantitative test against the standard Bonche–Flocard–Super halo criterion), and offers no comparison of the two ways the density could be divided. "Indicates a decoupling" is therefore an interpretation of the authors' own partitioning scheme. The honest word is "suggests, under our definition of halo."

**2. "Decoupling" overstates the paper's own mechanism.**
The next paragraph concedes that
> "the shape of the halo originates from the intrinsic structure of the weakly bound or continuum orbitals... in ⁴⁴Mg the essential level of the halo has a large contribution from the prolate Λ = 1 (p wave) component."

The halo's shape is set by a *specific partial wave inside the same deformed self‑consistent mean field*. That is the opposite of being "decoupled from the deformation of the core"; it is the core's single‑particle level structure that is choosing the halo orbital. The real, weaker phenomenon is that the **sign of the quadrupole of the dominant near‑threshold partial wave happens to oppose the core's quadrupole** — and even that is small, since the abstract hedges that the halo is only *"slightly oblate."* Naming a mild opposite‑sign partial‑wave effect a "decoupling of the halo orbitals from the deformation of the core" (abstract) is the single most vulnerable move in the paper.

**3. "Generic conditions" from exactly two nuclei, both already cited.**
> "The **generic conditions** for the existence of halos in deformed nuclei and for the occurrence of this decoupling effect are discussed." (abstract)

The only "conditions" actually stated are the closing line of the Summary:
> "it was concluded that the existence and the deformation of a possible neutron halo **depends essentially** on the quantum numbers of the main components of the single particle orbits in the vicinity of the Fermi surface."

That is an assertion supported by **two** examples (⁴⁴Mg, ³⁶Ne), and both are reproduced from Refs. [21, 22, 23]. Two contrasting examples is not a "generic condition," and "it was concluded"/"depends essentially" are the register of a proven theorem, not a two‑point observation. The word *generic* is the overclaim.

**4. "Fully taken into account" / "an adequate description."**
> "By solving the [HFB or RHB] equations in coordinate (r) space, the **mean field effects** of the coupling to the continuum can be **fully taken into account**." (p. 1)
> "In order to give **an adequate description** of possible halo in a deformed nucleus, a model should be used which includes... the continuum, large spatial distributions, deformation effects, and couplings among all these features." (Intro)

The first quietly papers over the no‑sea approximation (the sum over k>0 "in and above the Fermi sea"), the basis truncation, and the missing continuum–continuum/correlation effects — so "fully" is doing a lot of work. The second is self‑justifying/circular: it defines "adequate" as "has everything my method has," so the method is validated by the very feature it needs to justify. Both should be softened ("a self‑consistent mean‑field treatment of…", "a description that needs to include...").

**5. Experimental connections asserted, none computed.**
> "The decoupling between the deformations of the core and the halo **may manifest itself by some new experimental observables**, e.g., the double‑hump shape of longitudinal momentum distribution in single‑particle removal reactions and new dipole modes, etc." ... "The shape decoupling effects may also **has** some influence on the sub‑barrier capture process in heavy ion collisions [31]." (p. 5–6)

None of these observables (longitudinal momentum distributions, dipole strength, capture cross sections) is actually calculated in the paper — they are listed. "May manifest" is hedged, good, but the paragraph as a whole sells the work as physically predictive of experiments while containing a single speculative pointer. (And *"may also has"* is a grammar error that undersells how much of a hand‑wave it is.)

**6. "Pronounced" and "well deformed" vs. the mild numbers.**
> "In the very neutron-rich deformed nucleus ⁴⁴Mg, **pronounced** deformed neutron halo was found." (Summary)

The supporting number is *S₂ₙ = 0.44 MeV* and a "slightly oblate" halo. "Pronounced halo" and "slightly oblate halo" in the same paper is an internal tension the reader will notice; a weakly bound 0.44 MeV with a *slightly* mis‑shaped halo is the more defensible phrasing.

**7. Parameter‑set sensitivity is quietly dropped.**
> "with the parameter set **NL3**, ⁴⁶Mg is the last nucleus of which the neutron Fermi surface is negative... In the calculations based on **PK1**, ⁴²Mg is the last bound nucleus." (p. 4)

NL3 and PK1 disagree by *four neutrons* on the drip line, yet the paper treats ⁴⁴Mg as a robust weakly‑bound, halo‑carrying system and never asks whether the *oblate‑halo / decoupling* conclusion survives the PK1 choice (where ⁴⁴Mg is already two neutrons past the drip line). This is the most scientifically serious omission: the headline effect may be a property of the NL3 mean field rather than of the nucleus.

**8. The paper is a re‑presentation, framed as a discovery.**
Abstract: "A deformed relativistic Hartree‑Bogoliubov theory in continuum **has been developed**... and some results are presented." Both figures carry "originally published in Ref. [21]." Nearly every number (β₂ = 0.32; β₂ = 0.52; S₂ₙ = 0.44 MeV) is cited to [21, 22, 23]. The framing ("has been developed," "was found," "it was concluded") reads like original work, but the standalone content is a synthesis of work published 2010–2012. The honest frame is a *progress report* on the group's DefRHBC program, which it is, but the present tense of discovery overstates it.

## B. Grammar / prose (supports the "writing" critique)
- "In contrary to the nucleus ⁴⁴Mg, we observe now a prolate halo" → should be **"In contrast."**
- "The shape decoupling effects may also **has** some influence" → **"have."**
- "On one hand, ... **One the other hand**" → "On the **other** hand."

- The "Λ = 1 (p wave)" wording conflates the azimuthal/projection label Λ with the partial wave ℓ = 1 — a notation the reader has to decode.

## C. What an adversarial reviewer's first paragraph would say

> This is not a research paper but a lightly re‑edited summary of already‑published work by these authors (Refs. 21–23); the two density figures are reproduced from Ref. 21 and essentially every number quoted (β₂, S₂ₙ) is cited to the same set of papers, so the paper's *new* content is the "discussion." That discussion makes its central claim — that the prolate core and "oblate halo" of ⁴⁴Mg are *decoupled* — hang entirely on a model‑ and definition‑dependent decomposition of the neutron density into "core" and "halo," which the authors do not vary, do not benchmark against the standard radius‑based halo criterion, and do not check for robustness to a different parameter set (NL3 vs. PK1 move the drip line by four neutrons). I recommend the authors either (i) reposition this as a progress report and downgrade "indicates/generic/fully/concluded," or (ii) add at least a second definition of the halo, a second parameter set, and one actually‑computed halo observable before the "decoupling" claim can be sustained.

---

**Bottom line.** The physics is plausible and the formalism is standard, so the paper is not unsound — but it is an incremental progress report whose framing ("developed," "indicates a decoupling," "generic conditions," "fully taken into account," "pronounced," "it was concluded") systematically outruns the evidence, which is two re‑presented nuclei, a convention‑dependent density split, one parameter set's drip line, and zero newly computed observables.
