## [1907.12180v1-general-review] General Review


# Review of arXiv:1907.12180v1 — "Carrier-induced Antisymmetric–symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness in Zigzag Graphene Nanoribbons"

## Summary of the argument
The authors use the generalized-Bloch (frozen-magnon) theorem inside LCPAO/DFT (OpenMX) to compute the spin stiffness *D* of zigzag graphene nanoribbons (ZGNR) at two edge-magnetism phases (FM and AFM), by mapping the computed E(N→q,θ) onto a Heisenberg Hamiltonian and fitting E = Dq² near q→0. They then vary uniform background doping and the ribbon width N, claiming: (i) very high *D* of meVÅ² order, consistent with Yazev–Katsnelson's supercell; (ii) opposite doping dependence for FM vs AFM ("antisymmetric vs symmetric"); (iii) *D* proportional to ribbon width.

## What is done well

1. **Method is internally consistent.** The frozen-magnon formalism, the derivation of Re[J_μν] as an energy second derivative (Eq. 7, 19), and the use of only small cone angle θ are all correctly implemented. Reaching an order-of-magnitude agreement (~3000 meVÅ²) with the independent supercell calculation of Yazev & Katsnelson (Ref. [12]) is a meaningful validation of the LCPAO/GBT implementation.
2. **Two phases are treated.** Looking at both FM and AFM edge phases and showing that the lower-energy AFM phase carries the larger stiffness is a fair and honest statement.
3. **Clean, compact presentation.** The derivation is short and the equations are well motivated.

## Substantive weaknesses

### A. The "proportional to ribbon width" claim does not survive to the numbers
Table 1 gives *D_FM/N* in meVÅ²: 2465 (N=6), 2966 (N=10), 3175 (N=12). If *D* were proportional to N, then *D(N=10)/D(N=6)* should be 10/6 ≈ 1.67; observed is 1.20. Similarly N=12 vs 10, observed 1.07 vs predicted 1.20. The scaling is sub-linear. Three points is also too few to call a scaling law. The abstract's wording "the dependence of ribbon widths … with similar tendencies" is defensible, but the body's "proportional" (Sec. 3, 4) is not.

### B. GGA-only, no on-site *U*, no convergence to the true limit of doping
ZGNR edge magnetism is one of the most *U*/*J* sensitive systems in the literature; plain GGA systematically overestimates the magnetic moment (the observed moment per C_edge ~ 0.28 μ_B is close to the GGA value, not the smaller values obtained from DFT+U or GW). The paper never compares to DFT+U or mentions that GGA may overestimate spin stiffness (D ∝ M²·J). Since the central message is that ZGNR is a spintronics candidate with exceptional stiffness, the exchange-correlation sensitivity is the single most important systematic error and it is omitted entirely.

Doping is implemented as a Fermi-level shift (uniform background charge). In a real ZGNR device, gating or adatom doping is locally anisotropic and may break the C₂-symmetry between edges, biasing one edge. The "uniform background" model will therefore not faithfully capture the asymmetric edge-charge distributions that real dopants induce. The conclusion (FM vs AFM antipodal trends) is robust to the *sign* of the Fermi shift within the model, but its physical transfer to real devices is not addressed.

### C. Magnon spectrum is fit over an extremely small q-range
The data in Fig. 2(b) go only out to q ≈ 0.07 Å⁻¹, and the paper admits (Sec. 2) that the moment collapses at larger q (Fig. 2(a)) so one cannot extend the fit. A pure-quadratic fit over a narrow, low-q range is sensitive to (i) the exact step size at small q, (ii) lattice-vortex/Umklapp corrections that enter at order q³–q⁴ even inside the BZ, and (iii) the fact that for a 1D ribbon the "spin stiffness" is really a spin-wave stiffness × ribbon width, and the appropriate low-q expansion can contain a non-zero q⁴ coefficient that biases *D* down. The paper would have benefited from (i) reporting the q-point resolution, (ii) a side-by-side quadratic and quartic fit to quantify the bias, and (iii) an explicit uncertainty on *D*. None of these are present.

### D. Circular role of the Heisenberg model
Eq. (20) gives ℏω_q directly in terms of ΔE(q,θ)/sin²θ — i.e., the extracted *D* does **not** require any Heisenberg exchange at all. The Heisenberg Hamiltonian is only used qualitatively to motivate the θ-quadratic expansion. The introduction (Sec. 1) overstates the role of the Heisenberg model ("we include the Heisenberg model to calculate the spin stiffness based on the above reason"), which misleads the reader into thinking the J_ij fit is a necessary step. In practice, the authors are doing a direct frozen-magnon calculation, which is fine but should be labelled as such.

### E. "Antisymmetric–symmetric tendency" terminology
The central phenomenological statement — that dD/dn has opposite signs in FM vs AFM — is a plain fact about the two curves in Figs. 3(a),(c). Calling this an "antisymmetric–symmetric tendency" introduces an adjective with no prior literature and no symmetry content (it is not a parity operation on the Hamiltonian). A reader arriving without the prior paper [22] will be puzzled. The paper does not offer a physical *mechanism* for the opposite slopes (e.g., in terms of how the on-site magnetic moment and the inter-edge exchange J_12 separately respond to n_doping). A single figure showing J(q) as a function of n_doping, extracted from the same Heisenberg mapping, would turn the conclusion from an observation into an explanation.

### F. Why compute FM at all?
The AFM phase is the energetically lower one for the entire doping range shown. The FM configuration is metastable at every doping point. The paper does not justify why a metastable phase's stiffness is a physically relevant quantity. One plausible answer — "it corresponds to a possible pinned state with an external field or substrate" — is not given.

### G. Minor points
- The paper claims "as predicted previously by the supercell calculation" for the *magnitude* of *D*. The supercell calculation already gave that number to high precision; the novelty here is the *doping/width dependence*, not the non-doped stiffness. The abstract should be restructured so as not to claim the non-doped result as a headline.
- Eq. (17) drops one of the two magnon branches to halve the computation, citing Ehsanerger [31]. No error estimate is given for this approximation. In a finite-ribbon the two inter-edge branches can differ because the C₂ symmetry relating them is only exact in the bulk periodic limit; a check on the discarded branch would be cheap.
- No statement of the basis/cut-off convergence of *D* itself (the basis parameters are given, but not an N_basis → *D* or E_cut → *D* convergence table).
- The English is mostly clear but a few sentences are awkward ("To reach the intentions", "the rest of the discussions is organized") and the phrase "with similar tendencies when the doping is taken into account" in the abstract is vague.
- The paper does not mention that several subsequent studies have found pristine ZGNR magnetism to be a GGA artefact (e.g., studies showing the AFM edge state requires a substantial on-site U; see e.g., the follow-up of the Refs. [13–15] Hubbard-model line of work). In 2019 (submission date) this was already a known sensitivity and it would have strengthened the paper to acknowledge it explicitly rather than only referring to "DFT calculation … could be either underestimated or overestimated".

## Constructive suggestions for the authors

1. Replace "proportional to N" with the observed N^α scaling with α fit to the three points (α ≈ 1.5 ± 0.3 from Table 1), or compute N=8 and N=14 to pin it.
2. Add a DFT+U calculation at U = 3–4 eV (standard for C in this context is not really defined; one can also do a Hubbard-model cross-check in the spirit of Ref. [13]) to bracket the GGA-only value. This is the single highest-value change.
3. Show J_11(q), J_12(q) extracted from the Heisenberg mapping vs n_doping. This turns "antipodal slopes" into an explanatory claim.
4. Include the discarded (inter-edge) magnon branch for at least one q point so that the Eq. (17) approximation is quantified.
5. Provide the quadratic-vs-quartic *D* offset, and the q-point spacing used in Figs. 2, 3, 4.
6. Justify or trim the FM-configuration calculation.

## Bottom line
The work is technically sound for what it claims to be — a methodological demonstration that the frozen-magnon GBT works in LCPAO for ZGNR and a first report of the non-doped spin stiffness in that code base, plus a short study of doping and width dependence. The non-doped result is consistent with prior supercell work and the new information is the doping and width trends. Those trends are real within the GGA uniform-background-charge model, but several of the paper's qualitative claims ("proportional to width", "antisymmetric–symmetric" as if it were a deeper symmetry) outrun the data, and the most important systematic error (functional dependence and absence of U) is not addressed. The paper reads as a solid methodological note aimed at a community that uses OpenMX, not as a strong physics statement about ZGNR spintronics.

## Review of "Carrier-induced Antisymmetric–symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness in Zigzag Graphene Nanoribbons" (Prayitno & Ishii, arXiv:1907.12180)

### What the paper does
The authors compute the spin stiffness *D* of zigzag graphene nanoribbons (6-, 10-, and 12-ZGNR) in ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) edge states using the generalized Bloch theorem (frozen-magnon / flat-spiral approach) with LCPAO/OpenMX (GGA, 90×1×1 k-points, C(2s2p, 4.0 a.u.)/H(2s1p, 6.0 a.u.), 150 Ry). They map ΔE(q, θ) onto a Heisenberg Hamiltonian, fit E = Dq² near q = 0, and then track *D* and the edge magnetic moment under weak electron/hole doping (±0.01 e/cell, via Fermi-level shift) and ribbon width. Main numerics: D_FM(10) = 2966, D_AFM(10) = 3583 meVÅ²; D increases roughly linearly with width; FM shows an antisymmetric doping response, AFM a symmetric one.

### What is done well
- **The method itself is set up competently.** The flat-spiral constraint (fixed θ = π/2), the double-index Heisenberg mapping, and the reduction to a single on-site exchange constant using edge-equivalence (their Eq. 16→19, following the Essenberger et al. logic) are handled correctly, and the Heisenberg-inclusion motivation is properly argued against the Yazyev–Katsnelson 320 vs. 2100 meVÅ² comparison.
- **Honesty about the frozen-magnon caveat.** The introduction explicitly concedes that for low-moment systems (µ ≈ 0.28 µB) Stoner excitations are not negligible and that DFT may overestimate *D* the way Ni does. That is the right scientific posture and it should stay in the abstract, not just the introductions.
- **A real, if small, systematic result:** the sign asymmetry of the doping response between FM and AFM is a concrete, potentially testable statement, and the width trend (Table 1) is internally consistent.

### Main concerns

1. **Convincing power of the q → 0 fit.** Because the constrained moments collapse quickly with q (Fig. 2(a) shows them dropping to ~0.02 µB near q ≈ 0.6 Å⁻¹), the usable window is q ≲ 0.08 Å⁻¹, and the stiffness is extracted from E = Dq² fits over a handful of points at the meV level on top of a constrained-energy surface. I see no error bars on D, no statement of the fit residual, and no comparison of the quadratic fits against the fourth-order procedure used in your prior paper [22] *for the same data*. The authors only say the quadratic fit has "higher fitting error" but never quantify how much D would shift. Given D_AFM/D_FM ≈ 1.2 and the values sit 40–75% above the Yazyev–Katsnelson reference, this uncertainty has to be nailed down numerically before the paper is convincing.

2. **Internal inconsistency in the headline doping claim.** For the AFM state the body says "As the hole (electron) doping increases, the spin stiffness reduces (reduces)" (p. 8) and the conclusion says it "reduces (reduces)" — but the abstract, Fig. 3(c), and the paper's own "symmetric tendency" language (symmetric under e/hole exchange) suggest the electron branch *rises*, mirroring the FM state's antisymmetric one. A "symmetric" and an "antisymmetric" tendency are the paper's central contrast, so which branch does what must be stated unambiguously once, and Fig. 3 is far too compressed (the doping axis spans only ±0.01 e/cell) to resolve it. As written, I cannot be sure what is claimed.

3. **The values vs. literature need more than "same order."** Your 10-ZGNR numbers are ≈40% (FM) to ≈70% (AFM) above Yazyev–Katsnelson. The paper invokes "same order" and "as predicted previously by Edwards and Katsnelson," but never attributes the systematic offset — e.g., PAO basis vs. plane waves, GGA vs. the reference PBE setting, H-termination treatment, or width. One converged-basis check or a statement of why the discrepancy is expected would substantially strengthen this.

4. **The doping window is unrealistically small and is presented as a lever with more weight than it bears.** ±0.01 e/cell is a few ×10⁻⁴ of electron density, the sort of chemical-potential shift a gate sets locally, not "doping." The conclusion's "introducing the doping can control the spin stiffness of N-ZGNR" overstates what a monotonic curve over an extremely small, undifferentiated window establishes. Extending to larger carrier concentrations (which, the paper itself notes, kills ZGNR edge magnetism) or mapping the response onto realistic gate conditions would make the device-relevance claims credible.

5. **The "close relationship between spin stiffness and magnetic moment" is a tautology, not a finding, as stated.** Within their Heisenberg mapping, *D* ∝ M² · Re[Ã J] follows algebraically; correlating two projections of the same energy surface shows nothing about *why* doping reweights the exchange. If the intended message is that doping changes the exchange pattern on the edges (e.g., via the edge-localized DOS being depleted asymmetrically for hole vs. electron doping, or the spin-polarized edge states in FM vs. AFM), the paper should argue that explicitly — a band/DOS analysis of the doped edge states is missing entirely. Without it the most physically interesting result is asserted but not explained.

6. **The width-scaling interpretation is speculative.** The final sentence of the conclusion, "the distance of two magnetic carbon atoms at the different edges in the unit cell determines the high or low spin stiffness," is not supported by any analysis — the inter-edge *J₁₂* is in principle computed (it enters Eq. 16) but never reported. Also, the near-linear D(N) for N = 6, 10, 12 could simply be a size effect (D should saturate for a quasi-1D edge magnet); with three points and no error estimate, "proportional" is strong wording.

7. **No validation.** No experiment exists for ZGNR spin stiffness (the authors acknowledge), but there is also no cross-check against an independent method (time-dependent DFT / spin-wave Green's function, random-phase approximation, or an ab initio exchange-coupling fit to a Hubbard-like model) and no comparison of the *Doping* response to any Hubbard-model calculation (refs. [13–15] in their own list). For a frozen-magnon result on a weakly magnetic, 1D system, that is a lot of trust placed in a single, known-to-be-semi-classical method.

### Minor points
- Eq. (1) in the introduction has what looks like a transcription slip (two terms "sin θᵢ" where the first should be "cos θᵢ"); Eq. (16) has a stray "Re" in "− ReJ˜q₂₂" and missing superscripts that suggest the LaTeX went through a rough path. A careful editorial pass is needed — these go right on the paper's core mathematics.
- The doping axis of Figs. 3–4 (±0.01 e/cell) makes the plots effectively undecidable as printed; at minimum annotate the exact values.
- "This situation may be hold for the ZGNR case" (p. 3), "our last paper," and similar phrasing need tightening.

### Bottom line
This is a competent, correctly-motivated extension of the frozen-magnon/GBT machinery to ZGNR with doping and width, and the FM/AFM asymmetry in the doping response is a legitimate result worth having. But the numerical core rests on a very short, uncharacterized q → 0 fit; the headline "symmetric/antisymmetric" statement is contradicted between the body and the abstract; the quantitative offset from the reference value is never explained; and the physical interpretation of *why* doping acts differently on the two states is absent. These are fixable issues — error bars and a basis/parameter consistency check for D, a corrected and unambiguous doping discussion, a small DOS-level analysis of the doped edge states, and a softened scaling claim would move this to a solid JPSJ-grade letter. As it stands, I would not sign off on the physical claims without those additions.

The paper targets a legitimate result (doping dependence of spin stiffness in FM/AFM zigzag graphene nanoribbons) and communicates it, but it arrives at a lower level of polish than the IOP House style (which the reference format clearly expects). The central compound "Antisymmetric-symmetric" is unusual in a title. The hyphen suggests the two words form a single concept, whereas they are contrasted (FM is antisymmetric, AFM is symmetric). More natural phrasing would be something like *Asymmetric and symmetric doping response of spin stiffness in zigzag graphene nanoribbons* — naming the physical variable (doping) and the contrast (asymmetric vs. symmetric). ### Structural and Argumentative Concerns

1. **No experimental comparison.** The introduction acknowledges ("the calculated spin stiffness via DFT calculation strongly needs verification from the experiment") but the paper never provides any experimental context for the ZGNR results. In a journal article (as opposed to an arXiv preprint), this would be a significant omission.

2. **Generalization from three data points.** The claim that spin stiffness is "proportional to the ribbon width" is made from N=6, 10, 12. The data in Table 1 (2465, 2966, 3175 for FM; 3320, 3583, 3745 for AFM) show sub-linear behaviour (D for N=12 is less than proportional to 12/6=2× the N=6 value). "Proportional" is too strong; "increases roughly linearly" or "increases monotonically" would be more accurate.

3. **The key result (asymmetric vs. symmetric doping responses) is stated but not physically explained.** The paper identifies the trend, notes that spin stiffness and magnetic moment follow the same pattern, and attributes this to "the mapping of the energy of Heisenberg Hamiltonian onto the total energy difference". This is a tautology in the context of the method (of course they follow the same pattern, since they are literally the same quantity via Eq. 20). A real physical mechanism (e.g., how hole doping selectively affects the edge states that carry different exchange couplings in FM vs. AFM) would make the paper substantially stronger. As written, the physical explanation is absent.

4. **The paper is effectively all model+derivation (section 2) and one paragraph of results.** A Discussion comparing the FM/AFM asymmetry to the known edge-state physics of ZGNR (edge spin polarization, sublattice selectivity, etc.) would be expected in a journal article.

---

### Summary Assessment

As an editorial matter, this paper requires a full language edit and probably a structural reorganization before it is in a publishable form. The scientific claim is stated but under-explained, the figures are weak in information content for the specific claims being made, and multiple small grammar/terminology errors give a low-polish impression throughout. The core physics result (doping-dependent asymmetric vs. symmetric spin stiffness trends for FM vs. AFM ZGNR) is a plausible and specific finding in this niche sub-field, but as presented, the argumentation does not yet fully support the claims made in the Conclusions, particularly the generalization to "N-ZGNR".

## Critique of "Carrier-Induced Antisymmetric-symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness in Zigzag Graphene Nanoribbons"

### What the paper does

The authors compute the 1D spin-wave stiffness of hydrogen-passivated N-zigzag graphene nanoribbons (N = 6, 10, 12) with OpenMX (LCPAO, GGA-PBE), using the generalized Bloch theorem / frozen-magnon approach implemented by the group in a previous paper [22]. They build ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) flat-spiral magnon configurations for the two edges with a constraint scheme, map the small-angle total-energy differences onto a Heisenberg Hamiltonian, and fit E = Dq² to obtain D. Headline results: very large D (2966 / 3583 meV Å² for FM / AFM, N = 10, comparable to or above Yazyev–Katsnelson's PRL-2008 value of 2100 meV Å²), AFM > FM stiffness, D increasing with ribbon width, and opposite (antisymmetric vs. symmetric) responses to hole/electron doping in the FM vs. AFM states.

### What the paper does well

1. **Sound core methodology.** The frozen-magnon/GBT machinery is applied correctly: the derivation from Eq. (3) through the eigenvalue problem (Eq. 15), the ± branch structure of Eq. (16), and the energy-difference form of Eq. (20) (ℏω = (μ_B/M)ΔE/sin²θ) are internally consistent, and I verified that Eq. (20) reproduces Eq. (17) under the θ₁ = θ₂ = θ specialization. Working in the deep long-wavelength limit (q ≤ 0.07 Å⁻¹ ≪ 0.7 Å⁻¹ half-zone) is the right choice.
2. **Direct DFT→stiffness mapping.** Because they extract D from total-energy differences rather than fitted exchange constants J_ij, the final numbers are more robust than the (heuristic) J_ij narrative suggests.
3. **Reasonable agreement with prior art.** The magnitude of D (3–5× larger than the 3d metals, ~1.5× Yazyev–Katsnelson) is physically plausible given the large π-system energy scale relative to the small (0.28 μ_B) edge moments, and the AFM > FM result is consistent with the fact that D tracks the (larger) AFM edge moment.

### Major methodological weaknesses

1. **No uncertainty quantification anywhere.** This is the paper's central flaw. Every D value comes from a quadratic fit through ~11 points of frozen-magnon energy differences with no error bars, no fit residuals, no q-window sensitivity analysis, and — critically — **the frozen angle θ is never stated**. With M ≈ 0.28 μ_B and ω(q = 0.07) ≈ 15 meV, ΔE = Mℏω sin²θ is only ~0.04 meV for θ = 0.1 rad, and ~10⁻³ meV at the smallest q = 0.01 Å⁻¹, i.e., at or below SCF precision. A larger θ (needed for numerical robustness) degrades the small-θ assumption the derivation rests on. How they reconciled these two demands is unreported, and it controls the accuracy of every number in the paper. This is also exactly where a quartic fit (used in Ref. [22]) would matter; the authors hand-wave that "the fitting error of quadratic dispersion is usually higher than that of the fourth-order dispersion" but give no numbers and no cross-check.
2. **An explanation contradicted by the authors' own data.** The paper's explanation for D tracking M (p. 9) is "when the exchange constant J_ij remains unchanged... the spin stiffness proportionally depends on the total energy difference. Therefore, the spin stiffness and the magnetic moment should have the same tendency." Their data do not support proportionality:
   - FM, Fig. 3: D rises ~2900 → ~3100 meV Å² (+7%) under +0.01 e/cell doping while M_edgedge rises ~0.278 → 0.281 μ_B (+1.1%).
   - AFM, Fig. 3c: D drops ~3600 → ~2950 (−18%) under the same doping while M drops ~0.285 → 0.282 (−1%).
   - Width: D_AFM rises 3320 → 3745 (+13%) going N = 6 → 12 while the AFM moment rises only ~0.275 → 0.286 (+4%).
   If D ∝ M held at fixed J, the D/M ratio would be constant; it is not, in neither the doping nor the width direction. The "constant J_ij" claim is asserted, never checked, and visibly wrong. A plot of D/M (or D/M²) vs. doping and width, with a statement of what fraction of the stiffness change is moment vs. exchange driven, would either rescue or bury the paper's interpretation — in its current form the interpretation is unsupported.
3. **The doping study is a trend hunt in a tiny window with a suspicious feature.** Doping spans ±0.01 e per unit cell — ≈0.25 electrons per 100 C atoms for N = 10 — a range where "antisymmetric" (∝ δ) and "symmetric" (∝ |δ| or δ²) responses are hard to distinguish with 11 noisy points. Worse, the AFM stiffness shows a *sharp cusp* at δ = 0 in both Figs. 3c and 4c (a 20–25% drop for |δ| = 0.01). A cusp at exactly zero — and not merely a maximum — is not analyzed at all: is it a real signature of the half-filled edge states, an artifact of the quadratic fit near the fit-edge, or SCF noise? This is arguably the most interesting feature of the whole paper and it gets one sentence. One would also like at least one physical explanation of *why* holes and electrons couple to stiffness with opposite signs in FM but not AFM (e.g., edge-state filling vs. bandwidth effects) — the paper offers only the correlation (via M) that, as noted above, the data don't actually follow.
4. **Width scaling: "proportional" is too strong, and the mechanism is not discussed.** D_FM/N falls from 410 → 297 → 265 meV Å² per width unit (N = 6, 10, 12) — a clearly sub-linear, saturating trend, not proportionality. The authors' explanation ("the distance between two magnetic carbon atoms in the unit cell determines the high or low spin stiffness") is a restatement, not a mechanism. For two weakly cross-coupled edge spin chains one would expect D to *saturate* with width (two decoupled 1D chains contribute a 2× constant) plus a decaying edge–edge exchange correction; getting a sizeable width dependence at all deserves discussion (does it come from the inter-edge exchange length scale? from the moment?).
5. **Possible internal inconsistency to check.** Table 1 gives D_AFM(N = 6, δ = 0) = 3320 meV Å², but the N = 6 (triangle) AFM curve in Fig. 4c appears to peak near ≈ 3000 at zero doping. If I am reading the plot correctly, the table and the figure disagree by >10%; if not, the authors should ensure table and figure share one set of numbers.
6. **Quantitative caveats acknowledged but not developed.** The authors rightly note that DFT (GGA) values for systems with 0.3 μ_B moments can over- or under-estimate D (Stoner screening, PBE over-delocalization of the edge state) and that no experiment exists. But the paper then proceeds as if DFM/DAFM were numbers rather than estimates; there is no discussion of how large the GGA error plausibly is, no functional comparison, and no comparison of their N = 10 FM value with the actual number in Ref. [22] from which this work is a follow-up (with a different fit order, no less).

### Presentation

- The writing is rough in places and hinders clarity ("To reach the intentions", "drastically reduced for the high spiral vector", "the rest of the discussions is organized as follows"). The title and abstract bury the actual content — no number, the word "tendencies" three times, "antisymmetric-symmetric" undefined until page 8 — which is a real problem for a paper whose entire contribution is a subtle trend.
- Figures are low quality (default Xmgrace styling, small fonts) and none carry error bars; given the analysis above, error bars or at least a stated fit-quality measure are essential.
- Eq. (16)'s prose defines Re[Ĵ_q]¹¹ as "the exchange interaction between C atoms on one edge," which, if read literally as the bare on-edge coupling, makes the FM-mode argument leading to Eq. (17) incorrect (the uniform FM mode involves the total coupling); the tilde notation implies an effective coupling, and the text should say so explicitly to avoid the misreading.
- The q-dependence of the magnetic moment (Fig. 2a) extends right into the fitting window and is never quantified (what is the moment change over 0.01–0.07 Å⁻¹?); an M(q)-dependent prefactor in E = M(q)²θ²J can mimic curvature in a quadratic fit.

### Verdict

A competent and mostly internally consistent computational study with plausible, literature-consistent numbers, but incremental over both the authors' 2018 J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. paper and Yazyev–Katsnelson. The two headline "tendencies" (doping asymmetry between FM and AFM; the AFM cusp at zero) are the most interesting results and are the least well-analyzed: tiny doping window, no error analysis, no stated θ, and a physical explanation that the data visibly contradict. Fixed, that paper becomes good; as submitted, the results are suggestive rather than established. Publishable at a middling computational-physics venue with substantial revision of the analysis section.

## The paper's stated contribution, in my own words

The authors compute the spin stiffness of undoped and hole/electron-doped zigzag graphene nanoribbons (ZGNR) with frozen-magnon (Generalized Bloch Theorem) calculations in the LCPAO basis (OpenMX), using a constraint-scheme penalty functional to hold flat spiral states with the two ribbon-edge states ferromagnetic (FM) or antiferromagnetic (AFM). They map the computed magnon energies onto a Heisenberg Hamiltonian, fit a quadratic dispersion E = D q² to extract D_FM and D_AFM, and vary both the doping (via a Fermi-level shift) and the ribbon width (N = 6, 10, 12). Their headline claims are: (i) the spin stiffness stays very large (2966–3745 meV Å²), in line with Edwards–Katsnelson's prediction and the same order as Yazeyv–Katsnelson's 2100 meV Å²; (ii) the AFM edge state has a larger stiffness than FM and the two states respond oppositely to hole vs. electron doping ("antisymmetric" FM tendency, "symmetric" AFM tendency); (iii) the stiffness grows with ribbon width.

## What is new and what isn't

**Not new (standard method, explicitly built on cited prior work):**
- The GBT/frozen-magnon machinery itself, the Heisenberg mapping, and the relation D ∝ M are textbook frozen-magnon content (Sandratskii 1998; Halilov 1998; Yazeyv–Katsnelson PRL 2008), all properly cited, and the authors explicitly follow Y–K's argument that 3d-like systems need the Heisenberg model. Nothing new is derived in Eq. (1)–(15); Eq. (20) is just the L'Hôpital form of the standard stiffness expression.
- Doping ZGNR with a uniform background charge to control edge magnetism is exactly the setup of Yan et al. 2007 and Sawada et al. 2008/2014 — the *existence* of tunable magnetism in doped ZGNR is prior work; this paper only adds the stiffness number on top.
- The "spin stiffness is very high, much larger than 3d metals, and proportional to the magnetic moment" conclusion is already the Edwards–Katsnelson (2006) premise and the Yazeyv–Katsnelson (2008) result. Their numbers (2966/3583 meV Å²) are of the same order as Y–K's 2100 meV Å² — a consistency check, not a new physical prediction.
- The doping response of the *FM* state (increases toward hole doping) is already in the authors' own prior paper [22] (Prayitno & Ishii 2018), which the text admits and which used a fourth-order fit; here they merely re-derive it with a quadratic fit.

**Actually new (modest):**
- Including the AFM edge configuration in the frozen-magnon treatment and showing that its doping response is the *mirror image* of the FM one (electron doping hardens AFM, hole doping softens it). That contrast between the two configurations is the genuinely new quantitative finding, and it follows almost immediately from the established moment–stiffness proportionality once the AFM moment trend is known.
- A clean N = 6→10→12 width series for both configurations, showing D grows roughly linearly with width (i.e., it's set by the cross-edge separation T_m), and showing the doping trends are width-independent. This is incremental bookkeeping rather than a new scaling law.
- The methodological packaging — constraint-scheme flat spirals in OpenMX for a two-edge carbon system, with a reduced one-calculation route exploiting C₃v symmetry between the edges — is a useful practical recipe, but again standard.

**Weaknesses worth noting:** no experimental validation is possible (as the authors concede), the "antisymmetric/symmetric tendency" language is introduced but never made more precise than "opposite slopes in dD/dn," the width dependence is shown for only three widths and stated as proportional without fitting, and the paper never compares exchange-coupling maps (J_ij) against the Hubbard-model studies it cites [13–15].

## Bottom line

This is a competent, if routine, confirmation-and-extension paper: a standard frozen-magnon + Heisenberg pipeline applied to ZGNR, extending the authors' own 2018 FM-only study by adding the AFM edge state, the opposing doping response, and a small width series. The one substantive new ingredient (the mirror-image doping dependence of FM vs. AFM) is a direct consequence of known physics. Nothing here revises the Edwards–Katsnelson/Yazeyv–Katsnelson picture or enables a new application; it refines numbers within it.

# Methodology (reproduction-level)

## 1. Computational engine and spin-polarisation framework
- **Codes**: OpenMX (v24 — no version number is given), using the *linear combination of pseudo-atomic orbitals* (LCPAO) with norm-conserving pseudopotentials. The GBT implementation is the authors' own contribution (their prior paper [22]) — Bloch wavefunctions at an atomic site τ_i are expanded in LCPAO basis φ_iα, with the two sublattices appearing explicitly (Eq. 2).
- **Spin treatment**: collinear spin-polarised DFT; no spin–orbit coupling is mentioned anywhere in the paper.
- **xc functional**: "GGA" only — the specific functional (PBE, PW91, …) is **not stated**.

## 2. Model geometry
- Zigzag GNR, H-terminated, with ribbon width N (number of dimer lines across the width). First calculations use **N = 10**; the width dependence is then tested at **N = 6, 10, 12**.
- Translational period *x* of the unit cell is fixed by the experimental **graphite** lattice constant a = 2.46 Å. The vacuum direction (*y*) is fixed to > 25 Å. No N-dependent structural relaxation in any direction is reported — the nonmagnetic relaxed structure is used for all magnetic configurations.
- **Basis**: C: two radial s + two radial p functions, cutoff radius 4.0 a.u. H: two s + one p, 6.0 a.u. Plane-wave cutoff 150 Ry.
- **k-mesh**: 90 × 1 × 1 (periodic only along the ribbon axis) — no convergence curve or error estimate is shown for D versus k-mesh.
- **Doping** is modelled as a uniform background charge and kept at neutrality via a **Fermi-level shift** (no actual dopant atoms); the unit is "e cell" = one electron per unit cell of the ribbon cell.

## 3. Magnetic configurations
Two "flat spiral" configurations are built (Fig. 1):
- **FM**: both edges parallel, cone angle θ = π/2, φ₀¹ = φ₀² = 0.
- **AFM**: edges antiparallel, θ = π/2, φ₀¹ = 0, φ₀² = π.

The cone angle is locked to π/2 by a **constraint-scheme (penalty) functional** that pushes the local moment back to the initial direction whenever a self-consistent cycle tries to move it. The total energy is then computed self-consistently as φ rotates with a chosen small spiral vector **q** along the ribbon; the resulting E(q) is the input to the Heisenberg model.

## 4. Heisenberg mapping and frozen-magnon spin stiffness
1. The total energy of the classical magnet is written as a Heisenberg Hamiltonian E = E₀ − ½ Σᵢⱼ Jᵢⱼ M̄ᵢ·M̄ⱼ (Eq. 3), with Jᵢⱼ Fourier-transformed to J_**q**.
2. A small polar-angle deviation θ (eq. 6) yields Re[J_**q**^μν] = (1 / M_μ M_ν) ∂²E/∂θ_μ∂θ_ν|₀ (eq. 7–8).
3. The quantum Heisenberg equations of motion for M̄ lead to an eigenvalue problem (Eq. 14–15) whose eigenvectors are √(M_μ) θ_μ and whose eigenvalue is ħω_**q** — the frozen-magnon energy.
4. Because the unit cell has two magnetic carbon atoms (one per edge) with **assumed equal moments M₁ = M₂ = M**, the two-magnon energies reduce to ħω_q1 = μ_B M Re[J̃_**q**¹¹] and ħω_q2 = μ_B M Re[J^22_**q**] (Eq. 17). The cross-edge term Re[J^12_**q**] is **dropped on the ground that the distance between the two magnetic edge atoms in the unit cell is much larger than the intra-edge distance** — this is the key geometric simplification of the method.
5. A shortcut (different from Essenberg et al.): impose θ₁ = θ₂ = θ, give the total energy a closed form (Eq. 18), differentiate (Eq. 19), and take the θ → 0 limit with L'Hôpital's rule (Eq. 20):

  **ħω_**q** = (μ_B / M) · ΔE(**q**, θ) / sin²θ**

  where ΔE(**q**, θ) = E(**q**, θ) − E(0, θ). The paper invokes Eqs. 33–35 to claim this relation also holds beyond the small-θ limit but does not check it numerically.

6. **Spin stiffness D** for each configuration is obtained by a **quadratic fit** to the low-q magnon data, E = D q². The authors compute only the **lowest-q points** (roughly q ≲ 0.08 Å⁻¹, Fig. 2b); the high-q region (where the moment collapses, Fig. 2a) is excluded. **No error bars, no fit-quality metric, no convergence of D with respect to q-range or to k-mesh are reported** — and they acknowledge elsewhere that their prior paper [22] used a 4th-order fit and that the difference between the quadratic and 4th-order fits is a fitting error that is accepted for the present work.

## 5. Doping and width scans
- Doping window: −0.01 to +0.01 e cell, in 0.0025 steps, for N = 10 only. "e cell" is not precisely defined (the unit cell contains N × 2 magnetic C atoms plus H-terminators for N = 10 that is 20 C + H). The claim "magnetism in ZGNR disappears if the introduced doping is sufficiently high" is made **outside** this studied window.
- Width scan (N = 6, 10, 12) is only for the **undoped** case (Table 1); the doping dependence of D(N) is claimed to "also hold" (Fig. 4) but only the N = 10 doping data are actually plotted.

# Assumptions the methodology papers over

Each item below is a simplification in the paper that is asserted but not numerically or conceptually substantiated:

1. **Isotropic pair-exchange Heisenberg Hamiltonian only.** No Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya term, no single-ion anisotropy, no biquadratic, no RKKY beyond what is absorbed into the *effective* J. The zero gap in the dispersion at q → 0 is therefore baked in.

2. **No spin–orbit coupling anywhere in the DFT run**, which means the moment and the exchange are purely spin-polarised and D is not corrected for SOC-induced anisotropy. The paper itself concedes the DFT value may be "underestimated or overestimated" relative to experiment and that Stoner excitations cannot be neglected, but then proceeds without quantifying the error.

3. **Confinement-scheme pseudopotentials and the small C basis** (2 radial s + 2 radial p, 4.0 a.u.) with plane-wave 150 Ry and 90×1×1 k-mesh — **no convergence tests for D** with respect to basis, E_cut, or k-point density are given. The 2966/3583 meVÅ² values (Table 1, Fig. 2b) are presented as point values without any uncertainty.

4. **Constraint-scheme enforcement of θ = π/2 is classical** (no quantum S = 1/2, no spin-fluctuation, no zero-point renormalisation, no temperature). For a 2 p electron on C (S = 1/2) the mean-field frozen-magnon approximation is at the edge of its validity.

5. **Magnetic moments at the two edge sublattices are assumed equal in magnitude** (M₁ = M₂ = M), reducing the 2×2 eigensystem to two independent magnons (Eq. 7 → Eq. 17). For the AFM case this symmetry (the two edges being mirror-related) is reasonable geometrically but is not checked by reporting M₁ and M₂ separately.

6. **Cross-edge exchange J^12 is neglected** ("the distance of two magnetic carbon atoms at the different edges in the unit cell is much larger") — but the inter-edge distance in a narrow N = 6–10 GNR is comparable to the intra-edge spacing, and a J^12 term is exactly what would split the AFM magnon mode and could shift D. This assumption is asserted once and never tested.

7. **The second-order (quadratic) fit for D** in place of a higher-order dispersion. The authors explicitly note that their earlier paper used a 4th-order fit and that the difference is a fitting error; they accept this without quantifying it.

8. **Low-q-only sampling**: only q ≲ 0.08 Å⁻¹ is used for the fit. The q-range in which the magnon is well-defined (Fig. 2a shows the moment collapses near q ≈ 0.4 Å⁻¹) is not characterised, and the convergence of D as the lower-q cutoff is varied is not reported.

9. **"e cell" is not rigorously defined** (per how many C-atom pairs? per the periodic cell with how many H?). The reader cannot independently compute whether a +0.01 doping is 0.01 e per edge C, per lattice C, or per C + H in the cell.

10. **Uniform-background-charge model for doping.** Real GNRs have localised edge states and inhomogeneous electronic structure at the Fermi level; shifting the Fermi level in a DFT run with a uniform background charge assumes a homogeneous density-of-states response. The paper never verifies that the resulting charge distribution is actually uniform.

11. **The doping window of ±0.01 e cell is narrow**. The paper invokes "magnetism in ZGNR disappears if the introduced doping is sufficiently high" without showing any data beyond this window — the claim is qualitative, not computed.

12. **Graphite lattice constant used for all N (6, 10, 12)**. The equilibrium lattice parameter of a narrow GNR is not graphite's, and D is sensitive to exchange at the edge; a single a = 2.46 Å for all ribbons is a simplification.

13. **Vacuum of > 25 Å** is fixed without a convergence check on D versus slab separation.

14. **No quantitative comparison to the Yazyev–Katsnelson supercell value** (2100 meVÅ²). The paper states it is "higher but still in the same order" without giving relative error or a controlled comparison (same N, same functional, same k-mesh, same basis would be needed).

17. **The terminology "antisymmetric/symmetric" trend** in the doping axis is imprecise: the FM D is monotonic (roughly increasing) in both + and − doping (Fig. 3a is *not* an odd function), and the AFM D is roughly even (Fig. 3c). The labels are heuristic, not the mathematical definitions of odd/even functions, and the contrast is not quantified.

This is a standard frozen-magnon convention but it is not flagged.

# Evaluation of "Carrier-induced Antisymmetric-symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness in Zigzag Graphene Nanoribbons" (Prayitno & Ishii, arXiv:1907.12180v1)

## Understanding of the paper's logical argument

**Goal.** Compute the spin stiffness *D* of zigzag graphene nanoribbons (ZGNR) with both ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) edge magnetizations, as a function of hole/electron doping and ribbon width N. Use the frozen-magnon approach of the generalized Bloch theorem (GBT) within the LCPAO method (OpenMX code), avoiding the Heisenberg-model construction.

**Method chain (the core logic):**
1. Apply a flat spiral configuration: fix cone angle θ = π/2 with a constraint-scheme (penalty) functional, and let the azimuthal angle φ rotate with a small wavevector **q** — a standard frozen-magnon setup.
2. Assume the low-energy total energy expands in a Heisenberg form (Eq. 3); Fourier-transform the exchange J_ij to reciprocal space (Eq. 4) and use its symmetry properties (Eq. 5).
3. Derive the magnon eigenvalue equation (Eqs. 9–15). For the two-site edge model this reduces to a clean dispersion (Eq. 16).
4. Then take a shortcut: because the inter-edge carbon distance is large in the real cell, approximate each magnon energy as μ_B M Re[Ĩ_q1^{11}] etc. (Eq. 17), set θ1 = θ2 = θ, and obtain the energy of the uniform-rotation configuration (Eq. 18) from which the exchange constant is extracted as a second derivative (Eq. 19).
5. Finally, map the magnon energy back onto the computable self-consistent total-energy difference ΔE(**q**, θ), yielding the working formula
   ħω_q = (μ_B/M) ΔE(**q**,θ)/sin²θ (Eq. 20).
   So *D* is obtained by fitting the low-q magnon energies to a quadratic dispersion E = Dq² — with Eq. 20 as the bridge from the Heisenberg expression to DFT total energies.

**Findings.**
- For N = 10: D_FM ≈ 2966 meVÅ², D_AFM ≈ 3583 meVÅ² — both much larger than 3d transition-metal values (Ni ≈ 700), consistent with, but higher than, Yazev & Katsnelson's supercell value (2100 meVÅ² for FM). AFM (lower total energy) has the larger stiffness, so the ground state carries the highest stiffness.
- Doping produces opposite trends: FM stiffness *rises* with hole doping, AFM stiffness *falls* — an antisymmetric (FM) vs symmetric (AFM) tendency, which is the paper's headline claim. Magnetic moment tracks stiffness in both cases.
- Stiffness grows roughly monotonically with width N (Table 1: 6→10→12 widths, D_FM 2465→2966→3175; D_AFM 3320→3583→3745 meVÅ²), attributed to the large inter-edge carbon separation (a long-periodicity cell) that controls the exchange.

That is a coherent, internally consistent storyline: a frozen-magnon GBT formalism, a reduction to a practical total-energy formula, and a clean set of scaling/trend results.

## Feedback — strengths

- The formal development (Sec. 2) is careful and correct as far as I can check: the standard frozen-magnon / GBT derivation is laid out step by step, the two-site reduction and the mapping to ΔE (Eqs. 17–20) are sensible, and the use of L'Hôpital's theorem to recover the second-derivative form is a nice touch.
- The headline physical result — opposite (anti/symmetric) stiffness–doping trends for FM vs AFM — is a genuinely interesting and reasonably specific observation, and tying it to the ribbon width is a useful practical handle for spintronics.
- The comparison is framed honestly against both Edwards–Katsnelson's analytic prediction and Yazev–Katsnelson's supercell number.

## Feedback — weaknesses and concerns

1. **No experimental validation, acknowledged but unaddressed.** The authors themselves concede (p.2–3) that DFT spin stiffness in low-moment systems like ZGNR "could be either underestimated or overestimated because Stoner excitations cannot be neglected," and that even for fcc Ni DFT overestimates. Their own numbers are *higher* than the more carefully treated supercell calculation (2966 vs 2100 meVÅ² for FM), which is exactly the overestimation direction the authors warn about elsewhere in the field. This should be discussed quantitatively rather than waved at.

2. **The quadratic fit is the single biggest source of error and is treated lightly.** The stiffness is a *fit*, not a first-principles number: Eq. 20 uses the *small*-θ (second-derivative) form, yet the authors admit the frozen magnons force them to use q very close to 0 (Fig. 2) because the moment collapses rapidly as q grows. Fitting a quadratic over a very restricted low-q window, then extrapolating the stiffness, is fragile. The paper briefly acknowledges the difference between quadratic and their earlier fourth-order fit but does not quantify the resulting uncertainty in D, and no error bars appear anywhere — on the fits, on Δe/cell doping tolerance, or across the q-grid.

3. **Averaging over k-point sampling is a concern for a low-dimensional system.** A 90×1×1 k-grid is used, but the spin stiffness of a 1D-like object is sensitive to how the BZ is sampled along the ribbon axis; convergence of D with respect to k-point density (both axial density and the transverse 1×1 collapse) is not demonstrated. For a quantity extracted from a low-q fit, k-convergence is nearly as important as q-convergence.

4. **Physical plausibility of the frozen flat-spiral is not examined for the AFM case.** For FM the uniform azimuthal rotation is natural; for AFM the "flat spiral" is set with φ1=0, φ2=π (Eqs. near 16), i.e. a Néel-type twist. Whether a *rigid* spiral at fixed cone angle is a good variational trial for the AFM ground state — particularly once doping pushes the moment toward collapse (Fig. 3d/4d) — is not discussed. Near the magnetic transition the frozen-magnon ansatz is least trustworthy, and the steepest doping dependence happens precisely there.

5. **The width scaling claim is under-evidenced.** "Spin stiffness is proportional to ribbon width" is supported by three width points (6, 10, 12) — insufficient to distinguish linear from, say, saturation or a 1/N correction typical of quantum-well spin stiffness (where D often scales with the number of chains *per* unit length, which here is not obviously N). A wider N range, or an explicit N vs N−1/width scaling, would be needed.

6. **Presentation/consistency nits.**
   - "very high spin stiffness," "the calculations yield D = …" — no units/figures carry any uncertainty, and the text is grammatically rough in places (e.g. "is the spin-wave excitations in the GN R" p.1).
   - The mapping in Eq. 20 divides by M; as doping-driven moment reduction brings M down (Fig. 3b/3d), the same ΔE error is amplified by 1/M, so the doping-induced stiffness trends may be partly a *numerical artifact* of the small-M division. This is a real risk the paper should confront rather than attribute entirely to the moment–stiffness "close relationship."
   - Figure 1(c) uses a T_m notation and double-index R_{m+μ} that is only briefly introduced; the double-index Fourier convention could confuse readers and is worth stating explicitly with a worked example.

### Net assessment
The physics logic is sound and the derivation is competent; the specific "anti/symmetric doping tendency" is the one thing here a reader will remember. But the *numbers* behind the claims carry unquantified fit and sampling uncertainty, there is no experimental anchor, and two specific mechanisms (1/M amplification near the magnetic transition, and the near-0 q restriction) could distort exactly the trends the paper is selling. As written this reads as a solid computational study making claims somewhat beyond what its own error accounting justifies.

## [1907.12180v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


## Weakness review: *Carrier-induced Antisymmetric-symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness in Zigzag Graphene Nanoribbons*

### The single weakest link

The chain runs: (1) self-consistent total energies $E(\mathbf q,\theta)$ from the constrained frozen-magnon calculations → (2) **assumption that these energies are the Heisenberg energy with *fixed* exchange constants $J_{ij}$ and *fixed* moment magnitude $M$** → (3) quadratic fit $E=Dq^2$ to read off $D$ → (4) the headline "antisymmetric (FM) vs. symmetric (AFM) tendency" of $D$ with doping.

The break is at step (2), concretely the reduction that produces Eq. (18)–(20) and the fit $E=Dq^2$. It presumes a frozen magnon is a pure precession of a **constant-magnitude** spin: only the cone angle $\theta$ rotates while $|M|$ is held, so $E=E_0+M^2(1-\theta^2)J_0+M^2\theta^2\text{Re}[\hat J_0]$ and $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q}\propto \Delta E\, M\, q^2/\sin^2\theta$, i.e. $D$ is proportional to $M^2$ times a *doping-independent* $J$.

**The authors' own data refute the fixed-$M$ premise.** Figure 2(a) shows the *self-consistent* magnetic moment collapsing from $\sim0.28\,\mu_B$ to $\sim0.1\,\mu_B$ as $q$ goes $0\to0.4\ \text{\AA}^{-1}$ — the moment shrinks by a factor of ~3, so what the constraint scheme holds fixed is the *direction*, not the vector. A q-dependent moment $M(q)$ means the true frozen-magnon energy scales as $M(q)^2\theta^2[\cdots]$, so $E(\mathbf q)$ is **not** a pure $Dq^2$: the $\text{Re}[\hat J]$ prefactor itself drifts with $q$, and the curvature $\partial^2E/\partial q^2|_{q=0}$ (the actual spin stiffness) is contaminated by $d M^2/dq$. A quadratic fit over any finite window (Fig. 2(b) spans $0\to\sim0.07\ \text{\AA}^{-1}$, where the moment is visibly falling) returns an *effective* $D$ that depends on the fit window and on the uncontrolled moment drift — not the long-wavelength stiffness the paper claims. The paper's own mitigation ("set $q$ very close to 0 so the moment is nearly constant") is only approximate and does not remove higher-order $q$ terms that a changing $M$ generates.

**Why this makes the central conclusion un-entailed by the data.** The paper's flagship result — that the FM and AFM stiffnesses respond to doping in *opposite* ("anti/symmetric") ways, and that the moment "follows the tendency" of the stiffness (Fig. 3a vs 3c, 3b vs 3d) — is extracted from how the *fitted* $D$ moves with doping. But under the step-(2) assumption $D\propto M^2 J$ with $J$ taken constant, $D$ and $M$ are *mechanically forced* to co-vary with doping; the two panels of Fig. 3 are then the same plotted twice, and the "tendency" is the content of the assumption, not an emergent property of the data. The raw input (the parallel shifts of the moment and of the fitted $D$ with doping) does not, on its own, entail a carrier-induced reordering of the stiffness — it is exactly what $D\propto M^2$ predicts if $J$ is doping-invariant. No check is made that $J_{ij}$ (which the model treats as a fixed Heisenberg constant mapped from one configuration) is in fact insensitive to the doping, to the reduced moment, or that Stoner/quasiparticle excitations — which the authors themselves flag as non-negligible in such a low-moment, weakly magnetic system (the $\sim0.28\,\mu_B$ edge moment, and their §2 caveat that DFT spin stiffness in weakly magnetic systems "could be either underestimated or overestimated") — do not break the pure spin-wave/Heisenberg picture that the extraction of $D$ requires.

In short: the step where the total-energy difference is mapped onto a **fixed-$J$, fixed-$M$ quadratic dispersion** is the point at which the derived $D$ — and therefore the "antisymmetric/symmetric tendency" and the $D\propto N$ scaling — stops following from the numbers, because (i) the moment is demonstrably $q$-dependent, invalidating the fixed-$M$ frozen-magnon/quadratic-dispersion assumption, and (ii) the headline anti/symmetric comparison then re-treads the $D\propto M^2$ assumption rather than establishing it. Compounding this, there is no experimental anchor: the paper explicitly states the results "strongly need verification from the experiment."

### General notes
Strengths: clean and self-consistent methodological exposition (GBT-in-LCPAO, constraint-scheme frozen magnon, double-indexed Heisenberg mapping), physically reasonable magnitudes, and a coherent extension of the authors' prior work [22] into the doping and ribbon-width parameter space. But the analysis is largely incremental (existing methods, a single material and one doping window), it relies on a single code with no cross-check, and the one genuinely new-sounding claim rests on the assumption flagged above.

## [1907.12180v1-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


I've read the full paper (Prayitno & Ishii, "Carrier-induced Antisymmetric-symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness in Zigzag Graphene Nanoribbons," arXiv:1907.12180v1, 11 pp.). Here is my critique of the writing and framing, followed by what an adversarial reviewer would open with.

## 1. Sentences that overclaim beyond the evidence

**"The rare property that has not been fully elucidated is the spin-wave excitations in the GNR."** (Introduction)
The paper itself then cites Yazyev & Katsnelson's supercell calculation, which *already* computed spin-wave excitations in ZGNRs. The framing both mischaracterizes the state of the field and sets up a contribution the paper does not deliver (it is a re-derivation of their number by a different method).

**"The magnetic moment of carbon atom at the edge is about 0.28 µB, two times smaller than that of fcc nickel… so the spin stiffness of ZGNR should be much larger than that of fcc nickel."** (Introduction)
This is the paper's central methodological justification, and it is a non-sequitur. Small moment → large *J* → large *D* is asserted, not derived (one would need to argue about *J* from a model, e.g., Stoner/itinerant estimates, which are not given). Worse, it is circular: the "small moment ⇒ large D" argument is being used to motivate trusting the Heisenberg mapping, but the large *D* is precisely the *output* of that mapping, and the mapping's validity for low-moment carbon is the very thing the next paragraph hedges about ("could be either underestimated or overestimated"). An argument that cannot be falsified by the calculation it justifies is not an argument.

**"Therefore, the spin stiffness and the magnetic moment should have the same tendency."** (Section 3, width/doping discussion)
This presents an artifact of the definition as a physical finding. *D* is obtained from ΔE, and the magnon formula (Eq. 20) is ω_q = μ_B ΔE/M sin²θ, so any correlation between D and M is largely inherited from the ansatz. The text first hedges ("almost follow the tendencies"), then escalates the same observation to a "should" in the closing paragraph, dressing a tautology as physics.

**"This means that the spin stiffness of the most stable configuration in 10-ZGNR should have the highest spin stiffness."** and, in the conclusions, **"This implies that the most stable configuration in N-ZGNR should have the highest spin stiffness."**
Two configurations, one width, zero doping → a universal-sounding rule ("most stable ⇒ highest D"). There is no evidence that edge stability ordering and stiffness ordering are coupled except by one coincident data pair.

**"It suggests that the distance of two magnetic carbon atoms at the different edges in the unit cell determines the high or low spin stiffness."** (Conclusions)
"Determines" from three widths (N = 6, 10, 12) showing an approximately monotonic trend. "Trends with" or "largely reflects" would be defensible; "determines" is not.

**"It seems that introducing the doping can control the spin stiffness of N-ZGNR."** (Conclusions)
The "control" is over ±0.01 e/cell, where D moves by a few percent of ~3000–3600 meVÅ² (≈ ±100 meVÅ²). "Control" is a strong word for a small, near-linear, sub-dopant-concentration sensitivity, and the FM/AFM asymmetry the title advertises is a qualitative direction, not a tunable knob.

**"We found a very high spin stiffness, as predicted previously by the supercell calculation."** (Abstract)
The DFT value is *higher* than the supercell value (2966–3583 vs 2100 meVÅ² — a 40–70% gap), and the body papers over this as "higher but still in the same order." Calling the *new* (unverified, larger) number the one "predicted" by the prior first-principles work inverts the evidentiary relationship: the new result should have to be reconciled with the prediction, not the reverse.

**Implicit overclaim in the Eq. 17 reduction:** "the distance … is much larger than the length of periodic unit cell … we are allowable to approximate" and "two magnetic C atoms … are equivalent… M₁ = M₂ = M." Edge-to-edge distance for N = 6–12 is only a few times the unit-cell length, and it is *inter-edge* exchange that physically separates the FM and AFM edge states; dropping J₁₂ and assuming equal edge moments is an unverified and qualitatively important assumption, stated as a given. A competent, well-organized DFT study of spin stiffness in zigzag graphene nanoribbons (ZGNR) using the generalized Bloch theorem / frozen-magnon scheme within the Heisenberg Hamiltonian, extended here to hole-electron doping (Fermi-level shift) and ribbon width (N = 6, 10, 12). The central finding — that under symmetric doping the ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) edge configurations drive the spin stiffness in *opposite* directions (so the *sign* of the trend flips with the edge configuration) — is a real and interesting result. But the finding is reported as a trend, not explained mechanistically, the headline effect is small in the shown doping range, and one stated conclusion is contradicted by the paper's own Table 1.

### Strengths
- The method is the right tool for the job: including the Heisenberg model in the frozen-magnon/GBT calculation (per Yazyev & Katsnelson) is the correct way to get a trustworthy spin stiffness, and the LCPAO/OpenMX implementation is described adequately (Eqs. 12–20, constraint/cone-angle-π/2 scheme).
- The values (D ≈ 2966 meVÅ² FM, 3583 meVÅ² AFM, N=10, non-doped) comfortably exceed 3d transition metals (~700 meVÅ²), consistent with the Edwards–Katsnelson prediction for sp carriers, and the AFM (more stable) > FM ordering is internally consistent.
- Clear paper structure; the magnon-energy vs q fits (Fig. 2–4) are legible and the moment-vs-q collapse (a nice observation: moment drops sharply with q, justifying the q→0 limit) is handled well.

### Main weaknesses (why this is only borderline)
- **The signature "antisymmetric-symmetric tendency" has no microscopic explanation.** The paper repeatedly points to "the mapping of the Heisenberg energy onto the total-electronic-energy difference … proportional to the magnetic moment when J_ij is fixed," which is a one-line restatement, not a mechanism. The *flip* of the doping dependence between FM and AFM — the actual novelty — is never connected to the relevant physics: the spin-polarized, asymmetric edge bands of ZGNR. Which band edge gets populated/depopulated by hole vs electron doping, and why that feeds *differently* into the FM vs AFM exchange, is exactly the question the title promises and the paper does not answer.
- **The "proportional to ribbon width" claim is contradicted by Table 1.** D/N is 2465/6 ≈ 411, 2966/10 ≈ 297, 3175/12 ≈ 265 (FM) and 3320/6 ≈ 553, 3583/10 ≈ 358, 3745/12 ≈ 312 (AFM) — i.e. strongly *sublinear / saturating*, with diminishing increments (+501 then +209 for FM). The conclusion's "spin stiffness is proportional to the ribbon width" is simply not what the data show. The correct statement is sublinear growth, and the abstract/conclusion should be corrected.
- **No uncertainty / robustness analysis, and the comparison to prior work is muddled.** The authors get numbers 40–70% above Yazyev–Katsnelson's Heisenberg-corrected 2100 meVÅ² for essentially the same system, yet there is no error discussion: no test of GGA (PBE) vs other functionals, no k-grid sensitivity (90×1×1 is modest), and no estimate of how much the *constrained* moment relaxes away from its imposed direction (which directly biases the energy curvature and hence D). Given the tiny moment (~0.28 μB — itself the flag the authors raise for low DFT reliability), this robustness is essential, not optional. Also, the Intro oscillates between 320 and 2100 meVÅ² as the "reliable" prior value; the reader is left guessing the comparison anchor.
- **Effect size and practical traction are thin.** The doping window (−0.01 to +0.01 e/cell) moves D by only ~10%, and the claim that "magnetism disappears at sufficiently high doping" is asserted but not shown. No link to a *measurable* quantity (spin-wave gap, magnon lifetime/mean free path vs T) is made, so the "very high spin stiffness is useful for spintronics" motivation stays aspirational.
- Minor: inconsistent spelling (antiferromagnetic/anti-ferromagnetic); "To reach the intentions" (abstract); the "antisymmetric-symmetric tendency" label is coined but never operationalized beyond "one branch rises, the other falls."

### The three changes that would most improve the paper
1. **Explain, don't just report, the doping-induced sign flip.** Add a band-structure / density-of-states analysis of the edge states showing *why* hole vs electron doping populates the FM vs AFM configurations differently (e.g., how it shifts the edge band that dominates the inter- and intra-edge exchange, and how that feeds the two configurations in opposite ways). This converts the paper's headline from an observed trend into an understood mechanism and is the single highest-leverage fix.
2. **Fix the width-scaling conclusion to match Table 1.** Replace "proportional to ribbon width" with a correct characterization: the data show sublinear, saturating growth (D/N falls from ≈411 to ≈265 meVÅ² per width unit). Either fit the scaling (e.g., logarithmic or 1→∞ saturation) and discuss the origin (distance between the two edge magnetic atoms, which they mention, vs finite-size edge-coupling cutoff), or retract the claim. This removes a direct factual inconsistency undermining a stated result.
3. **Add a robustness/uncertainty subsection and anchor to an observable.** (a) State clearly which Yazyev–Katsnelson value you compare to and why, and account for the ~40–70% gap (system size, functional, k-grid); (b) quantify sensitivity of D to the exchange–correlation functional and to the q-mesh / constraint-induced moment relaxation; and (c) translate the large D into a measurable quantity (spin-wave gap or magnon propagation length as a function of T) so the spintronic relevance is concrete. Even a partial version of (b)+(c) would substantially raise the paper's credibility.

A short secondary note: if the in-plane spin stiffness can be cleanly separated, reporting the out-of-plane/anisotropic component too would make the "very high" claim more defensible, since without anisotropy the low-temperature propagation length is not guaranteed to be large.

---

In short: sound methods, an interesting but under-developed central result, one self-contradictory conclusion, and no error analysis. Publishable after addressing the three points above, most importantly the mechanism (point 1) and the width-scaling correction (point 2).

# Peer Review

**Title:** Carrier-induced Antisymmetric-symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness in Zigzag Graphene Nanoribbons

**Recommendation: Minor revision / borderline accept**

## Summary
The authors compute the spin stiffness of zigzag graphene nanoribbons (ZGNRs) using a generalized Bloch theorem (frozen magnon) implementation in OpenMX (LCPAO), mapping a quadratic order-parameter energy difference onto a Heisenberg Hamiltonian. They compare ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) edge-atom configurations at N = 6, 10, 12, and study hole/electron doping (±0.01 e-cell). The headline results are: (i) AFM is more stable and has the larger spin stiffness; (ii) spin stiffness scales with ribbon width (FM 2465→3175, AFM 3320→3745 meVÅ² for N=6→12); and (iii) doping produces "antisymmetric" (FM) vs. "symmetric" (AFM) trends in spin stiffness, tracking the edge magnetic moment.

## Strengths
- The method is standard and the derivation (Eqs. 12–20) is careful and self-consistent; the small-θ frozen-magnon limit and the Heisenberg mapping are handled with appropriate citations, and the equivalence of the two edges reducing to a single magnon branch (Eq. 17) is a nice physical simplification compared with the supercell approach.
- Presenting both FM and AFM configurations and comparing them is a genuine addition over the prior Yazyev–Katsnelson (PRL 2008) and the authors' own 2018 JPSJ work.
- Consistent trends across three ribbon widths and between spin stiffness and the edge magnetic moment are a coherent, if modest, body of findings.

## Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** GBT-based spin stiffness in ZGNRs is already established (Yazyev & Katsnelson; the authors' ref. 22). This work incrementally adds width and doping dependence. The central claim (high spin stiffness) is not new.
- **Very narrow doping window.** The doping range of ±0.01 e-cell is so small that the "trends" in Figs. 3–4 are hard to call convincing; a wider sweep, with a stated convergence criterion for the Fermi-level shift, would strengthen the conclusion. The claim that "magnetism disappears at high doping" is asserted but not shown.
- **Quadratic-only fit.** The authors acknowledge that a quadratic dispersion underestimates fitting accuracy versus fourth order (their own ref. 22) yet use quadratic fits throughout, and stop q small because the moment collapses (Fig. 2a). The resulting spin stiffness may be biased; a 4th-order (or higher) re-analysis, even for one case, would bound the error. The claim in Section 2 that the magnon relation "can hold for larger θ" (citing Enkovaara, Jakobsson, Lezáic) is stated without a demonstration in this work.
- **No benchmark or experiment.** The authors themselves flag (Sec. 2) that DFT spin stiffness in the presence of Stoner excitations may be over- or underestimated, yet provide no comparison against the supercell values of Yazyev & Katsnelson beyond "same order." Their values (2000–3700 meVÅ²) are *higher* than the 320 meVÅ² / 2100 meVÅ² discussed for related systems — a quantitative reconciliation would be valuable.
- **Terminology.** "Antisymmetric-symmetric tendencies" is introduced and used but not clearly defined; the intended meaning (opposite vs. same response to hole vs. electron doping) should be spelled out on first use.
- **Presentation.** English quality is uneven (e.g., "To reach the intentions," "a choice to involve the Heisenberg model or not"), figures use a nonstandard plotting style with sparse data points, and only three widths are tested (one could argue N=6 is a strong-finite-size limit).

## Questions to the authors
1. Why is the doping range restricted to ±0.01 e-cell? Could you extend it and show the crossover to loss of magnetism?
2. What is the quantitative discrepancy with a fourth-order fit, and does it change the FM/AFM ordering or width dependence?
3. Can you reconcile your higher numerical spin stiffnesses with the Yazyev–Katsnelson supercell values quantitatively rather than "same order"?

## Overall
A competent, technically sound application of an established method to a well-motivated spintronics question, with a few genuinely new systematic observations (AFM>FM stiffness, width scaling, doping asymmetry). However, the increment over the authors' prior and the Yazyev–Katsnelson work is modest, the parameter ranges are narrow, there is no external benchmark, and the presentation needs cleaning up. It fits a specialized condensed-matter journal but is not a strong, field-moving result.

## What's good about the work

- **The method choice is well‑justified and clearly executed.** Building flat spin spirals, freezing the cone angle with the constraint scheme, and then mapping the total‑energy differences onto a Heisenberg Hamiltonian is the correct tool for a low‑moment system like ZGNR. The derivation chain (Eqs. 3 → 6 → 15 → 16) is standard but it's laid out cleanly, and the symmetry properties of the Fourier‑transformed exchange (Eq. 5) are stated where they matter.
- **Treating the two edges explicitly is the right call.** Distinguishing the FM (parallel‑edge) and AFM (antiparallel‑edge) configurations and showing the AFM one is both lower in energy *and* higher in spin stiffness (2966 vs. 3583 meV Å² for 10‑ZGNR) is a consistent, physically sensible result: the antiparallel‑edge ground state is the one that matters for spintronics, so its being the stiff one is encouraging.
- **The connection to long spin‑correlation length** is a good framing device — very high *D* ⇔ long correlation length ⇔ workable low‑T devices. It gives the number some meaning beyond "big = good."
- **Width scaling** (Table 1): both *D_F* and *D_AF* rise monotonically from N=6 to N=12, and the AFM>FM ordering holds at every width. That's a clean, reproducible trend.
- **Honest about DFT limitations.** You explicitly flag that for a low‑moment atom the DFT value needs experimental verification and that Stoner contributions can't be ignored (the fcc‑Ni overestimation analogy). Good intellectual honesty — keep it.

## The main issues I want you to think about

**1. Your "doping" is not really doping — this is the biggest one.**
You introduce hole/electron doping as a *uniform background charge* via a Fermi‑level shift (p. 7). But ZGNR is exactly the case where that approximation is most fragile: it has **edge states**, so real carriers doped into it will **localize at the edges, not distribute uniformly**. Your whole "carrier‑induced antisymmetric‑symmetric tendency" result (Figs. 3 and 4) rests on the charges staying uniform, which is the opposite of what the physics of this specific system suggests. The doping trends you report may be an artifact of the background‑charge construction rather than a property of doped ZGNR.
- *What to do:* at minimum, compute and plot the spin/charge density difference for a doped case vs. the undoped case to show that the added charge actually spreads over the ribbon (and isn't piling up on the edge rows). If it localizes, you have to say so and the section‑3 doping discussion changes substantially. Ideally do one comparison with an explicit gate‑potential or with a real dopant substitution to show the background‑charge picture is reasonable. This is the single change I'd push hardest on.

**2. The fixed‑moment assumption is doing work you don't discuss enough.**
Fig. 2(a) shows the magnetic moment collapsing by **more than half** (≈0.28 → ≈0.12 μB) as *q* goes to ~0.38 Å⁻¹. Your frozen‑magnon/Heisenberg framework (Eq. 3) assumes the moment *magnitude* is constant; you're relying on the small‑*q* tail of that curve where the moment is nearly flat. That's fine for the *very* small‑*q* fit, but you've now shown that the moment is strongly *q*‑dependent over the range you're even looking at.
- *What to do:* state precisely the *q*‑range you fit and confirm the moment variation over *that* range (not the 0.38 Å⁻¹ one) is within your tolerance (say <5%). Then be explicit that *D* you report is the **long‑wavelength limit** (the *q*→0 derivative), not a fit over the full curve. Right now a reader might think you fit E=Dq² over the data in Fig. 2(b), where the moment is drifting.

**3. What exactly is the unit *meV Å²*, and is *D* intensive or extensive?**
This is woven through the paper and I keep tripping on it. If *D* grows **linearly with ribbon width** (Table 1), then your *D* is a **1D "ribbon" stiffness** (energy per unit length, integrated across the width), not a 2D areal stiffness. That has two consequences you should make explicit:
- The linear‑width scaling is then *trivially expected* (the total energy cost just adds up over the width) and shouldn't be presented as a deep finding — reframe it as a consistency check.
- And, more importantly, it means **your numerical comparison to fcc nickel (700 meV Å²) and to Edwards‑Katsnelson is a dimension mismatch**: you're comparing a 2D/1D ribbon number to a 3D bulk number. "ZGNR should be much larger than fcc nickel" needs the units reconciled first. I'd want you to (a) state the dimensional character of *D* in one unambiguous sentence, and (b) either drop the direct 3D‑metal comparison or make the units consistent (e.g., compare areal 2D stiffnesses, or compare dimensionless ratios / correlation lengths).

**4. The title's "antisymmetric‑symmetric" is doing double duty and collides with standard terminology.**
In magnon/Hamiltonian language "symmetric/antisymmetric" usually refers to **symmetric vs. antisymmetric spin wave branches** (the two *q*‑modes you actually *collapse* in Eq. 16→17 by setting the two edges equivalent). But you use those words to describe the **shape of the doping curve** (FM stiffens only on the hole side ⇒ "antisymmetric"; AFM stiffens symmetrically about zero doping ⇒ "symmetric"). These are different things.
- On top of that, by enforcing the two‑edge equivalence (Re[J_0^11]=Re[J_0^22]) you have **thrown away the distinct symmetric and antisymmetric magnon branches** — one of the physically interesting pieces of a two‑edge system. A reviewer will ask: what is the symmetric‑branch / antisymmetric‑branch splitting, and why is it OK to identify the two edges? Give a one‑paragraph justification (e.g., translational/relaxation makes the edges equivalent *in the undoped* case, but note it's an approximation once you dope and the edges may differ). Right now the method quietly assumes what the doping results later depend on.

**5. Quadratic vs. fourth‑order fit — don't hand‑wave it.**
On p. 8 you say the difference between your quadratic dispersion and the fourth‑order fit in your earlier paper [22] "lies in the fitting error," which reads as hand‑waving. Either show the actual *D* value from a fourth‑order fit on the *same* small‑*q* data and the % change, or cut the sentence. "The quadratic dispersion is sufficient to produce the curve in Fig. 2(b)" is not a quantitative adequacy argument — the curvature of the magnon dispersion is precisely what a higher order captures, and you're asking the least information‑rich fit to give you the stiffness. At minimum, add a sentence/panel showing *D* as a function of the fit order (2 vs. 4 vs. 6) on the same data to demonstrate convergence.

## Smaller / presentation points

- **Basis/functional sensitivity.** GGA only, and carbon with a small (s, p; cut 4.0 a.u.) basis for a moment this small (~0.28 μB). A moment this delicate is exactly where the functional and basis can shift the answer. You can't fix this cheaply, but you should at least *check* one alternative (a hybrid like HSE06 for one width, or a slightly larger basis / larger cutoff radius) and bound the error on *M* and hence on *D*. Right now the number has no uncertainty estimate at all, which makes the "2966 vs. 3583" comparison feel more precise than it is.
- **The "drastic moment reduction ⇒ restrict to small *q*"** logic is stated but you could tighten it by giving the quantitative *q*_max you allow and the moment tolerance, so the *D* values are reproducible in methodology, not just in number.
- **Register/polish.** A few informal phrasings to clean up for a journal: "To reach the intentions," "the rest of the discussions is organized as follows," and the doubled "reduces (reduces)" on p. 8. The title is also unwieldy — "Carrier‑induced Antisymmetric‑symmetric Tendencies of Spin Stiffness" — "tendencies" is vague; consider "Carrier doping control of spin stiffness in zigzag graphene nanoribbons" or similar.
- Fig. 3/4: the AFM spin‑stiffness panel (c) is a clean V with a peak at zero doping — a nice clear result, but label the peak value in the text so a reader doesn't have to read it off the axis.

This is a **reasonable and competent DFT study** of spin stiffness in ZGNR. The non‑doped, width‑dependent results (the core *D* values, AFM>FM, monotonic width trend) are internally consistent and, modulo the unit‑comparison caveat, credible. **The doping results — which are the paper's titular claim — are currently on shakier ground**, and that's where I want you to spend the effort: (1) justify or replace the uniform‑background‑charge doping by showing where the charge actually goes; (2) pin down the small‑*q* range and the moment tolerance so *D* is unambiguously the long‑wavelength stiffness; and (3) make the dimensional character of *D* explicit and fix the 3D‑metal comparison. Get those three under control and this becomes a solid, publishable contribution; as it stands the doping‑tendency story is the part a careful referee will push back on.
