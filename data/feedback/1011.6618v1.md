# Review: *$\bar{q}q$ Potential at Finite $T$ and Weak Coupling in $\mathcal{N}=4$* (Taliotis, arXiv:1011.6618)

## Overview

The paper computes the heavy-quark (static quark-antiquark) color-singlet potential in thermal $\mathcal{N}=4$ $SU(N)$ super-Yang–Mills at weak coupling and large separation ($rT\gg1$). Using background-field/linear-response methods in the Temporal Axial Gauge, the author evaluates the gauge-boson self-energy $\Pi^{00}$ to $O(g^2T^2)$, obtains a Debye mass, and then — following the pQCD prescription of Laine–Mueller (ref. [38]) — includes the leading chromomagnetic (linear-in-$|\mathbf{q}|$) correction to recover the long-distance power-law fall-off of the potential.

## What works well

**1. The Debye-mass bookkeeping is a genuine pedagogical contribution.** The paper decomposes $\Pi^{00}_{mat}$ into gluon [12], fermion [14], and two scalar diagrams [15], [17], and makes the degree-of-freedom counting transparent: each bosonic dof contributes $\tfrac16 g^2 T^2$ and each fermionic dof $\tfrac1{30}g^2 T^2$, summing to $m_D^2 = 2Ng^2T^2$ (eq. 19). This decomposition is cleaner and more self-contained than is usually found in the QCD literature, and the appendices (Feynman rules in TAG, explicit contour evaluation of $\Pi_{s_2}^{00}$) are a useful reference.

**2. The motivation is sound.** The central claim — that $\mathcal{N}=4$ is a "toy" whose perturbative structure mirrors QCD, so that the AdS/CFT results (power-law fall-off, [19, 32, 34]) and the pQCD result (repulsive $1/r^4$ [38]) should agree in the weak-coupling limit in absolute value — is a legitimate and interesting testbed for the AdS/CFT correspondence. Reproducing [38] on the field-theory side in a supersymmetric setting is a reasonable target.

**3. Gauge-invariance framing is correct.** Identifying the color-singlet static potential built from the $00$-component of the dressed propagator as gauge invariant, and computing in TAG, is the right approach.

## Substantive weaknesses

**1. The central "result" (eq. 22) is not really a field-theory computation — it is a transplant of the Laine–Mueller QCD result.** The paper is honest about this (it "applies the analysis of [38]"), but the weakness deserves emphasis: the $1/r^4$ term is *assumed* well-defined and then grafted onto the Yukawa piece. The author concedes that "it has not been rigorously proved that this expansion in $|\mathbf{q}|$ is well defined." So the single most novel-looking equation in the paper rests on an unproven extrapolation rather than a first-principles loop calculation. The genuinely computed quantity is only $\Pi^{00}$ through $O(g^2T^2)$ and $O(g^2T|\mathbf{q}|)$, i.e. the *input* to the [38] prescription, not the $1/r^4$ potential itself.

**2. The repulsive-vs-attractive sign "discrepancy" is treated too casually and, I would argue, mischaracterized.** The paper presents [38] and AdS/CFT [19] as yielding the same power but opposite signs ([38] repulsive, [19] attractive), then simply flips an overall sign to "agree" [Discussion, p.8, p.3]. Several points are underdeveloped:
   - The sign of the *potential* in the AdS/CFT computation is not just a convention; its relation to the force on the Wilson loop and to the real part of the free energy has been a long-standing subtlety. A one-line "we flip the sign" does not adequately address why the perturbative and holographic sign conventions should be reconciled that way.
   - The claim that the $1/r^4$ is "independent of $N$ and $g$" is striking and deserves scrutiny. In eq. 22 the factor $2g^2N t/m_D^4$ cancels to $1/((4\pi)^2T^3r^4)$ precisely because $t \propto m_D$ and $m_D \propto \sqrt{Ng^2}$. This is an algebraic consequence of the QCD coefficient $t$ being imported verbatim. It is not an independent check that SUSY cancels couplings; it is baked into the input. Given that $\mathcal{N}=4$ has a vanishing beta function (shown neatly in App. B.2), one might *expect* coupling-independence, but the paper does not explore whether the vanishing of $\beta(g)$ plays a role.

**3. Missing the genuinely interesting SUSY question.** The vanishing beta function and the exact cancellation structure of $\mathcal{N}=4$ are the defining features of the theory, yet the weak-coupling analysis stops at one-loop Debye screening, which is blind to the non-renormalization theorems. A more incisive paper would ask: does the vanishing $\beta$-function or the extended supersymmetry modify the magnetic-sector (magnetostatic) mass or the higher-derivative terms that determine the long-range potential? Instead, the paper explicitly finds that both fermions and scalars drop out of the linear-|q| magnetic term, so SUSY reduces to "QCD with extra spectator states." This is a finding, but the paper undersells the disappointment and misses the opportunity to connect to the fact that the chromomagnetic sector in a supersymmetric theory has additional structure one might expect.

**4. The heavy quarks are external to the $\mathcal{N}=4$ multiplet** (footnote 1). This is a physically sensible choice (one wants a heavy probe, and the adjoint "natural" heavy states are not available in the same way), but it means the "system under study" is really $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM + heavy quarks in the fundamental — i.e., a theory that is *not* $\mathcal{N}=4$ invariant. This should be stated more prominently in the abstract/introduction as a caveat, because it softens the "we compute an observable of $\mathcal{N}=4$ that mimics QCD" claim. The external quarks carry the same $\mu_i$ (footnote 7), and the $\frac{1}{\mu_i}\gg\frac{1}{T}$ assumption (eq. 8) is then doing quiet work to drop chemical-potential effects.

**5. No error analysis on the regime of validity.** The conditions are $rT\gg1$, $r\gg1/T$, $g\ll1$. The Yukawa and the power piece are to be compared at large $r$, but there is no numerical illustration (e.g., for $g\sim0.3$, $T\sim$ GeV, where does $1/r^4$ overtake the Yukawa? Is that $r$ actually $\gg 1/T$?). A plot would have made the crossover region — where the two approximations must be combined — concrete.

## Technical / presentational

- **Overlong and underbalanced.** 8 pages of body plus 7 of appendices, of which Appendices B (Feynman rules) and the contour calculation are useful but the ratio of boilerplate to new physics is high. The Feynman-rule appendices, while self-contained, are standard and inflate the length without adding to the physics argument.
- **The $\mathcal{N}=4$-specific content is thin.** Most of the loop algebra is a direct adaptation of QED/QCD results (the author says so explicitly, borrowing eq. (8.65) and (5.51) from [48]). A reader who knows QCD thermal field theory learns little new; a reader who doesn't will be lost, since the paper does not pause to explain *why* the magnetic term is gauge-invariant or *how* the Laine–Mueller resummation actually works — it simply invokes [38].
- **A small concern on eq. (14) vs (12):** the bosonic matter part carries a $O(q^2 \log)$ remainder and a linear $T|\mathbf{q}|$ term, while the fermionic part is $O(q^2)$ with no linear term — this asymmetry is the crux of the "fermions do not contribute" claim, but it is stated as fact rather than argued (the statement "there is not a linear term in $q^\mu$" for the fermions is asserted, and the caveat about "leading terms in $\mathbf{q}$" being delicate points to possible subtlety that is not resolved).

## Bottom line

A competent, careful, and self-contained one-loop Debye-mass calculation in a supersymmetric thermal gauge theory, with genuinely useful appendices. But its headline result (the repulsive $1/r^4$ potential, eq. 22) is not computed but imported from QCD, its SUSY content is largely decorative (spectator states that decouple from the magnetic sector), and the interesting physical tension it set out to test (perturbative vs holographic power fall-off) is resolved by a sign flip rather than an analysis. It is a reasonable workshop-level report rather than a strong journal paper.

# Review: "q q̄ Potential at Finite T and Weak Coupling in N = 4 SYM" (arXiv:1011.6618v1)

## Summary and verdict

This paper computes the static free-energy potential of a q q̄ pair in N = 4 SU(N) SYM at finite temperature, weak coupling, and large separation (rT ≫ 1). It is a two-part calculation: (i) a one-loop evaluation of the static gluon self-energy Π₀₀(0, q) from gluon, gaugino, and scalar loops, yielding the Debye mass m_D² = 2NG²T² (eq. 19) and a subleading linear-in-|q| term (eqs. 12, 20); and (ii) a heuristic extension of the 1987 Gale–Kapusta pQCD analysis (ref. [38]) to N = 4 SYM, producing a large-distance potential V(r) = +1/[(4π)² T³ r⁴] (eq. 22) — repulsive, and independent of both the color N and the coupling g. The paper concludes by comparing this with the attractive 1/r⁴ fall-off found in AdS/CFT (refs. [19, 34]), reading the matching power fall-off as qualitative support for applying AdS/CFT methods to QCD-like questions.

The computation itself is, in my reading, essentially correct at the level it claims: I checked the bookkeeping and the Debye mass is the known result (8 bosonic and 8 fermionic adjoint degrees of freedom with (1/6)g²T² and (1/12)g²T² per degree of freedom give 2Ng²T², consistent with the finite-temperature N = 4 free-energy literature, e.g. Blaizot, Iancu, Krämer, Rebhan, ref. [46]). The scalar-loop evaluation in Appendix D is done carefully and is instructive, and the appendix material (two-component spinor Feynman rules at finite T) is a useful addition. But the new physics content is thin and the headline comparison is weaker than the paper presents it to be. I list the issues in order of importance below.

## Major issues

**1. The "agreement with AdS/CFT" is a dimensional-analysis statement, and the sign actually disagrees.** The central claim of the introduction (that "the potential has a power fall-off which agrees precisely with [38] and (the absolute value of) [19, 34]") deserves a critical re-read. At large r, after Debye screening washes out everything, the only remaining scale is T, so *any* static conf form theory potential of interest must be V(r) ~ C·T³/r⁴ for some dimensionless C. The fact that weak-coupling perturbation theory, pQCD, and AdS/CFT all land on power 4 contains essentially no information — it is what dimensional analysis already dictates. The differences carry all the physics, and there they disagree: perturbation theory gives +1/(16π²T³r⁴) (repulsive, no g, no N), whereas strong-coupling AdS/CFT gives an attractive potential scaled by √λ. The paper concedes the sign mismatch in the discussion ("we were, by including the scalars contribution, hoping to obtain the result of [38] with an additional overall negative sign... We find that... we obtain the repulsive potential of equation (22)"), and then in the final paragraph reverses into "one may conclude that studying non-perturbative phenomena of QCD by applying the AdS/CFT correspondence may not be far from reality." That conclusion does not follow from the result that the paper itself states; the discussion should be restructured so that the sign disagreement is a central, explicit result rather than an aside.

**2. A color-factor/representation mismatch is baked into the comparison.** Equation (10) sets Q₁Q₂ = −C₂(G)g² = −Ng² via "the tensor product of the adjoint times the (anti-)adjoint representation." But the standard heavy-quark potential — and specifically what [19, 34] compute — is for quarks in the **fundamental** representation, for which the singlet color factor is Q₁Q₂ = −C_F g² = −(N²−1)g²/(2N), not −C_A g² = −Ng². These differ by a factor of order 2 (N/2 vs. N) and do not coincide in any large-N limit. As written, the weak-coupling computation is of a heavy *adjoint* source while the strong-coupling benchmark is a *fundamental* quark. Either the intended comparison should be corrected (e.g. redo eq. (10) with the fundamental generator and track C_F through eqs. 21–22, noting that the 1/r⁴ term picks up an extra C_F/… factor), or the paper should state clearly that it computes the potential of adjoint probes and that the comparison with [19] is then only qualitative.

**3. The "magnetic mode correction" step is an admitted extrapolation presented as a main result.** Section 4.2 itself notes that "it has not been rigorously proved that this expansion in |q| is well defined," and then applies it nonetheless to produce "the second main part of our investigation." There are two problems here. First, nomenclature: the linear-in-|q| term in the *static* (q₀ = 0) gluon self-energy is an electric-sector (HTL) effect; calling it a "magnetic mode correction" follows Kapusta's 1987 paper, but it is precisely the *soft magnetic* sector that is nonperturbative (magnetic screening mass at scale g²T) — and the paper does not engage with why a purely electric one-particle-irreducible result should capture physics that, in QCD, is associated with the magnetic sector. Second, the robustness of the pQCD 1/r⁴ tail itself was actively in controversy in 2009–2010 (e.g., Mocsy–Strickland–Zanon's reanalysis of heavy-quark free energies at weak coupling questioned aspects of this program). The paper, dated November 2010, should at least acknowledge that the borrowed input is contested; instead it presents the 1/r⁴ result as established.

**4. A self-contradiction in Section 3.** The paragraph at the start of Sec. 3 argues that "there is no scalar propagator involved in this computation" because "the potential we compute involves a color singlet and hence the fermion pair should be of the same flavor," concluding via the ε_ijk vertices that "scalar propagators do not contribute to the calculation under consideration." This is flatly contradicted by the paper's own Sec. 3.3, which computes nonzero scalar-loop contributions (eqs. 15, 17; Appendix D) that are precisely *scalar propagators* running in internal loops, and without which m_D² would be wrong (the 6 scalars contribute 1·Ng²T² out of the total 2Ng²T²). The actual correct statement is narrower and should be said so: (a) scalar loops do contribute to Π₀₀, and were computed; (b) diagrams involving *external* quark–scalar vertices do not connect the single-flavor singlet pair (by the ε_ijk flavor structure). As written, the text argues something false and then disproves itself one subsection later. The footnote (†† on the "massive, external" fermions) helps, but the main text should still be corrected.

## Minor issues

**5. Sloppiness in the fermion subleading claim.** Equations (14) and (17) demonstrate the *absence* of a linear-in-|q| term for the fermion and scalar loops only by stating that "in extracting the leading terms in |q| one should be careful (see (A15a), for instance)." Since the *entty* of Section 4.2 rests on "only the gluon contributes to the linear term" (and hence "the analysis of [38] proceeds unchanged up to an overall factor"), this should be demonstrated explicitly for the fermion case, not deferred to a parenthetical pointing at a proof of a different integral.

**6. Equation (4) is garbled:** "F = k²/k² Π₀₀^F + 2G = Π_ii − Π₀₀" does not read as a well-defined identity in the extracted form and needs to be rewritten clearly (presumably F = (|q|² Π₀₀ − Π_ii)/(|q|² − 2Π₀₀) or similar).

**7. Chemical potentials and the regime (8):** the paper sets up Π₀₀(q₀, |q|; T, μᵢ/T) and immediately restricts to μᵢ/T ≪ 1, so the μᵢ bookkeeping (the contour with rectangular pieces in (A12a)) is never exercised. That is defensible, but (A12a) is then included only as decoration; either present the R-charge chemical potential dependence at least to leading order (it is an interesting N = 4 question, cf. ref. [47]) or cut the μ-dependent formalism rather than carrying it through two appendix pages and then never using it.

**8. Presentation.** The abstract is hard to parse ("for N = 4 SU SYM with gauge group SU(N)"). Typos are numerous ("Lagranian," "Diagramatic," "reach in regards," "pertubative," "cromo-charged," "power low fall-off," "obtain in the framework of AdS/CFT"). The finite-T rule in (B7) is called an "empirical rule" and the signs it prescribes should be verified against a single worked diagram (e.g., check the s1 sign in eq. 15 against it explicitly). None of this invalidates the physics, but the manuscript needs a full language pass.

**9. Missing ingredients that a complete treatment would contain.** No imaginary part (thermal width/dissipation rate) of the potential is computed — this is arguably as interesting as the real part for comparison with [19]; no explicit matching to, or comparison with, the known HTL effective action for N = 4 SYM; no discussion of whether the large-N limit is taken anywhere (the result is exact in N at one loop, but [19] is large-N).

## What is worth keeping

- The per-degree-of-freedom Debye-mass breakdown (boson 1/6, fermion 1/12) is a nice explicit SUSY sanity check and useful for teaching.
- The observation that supersymmetry suppresses every non-gluon contribution to the *subleading* linear-in-|q| term, leaving the pQCD structure intact, is a genuinely N = 4-specific feature of the result and is the most interesting technical statement here.
- The final coefficient 1/[(4π)²T³] being independent of g and N is a concrete, checkable number that distinguishes the weak-coupling result from the √λ-scaled strong-coupling one; it should be highlighted as *the* quantitative difference, not buried.
- Appendix D is a good model of how to evaluate a thermal-loop diagram from scratch.

## Overall assessment

This is a correctly executed but incremental calculation: a standard one-loop HTL computation plus a borrowed, admittedly unproven pQCD technique, with the central advertised comparison to AdS/CFT reduced to matching a power that dimensional analysis already forces, and with an adjoint/fundamental color-factor mismatch in the comparison itself. The physics claims that survive are modest but interesting (SUSY cancellation of the fermion/scalar linear subleading term; the repulsive, N- and g-independent 1/r⁴ coefficient 1/(16π²T³)). With fixes to the color factor, a rewritten Sec. 3, an explicit demonstration of the fermion subleading cancellation, and a much more honest framing of the AdS/CFT comparison, this would be a respectable journal article; in its present form it reads as a solid student-level computation with an overstated conclusion.

## [1011.6618v1-critique] Critique


## Critique: *$\bar{q}q$ Potential at Finite $T$ and Weak Coupling in $\mathcal{N}=4$* (Taliotis, Nov 2010)

### What the paper does

A one‑loop, weak‑coupling finite‑temperature calculation of the heavy‑quark color‑singlet potential in $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM with gauge group $SU(N)$, in temporal‑axial gauge. It (i) computes the Debye mass from the $|{\bf q}|\to 0$ expansion of the chromoelectric self‑energy $\Pi^{00}$, (ii) writes the resulting Yukawa potential, and (iii) borrows the Gale–Kapusta "magnetic correction" argument to obtain a large‑distance $1/r^4$ term, aiming to compare the weak‑coupling pQCD answer with the strong‑coupling $AdS/CFT$ result.

### What is sound

- **Set‑up and gauge‑invariance argument (§2.1).** The free energy of a static color‑singlet $\bar{q}q$ pair *is* gauge invariant, so a perturbative computation is legitimate — this is a correct and worth‑stating point. The tagged‑gauge (no‑ghosts) formulation and the reduction of $V(r)$ to the $\Pi^{00}({\bf q}\to 0)$ sector, via eqs. (6)–(10), are standard and correctly executed.
- **The electric‑sector calculation (§3, §4.1).** The three pieces — gluon loop (12), fermion loops (14), scalar loops (15),(17) — are each one‑loop, $O(g^2)$, and the bookkeeping for the $\mathcal{N}=4$ degrees of freedom checks out: gluon $+N/3$, two adjoint Dirac (four Weyl) $+2N/3$, six real adjoint scalars $+N$, i.e. $\;m_D^2 = 2N g^2 T^2$ (19). The new genuine computation, the second scalar diagram (Appendix D, contour integral leading to (D5)/(17)), is carried out correctly and reproduces a result of the expected form. The linear‑in‑$|{\bf q}|$ piece feeds the coefficient $t$ consistent with eq. (20). The consequent Yukawa potential (21) is the expected answer and is fine. This is the solid half of the paper.

### Substantive problems

1. **The "second main result," eq. (22), is not actually derived — the two sides of the displayed equation are mutually inconsistent (and dimensionally so).** The paper writes
   $$V(r)=\frac{2g^2N}{\pi^2}\frac{t}{m_D^4 r}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^2}\frac{1}{T^3 r^4}.$$
   Substituting $t=m_D/(16T)$ and $m_D=\sqrt{2}\,g\sqrt{N}\,T$ into the left‑hand side gives $\;\dfrac{1}{16\sqrt{2}\,\pi^2\,g\sqrt{N}}\dfrac{1}{T^4 r}$, i.e. a term proportional to $1/r$ whose dimension is $[E]^{-3}$. The right‑hand side is proportional to $1/r^4$ and has dimension $[E]$ — the correct dimension for a potential, and the one expected for the magnetic term. The two sides differ in functional form, in dimension, and in all $g,N$ dependence. So the "result" $\;1/[(4\pi)^2 T^3 r^4]$ is asserted, not shown to follow from the paper's own intermediate.
2. **A related dimensional slip in $t$.** In eq. (18), $\Pi^{00}=-m_D^2+2m_D^2 t\,|{\bf q}|+\dots$; since $\Pi^{00}$ and $m_D^2$ are $[E]^2$, the $|{\bf q}|$-linear term forces $t$ to have dimension $[E]^{-1}$. But eq. (20), $t=m_D/(16T)$, is dimensionless. One of these is off by a factor of scale. This is the same $t$ fed into eq. (22), so the magnetic result inherits the inconsistency.
3. **The "independent of $N$ and $g$" claim is unsubstantiated and in tension with the source it cites.** The magnetic $1/r^4$ term in pQCD (Gale–Kapusta [38]) is an $O(g^4)$ exchange with a singlet color factor — its coefficient is not $N,g$‑independent. The paper claims the fermion + scalar magnetic linear contributions happen to vanish (which the body does show, e.g. no $|{\bf q}|$ term in (14),(15),(17)), letting the analysis "proceed unaltered," but the jump from an $O(g^4/N_c)$‑type pQCD coefficient to a literal $1/(4\pi)^2$ constant is not argued and is exactly the step (1) above glosses over.
4. **An unresolved sign disagreement with the very result being compared to.** The magnetic correction (22) is **repulsive** ($+1/r^4$), whereas the $AdS/CFT$ large‑distance fall‑off (Albacete–Kovchegov–Taliotis [19]) is **attractive**. The Discussion notes that they "were hoping" to pick up the overall minus sign of (19) but did not, and settles for "also agreeing with our intuition." So the motivating thesis — that weak‑coupling $\mathcal{N}=4$ qualitatively matches the holographic potential — is *undercut by the paper's own central magnetic result*. The discrepancy is surfaced but left dangling.

### Minor issues

- The abstract's per‑degree‑of‑freedom accounting ("each of the $8N$ bosonic dof contributes $\tfrac16 g^2T^2$") does not match the clean component split actually computed in the body (gluon $N/3$+scalars $N$ vs. fermions $2N/3$); the phrasing is loose and should not be read literally.
- The fermion‑sector counting in §3.2 (multiply [48] by $\delta^{ab}N$, then $\times4$, $\times\frac12$, and replace the trace factor 4 by 2 to net $2N\delta^{ab}$) is terse; the result is consistent but presented in a way that hides a spot where a sign/factor error could lurk.
- The reference list is padded with ~20 same‑group heavy‑ion $\bar{q}q$/DIS/shock‑wave papers ([8]–[29]) only tangentially connected to the actual one‑loop potential calculation.
- Small writing typos ("yielding to", repeated figure/eq cross‑reference slips) and a long, mostly pedagogical appendix bulk; the fully new physics is really the scalar $s_2$ integral plus the (flawed) magnetic application.

### Novelty and impact

The large‑distance $1/r^4$ fall‑off is already established both in pQCD ([38]) and in $AdS/CFT$ ([19],[32],[34]); the paper re‑derives the pQCD structure in the $\mathcal{N}=4$ supermultiplet. Its distinctive contribution is confirming the weak‑coupling $\mathcal{N}=4$ side — but that confirmation comes with the *wrong sign* relative to the holographic answer, and the headline magnetic result does not survive its own dimensional check. Net effect: a serviceable, mostly‑correct one‑loop exercise that raises a real puzzle rather than resolving it.

### Bottom line

The Debye‑mass and Yukawa sectors are executed competently and are reliable. The magnetic‑mode "result" (22), presented as the second main point, is not actually established by the derivation — the displayed intermediate and final forms disagree in dimension and in $g,N$ dependence — and the resulting potential has the opposite sign to the $AdS/CFT$ result the paper aims to benchmark. A reader should treat (20)–(22) as unverified. This keeps the paper on the lower end of the scale: correct where it is complete, but its most novel claim is both algebraically shaky and in tension with the target it set out to test.

## [1011.6618v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Stated contribution (in my own words)

The paper computes, via one-loop finite-temperature perturbation theory in temporal-axial gauge, the heavy "quark–antiquark" color-singlet free energy at large separation (rT ≫ 1) and weak coupling (g ≪ 1) in N = 4 SU(N) SYM. Concretely: (i) it evaluates the thermal gluon self-energy Π⁰⁰ to O(g²) by adding the gluon, gaugino/quark, scalar (seagull and box) loops to the textbook QCD results, obtaining the Debye mass m_D² = 2Ng²T², which yields the usual Yukawa potential V(r) = −Ng²/(4πr) e^−m_D r; and (ii) it imports the Gale–Kapusta treatment of the linear-in-|q| (electric-mode) self-energy term to extend the potential to the even larger distances, where the potential becomes a coupling- and N-independent, **repulsive** 1/r⁴ potential, V(r) = 1/((4π)²T³r⁴). The closing claim is that this weak-coupling N=4 result reproduces the *power* (though not the sign) of both hot-QCD pQCD results and of the author's own earlier large-coupling AdS/CFT computation, and the author reads this as tentative evidence that AdS/CFT can capture nonperturbative hot-gauge-plasma physics qualitatively for QCD.

## Novelty verdict against the cited prior work

**What is genuinely new (modestly):**

## 1. What "the experiment" is: a reproduction‑grade summary

**Physical content.** Compute the static free energy of a color‑singlet pair of heavy probes at infinite separation in thermal equilibrium of 𝟝=4 𝕌(1)×𝑆𝑈(𝑁) SYM at temperature 𝑇, for probe separation 𝑟 with 𝑇𝑟 ≫ 1 (and any chemical potentials 𝜇ᵢ satisfying the regime in §2.3 below). The probes are taken to be *external, static, adjoint‑charged heavy fermions* — a footnote (after the §3 discussion) makes explicit that they may be imagined as particles that couple to gluons but *not* to the 𝟝=4 scalars; they are not part of the 𝟝=4 spectrum.

**Gauge‑invariant quantity.** The only gauge‑invariant object that exists for this system is the real part of the expectation value of a spatially‑extended static Wilson loop / Polyakov‑loop correlator, i.e. the *free energy*. (The chromo‑E and chromo‑B fields are not gauge invariant in a non‑abelian theory, so one cannot speak of "the force" gauge‑invariantly; the paper argues this in §2.1.) This choice fixes the order and the observable.

**Gauge choice.** Temporal Axial Gauge (TAG), 𝐴⁰=𝐴³=0, because *static* external sources couple purely to the 00 component, and in TAG the ghosts *decouple from the gauge sector entirely* so the gluon self‑energy at 𝒪(𝑔²) needs no ghost diagrams (stated in Appendix B.3).

**Kinematics.** Static limit: after the thermal (Matsubara) sum, the probe momentum at spatial momentum 𝒒 is 𝑞⁰→0 (zeroth Bose Matsubara frequency). The heavy probes are *static* so their velocity corrections are discarded (the free energy, not the Hamiltonian, is the observable).

**The integral (the entire "measurement").** From linear response (citing Kapusta & Gale, "FTFT", 2006), the free energy reduces to one 3D Fourier integral:

  𝑉(𝑟) = 𝑄₁𝑄₂ ∫ d³𝒒/(2π)³ · 𝑒^{i𝒒·𝑟} / [ 𝒒² − 𝜋⁰⁰(0,𝒒) ]

where 𝜋⁰⁰(0,𝒒) is the *thermal* (zero‑temperature pieces subtracted) 00 component of the gauge‑boson self‑energy, evaluated at the static limit. The charge product for a color‑singlet adjoint pair is

  𝑄₁𝑄₂ = (𝑔𝑇ᵃ)⊗(−𝑔𝑇̄ᵃ) = −𝐶₂(𝐺)𝑔² = −𝑁𝑔².

(Adjoint Casimir for 𝑆𝑈(𝑁) with Tr[𝑇ᵃ𝑇ᵇ]=½δᵃᵇ.) The regime condition 𝑇𝑟 ≫ 1 means the dominant 𝒒 contributing to the integral is 𝒒≪𝑇, so one only needs 𝜋⁰⁰(0,𝒒) for 𝒒≪𝑇, i.e. the *hard‑thermal loop (HTL)* expansion to 𝒪(𝑔²).

**Computing 𝜋⁰⁰(0,𝒒) to 𝒪(𝑔²) in thermal field theory.** This is a sum of three one‑loop "polarization bubble" diagrams (Matsubara sums replaced by real‑axis contour integrals using the standard 𝑇∑ₙf(iωₙ)=∮dp₀/(2πi) 1/(e^{β(p₀−μ)}∓1) f(p₀) formula, Appendix A eq. Gluon bubble, matter part (from eq. (8.65) of Kapusta & Gale, eq. (41) of Kajantie & Kapusta 1985, with color factor from 𝟝=4):

Two scalar bubbles: the s1 bubble comes from the 3‑point (seagull) scalar‑gluon vertex from the 3‑point scalar self‑energy; its color factor is fᵃᵈᵈ fᵇᵈᵈ fᶜᵇᵈ fᵈᵃᵉ, using fᵃᵈ fᵇᵈ = 𝑁𝛿ᵃᵇ; the s2 bubble is the standard bubble with the 4‑point scalar‑gluon vertex (the paper works it out in Appendix D in some detail and gets eq. ## 2. Every assumption / approximation the paper papers over

**A. Arithmetic / bookkeeping error in the abstract and §5 (verifiable inconsistency).**
The abstract (§1) and the discussion (§5) say:

> "each of the 8𝑁 bosonic degrees of freedom contributes 1/6 𝑔²𝑇² to 𝑚𝐷², while each of the 8𝑁 fermionic degrees of freedom contributes 1/12 𝑔²𝑇²."

That is wrong by a factor of 2, and it is *inconsistent with the paper's own Eq. (12)–(17)*. Checking: 8 × 𝑁 × (1/6) = 4𝑁𝑔²𝑇²/3, which equals the gluon contribution (−1/3 − 1/3)𝑁𝑔²𝑇² = 2𝑁𝑔²𝑇²/3 only if "8𝑁 bosonic" means *only* the gluons (8 real gluon degrees of freedom = 4 gluon polarizations × 𝑁 adjoint colors). But then the scalar loop contributions (Eqs. (15), (17), totaling −𝑁𝑔²𝑇² in real degrees of freedom) would be double‑counted or un‑accounted‑for, and the fermion total (8×𝑁×1/12 = 2𝑁𝑔²𝑇²/3) matches the fermionic sector (Eq. (14), exactly −𝑁𝑔²𝑇²/3 × 3 = −𝑁𝑔²𝑇²) only in total, not per dof. In fact, re‑deriving from Eqs. (12)–(17):
- 8 real gluon dof → total 2𝑁𝑔²𝑇²/3 → per gluon dof: 𝑔²𝑇²/12.
- 6𝑁 real scalar dof (3 complex scalars × 𝑁) → total 𝑁𝑔²𝑇² → per scalar (real) dof: 𝑔²𝑇²/6.
- 8 real fermion dof per Weyl (2 Weyl components × 2 for particle+antiparticle effectively) — the fermion sector gives −𝑁𝑔²𝑇² total over 8𝑁 real Weyl dof = −𝑔²𝑇²/8 per real fermion dof.

The *true* per‑superfield contribution is (2𝑁𝑔²𝑇²/3) for the gluon + (𝑁𝑔²𝑇²) for the three scalars = 5𝑁𝑔²𝑇²/3 for the 8 bosonic *complex* degrees of freedom (4 gluon + 3 scalar × 2 real), i.e. (5/24)𝑔²𝑇² per complex bosonic dof, or (5/24)·𝑁 per adjoint color — *not* 1/6. The fermionic part is (1/8)𝑔²𝑇² per real Weyl dof, i.e. (1/4)𝑔²𝑇² per complex Weyl dof = (1/4), *not* 1/12. **Bottom line**: the per‑do bookkeeping in the abstract / §5 is wrong by O(1) factors, even though the *total* 𝑚𝐷² = 2𝑁𝑔²𝑇² in Eq. (19) is correct and is the quantity that actually flows into the potential. A reader reproducing the calculation from the abstract's per‑do statement will get a Debye mass that is off by a factor of √(8/9) or similar and not be able to get the 1/𝑟⁴ coefficient right. This is the most concrete "paped‑over" error in the paper.

**B. Sign / overall coefficient of Eq. (22) is not independently verified.**
The 1/𝑟⁴ result is not derived in this paper; it is *imported* from the 1987 pQCD calculation (Gale & Kapusta, Toimela). The paper only states that the 1/𝑟⁴ term is "gauge invariant [38,49]" and "it is believed [38] that it is very likely to be the case" that the expansion is well‑defined. Three things this papers over:

The paper explicitly *wants* it to be attractive (matching AdS/CFT) and the scalars to flip the sign; it finds they don't, but does not address why the *electric*‑sector linear‑in‑𝒒 term should give the *opposite* sign from the strongly‑coupled result.

**C. The "static, color‑singlet, infinite‑𝑇‑extent Wilson loop" assumption.**
The gauge‑invariance argument in §2.1 (the free energy is gauge invariant, the force is not) is standard but is *not* a fully rigorous proof that the 𝑟→∞ limit of the Wilson‑loop potential in a deconfined 𝟝=4 plasma equals the expression (6)/(9). Specifically:
   - The pair is assumed to be in a *color singlet* state at all times; for a *static* probe this is exact (the color flux cannot change in the static limit), but the paper does not discuss how the singlet constraint is imposed on the heavy probes in the first place (are they in the adjoint? fundamental? The color factor in Eq. (10) uses the *adjoint* Casimir of 𝑆𝑈(𝑁), implying the probes are adjoint‑charged, but §2.1's wording "quark-antiquark" more naturally suggests *fundamental*). This is a genuine ambiguity: a fundamental‑charge probe would have 𝑄₁𝑄₂=−𝐶𝐹𝑔²=−(𝑁²−1)/(2𝑁)𝑔²=−𝑔²/2+𝒪(1/𝑁²), not −𝑁𝑔².
   - The Wilson loop's *time* extent is taken to infinity (static, zero velocity), but in a deconfined plasma there is no strict "potential" — only a free energy and a damping rate. The paper computes the *free energy* (the real part of the potential), but the imaginary part (Landau damping, thermal width) exists at the same 𝒪(𝑔²) and is not computed. For a *static* source this is standard; for a *finite‑velocity* source the imaginary part is of the same order as the real part at large 𝑟 and cannot be neglected.

**D. The HTL / 𝟞𝒒𝒈𝑇 expansion and the validity window.**
The linear‑in‑𝒒 term and the Debye mass are *both* leading HTL results, valid for 𝒒≪𝑇 but 𝒒≫𝑔𝑇 (the hard loop window). The paper works in the 𝒒→0 limit (𝒪(𝒒/𝑇)→0), which is the edge of the HTL regime: at 𝒒=0 the expansion is *not* strictly valid, and the true static gluon self‑energy at 𝒒=0 is *finite* (the 𝟞𝐷² term) but its 𝒪(𝒒) slope receives contributions from *soft* gluons (𝒒~𝑔𝑇) that are missed at strict HTL order. This is the same issue that plagues the pQCD result [38] itself (the 1/𝑟⁴ tail is actually a 1-loop HTL effect *plus* a non‑perturbative 𝒪(𝑔⁴𝑇²) magnetic‑mass correction that is not captured at strict 𝒪(𝑔²)).
   - The magnetic mass 𝟞𝑚~𝑔²𝑇 (non‑perturbative, screened at the 𝒪(𝑔²) level in 3‑dimensional effective theory) is *not* included; this is the same omission that limits the pQCD result it builds on. For 𝟝=4, the magnetic mass would in principle be computable in perturbation theory via the 3D 𝟝=3 𝑆𝑈(𝑁)𝒩=2 effective theory, but the paper does not do this and notes it is "motivated" but not done.
   - The 𝟞𝒒 log(𝒒/𝑇) term in 𝜋𝑔 (Eq. 12) is dropped when the potential is computed, even though it is of 𝒪(𝑔²) like the linear term. For 𝒒𝒯~1 (the peak of the potential's 𝒒‑integral), this term is as large as the linear term and its 𝒪(𝒒 log(𝒒/𝑇)) contribution to the potential is a 1/𝑟⁴ log(𝑇𝑟) correction that the paper does not discuss.

**E. Chemical potentials are ignored at 𝟞𝑇(μ/𝑇)≪1.**
Eq. (8) requires μᵢ/𝑇≪1 for all global‑symmetry chemical potentials, and the text explicitly says "the μᵢ's will not contribute (to zeroth order) in the view of (8)." This is fine for μ/T→0 but means the result does **not** apply to 𝟝=4 at finite baryon density (which is physically the most interesting regime for comparison to heavy‑ion data). For μ~𝑇 the Debye mass gets additional 𝒪((μ/𝑇)²𝑁𝑔²𝑇²) corrections and the linear term gets μ‑dependent pieces.

**F. The "same chemical potential for all four Weyl fermions" assumption.**
Footnote after Eq. (13) says the four Weyl fermions (gaugino + 3 matter) are assumed to have the *same* chemical potential μ for simplicity. In fact, 𝟝=4 with R‑symmetry has *three* independent R‑symmetry chemical potentials (μ₁, μ₂, μ₃) for the three matter Weyl species, and the gaugino has *zero* chemical potential (it is part of the gauge multiplet, not the flavor sector). Setting all four equal is not the physical 𝟝=4 ensemble; it is an approximation that makes the R‑symmetric sector degenerate. The result is *incorrect* for the physical large‑𝑅 ensemble even at leading order in μ/T, though at μ=0 (the case actually computed) it is fine.

**G. The scalars are assumed to *couple only to gluons, not to the heavy probes* (external‑source assumption).**
The footnote after §3 says the heavy probes "couple (directly) only to the gauge bosons but not to the scalars" — i.e. the probes are *test particles that decouple from the scalar sector*. This is a legitimate assumption *if* the probes are in a *representation that the 𝟝=4 scalars do not couple to* (e.g. the *singlet*), but for an *adjoint* probe (implied by the 𝑁𝑔² color factor in Eq. 10) the 𝟝=4 scalars *do* couple to the probe via the 𝑓^{abc} scalar‑gluon‑fermion vertex (Fig. 4/5). The scalar loops in the self‑energy (Eqs. 15, 17) already include the scalar‑gluon vertex *in the loop*, but the *tree‑level* scalar exchange between the two probes (the "Yukawa" scalar‑exchange potential) is *not* computed. At 𝒪(𝑔²) in the potential, the scalar exchange between two adjoint probes is of the *same* order as the gluon exchange and would modify the 1/𝑟 (not just the 1/𝑟⁴) term. The paper implicitly sets this to zero, which is only consistent if the probes are *singlets* under the 𝟝=4 gauge group — but then the color factor 𝑄₁𝑄₂=−𝑁𝑔² in Eq. (10) is wrong. This is a genuine inconsistency in the setup that the paper does not resolve.

**H. Large‑𝑁 is not taken.**
The 𝟝=4 result 𝑉(𝑟)∝1/𝑟⁴ with coefficient 1/[(4π)²𝑇²] is *independent* of 𝑁 and 𝑔, which is a genuine and interesting result. But the *derivation* keeps all orders in 1/𝑁 implicitly in the color factors (𝑁𝛿^{ab} from 𝑓^{acd}𝑓^{bcd} is exact for all 𝑁), so the 𝑁‑independence is exact at this order, not an asymptotic statement. However, the comparison to AdS/CFT (which is *strictly* large 𝑁) is only meaningful in the 𝑁→∞ limit, and the paper does not comment on the 𝒪(1/𝑁) corrections to Eq. (21) that would come from subleading color structures.

**I. The "static limit 𝒒⁰=0 taken *before* the 𝒒→0 expansion" assumption.**
In Eq. (13) and (15), the Matsubara sum is evaluated at 𝒒⁰=0 first, and *then* the 𝒒→0 expansion is taken. This is the *opposite* order from the standard HTL derivation (which takes 𝒒→0 including the 𝒒⁰=0 component). For the *static* potential (𝒒⁰=0 exactly) this is correct, but it means the linear‑in‑𝒒 term in Eq. (12) is a *static* linear term, not the full HTL linear term (which includes 𝒒⁰ and |𝒒| separately). At 𝒒⁰=0 the distinction doesn't matter at 𝒪(𝑔²), but it should be noted.

**J. The 1/𝑟⁴ result is compared to AdS/CFT without noting the large‑𝑁 / strong‑coupling mismatch.**
The AdS/CFT result [19,34] is at *strong* ’t Hooft coupling (λ=𝑔²𝑁→∞) and *large* 𝑁. The pQCD result [38] is at *weak* 𝑔 and *finite* 𝑁. The paper's result is at *weak* 𝑔 and *finite* 𝑁. The agreement in *power* (1/𝑟⁴) is claimed to be "amazing," but this is not a controlled match between the same regimes — it is a match of two different limits of the same theory, and the power could change at intermediate coupling. The paper overstates the robustness of this agreement.

## 3. Summary

**Reproducibility**: The calculation is *in principle* reproducible from Kapusta & Gale + the 𝟝=4 Feynman rules + the 1987 Gale–Kapusta angular integral. The Debye mass (Eq. 19) is correct and the per‑do bookkeeping in the abstract is wrong by a factor of 2 (flag A). ## [1011.6618v1-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


# Evaluation of arXiv:1011.6618v1 — "$q\bar q$ Potential at Finite $T$ and Weak Coupling in $\mathcal N=4$" (A. Taliotis, Nov 2010)

## My reconstruction of the paper's logical argument

1. **Motivation.** AdS/CFT computations of the static heavy-quark potential in $\mathcal N=4$ $SU(N)$ at finite $T$ and strong coupling (refs. [19, 32, 34, 34′]) found a long-distance power falloff $V\propto 1/r^4$, and an analogous power falloff had already been found in perturbative QCD by Gale & Kapusta [38]. The paper's thesis: compute the same observable in $\mathcal N=4$ SYM at *weak* coupling to see whether the weak-coupling field-theory side reproduces the same fall-off, i.e., whether AdS/CFT at strong coupling is qualitatively tracking weak-coupling reality.

2. **Setup (Sec. 2).** The color-singlet static potential is gauge invariant, hence a legitimate perturbative target. Working in Temporal Axial Gauge, the potential is extracted from the dressed gluon propagator via linear response; for static sources only $\Pi^{00}(0,\mathbf q)$ is needed, and with $rT\gg1$ the $r$-integral is dominated by $\mathbf q\to0$, so $V(r)=-Ng^2\int\!\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}}{\mathbf q^2-\Pi^{00}(0,\mathbf q)}$ with $Q_1Q_2=-Ng^2$.

3. **Input calculation (Sec. 3).** Compute the *thermal* part of $\Pi^{00}$ to $O(g^2)$ using finite-temperature field theory (Matsubara sums, $a$-th order static limit), borrowing the QCD results of Kapusta & Gale [48] and re-deriving/adapting them for $\mathcal N=4$: color traces in the adjoint (factor $N$), Weyl instead of Dirac spinors (trace factor 2 not 4), and — the genuinely new input — the two scalar-loop diagrams, where the scalar vertices change fermion flavor so scalar propagators never appear between gluon vertices. The pieces: gluons $-N g^2T^2/3$ (eq. 12), four Weyl fermions $-2Ng^2T^2/3$ (eq. 14), 6 real scalars $-Ng^2T^2$ (eqs. 15, 17).

4. **Debye mass (Sec. 4).** Summing: $\Pi^{00}(q_0=0,\mathbf q\to0)=-m_D^2+2m_D t|\mathbf q|$ with $m_D^2=2Ng^2T^2$ (19) and $t=m_D/16T$ (20). Using only $m_D^2$ in (9) gives the standard Yukawa result $V=-g^2N e^{-m_Dr}/(4\pi r)$ (21).

5. **Magnetic correction (Sec. 4.2).** "Applying the analysis of [38]" to the linear $|\mathbf q|$ term gives the headline result $V(r)=\frac{2g^2N}{\pi^2}\frac{t}{m_D^2}\frac1{r^4}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^2}\frac{1}{T^3 r^4}$ (22): a *repulsive* $1/r^4$ fall-off that is $N$- and $g$-independent, and which agrees with [38] and (in absolute value) with [19,34]. Section 5 argues this qualitative match supports the hope that AdS/CFT captures physics "not far from reality."

## Feedback

**Checks that pass.** The Debye-mass part is sound and self-consistent. Reading the per-channel results off my own species counting (per real adjoint scalar $Ng^2T^2/6$, per Weyl adjoint fermion $Ng^2T^2/6$, gluons $Ng^2T^2/3$) I get exactly $m_D^2=2Ng^2T^2$, and the per-DOF bookkeeping in the abstract (bosons $1/6$, fermions $1/12$, giving $8/6+8/12=4/3+2/3$) closes. Equation (18) is also consistent with (12): with $m_D$ of (19) and $t$ of (20), $2m_Dt|\mathbf q|=\tfrac14 Ng^2T|\mathbf q|$, precisely the gluon linear term in (12), and the statement that fermions and scalars have *no* linear $|\mathbf q|$ term is used correctly. The appendices are genuinely helpful — the derivation of the $s_2$ diagram (App. D), the proof of (A15a), and the complete finite-$T$ Feyman rules make the paper a fine pedagogical document.

**Problem 1 — Eq. (22) does not follow from Eqs. (18)–(20).** This is the paper's "second main result," and it contains a dimensional inconsistency. Substituting (19) and (20) directly into the *left* expression of (22):
$$\frac{2g^2N}{\pi^2}\frac{t}{m_D^2}=\frac{2g^2N}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{1}{16T\,m_D}=\frac{g\sqrt{2N}}{16\pi^2T^2},$$
which gives $V=\dfrac{g\sqrt{2N}}{16\pi^2}\dfrac{1}{T^2r^4}$ — with units of energy squared, and with an explicit $g\sqrt{2N}$ dependence. The right-hand side $(1/16\pi^2)(1/T^3r^4)$ has units of energy and is $N$-$g$ independent. The two displayed expressions differ by the dimensionful factor $g\sqrt{2N}/T$; equivalently, getting the RHS from the LHS requires $t=1/(16T)$ rather than $t=m_D/(16T)$. One of (20) or (22) carries a stray factor of $m_D$. Consequently the striking claims in Sec. 4.2 — that the potential is "repulsive and in addition $N$ and $g$ independent" — are artifacts of the algebra as written; the correct left-hand expression depends on both $g$ and $N$.

**Problem 2 — the [38] machinery is imported as a black box.** The $r$-space $1/r^4$ structure comes from Fourier-transforming the non-analytic $|\mathbf q|$ piece, i.e. integrals of the form $\int\!d^3q\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\,|\mathbf q|/(\mathbf q^2+m_D^2)^2$, which is logarithmically UV divergent and whose value depends on where the $|\mathbf q|$ expansion is cut off (hard scale $\sim T$ vs. electric scale $\sim gT$). In QCD this is handled inside the HTL/dimensional-reduction framework of [38]; the paper's one-sentence "assuming this and applying the analysis of [38]" neither transfers that bookkeeping to $\mathcal N=4$ nor states the validity window ($g^2T\ll r^{-1}\ll m_D$) in which a $1/r^4$ power law can even appear. The coefficient in (22) is therefore unsupported, not just the sign.

**Problem 3 — the sign, which is the whole comparison to AdS/CFT.** The paper reports a *repulsive* $1/r^4$ while AdS/CFT [19] gives an *attractive* one, and the author frankly notes the mismatch ("we were hoping … to obtain the result of [38] with an additional overall negative sign"). But even the naive argument is questionable here: with $Q_1Q_2=-Ng^2<0$ and the linear self-energy term *positive* ($+2m_Dt|\mathbf q|$), expanding $1/(\mathbf q^2+m_D^2-2m_Dt|\mathbf q|)$ gives a *positive* correction that, multiplied by $Q_1Q_2$, is *attractive*. So the advertised "repulsive" sign depends entirely on the unspelled details of the [38] formula. The headline "agreement with AdS/CFT" reduces to: same power ($1/r^4$), opposite sign, unverified coefficient — a qualitative agreement, and a thin one at that.

**Problem 4 — presentation gap in Eq. (15).** The $s_1$ chain writes $T\sum_n\int\!\frac{d^3\mathbf k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{(k_n^0)^2-\mathbf k^2}\Big|_{(k_n^0)^0=1}$ as $-6g^2N\delta_{ab}T\int\!\frac{d^3\mathbf k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac1{k^2}$ and then invokes "the left integral of (A14b)," which is $\int_0^\infty dp\,\frac{p}{e^{\beta p}-1}=\pi^2T^2/6$. Those are different objects: the zero-mode 3D integral $\int d^3k/k^2$ is linearly UV divergent, while (A14b) is a convergent Bose integral. The step converting the zero-mode piece into a thermal-scale integral (or the subtraction of the divergent vacuum piece) is missing. The final coefficient ($Ng^2T^2/2$, i.e. $1/6$ per real scalar) matches the standard HTL assignment, so the number is very likely right, but the derivation as printed is not a legitimate chain of equalities.

**Minor issues.** (i) The Debye mass itself (19) is already known for $\mathcal N=4$ (refs. [44]–[46]), so the paper should be more explicit that (19) is a consistency check and (4.2) the actual new content — Sec. 4's "This is one of our main results" overstates it. (ii) The dressed propagator (5) is called exact "to all orders," but it resums only the $q^0=0$ self-energy in temporal gauge; for the potential only that component matters, so the point is defensible but worth a sentence. (iii) No discussion of the window of validity of the $1/r^4$ result (see Problem 2). (iv) The abstract's per-DOF fermion coefficient is hard to read from the typeset fraction, but it is consistent with the total if read as $1/12$.

**Bottom line.** The logic is clear, the bookwork (Debye mass, per-channel decomposition, appendix derivations) is largely correct and well done, and the conceptual program — use $\mathcal N=4$ SYM to confront AdS/CFT predictions with weak coupling on the same observable — is appealing. But the paper's centerpiece, the magnetic-mode result (22), fails its own consistency check (a dimensionful mismatch between its two expressions), rests on an unverified black-box import of [38], and lands on the *opposite* sign from the AdS/CFT result it was meant to confirm. In this state the claimed "precise agreement" is not established.

## [1011.6618v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


I've read the full paper (Taliotis, "q q̄ Potential at Finite T and Weak Coupling in N=4 SYM", arXiv:1011.6618v1). Here is the evaluation.

## Argument chain (as the paper builds it)

## The single weakest link: §4.2, eqs. (20)→(22)

**(a) It mislabels the physics it uses.** The correction is billed as a *magnetic mode* correction, but every input — the slope 2m_D t|q| in eq. (18) — comes from the *static electric* self‑energy Π⁰⁰, computed in §§3.1–3.3. The magnetic sector, G = Πⁱⁱ − Π⁰⁰ (defined in the paper's own eq. 4), is never computed at all. (18)/(20)/(22) use m_D as a mass. Read consistently (m_D = √(2Ng²T²), t = m_D/16T), a Fourier‑transform extraction of the slope term gives V(r) ∝ 1/(Ng²T³r⁴) times an O(1) constant — the 1/(Ng²) dependence is *not* cancelled. The stated LHS of eq. (22), (2g²N/π²)·t/(m_D⁴r⁴), even as written is dimensionally inconsistent with its RHS (4π)⁻²(T³r⁴)⁻¹ (which has dimension of energy; the LHS does not). "Independent of N and g" appears only because a factor of m_D² = Ng²T² has been swapped into t. The celebrated number (16π²T³r⁴)⁻¹ is thus an artifact of the conflation, not a consequence of the loops the paper itself evaluates.

**(d) The final inference overreaches on top of it.** The punchline — "same power fall‑off (absolute value) as [19], so N=4 SYM ≈ QCD and AdS/CFT may work for QCD" — rests on matching the *power* r⁻⁴. But dimensional analysis alone dictates that any subleading large‑r correction has the form f(g,N)/T³r⁴; matching a power (with the sign flipped, repulsive here vs. attractive in [19], and with coefficients that disagree once (c) is accounted for) is extremely weak evidence, and it is built on the unentailed coefficient of step (c).

Everything above the link (steps 1–2, the Debye mass, the Yukawa potential) is sound and standard; everything below it is the comparison the paper was written to make. That is why §4.2 is the single weakest link: it is the step that converts entailed data (Π⁰⁰ at O(g²)) into the headline 1/r⁴ claim, by an unverified analogy, against the paper's own appendix, with an internally inconsistent substitution.

**Title:** *qq̄ Potential at Finite T and Weak Coupling in 𝒩=4* — A. Taliotis, arXiv:1011.6618v1

### What the paper does

Computes the static color-singlet heavy-quark potential in 𝒩=4 SYM (SU(N)) at finite temperature, weak coupling, and long range (r ≫ 1/T). The setup is standard thermal QFT: the potential follows from the spatial–spatial component of the gluon self-energy Πᵘᵛ, and because the static singlet potential is gauge invariant the author legitimately works in the temporal axial gauge and reduces the problem to Π⁰⁰(q⁰=0, **q**). The three one-loop (in g) thermal contributions to Π⁰⁰ — gluon/ghost, fermion, scalar — are evaluated (borrowing the QED/pQCD results of Kapusta–Gale and adapting the color/symmetry factors), combined into a Debye mass and a linear-in-|**q**| chromomagnetic term, and then used to build the potential via the electric modes (Yukawa) and, "in the spirit of" Gale–Kapusta, the magnetic modes (a long-distance power law).

### Soundness — electric mode: correct

The electric-mode part is clean and I was able to verify the arithmetic: each of the 8 bosonic d.o.f. per color contributing (1/6)g²T² and each of the 8 fermionic d.o.f. (1/12)g²T² is just a repackaging of the standard per-species results (gluon → C_A/3 = N/3; 2 adjoint Dirac fermions → (1/3)T(adj)·2 = (2/3)N; 3 complex adjoint scalars → N). Summed, **m_D² = 2Ng²T²** is the established one-loop 𝒩=4 result, and eq. (21) correctly yields the screened Yukawa potential. The gauge-invariance argument (eq. (21) needs only the q⁰=0 channel; eq. (10) gets Q₁Q₂ = −Ng² from the Casimir) is sound. The appendices (conventions, T=0 and finite-T Feynman rules, the full diagram inventory, a detailed contour evaluation of the scalar s₂ diagram) are genuinely useful and make the paper self-contained — a real virtue for an instructive reference.

### Soundness — magnetic mode: the novel claim is fragile

The genuinely new content is the claim, ported from the QCD pQCD result [38], that at large r the potential falls as a **repulsive 1/r⁴** law that is **independent of both N and g** (eq. (22)). Two problems:

1. **Equation (22) is internally inconsistent as printed.** The left-hand side is written (2g²N/π²)(t/m_D⁴)·r⁻², but the right-hand side is (4π)⁻²·(T³r⁴)⁻¹ — the power of r differs (r⁻² vs r⁻⁴), and, using the paper's own t = m_D/(16T) (eq. 20) and m_D = 2Ng²T² (eq. 19), the LHS prefactor carries factors of N and g (∼ N⁻¹g⁻⁴T⁻⁷) that do not reduce to the g⁰N⁰ T⁻³ of the RHS. As displayed, the headline "second main result" cannot be followed or checked.
2. **The step is imported, not derived.** The author explicitly notes it is "not rigorously proved that this expansion in |**q**| is well defined, it is believed" the expansion holds, and the whole magnetic-mode conclusion is carried over "in the spirit of" [38] on the (reasonable but unstated) grounds that the fermion and scalar loops have no linear-|**q**| term. That unproven step, plus the unverified QCD→𝒩=4 analogy, is what the novel result rests on. It should be worked out independently, or explicitly relegated to a conjecture it is not rigorously derived.

### Motivation/impact

The stated motivation — bridging the weak-coupling field-theory potential to the strong-coupling AdS/CFT one — is **not achieved**: the hope was that the scalar sector would flip the sign of the long-distance law to the *attractive* 1/r⁴ seen in AdS/CFT [19], but the author correctly finds the scalars contribute nothing to the magnetic channel, so the weak-coupling result stays *repulsive*, agreeing with pQCD and not with AdS/CFT. This is an honest negative result, but it deflates the paper's own framing. The Debye mass / Yukawa are known, and the 1/r⁴ structure is essentially a re-application of a published QCD result to a supersymmetric Debye mass; the one mildly interesting byproduct (apparent N,g-independence of the long-distance coefficient) is precisely the part left under-derived.

### Minor issues

Notational loose ends (q vs Q; the "each of the 8N … contributes" packaging hides the per-species coefficients); the reduction from the full 3-D integral (eq. 6) to eq. ### Summary

A competently and clearly written, self-contained perturbative computation. The well-defined part (Debye mass, screened Yukawa) is correct, and the pedagogical appendices are a plus. However, the paper's distinctive contribution — the long-distance 1/r⁴ law — is imported by analogy through an unproven step, its key displayed equation is not self-consistent, and the paper's motivating reconciliation-with-holography is not realized. For a prestigious journal this would not pass as a strong result in its current form; the magnetic-mode section needs an independent, checked derivation (or honest demotion to conjecture) and eq. (22) corrected before the central claim is defensible.

**Recommendation:** Major revision (with the caveat that, even after fixing the equation, the novelty is limited enough that the paper is borderline for a top venue and better suited to a more specialized forum).

## What is correct (verify and keep)

## Issues to fix

1. **$m_D$ vs $m_D^2$ is mixed up.** Eq. (19) literally writes "$m_D=2Ng^2T^2$," which is a *squared* mass, yet eq. (21) uses $e^{-m_D r}$, which needs a mass. which gives a contact $\delta^3(r)$ term and a coefficient $\sim Ng^2 m_D^2$, not the $Ng^2$ in (21). The clean Yukawa (21) comes from the *dressed static propagator* $D_{00}(0,q)=1/(q^2-\Pi^{00})$, i.e. the $q^2$ in the numerator should not be there for the potential. The reduction to (21) currently skips a step; write it out so it actually reproduces (21).

3. **The magnetic / $1/r^4$ result (§4.2) — the weakest part.**
