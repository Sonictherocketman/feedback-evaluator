# Review of arXiv:1810.00296v3

**"Self-consistency and covariance of light-front quark models: testing via P, V, and A meson decay constants, and P→P weak transition form factors" (Chang, Li, Li, Su, Yang)**

## Summary of the argument

The paper takes a constituent light-front quark model (LFQM) and a covariant light-front quark model (CLFQM) built on the same Gaussian wave function with a single parameter β fit to f_P, and tests whether they are *self-consistent* and *covariant*. The self-consistency criterion is internal: for a spin‑1 meson, the decay constant extracted with the longitudinal (λ=0) polarization projector should equal that extracted with the transverse (λ=±) one, since both describe the same bound state. The authors show:

1. Under the traditional "type‑I" correspondence (χ→ψ mapping with D_M,con→D_LF, Eq. 28/2), both CLF and SLF models are inconsistent: [f_M]^{λ=0} ≠ [f_M]^{λ=±}, driven by the B_1^(2) term (Eqs. 50–52).
2. Under the "type‑II" correspondence (an extra M→M̃_0 replacement in every term of the integrand, Eq. 32), the inconsistency disappears *numerically* (≐), because the B_1^(2) integrals vanish.
3. Under type‑II, SLF results = CLF valence contributions [Q]_{SLF}=[Q]_{val} (Eq. 78), and the zero‑mode pieces exist formally but vanish numerically.
4. The residual light‑like vector ω dependence that breaks covariance (g_V, Eqs. 93–95) is likewise zero only under type‑II (Eqs. 96, 99), so covariance is recovered.

## Strengths

- **The covariance analysis (Sec. 3.4) is the most valuable piece.** It goes beyond "self-consistency" and shows that the type‑I CLF result violates Lorentz covariance through an unphysical, frame‑dependent constant g_V ∝ 1/(ω·p), and that the covariance condition g_V=0 (Eq. 96) is satisfied exactly under type‑II. This elevates the argument from a bookkeeping convenience to a principle of the framework.
- **Honest bookkeeping of "formal" vs "numerical" equality** (the ≐ symbol). The authors repeatedly stress that zero‑mode terms vanish numerically, not identically — this should be taken seriously (see criticism 2 below).
- **Genuinely predictive content.** β is fixed from f_P only, and the other decay constants in Tables 4 and 6 are then predicted and compared to LQCD/QCDSR. Light‑ and open‑flavor entries are in decent agreement.
- **Consolidation.** It is the reference that pins down the "self‑consistent version" used by much subsequent LFQM phenomenology, and it extends the existing Choi–Ji (2014) idea to axial‑vector mesons and form factors.

## Main criticisms

**1. The M→M̃_0 replacement is asserted, not derived — the central result is empirical.** This is the paper's most important and weakest point. Type‑I was *derived* in prior work by matching CLF/SLF for zero‑mode‑free quantities; the extended type‑II replacement ("M→M̃_0 in each and every term") is exactly the step the authors say "has not yet been clarified explicitly" (p. 3). The evidence for it is that it *makes the desired consistency relations hold*. Read in that order, the logic is close to circular: a sufficiently flexible deformation of the integrand can be chosen to force λ=0 to match λ=±. The physical motivation (SLF constituents are on‑shell, so M̃_0 is the natural invariant) is reasonable and is why the result is credible, but the paper would be much stronger with one paragraph explaining *why* M→M̃_0 must act on all terms and not just the D‑factor — ideally a structural or heavy‑quark‑limit argument rather than "it works."

**2. "Vanishes numerically" is doing more work than it should.** The entire resolution rests on ∫dx d²k_⊥ χ_M B_1^(2)/(…)=0 (Eq. 96). This is a property of the *specific Gaussian* wave function chosen, not an identity. For a generic or different vertex form the same cancellation is not guaranteed, and the authors concede this in the penultimate paragraph (p. 31: the replacement "would possibly be less or even not essential in other cases"). A brief scan over one or two alternative wave functions (e.g. a relativistic Breit–Wigner or a variable‑shape form) would show whether the B_1^(2)→0 is robust or an artifact of the Gaussian's x↔x̄ symmetry. As it stands, the "resolution of the zero‑mode problem" is a numerical observation, and the covariance proof inherits this caveat.

**3. The self‑consistency criterion is legitimate but its status should be stated more carefully.** Requiring f_V from λ=0 to equal f_V from λ=± is a sound requirement for a *consistent* model. But the standard SLF model was never claimed to define a transverse‑polarization decay constant — practitioners simply report [f_V]^{λ=0}. So the "violation" in the standard SLF model (Table 3, [f_V]^{λ=0}_{SLF}=211 vs [f_V]^{λ=±}_{SLF}=227) is an inconsistency that only appears if you extend the model into a regime it was not designed for. The fix is fine, but a sentence clarifying that the λ=± channel is being used as a *diagnostic* rather than a competing prediction would sharpen the argument. This also touches the related point (p. 30) about the ambiguous decomposition of the hadronic matrix element: part of the λ=0/λ=± mismatch may reflect a choice in how the unphysical ω^μ projection is split, not a failure of the dynamics.

**4. Phenomenology is reasonable but the "generally in consistency" claim is a touch generous in the heavy sector.** Table 4 shows real tensions against LQCD once the (small) quoted β‑uncertainties are taken seriously: f_{B_c^*}=465±7 vs LQCD 422±13 (~5σ), f_{B_s^*}=246±4 vs 223±5, f_{B^*}=205 vs 185.9, f_{J/ψ}=382±96 vs 418±9. The errors quoted are the propagated β uncertainties, which do not include systematic model uncertainties (wave‑function shape, on‑shell constituents, single universal β per channel). For a constituent model the heavy‑quark results are acceptable, but the paper would be more defensible reporting a larger, model‑informed error band and discussing these specific LQCD tensions rather than lumping them under "generally consistent." The 353±25 (7%) on f_{B_c'} in Table 6 similarly signals that the heavy‑flavor extrapolation is at the edge of the model's comfort.

**5. Positioning relative to other covariant approaches could be deeper.** The discussion of the zero mode and the light‑like vector engages Jaus, Choi–Ji, and Cheng et al., but the broader landscape of ways to handle the spurious ω^μ (e.g. the Karmanov–Smirnov / Carboneill–Mathiot decomposition of hadronic matrix elements, refs 87–90, cited only in a footnote) is underused. Comparing the g_V=0 covariance condition against that independent decomposition method would be a strong cross‑check and would strengthen the covariance claim, which is the paper's headline selling point.

## Constructive suggestions

- Add an argument (even a limiting‑case one) for why M→M̃_0 must be applied term‑by‑term rather than only to the D factor.
- Demonstrate the B_1^(2)→0 cancellation is not Gaussian‑specific by testing one alternative vertex/wave function.
- Replace the single‑number β error in Tables 4/6 with a model‑systematic band, and explicitly flag the heavy‑flavor LQCD tensions (B_c^*, B_s^*).
- One paragraph cross‑checking the covariance (g_V=0) against the Karmanov–Smirnov/Carboneill–Mathiot physical/unphysical decomposition.

## Bottom line

A clear, well‑organized, and internally consistent study that consolidates the "self‑consistent" LFQM/CLFQM framework and adds a genuinely useful covariance proof. Its central device — the type‑II M→M̃_0 replacement — is physically motivated but ultimately validated empirically rather than derived, and its key cancellation is a property of the chosen Gaussian wave function; both points are honestly acknowledged. The phenomenology is good in the light sector and reasonably acceptable (though with understated uncertainties) in the heavy sector. A solid, citable reference in a niche phenomenological subfield rather than a field‑defining result.

**Paper:** *Self-consistency and covariance of light-front quark models: testing via $P$, $V$, and $A$ meson decay constants, and $P\to P$ weak transition form factors* (Chang, Li, Li, Su, Yang; arXiv:1810.00296v3, hepph).

## Summary of the claim

The covariant light-front (CLF) quark model and the standard light-front (SLF) quark model are connected by a "correspondence" between the covariant vertex and the light-front wavefunction, derived from matching on zero-mode-free quantities ($f_P$, $f_+$). Two prescriptions exist: **type-I** (replace only the $D$ factor, $M\to\tilde M_0$) and **type-II** (replace $M\to\tilde M_0$ in every term of the integrand, per Choi–Ji '14). The authors argue that under type-I:

- CLF is *not self-consistent*: $[f_{V/A,A}]^{\lambda=0}\neq [f_{V/A,A}]^{\lambda=\pm}$, because the $[b]_1^{(2)}$ term in the longitudinal ($\lambda=0$) mode has no counterpart in the transverse ($\lambda=\pm$) mode.
- The SLF model is *also not self-consistent*: $[f_V]^{\lambda=0}\neq [f_V]^{\lambda=\pm}$ when the $\lambda=\pm$ component (normally discarded because $\mu=+$ is the "good" component) is actually extracted.
- Manifest covariance of CLF is *violated* in type-I via an unphysical lightlike-vector coefficient $g_{V,A}$ that is reference-frame dependent.

Under type-II, all three problems resolve *simultaneously and for the same reason*: the $[b]_1^{(2)}$ integrals are odd about $x=\tfrac12$ in a way that makes them vanish numerically, so the $\lambda=0$ and $\lambda=\pm$ results coincide, the SLF result equals the CLF valence result, and $g_{V,A}=0$ in all frames. The authors verify this across $f_P$, $f_V$, $f_{1A}$, $f_{3A}$ and the $P\to P$ form factors $f_+(q^2)$, $f_-(q^2)$, and give updated predictions for $f_V$, $f_A$ (Tables 4 and 6) that compare well with experiment, LQCD, and QCD sum rules.

## What the paper does well

**A coherent, unified diagnosis.** The most interesting result is that *three* apparently distinct pathologies — the $[f_V]_{\text{CLF}}^{\lambda=0\neq\pm}$ inconsistency (the "puzzle" of refs. [28,33]), a SLF self-consistency problem that the authors show is new, and the manifest-covariance violation of ref. [28] — all trace back to one coefficient, $[b]_1^{(2)}$, which is nonvanishing only in the longitudinal mode. That this single term explains self-consistency *and* covariance *and* the ambiguous decomposition of the matrix element (the $c_\pm$ decomposition in the discussion of ref. [92]) is a genuinely clean piece of work. Section 3.4, where $[g_{V,A}]^{\lambda=0}$ is shown to be proportional to the same $[f_{V,A}]_{\text{full}}^{\lambda=0}-[f_{V,A}]_{\text{full}}^{\lambda=\pm}$ integrand as the self-consistency error (Eq. 97), is the strongest argument in the paper.

**Extension of Choi–Ji's prescription to zero-mode-affected quantities.** Choi and Ji introduced $M\to\tilde M_0$ in '14 and verified it only for $V$ mesons and $f_P$-type, zero-mode-free quantities. Here the authors apply it (and check it holds) for $f_{1A}$, $f_{3A}$, and $f_-(q^2)$, which is exactly where the prescription has not been tested. That is the empirical contribution of the paper.

**Physical plausibility of "why" type-II works.** The heavy-quark-limit argument ($M\sim m_Q\gg m_{\bar q}$, so $x\sim m_{\bar q}/M$ and $\tilde M_0\simeq M$) that shows type-I and type-II merge in that limit is a nice consistency check against a known result of ref. [28]. The connection between the Melosh transform, the spin-orbital wavefunction built from $\tilde M_0$, and the requirement that the constituent quarks be on-shell is a *physical* motivation for the replacement, not just a book-keeping trick — though it stops short of being a derivation.

**Updated phenomenology.** Tables 2, 4 and 6 give a consistent set of predictions. Overall the new $f_V$ numbers (e.g. $f_D=253\pm7$ MeV vs LQCD $223.5\pm8.4$, QCD-SR $250\pm8$) and $f_A$ numbers fall in the right range. There is some genuine value in producing a self-consistent, covariant prediction set even for a model that most people regard as phenomenological.

## Concerns and weaknesses

**1. The central claim of "numerical vanishing" is never elevated to a theorem.**

The whole type-II resolution rests on

$$\int dx\,\Delta^M_\text{full}(x)=0,\qquad \int dx\,d[f_-]_{\text{z.m.}}/dx=0,$$

which the authors demonstrate *only* by plotting $\Delta(x)$ (Figs. 2,3,4,5) and showing the positive area near small $x$ and the negative area near large $x$ "cancel" — and the integrands in the figures are evidently not exactly anti-symmetric. This is a numerical check. It would be substantially stronger to: (i) show the integrand is exactly anti-symmetric under $x\leftrightarrow 1-x$ (or identify the property of the Gaussian that ensures the integral vanishes), and (ii) verify the cancellation holds for a *different* parametrization of $\psi_s$ (e.g. a power-law or polynomial form) so it is not an artifact of the Gaussian choice. The current state is consistent with a Gaussian-specific feature, and the authors do not address this.

**2. The prescription is a phenomenological rule, not a derivation.**

The type-II replacement $M\to\tilde M_0$ "in each and every term of the integrand" is presented with reasoning (Melosht transform, on-shell conditions) that is suggestive but stops well short of a derivation. The authors themselves acknowledge in §3.4 that "the replacement $M\to\tilde M_0$ would possibly be less or even not essential in other cases" — i.e. for a different choice of vertex operator. The paper is therefore really "for *this* vertex and *this* GF form, type-II works" and the extrapolation to a general framework is made but not proved. The authors could have more explicitly framed the result this way.

**3. Circularity in the "validation."**

Note that $[Q]_{\text{SLF}}=[Q]_{\text{val}}$ (Eq. 78) is a strong match between two models, but both the SLF model *and* the CLF $h_M$ functions are in fact normalized/fitted to $f_P$, which is precisely the zero-mode-free quantity on which the two approaches are known to agree (Eq. 43). The agreement on $f_V$ and $f_A$ is therefore partly a consequence of the shared input. It would be genuinely independent validation if type-II could be applied to an observable *not* used in determining $\beta$ — for example the $P\to P$ form factors at a nonzero $q^2$ where the $f_-$ zero-mode piece is nonzero in type-I. The paper does compute $f_-(q^2)$ (Eq. 84) and shows type-II makes its zero-mode vanish, but does not compare a type-I vs type-II numerical value of $f_-$ against an external prediction (LQCD or light-cone sum rules) that would discriminate between the two prescriptions. This is a missed opportunity for a stronger empirical claim.

**4. Error bars are systematically low.**

The error estimates in Tables 4 and 6 are stated to be "due to the uncertainties of the parameter $\beta$." This propagates only the fit uncertainty on $\beta$. What is *not* included: the uncertainty from the Gaussian ansatz itself (how much would $f_V$ change if $\psi_s$ were a different shape with the same $\beta$ fitted), the uncertainty from the universal-$\beta$ assumption across different $q\bar q$ systems, and the systematic uncertainty of the type-II prescription itself (there is no error bar expressing "this is what you get if you use type-I instead" for $f_V$ or $f_A$ — which would have been a clean, honest measure of the scheme's sensitivity; numerically it would be ~10% for $f_V$ and ~10–20% for the heavier $f_A$). The $f_{J/\psi}$ entry in Table 4 carries 382 with a 96 MeV error, ~25%, which is honest; the $f_{\rho}=211\pm1$ MeV is clearly too optimistic given how much of the calculation is determined by assumptions that are being tested.

**5. The SLF $\lambda=\pm$ component is new but also somewhat expected.**

The claim that the SLF model *itself* is not self-consistent (Eq. 54 type-I) is the one genuinely new diagnostic result. It is derived from comparing Eqs. (44) and (45) and the argument is straightforward. However, in practice, nobody computes $[f_V]^{\lambda=\pm}$ in the SLF model because $\mu=+$ is the canonical "good" component; the physical SLF $f_V$ is defined *by* the $\mu=+$ component. Showing that a different (and non-standard) component gives a different number is a correct statement, but its physical interpretation as a "self-consistency violation" of the SLF model deserves more care in the paper — the question is whether it is a violation or simply a consequence of the SLF approach's construction being tied to $\mu=+$. The type-II "fix" (replace $M\to\tilde M_0$) effectively makes the two components agree, which is a positive result; but the authors could more carefully say what SLF physical quantity they are claiming should agree.

**6. Minor.**

- The paper uses $\ddot M_0$ (with two dots) and $\tilde M_0$ (with a tilde) inconsistently in places for the kinetic invariant mass. It is defined clearly at first use (Eq. 2), but a later equation in Section 2.1, Eq. (11), uses $\ddot M_0$ in the exponent of the Gaussian — that should be $\tilde M_0$ to be consistent with the definition. I could not verify from the image whether this is a real typo or a rendering issue.
- Table 1: the $f_{\eta_s}$ row has "LQCD: $186.2\pm1.4[69]$". Ref. [69] (Aoki et al., EPL 134(2017) or similar) is a general LQCD review; the citation should be a specific analysis.
- The introduction says "the standard light-front (SLF) quark model [1–4]" where [1] is Terentev 1976 and [2] is Berestetsky–Terentev 1977 — these are foundational references for the light-front quantization of QED, not specifically for the *quark model*. The quark-model references should be [3,4,8].

## Overall assessment

This is a careful, internally consistent study that makes three real contributions to the light-front quark-model literature: (i) a unified diagnosis of why type-I is self-inconsistent and non-covariant, (ii) a demonstration that the Choi–Ji "type-II" prescription resolves all three problems simultaneously and can be extended to axial-vector mesons and $f_-(q^2)$, and (iii) an honest note that the standard SLF model also has an analogous (less well known) self-consistency structure that type-II also fixes. The argument is well-motivated and the numerical work is solid. The main shortcoming is that the central claim (vanishing integrals) is demonstrated numerically but not established analytically, and the empirical "validation" is partly circular because all models are fit to $f_P$. The error bars under-report model uncertainty, and the authors should more explicitly state the scope of their prescription as applying to a specific vertex-operator form.

This is a worthwhile paper for the light-front quark-model community, a solid step toward clarifying the CLF formalism, but not a definitive resolution of the self-consistency question.

# Critique

**Paper:** "Self-consistency and covariance of light-front quark models: testing via P, V, and A meson decay constants, and P → P weak transition form factors" — Chang, Li, Li, Su, Yang (arXiv:1810.00296v3, hep-ph, 2018), 37 pp.

## What the paper does

The authors audit the internal consistency of two mesonic quark models — the Standard Light-Front (SLF) and the manifestly covariant Light-Front (CLF) model — under two ways of stitching the covariant Bethe–Salpeter description to the LF one (ref. [39]):

- **type-I:** replace the covariant bound-state function $\chi$ by the LF wave function $\psi$ and the covariant vertex denominator $D_{\rm con}\to D_{\rm LF}$;
- **type-II:** type-I plus an extra $M\to M_0$ replacement (physical mass $\to$ kinetic invariant mass) applied in *every* term of the integrand.

They then test, across $f_V$, $f_{1A}$, $f_{3A}$ and the $P\to P$ form factors $f^+,f^-$, whether the result is independent of the chosen polarization state ($\lambda=0$ vs $\lambda=\pm$). Their headline conclusions: type-I is self-inconsistent ($[f_M]_{\lambda=0}\ne[f_M]_{\lambda=\pm}$, driven by the $B_1^{(2)}$ term, Eqs. 46/47, 63–66) and non-covariant (residual $\omega$-dependence, Eq. 98, $[g]_{\lambda=0}\ne0$); type-II "resolves" both because the $B_1$ integrands become odd under $x\leftrightarrow\bar x$ and integrate to zero, giving $[Q]_{\rm SLF}=[Q]_{\rm val}=\dot{[}Q\,]_{\rm full}$ and $[g]_{\lambda=0}=0$ (Eqs. 57, 77, 78, 99), with $=\dot{}$ denoting "equal only numerically, not formally."

## Strengths

1. **The self-consistency test is a legitimate and important diagnostic.** A model that hands you different decay constants for the same meson depending on the bookkeeping polarization is internally broken. Extending the check from $f_V$ (the known puzzle) to $f_{1A}$, $f_{3A}$ and $f^-$ broadens a point that previously rested on a single observable.
2. **The covariance analysis (Sec. 3.4) is the genuinely new conceptual contribution.** Connecting the residual $\omega$-dependence (Eq. 91, 96, 93–95) to the *same* $B_1$ term that drives the self-consistency problem, and showing both are repaired together by $M\to M_0$, is a clean and nontrivial unification. Noting that the type-I violation is $\propto 1/p^+$ and thus reference-frame dependent is a sharp observation.
3. **Showing the SLF model itself needs $M\to M_0$ for self-consistency** (Eqs. 54, 72), not just the CLF one, is a fresh twist that reframes a problem usually discussed only for the covariant scheme.
4. **The $f^-(q^2)$ SLF formula (Eq. 82) is, per the authors, new and correct in derivation.**
5. Careful, systematic presentation; the analytic claims are backed by explicit numerics (Tables 3, 5, Figs. 2–5) that make the type-I vs type-II contrast unambiguous.

## Weaknesses

1. **The resolution is a numerical identity obtained *by construction* — a mild circularity at the heart of the paper.** In type-II the self-consistency "success" simply means $\int dx\, \Delta_M^{\rm full}=0$ (Eqs. 53, 71, 77). That integral vanishes because, once you impose $M\to M_0$, the offending integrands become antisymmetric under $x\leftrightarrow\bar x$. So the scheme is tuned so that the quantity the authors then check for consistency is forced to zero, and its own success is quoted as evidence of its validity. The physical motivation given (constituents on-shell; CLF vertex = SLF Melosh structure, end of Sec. 3.4) is reasonable but post-hoc. The paper is honest about $=\dot{}$ being "numerical only," which is the right hedge, but readers should be clear this is an algebraic cancellation engineered by a specific replacement, not a derivation of self-consistency from first principles.
2. **The quoted prediction uncertainties (Tables 4, 6) are misleadingly small.** Errors like $f_\rho=211\pm 1$ MeV or $f_{3A}^{(qq\bar q)}=220\pm 1$ MeV come *only* from propagating the $\beta$ uncertainty from the $f_P$ fit. They ignore all real model error: Gaussian-vs-other wave-function form, the assumption that $\beta$ is universal across $P/V/1A/3A$ for a given $q\bar q$ system (a pseudoscalar and vector of the same flavor need not share a width; there is no spin-dependent width here), fixed constituent masses, and the leading-Fock-state truncation. A constituent-quark model carries 10–20% shape/model uncertainty, not sub-1%. The "in consistence with data" is therefore much less impressive than the error bars suggest, and the "updated predictions" would be better communicated with honest, larger (systematic) error bars.
3. **The most novel result — $f^-(q^2)$ — is the least validated.** Table 4/6 benchmark the decay constants against PDG/LQCD/QCDSR, but there is no comparison of the newly derived $f^-(q^2)$ (or even the $P\to P$ form factors) to any data or lattice result. Fig. 5 only shows type-I vs type-II $\Delta$-curves, with no external anchor. So the section that adds the most new content is the one with no external validation.
4. **The "$1A$" test is thin.** In the isospin limit $m_1=m_2$ the $1A(q\bar q)$ decay constant is identically zero (or antisymmetric in $x$), so the only nontrivial flavor is $(cq\bar)$. One effective case is a weak test of a broader claim.
5. **Dependence on decomposition conventions.** The authors acknowledge (Sec. 3.4) that the *appearance* of the type-I inconsistency for $\lambda=\pm$ depends on how the $\omega$-dependent term is decomposed; it is only "trivial" in type-II. This is handled honestly, but it means the "puzzle" being solved is partly a convention choice, which slightly weakens the framing of a definitive resolution.
6. **Occasional over-claiming in interpretation.** The statement that type-II CLF "can be regarded as a covariant expression for the SLF quark model with zero-modes properly included" (Sec. 3.4) is a strong ontological claim resting on the somewhat circular equality $[Q]_{\rm SLF}=[Q]_{\rm val}$. The closing caveat (other vertex operators may not require $M\to M_0$) is appropriately self-limiting, but the strong language elsewhere overstates what a consistent prescription establishes.

## Bottom line

A competent, careful, and internally consistent extension of Choi–Ji's type-II scheme to additional spin-1/spin-0 observables, with a genuinely nice unification of self-consistency and manifest covariance. It is well motivated and the numerical evidence is solid. It does not reach "excellent," however, because the core result is a by-construction cancellation rather than a from-scratch validity argument, the precision of the headline predictions is overstated, and the new form-factor result goes unvalidated against data. A solid, publishable sub-specialty contribution.

The paper systematically verifies whether two prescriptions for mapping a covariant Bethe–Salpeter amplitude onto light-front quantities—the traditional "type-I" correspondence and Choi & Ji's "type-II" correspondence (which adds a replacement M→M₀, the dynamical invariant mass, throughout the integrand)—give self-consistent and covariant results. The one genuinely new structural insight is the covariance–self-consistency unification (§3.4). However, the physical surprise is low: no reader familiar with Ref. It is a "consistency audit" paper of high internal quality but limited reach.

## [1810.00296v3-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies

## My understanding of the logical argument

**The problem being attacked.** In light-front (LF) quark models, spin-1 meson decay constants must be extracted from *both* polarization channels: the longitudinal ($\lambda=0$) and transverse ($\lambda=\pm$) polarization states. These two channels give the same physical $f_M$ only if the underlying model is self-consistent (a consequence of relativistic covariance of the bound state). The "covariant light-front" (CLF) model was supposed to cure the historical "zero-mode problem" of the standard LF (SLF) model, but Jaus/Cheng et al. had already noticed a *residual* inconsistency: in the "type-I correspondence" (matching the manifestly covariant Bethe-Salpeter vertex $M$-dependence to the SLF one by replacing $M \to \hat M_0 \equiv \sqrt{M_0^2-(m_1-m_2)^2}$ *only in the denominator factor* $D_M$), the two polarization channels disagree for $f_V, f_{^1A}, f_{^3A}$:

$$[f_M]_{\rm full}^{\lambda=0} \neq [f_M]_{\rm full}^{\lambda=\pm}\quad\text{(type-I).}$$

The difference is entirely traceable to one coefficient $B_1^{(2)}$ multiplying the spurious lightlike vector $\omega^\mu=(0,2,0_\perp)$, which contributes only to the $\lambda=0$ channel.

**The proposed fix ("type-II").** Choi & Ji (2014) proposed pushing the replacement *through the whole integrand* — $M \to \hat M_0$ everywhere — rather than only in the $D$ factor. The paper's central empirical observation is that under type-II the *residual*, $B_1^{(2)}$-driven term $\Delta_{\rm full}^M(x)$ is an odd-ish function of $x$ whose integral over $x$ vanishes, so the two channels become numerically (but not formally) equal:

$$[f_M]_{\rm full}^{\lambda=0} \doteq [f_M]_{\rm full}^{\lambda=\pm}\quad\text{(type-II).}$$

**Three claims the paper establishes across P, V, $^{1,3}$A mesons and $P\to P$ form factors:**

1. **The same $M\to\hat M_0$ replacement is also required for self-consistency of the *standard* SLF model** ($[f_M]_{\rm SLF}^{\lambda=0} \doteq [f_M]_{\rm SLF}^{\lambda=\pm}$). This is a genuinely new result of this paper — previous work implicitly assumed the SLF type-I prescription was self-consistent.
2. **In type-II, the SLF valence result coincides with the CLF valence (and, numerically, with CLF full):** $[\mathcal Q]_{\rm SLF} \equiv [\mathcal Q]_{\rm val.} \doteq [\mathcal Q]_{\rm full}$, because zero-mode contributions exist only formally and integrate to zero. So the long-standing "SLF misses zero-modes" objection (which motivated the type-I correspondence in the first place) is neutralized.
3. **Manifest covariance, which is violated in type-I** (the spurious $\omega^\mu$ dependence leaves a nonzero, frame-dependent unphysical constant $g_{V,A}\neq 0$ at $\lambda=0$), **is recovered in type-II** because the same $B_1^{(2)}$ integrals that drive the self-consistency violation must vanish for covariance, and they do exactly under type-II.

The paper closes the loop with a physical interpretation: the vertex functions used in CLF were actually derived as the Melosh-rotation form of SLF spin-orbital wave functions with on-shell constituents, so $\hat M_0$ (the on-shell kinematic invariant mass) is the *natural* mass parameter, and the type-I prescription was using a kinematically inconsistent mass in the vertex. The updated numerical predictions for $f_\rho, f_{K^*}, f_{D^*}, f_{D_s^*}, f_{J/\psi}$, $f_{B^*}, f_{B_s^*}$, etc. are compared with experiment, LQCD and QCD sum rules with reasonable agreement.

## Assessment and feedback

**Strengths.**
- The diagnosis is clean: every inconsistency is reduced to a single coefficient $B_1^{(2)}$ multiplying $\omega^\mu$, which makes the logical structure very transparent.
- The covariance section (§3.4) is the best part — showing that the *same* integrals that must vanish for self-consistency (Eq. 96) are precisely the same that $g_{V,A}\propto \int dx\,\chi_M B_1^{(2)}/(\omega\cdot p)$ must vanish is a genuinely nice unification of two apparently separate pathologies into one origin.
- The demonstration that SLF *also* needs the type-II correction is a real addition; it corrects a longstanding tacit assumption (that $[f_V]_{\rm SLF}^{\lambda=0}$ is the trustworthy number and $\lambda=\pm$ is missing dynamics). This is a useful pedagogical and conceptual contribution within the community.
- The numerics are thorough: 20+ independent quantities are tested, and the heavy-quark-limit discussion ($M_{B^*}$, $M_{B_s^*}$) gives a clean asymptotic regime where type-I and type-II must converge, which bounds the type-I scheme's failure mode.

**Weaknesses and reservations.**
1. **Circularity of the "self-consistency" criterion.** The type-II prescription was *derived* (by Choi & Ji) precisely by matching the CLF and SLF expressions on the quantities that are expected not to carry zero-modes (e.g. $f_P$, $f_+$), and the paper's criterion of "self-consistency" is then tested on the $B_1^{(2)}$-driven *difference* between the two polarization channels. Because the two channels only differ by one specific coefficient, *any* prescription that makes $\int dx\,B_1^{(2)}\cdot(\text{kinematic weight})=0$ will pass. The paper does not establish that type-II is *the* self-consistent prescription (or the unique one), only that it is *a* self-consistent one. A more rigorous argument would link the vanishing integral to a first-principles requirement (e.g. a Ward-Takahashi-like identity, or to solving the QCD bound-state equation).
2. **Numerical cancellation is fragile in principle, though the paper is honest about it.** They use $\doteq$ to mean "equal only numerically," and the cancellation $\int_0^1 dx\, \Delta_{\rm full}^M(x)=0$ is a cancellation between positive and negative regions. The robustness across many mesons and the heavy-quark-limit argument mitigate this, but the underlying "oddness" of $\Delta_{\rm full}$ is not *proven* — it's exhibited case by case. A short analytic demonstration that $\Delta_{\rm full}^M(x) = -\Delta_{\rm full}^M(1-x)$ under type-II (which the plots suggest strongly) would upgrade the result from empirical to structural.
3. **Scope is bounded by the chosen vertex model.** The paper (correctly) notes in the last paragraph of §3.4 that the vertex operators $\Gamma_M$ in Eq. (26) are model-dependent, and the $M\to\hat M_0$ replacement may be unnecessary for other choices of vertex. So the claim is really confined to the Gaussian-WF, on-shell-constituent, Jaus/Cheng family of vertex models. This deserves more emphasis in the abstract and conclusion — right now, a reader might over-generalize.
4. **Predictions are dominated by a single scale fit.** All predictions ride on $\beta$ extracted from $f_P$ (Table 2). The uncertainties quoted (e.g. $f_\rho=211\pm1$ MeV) are dominated by $\beta$ and thus by the input $f_P$ precision — they are not a genuinely independent test of the type-II prescription. The comparison to LQCD (e.g. $f_\rho$: 199±4, $f_{B^*}$: 185.9±7.2 vs. this work's 205±5) is reassuring but the overlap in uncertainties is modest; a couple of points (notably $f_{B_s^*}$: 465±7 vs. LQCD 422±13) sit slightly away from the lattice in a way worth explicitly flagging as a mild tension.
5. **Some claims in the abstract are stronger than the evidence strictly supports.** For instance, "the zero-mode contributions … vanish numerically" is demonstrated at a handful of discrete points $q^2 = 0, 0.5, 1\,{\rm GeV}^2$ for $f_-$, not as a function of $q^2$; and the claim $[\mathcal Q]_{\rm val.} \doteq [\mathcal Q]_{\rm full}$ for axial mesons holds "up to" the $m_1-m_2\to 0$ (isospin-symmetric) case, which is correctly footnoted but easy to skim past.

**Bottom line.** The paper is a careful, systematic, and genuinely useful consolidation in the LF-quark-model subfield. Its strongest contributions are (i) showing that SLF *also* needs the type-II replacement — this is new and corrects a common misreading of SLF — and (ii) unifying the self-consistency failure and the covariance failure of type-I CLF into a common origin. These are not field-shaping, but they are real advances for the community that uses these models. The conceptual framework (use $\hat M_0$ in every term rather than just $D$) is ultimately Choi & Ji's, so the originality of the *mechanism* is modest; the originality of this paper lies in the breadth of verification and the covariance section. I would not want to see the headline claims ("the CLF quark model is self-consistent") taken as universally true — they are valid for *this* class of Gaussian-WF, on-shell-vertex models, and the cancellation is numerical, not structural.

## Evaluation of "Self-consistency and covariance of light-front quark models" (Chang, Li, Li, Su, Yang; arXiv:1810.00296v3)

**Strangest / most load-bearing quantitative claim.** The paper is mostly *internal* consistency work — its headline theorems (the type-II correspondence M →M₀ resolving the f_V "polarization inconsistency puzzle," the SLF-valence = CLF-valence identity, and the covariance recovery) are **exact numerical equalities** that descend from algebraic manipulation, not statistical comparisons. Since the prediction target differs from the fit target and the two comparison frameworks are fully independent, this is a genuine out-of-sample check, not a circular fit-then-match. However, the model is extremely low-capacity: a single shape parameter (β), a Gaussian ansatz, and fixed constituent masses, so the "consistency" carries most of its weight on those prior assumptions.

### Are baselines current?
Yes, for a December 2018 paper. PDG [68] is the 2018 update (Phys. Rev. D 98, 030001); LQCD cites 2012–2017; QCDSR cites 2009–2016. All are period-appropriate.

**Partially — and this is the main weakness.**
- β has fit error bars (Table 2) that propagate into the f_V/f_A predictions (Tables 4, 6). Good.

But as a *quantitative* paper its external benchmark (Table 4) is asserted, not tested statistically, one of its ten predictions (f_{J/ψ}) is effectively wrong relative to lattice/experiment, and the "self-consistency" results are identities rather than measurements.

## [1810.00296v3-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


I've read the paper in full (Chang, Li, Li, Su, Yang; "Self-consistency and covariance of light-front quark models: testing via $P$, $V$, and $A$ meson decay constants, and $P\to P$ weak transition form factors," arXiv:1810.00296v3). Below is my evaluation.

## The paper's argument chain

1. **Two correspondence schemes** between the manifestly-covariant (CLF) and the standard (SLF) light-front quark models:
   - *type-I* (Cheng *et al.* [34]): replace the vertex function $\chi_M \to \psi_M$ and $M\to M_0$ only inside the $D$ factor.
   - *type-II* (Choi & Ji [39], one author is on both papers): additionally replace $M\to M_0$ in every term of the integrand.

2. **Diagnosis**: under type-I, $[f_V]^{\lambda=0}_{\rm CLF}\neq[f_V]^{\lambda=\pm}_{\rm CLF}$ because of an extra $B_1^{(2)}$ contribution to the $\lambda=0$ extraction; the same defect shows up in $f_{1A}$, $f_{3A}$, $f_-(q^2)$, and in the lightlike-vector $\omega^\mu$ coefficient $g_V$ (the covariance-violating piece).

3. **Claim**: type-II is the self-consistent scheme. It (a) makes $f_V$ and $f_A$ polarization-independent, (b) makes the SLF result coincide with the CLF valence contribution, (c) makes the zero-mode contribution to $f_{V,{}^{1}A,{}^{3}A},f_- (q^2)$ vanish, and (d) restores covariance ($g_{V,A}=0$). The paper extends the Choi–Ji test (which only did $f_V$) to the $1A$ and $3A$ channels and to $P\to P$ form factors, and adds the covariance argument in §3.4.

## The single weakest link

The step at the top of the third bullet of §3.1:

> "However, within the type-II scheme, the positive $\Delta_{\rm full}^V$ at small $x$ and the negative one at large $x$ can exactly cancel each other, resulting in $\int dx\,\Delta_{\rm full}^V = 0$."

This single cancellation $\int dx\,\Delta_{\rm full}^{V}(x)=0$ is the hinge on which **every** downstream conclusion turns. Concretely:

- The "self-consistency" of type-II, Eq. (53) $[f_V]^{\lambda=0}_{\rm full}\stackrel{=}{=}[f_V]^{\lambda=\pm}_{\rm full}$, is equivalent to exactly this integral being zero (via Eq. 50–51, $\Delta_{\rm full}^V(x)=\frac{{\rm d}[f_V]^{\lambda=0}_{\rm full}}{{\rm d}x}-\frac{{\rm d}[f_V]^{\lambda=\pm}_{\rm full}}{{\rm d}x}$).
- The polarization-independence for the $1A$ and ${}^{3}A$ mesons (Eqs. 71, 72) is the same integral in different variables.
- The SLF–CLF valence equivalence under type-II (Eq. 55) requires it.
- The covariance recovery in §3.4 is equivalent to it again: the covariance condition $g_{V,A}=0$ is $\int dx\,d^2k_\perp \,\chi_M(x,k_\perp^2)\,B_1^{(2)}/D_{M,con}=0$, which is literally the $\Delta_{\rm full}$ integral rewritten (Eq. 97). So the §3.4 "independent" covariance argument is the *same* identity in different clothing — it is not new evidence.

What the paper actually does, however, is **numerically plot** $\Delta_{\rm full}$ (Figs. 2, 3, 4) and observe area cancellation. The paper never gives an analytic proof (Ward identity, a consequence of Lorentz covariance, a symmetry of $B_1^{(2)}=A_1^{(1)}C_1^{(1)}-A_1^{(2)}$, or a structural consequence of the choice $D_{M,LF}=M_0+m_1+m_2$ combined with the Gaussian form of $\psi_s$) that the integral must vanish. The authors know this; throughout the paper they use the symbol "$\stackrel{=}{=}$" ("numerically but not formally equal") for precisely these relations, and even concede in the closing paragraph that "$\psi_s$, $\psi_p$ are not the only choice ... the replacement $M\to M_0$ would possibly be less or even not essential in other cases." The caveat is a quiet admission that the central claim is not established for the class of models it is being asserted for.

There is a more pointed structural problem with the same step: the identity is a **coincidence of the particular choice of $B_1^{(2)}$, $D_{M,LF}=M_0+m_1+m_2$, and the Gaussian wave function with parameters $\beta$ fitted to $f_P$ data**. The paper verifies it for a finite set: $\rho, D^*$ for $f_V$; $b_1, a_1, D_1$ ($1A$, ${}^{3}A$) for $q\bar q$ and $c\bar q$; $D\to\pi$ for $f_-$. A single change in these inputs (a different vertex structure in the CLF kernel, a chiral-invariant form of $\chi_V$, off-shell quarks in the bound state) could break the cancellation and re-open the $\lambda$-dependence, while the *prescription* "type-II is self-consistent" would stand unchanged in the paper's text. That is a conclusion not entailed by the data shown.

A secondary structural weakness, downstream of the same step: the final summary statement ("the valence contribution of the CLF model should be the same as the SLF one **not only numerically but also formally**, $O^{\rm val}=O^{\rm SLF}$", §3.4 last paragraph) is contradicted by the paper's own results. Eq. (78) shows $[Q]_{\rm SLF}=[Q]_{\rm val}\stackrel{=}{=}[Q]_{\rm full}$: the equality with the *full* CLF result is marked "$\stackrel{=}{=}$" (numerical only). So the "formal" SLF–CLF equivalence the paper ends on really only holds in one of the two directions, again because the cancellation is numerical.

## What is sound

- The identification of the $B_1^{(2)}$ term as the source of the $\lambda$-dependence is clean and well-traced.
- The extensions to ${}^{1}A$, ${}^{3}A$ and $P\to P$ form factors are genuinely useful bookkeeping for the community.
- The numerical results for $f_{V,A}$ (Tables 4, 6) are internally consistent and in reasonable agreement with experiment and LQCD.
- The observation that the standard $+$/− good/bad component separation in SLF is itself a small $\lambda$-dependence (the paper's first observation in §3.1: the type-I SLF scheme also has $[f_V]^{\lambda=0}_{\rm SLF}\neq [f_V]^{\lambda=\pm}_{\rm SLF}$) is a non-trivial addition to the literature.

## Scoring

Originality: extends a pre-existing proposal (Choi–Ji) to additional channels and ties the self-consistency problem to covariance. Not a new idea, but a useful extension. **3**.

Overall: internally consistent phenom paper; the central claim is *not* established analytically, the covariance argument is circular (same integral re-stated), and the closing caveat concedes generality. **3**.

Impact: meaningful for the LF-quark-model subgroup, but the conclusion "type-II is *the* self-consistent scheme" is not airtight; the field has not adopted the prescription as a rule. **2**.

## [1810.00296v3-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


## Critique of writing and framing — arXiv:1810.00296v3

This paper extends the Choi–Ji "type-II" correspondence (an extra replacement $M\to\hat M_0$ in the light-front integrand) from the vector-meson decay constant $f_V$ to $f_{1A}, f_{3A}$ and the $P\to P$ form factors, and to the SLF model. The computations are careful and the tables/figures are thorough, but the **framing repeatedly upgrades a model-dependent numerical observation into a claim of restored fundamental properties**. That is where the writing outruns the evidence.

### Sentences that overclaim

1. **"the results obtained are guaranteed to be covariant after the spurious contribution ... is canceled by the inclusion of zero-mode contributions"** (Sec. 1). *Guaranteed* is doing a lot of work: the paper itself later (Sec. 3.4) shows the type-I CLF result is *not* covariant, and the type-II "recovery" rests on two integrals of $\chi_M B_1^{(2)}/D_M$ happening to vanish — numerically, for the one set of vertex functions used. A guarantee requires this to hold for a class of vertices, which the authors concede is *not* the case ("the replacement $M\to\hat M_0$ would possibly be less or even not essential in other cases," p.31, and the explicit caveat on p.22 about quarkonium). The strong language in the introduction is not supported by the caveats in the body.

2. **"the problem ... can be 'resolved'"** (abstract; Eq. 3 region; conclusion). The quotes around *resolved* are a warning the authors themselves give, but the surrounding prose treats it as settled. In fact the "solution" is that the positive and negative parts of a $B_1^{(2)}$-weighted integrand cancel in $\int dx$ (p.14, "the positive $\Delta^V_\text{full}$ at small $x$ and the negative one at large $x$ can exactly cancel"). A near-exact integral cancellation of model-dependent sign-structured pieces is an *accident to be explained*, not a *resolution* to be reported. Calling a cancellation of two large, opposite-sign integrands a resolution of a self-consistency puzzle is the single strongest overclaim in the paper.

3. **Eq. (57):** "$[f_V]_\text{SLF}^{\lambda=0} = [f_V]_\text{val.}^{\lambda=0} \doteq [f_V]_\text{full}^{\lambda=0} \doteq \dots = [f_V]_\text{SLF}^{\lambda=\pm}$, (type-II)." The $\doteq$ notation is defined as "equal to each other only numerically but not formally" (p.14). Stacking four numerical equalities and writing them as a chain of "=" reads as an identity; the honest statement is: *for these Gaussian wave functions and these particular vertices, five quantities that differ at the integrand level happen to match to working precision*. The chain obscures that each link is a separate numerical coincidence.

4. **"the manifest covariance of the covariant light-front quark model is violated in the traditional type-I scheme, but can be recovered by taking the type-II correspondence"** (abstract / Sec. 3.4). "Recovered" implies covariance was merely hidden. What is shown (p.29) is that $[g_{V,A}]^{\lambda=0}=0$ in type-II, while in type-I it is nonzero and *frame-dependent*. The mechanism is again the same integral vanishing. "Recovery of covariance" overstates what a vanishing frame-dependent constant in one model proves about covariance.

5. **"we therefore use [the fitted $\beta$ values] ... in our following numerical calculations." / "generally in agreement with that obtained by the variational principle [74]"** (Table 2 discussion, p.12). The fit $\beta$ values are close to [74], but the paper's own predictions for heavy states show the accumulated model sensitivity (Table 4): $f_{J/\psi}=382_{-96}^{+96}$ vs. data $411\pm5$ and LQCD $418\pm9$; $f_{D_s^*}=314\pm6$ vs. $290\pm11$; $f_{B_c^*}=465\pm7$ vs. $453\pm20$. "Generally in consistence with the experimental data" (p.17) is generous when one of the headline charm states is off by $\sim3\sigma$ and its error band dominates. The agreement that *is* claimed is largely on the light mesons the model was fit to.

6. **"This confirms the findings of Ref. [39] and implies that the type-II correspondence might be a self-consistency scheme"** (p.14). "Confirms" overstates: Ref. [39] *postulated* the replacement; this paper *tests* it in a specific channel and vertex set. The word "implies ... self-consistency scheme" is fine, but the paper's own summary bullet points it up as an *achieved* self-consistent scheme (Sec. 4) without carrying the "might" through.

7. **Equivalence of the models, p.30:** "the CLF results in the type-I scheme would approach to that in the type-II scheme in the heavy-quark limit." This depends on $M_0\simeq M$ being "a good approximation" justified by hand-waving the dominant $k_\perp\lesssim1$ GeV region — an unquantified assumption presented as a theorem.

### Grammar/polish notes (framing-relevant)
- "In consistence with" (p.17), "systematical way," "recapitulate," "can be easily found" repeated ~10×, "find surprisingly" (Sec. 3.1) — the frequent insertion of *easily/surprisingly* softens claims rhetorically but masks that several results follow directly from the definition of the type-II rule rather than from independent work.

### What an adversarial reviewer would put in their first paragraph

> "The type-II correspondence advocated by Choi and Ji (Ref. 39) is not derived from a symmetry; it is an ad hoc prescription — replace $M$ by $\hat M_0$ in every term of the light-front integrand — introduced to force five a priori unrelated quantities to agree. The present work adds essentially no new mechanism: the vanishing of the 'zero-mode' contribution, $[f_V]_\text{z.m.}^{\lambda=\pm,0}\doteq 0$, is just the statement that a positive hump and a negative trough of a model-dependent integrand cancel in $\int dx$. The authors present this coincidental cancellation as a *resolution* of the 'CLF inconsistency puzzle' and as the *recovery of Lorentz covariance*, but both claims rest on one particular set of Gaussian wave functions and a single family of vertices — precisely for which the authors acknowledge the replacement 'would possibly be less or even not essential in other cases.' I therefore ask the authors to (i) justify the $M\to\hat M_0$ rule rather than merely motivate it, (ii) test it against at least one independently chosen vertex model in which the cancellation is not built in, and (iii) temper 'resolved/recovered/guaranteed' to 'found to hold for the present class of models.'"

### Bottom line
Technically competent and useful to the light-front-model community, but the paper's central selling point — that an ad hoc replacement *resolves* a self-consistency puzzle and *restores covariance* — is a numerical, model-specific observation dressed in universal language. The evidence supports *"the type-II rule makes $f_V, f_{1A}, f_{3A}$ self-consistent **in this model**"*; it does not support *"covariance is recovered"* or *"guaranteed."*

The *type-II* correspondence — which adds an extra replacement $M\to\bar M_{0}$ (the kinetic invariant mass) into every term of the integrand — was proposed by Choi & Ji (Phys. Rev. D **89**, 033011 [39]) to resolve the $f_V$ inconsistency.

Here the authors systematically test both correspondences across a broad and relevant set of observables: $f_P$, $f_V$, $f_{_{1}A}$, $f_{_{3}A}$, and the $P\to P$ form factors $f_{+}(q^2)$ and $f_{-}(q^2)$. The central claims are: (i) the type-II correspondence yields self-consistent ($\lambda$-independent) results in both the CLF and, crucially, the *standard* SLF quark models; (ii) the zero-mode contributions to these quantities exist only formally and vanish numerically under type-II; (iii) $|\mathcal Q|_{\rm val}\doteq|\mathcal Q|_{\rm full}$ and $|\mathcal Q|_{\rm SLF}\doteq|\mathcal Q|_{\rm val}$; and (iv) the manifest covariance of the CLF model is violated in type-I (via a residual unphysical constant $g_{V,A}\neq 0$) but recovered in type-II. Updated numerical predictions for $f_V$, $f_A$ are given and compared to experiment, LQCD, and QCDSR.

## Strengths

1. **Clear, well-motivated problem.** The self-consistency requirement (polarization independence of a covariant decay constant) is physically sound, and the paper frames the existing "puzzle" cleanly.
2. **Breadth of the test.** Rather than only re-examining $f_V$, the authors extend the analysis to $f_{_{1}A}$, $f_{_{3}A}$, and $P\to P$ form factors, giving the type-II proposal a much broader empirical footing. The finding that the $\lambda=0/\lambda=\pm$ discrepancy also appears in the *standard* SLF model (Eq. 54) is a genuinely useful new observation, and the demonstration that the covariance violation and the self-consistency defect share a common origin and are resolved simultaneously (Sec. 3.4) is a nice unifying point.
3. **Internal consistency and checking against prior work.** The derivations reproduce and agree with the known results in the literature (Refs. [28, 33, 34, 39]), which makes the analysis trustworthy and self-contained.
4. **Honest treatment of the key caveat.** The authors are explicit, throughout, that the crucial cancellations (e.g. $\int dx\,x\,\Delta^V_{\rm full}(x)=0$) hold *numerically* (they introduce the symbol $\doteq$ for this), not as formal identities. This candour is a virtue.
5. **Reasonable phenomenology in the light sector.** Table 4 light-flavor predictions ($f_\rho=211\pm1$ vs data $210\pm4$; $f_\phi$; $f_{\Upsilon(1S)}=713\pm34$ vs data $708\pm8$) are competitive with lattice and sum-rule estimates.

## Weaknesses and concerns

1. **The central result rests on numerical, not proven, cancellations.** The headline "resolution" of the self-consistency puzzle is the statement that the residual $\mathcal B_1^{(2)}$ contribution integrates to zero. The paper demonstrates this only by numerical integration of a specific Gaussian s/p-wave wave function with a $\bar M_0$ on-shell condition. There is no analytic argument showing that $\int dx\,x\,\Delta^M_{\rm full}(x)=0$ (and the analogous $A$-meson and form-factor identities, Figs. 2–5) holds beyond the chosen ansatz. A "resolution" that works because a particular integral happens to cancel is weaker than the framing ("the puzzle can be *resolved*") implies. As the authors themselves note in Sec. 3.4/4, the LF vertex operators are model-dependent and "$M\to\bar M_0$ would possibly be less or even not essential in other cases" — so generality of the conclusion is unestablished. A derivation of the identity from the on-shell condition (or at least a statement of the conditions of the WFs for which it holds) would substantially strengthen the paper.

2. **Error bars are systematically underestimated.** The quoted uncertainties in Tables 4 and 6 (e.g. $f_\rho=211\pm1$ MeV) are propagated solely from the $\beta$ value obtained from the $f_P$ fit. These do not reflect the sizeable phenomenological/systematic uncertainty of a non-perturbative model. This is what makes several heavy-flavor predictions look "precise" while actually tensioning with lattice results: $f_{D_s^*}=314\pm6$ vs LQCD $268.8\pm6.6$ (a $\sim 45$ MeV, several-σ gap); $f_{B^*}=205\pm5$ vs LQCD $185.9$; $f_{B_s^*}=246\pm4$ vs LQCD $223.1$; $f_{B_c^*}=465\pm7$ vs LQCD $422\pm13$. Light-sector agreement (where the $\beta$ fit is anchored) does not transfer to these channels. The paper should carry model-systematic estimates rather than implying $\mathcal O(1{-}6)$ MeV accuracy.

3. **No new prediction-driven discovery.** The physical payoff (updated tables, Figs.) is confirmatory: the type-II numbers land where QCDSR and (mostly) experiment/lattice land. The paper is, in essence, a consolidation and extension of Ref. [39]'s proposal plus a new SLF-side and covariance argument. That is valuable, but it is not novel *physics* — readers seeking a new observable or a rigorous theorem will find neither.

4. **Minor presentation issues.** Inconsistent abbreviations for QCD sum rules (Table 4 uses "QCD SR" while the text uses "QCDSR"). The density of the type-I/type-II, $\doteq$, full/valence notation is heavy and, in places (e.g. the chain of equalities in Eq. 57 and its generalization, Eq. 77), the reader must work carefully to track *which* equality is formal and which is numerical.

## Assessment

This is a technically competent, well-motivated, and honestly written study that clarifies a real foundation question in the LF-quark-model subfield. Its derivations are internally consistent and correctly benchmarked against prior work, and it adds two genuinely useful observations (the SLF-side $M\to\bar M_0$ requirement and the covariance-recovery argument) on top of the Choi–Ji type-II proposal. Its main limitations are (a) that the decisive "resolution" of the inconsistency is numerical rather than proven, limiting the generality of the central claim, and (b) that the quoted uncertainties do not capture model systematics, which exaggerates the apparent precision of the (somewhat tensioned) heavy-flavor predictions.

As far as a journal-level decision: this is a sound, self-contained technical paper that would merit acceptance in a solid hadronic-physics venue (e.g. Physical Review D); it is not, I would judge, at the level of novelty or depth needed for a top-tier general journal, primarily because the central identity is demonstrated numerically for a specific ansatz rather than established generally.

## [1810.00296v3-mentor] Mentor


I've read the full paper (arXiv:1810.00296v3, Chang, Li, Li, Su, Yang, *Self-consistency and covariance of light-front quark models: testing via P, V, and A meson decay constants, and P→P weak transition form factors*). Here is my review as a senior scientist would give you, before you put this in front of a community.

## What the paper does

You test the internal consistency of two closely related frameworks — the standard light-front (SLF) quark model and the manifestly-covariant light-front (CLF) quark model — by computing current matrix elements (P, V, ¹A, ³A decay constants and the P→P f₊, f₋ form factors) two ways and checking four things: (i) self-consistency (results from λ=0 vs. λ=± polarizations agree), (ii) the size of the zero-mode (z-m) contribution, (iii) the SLF ↔ CLF correspondence, and (iv) Lorentz covariance (vanishing of the unphysical ω-dependent constant $g_{V,A}$). The central mechanism is the "type-I vs. type-II" correspondence: type-I uses only $D_{M,\mathrm{con}}\to D_{M,\mathrm{LF}}$, while type-II additionally replaces $M\to\widetilde M_0$ in every term of the integrand (the latter from Choi & Ji, your Ref. [39]).

## What works well

- **The problem is real and the execution is clean.** The $f_V$ "inconsistency puzzle" (Eq. 1, the ~10% λ-split from the $B_1^{(2)}$ term) was a genuine open annoyance in the CLF literature. You don't just note it — you build it into a general test and run the same test on $f_{1A}, f_{3A}, f_\pm$.
- **The strongest new finding is the SLF side.** Eq. (54) — that the *traditional* SLF quark model is itself non-self-consistent, $[f_M]^{\lambda=0}_{\rm SLF}\ne[f_M]^{\lambda=\pm}_{\rm SLF}$ in type-I, and needs the *same* $M\to\widetilde M_0$ replacement — is a nice reframe. The fix is not a CLF bookkeeping trick; it is a statement about the on-shell kinematics of the valence Fock state that applies to both approaches. I think this elevates the paper beyond "confirming [39]."
- **The covariance argument (Sec. 3.4) is well constructed.** Showing that $g_{V,A}^{\lambda=0}$ is frame-dependent ($\propto 1/p^+$, nonzero in type-I, zero in type-II, Eq. 96–99) and that this is the *same* term responsible for the self-consistency failure (Eq. 97) is a satisfying unification. That one $B_1^{(2)}$ object both breaks self-consistency and breaks covariance is the paper's most elegant result.
- **Honest notation.** Distinguishing formal equality (=) from numerical equality ($\doteq$) throughout is the right call and keeps the "resolution" from being oversold.
- Numerics are sensible: β fitted to $f_P$ and compared to the variational principle, errors propagated, predictions compared to LQCD/QCDSR/data (Tables 4, 6) — mostly in agreement.

## What I would push back on before you call this done

1. **The central claim is numerical, and you should push harder on whether it is structural.** The whole "resolution" rests on $\int dx\,\Delta^M_{\rm full}(x)=0$ in type-II (Eqs. 53, 71, 72, 89). Right now this is demonstrated by doing the integral with your specific Gaussian s-wave WFs and your specific vertex operators. Why does it vanish? My reading is that in type-II the integrand becomes odd under $x\leftrightarrow\bar x$ (or is a total derivative) because $\widetilde M_0$ is $x\leftrightarrow\bar x$-symmetric while $M$ is not. If that's the reason, it would hold for *any* even-in-$k_z$ radial WF and be a structural statement — and that's a much stronger claim than "we checked three WFs." You currently only gesture at it in the closing paragraph ("all constituents on mass shell $\Rightarrow$ $\widetilde M_0$ is the natural scale"). **Do the $x\leftrightarrow\bar x$ symmetry argument explicitly**, state the minimal WF/vertex conditions under which $\int\Delta=0$, and demote "for other vertex functions this may not be necessary" (p. 31) from a footnote-caveat to a scoped result. That is the difference between "the numbers cancel for our ansatz" and "type-II self-consistency is guaranteed."

2. **The spin-1 zero-mode tension is under-exploited.** You open by saying the take-the-$\mu=+$ recipe "is in fact always invalid for many cases, for instance the composite spin-1 systems [28]" (p. 2) — the standard argument that valence-only Fock-space truncations *fail* for spin-1. Yet your headline result is that spin-1 ($f_V$) *is* self-consistent and the z-m contribution *numerically vanishes* in type-II. These two statements sit in tension and you never directly reconcile them. A reader will ask: if spin-1 zero-modes are generically nontrivial, what makes *these* spin-1 decay constants in the rest frame, at these $q^2$, safe? Answering it (likely: the specific operator structure + rest-frame + time-zero current means the z-m integrand has no pole, i.e. $\hat N_2=0$ at the physical point) would turn the paper's biggest potential objection into a selling point. Right now it reads like you've asserted self-consistency for exactly the regime the literature warns about.

3. **"Resolving the puzzle" is correspondence-tuning, not a physical prediction.** Be clear-eyed in the intro/summary: type-II was *chosen* (by [39]) to make $f_V$ consistent, and the rest of the paper shows this *same* choice also fixes the other quantities. That's reassuring and worth saying plainly — it's a *unified* parameter of the correspondence, not an ad hoc fix per observable. But don't let the framing overclaim that a physical puzzle has been "solved"; what you've shown is that the $M\to\widetilde M_0$ correspondence is the self-consistent *and* covariant way to map the covariant BS model onto the LF one. That is a solid, publishable claim, but it's a consistency result, not new physics.

4. **Quantify your deviations, not just note them.** Your $f_{D_s^*}$ = 314 vs LQCD 268.8 and $f_{J/\psi}$ = 382 vs 418 are visibly off. In a "testing" paper the numerics are your credibility, so a short paragraph on which states are under/over-predicted and the probable reason (heavy-light endpoint sensitivity of the Gaussian, β universality assumption) would pre-empt reviewer concern. The $f_{B^*_{c,\text{...}}}$ entries have huge relative errors — fine, but one line explaining them helps.

5. **Minor.** (a) The "type-I/type-II" labels collide with unrelated "type" usage elsewhere in the LF literature; a one-sentence definition up front (you do have it, but it's buried) is worth repeating at first use in Sec. 3. (b) Eq. (9) normalization is written with a sum over helicities but the $S$-factor in (10) is not shown to satisfy it — a one-line reference closes it. (c) The $\hat k_1^-$ on-shell condition (Eq. 38) is used to argue the z-m "absent" regime; make explicit you're evaluating it in the same rest frame as the $g_V$ analysis so the frame-dependence point doesn't look inconsistent across sections.

## Bottom line

This is a competent, thorough, and honestly-presented piece of internal-consistency work with one genuinely new contribution (the SLF-needs-the-same-fix + the covariance/self-consistency unification). It will read as a solid, useful paper to the CLF/SLF quark-model community and its "use type-II / include $M\to\widetilde M_0$" recommendation will probably get adopted. It falls short of being excellent only because the core mechanism is borrowed from [39], the key cancellation is validated numerically rather than proved structurally, and the spin-1 tension is left implicit. Close gaps (1) and (2) and this moves from a good verification paper to a strong one.

**Suggested next edits, in priority order:**
1. Prove/state the $x\leftrightarrow\bar x$ symmetry reason for $\int\Delta_{\rm full}=0$ and its conditions.
2. Add a paragraph directly reconciling "spin-1 = hard zero-mode regime" with "spin-1 self-consistent here"; state where the valence truncation remains unsafe.
3. Add a short deviations paragraph to the numerics.
4. Tighten the "puzzle resolved" framing to "type-II is the self-consistent, covariant correspondence."
