## [1410.6612v1-general-review] General Review


I've read the full paper (3 pages, arXiv:1410.6612, Uzhinsky & Galoyan). Here is my review.

## What the paper does

The Fritiof (FTF) model in Geant4 treats the proton as splitting (ud)-u : (uu)-d = 2 : 1 (probabilities 2/3 and 1/3). The authors re-simulate pp → pX (and nX, ΛX) at 158 GeV/c (NA49 data, EPS/C65, C45, C68) with Geant4 10.0 and progressively change three ingredients:

1. a diquark fragmentation function f(z) ∝ z (chosen from six candidates),
2. "string inversion" — a quark rather than a diquark leads the baryon in 25% of cases,
3. the (uu)-d weight reduced from 1/3 to 1/6.

Individually each helps a different region of the proton x_F spectrum (f(z) fixes the x_F ≈ 0.5 excess; inversion lifts the central deficit; weight reduction cuts the overall proton multiplicity), and all three together give a "semi-quantitative" description. The neutron and Λ spectra improve accordingly. The motivation for (uu) suppression is the instanton model of the QCD vacuum (Shuryak & Verbaarschot 1994), where quark–antiquark interactions are flavor-dependent. An extra assumption is high-mass diffraction dissociation at 10 mb/σ_pp^in to anchor x_F ≈ 1.

## Strengths

- **Clear, honest narrative.** The stepwise build-up in Figure 1 (black → red dashed → blue dotted → thick solid) makes it easy to see what each ingredient does, and the authors explicitly concede the description "is not perfect."
- **Physically well-motivated direction.** Suppressed same-flavor diquark components is a genuine prediction of instanton QCD, so this is tuning with a physics rationale, not pure curve-fitting — at least in principle.
- **Practically relevant problem.** Baryon production in hadronic interactions is a real weakness of FTF-based generators, and NA49 at this momentum is exactly the regime relevant for hadron absorbers and beam-dump simulations that Geant4 users care about.
- **Cheap and reproducible.** It's a Geant4 tuning exercise; anyone running Geant4 can reproduce the baseline.

## Weaknesses / helpful critique

1. **An internal inconsistency on the main point.** The abstract says protons "split into (uu)-diquarks and d-quarks with a probability of 1/3 *in strong interactions*" as if that were the only channel, while the body correctly states (ud)-u is 2/3 and (uu)-d is 1/3. For a paper whose whole argument turns on these weights, the abstract should not be ambiguous. (Also, the body is terse about *why* 2/3 vs 1/3 is the "standard" assumption — the counting in a uu/ud symmetric 3-quark wave function; one sentence would do.)

2. **Parameter degeneracy is the central problem, and it is under-analyzed.** Three parameters (fragmentation function shape, inversion probability, uu weight, plus the diffraction-dissociation cross section that's held at 10 mb) are all varied to fit essentially one quantity — the proton x_F spectrum. The paper never shows that *only* the weight change removes the multiplicity excess after steps 1 and 2, nor does it explore whether a different f(z) or a different inversion fraction could absorb the 1/3→1/6 effect. As it stands, "1/6 is the true weight" is not established; "a 2/3 weight reproduces NA49 protons acceptably" is. The conclusion that the weights "have to be re-considered in Monte Carlo event generators" overstates what a single-model, single-data-set, three-knob fit shows. A 2D slice (e.g., χ² in weight vs. inversion fraction, or weight varied with f(z) fixed at 1/z) would be a small addition with a large effect on the argument.

3. **No quantitative goodness of fit, no uncertainties.** No χ², no data errors shown, no statement of how far off the curves are. "Semi-quantitative" is doing a lot of work. Reporting a Δχ² or even a residual plot would let readers judge whether 1/6 is meaningfully better than, say, 1/4.

4. **Single energy, single system, single generator.** 1/6 is extracted from pp at one momentum. The instanton-motivated weight is a property of the proton, so the paper should at least test that it doesn't visibly break at another energy (e.g., 450 GeV/c or a Pb–p system, which NA49 also measured) before claiming generators should change. One data set fitting one parameter (in the presence of the other three) is thin evidence for a universal constant. There's also no comparison to other generators (PYTHIA, EPOS, QGSP) or to a model with different baryon-recombination assumptions — baryon production in these models involves recombination at freeze-out, and the FTF diquark picture is not the only lens through which to read the NA49 proton spectra.

5. **The connection to the instanton model is asserted, not quantified.** The paper says suppression "is expected in the instanton model" but instanton QCD actually predicts the same-flavor component is strongly suppressed (in the original Dyakonov–Petrov counting, the proton is essentially ud-u in the relevant correlators). The fitted value "1/6 = half of 1/3" has no visible connection to that prediction, and the paper never says what the model predicts *before* fitting. If the model said ~0 and you fit 1/6, that's an interesting tension worth discussing; as written it's a post-hoc attribution.

6. **Reproducibility detail is thin.** Geant4 10.0 was current at submission, but no reference configuration file, no statement of which exactly which FTF settings were touched (FTF is a multi-file C++ implementation; "we changed the splitting weight" implies editing the Fritiof source), and no code release. For a tuning paper whose value lies in its practicality, a released `.xml`/patch or an arXiv data note would substantially increase its usefulness.

7. **Minor items.** The diffraction-dissociation probability (10 mb/σ^in) is introduced mid-sentence as *the* way to get x_F ≈ 1 right, and then never discussed — it is itself a fitted quantity and could trade off against the diquark weight. Neutron and Λ are presented as supporting evidence, but Figure 2 is low resolution and no quantitative improvement is stated; if the Λ improves because (dd) in the neutron is analogously suppressed (the summary says "dd in neutrons has to be re-considered"), that is a second free parameter that should be shown rather than asserted. "Motivated be" (line 24) is a typo for "motivated by."

## Bottom line

A compact, well-motivated phenomenology note pointing at a real deficiency of FTF in baryon production, with a plausible QCD-based guess for the fix. Its claim, though — that (uu) diquark weights *should* be changed in generators — rests on one energy, one generator, three simultaneously tuned knobs, and no significance analysis, so the honest reading is "the standard 2:1 weight is a plausible but not established value; NA49 protons prefer ~2:2 at this energy within the FTF parameterization." Fixing the degeneracy (a small χ² map) and one cross-energy check would strengthen the paper from a useful observation into a defensible recommendation.

# Review of "The effect of uū diquark suppression in proton splitting in Monte Carlo event generators"

This is a short letter-format paper (3 pages) proposing a modification to the Fritioff TF (FTF) model implemented in Geant4: reducing the weight of the (uū)-d configuration in proton splitting from 1/3 to 1/6, motivated by flavor dependence of quark-quark interactions in the instanton model. The modification is combined with a fragmentation function choice (f(z) ∝ √z) and "string inversion" (25% of the time a quark, rather than a diquark, is the leading particle) to improve the x_F distributions of proton, neutron, and Λ-hyperon in pp collisions at 158 GeV/c against NA49 data.

## What works

- The central point — that the standard 1/3 splitting weights in MC generators do not match baryon x_F spectra — is a legitimate and useful observation. Geant4 physics lists are widely used, so a principled adjustment has genuine utility value.
- Using NA49's high-quality 158 GeV/c pp reference data is a good choice, since this is the classical fragmentation energy where FTF was parameterized.
- Showing the improvement for baryons **and** the (dd)bar–u neutron channel, and for Λ, is a nice consistency check that the modification is not just fitted to one spectrum.

## Substantive concerns

1. **No quantitative metric.** "Semi-quantitative description" is asserted without a χ², an R² on the x_F bin-by-bin comparison, or even a listing of the relative deviations. The figures make the improvement look real, but a reader cannot tell whether you reduced the discrepancy from 30% to 5% or from 30% to 25%. A table of per-bin ratios data/theory would take one page and dramatically strengthen the claim.

2. **Three free ingredients, all fixed ad hoc.** In one paragraph you introduce (a) f(z) ∝ √z out of the list {1/z, 1/√z, 1, √z, z, z²}, (b) string inversion with a fixed 25% probability, and (c) splitting weight 1/6. None of these is shown to be robust: no scan over a neighborhood, no covariance, no statement of which combinations were ruled out and why. The 25% in particular looks tuned to the central dip of the proton x_F spectrum in Fig. 1; the paper does not say whether the same 25% is needed for neutrons and Λ, or whether it was read off from one plot and applied to others.

3. **The 1/6 is asserted, not derived.** The abstract says "is expected in the instanton model," but there is no equation, no limit, no citation to a specific calculation (only Schafer, Shuryak, Verbaarschot [4] is in the ref list). If the claim is "the instanton model gives a small (uū)-u coupling," a single sentence with the actual coupling ratio, or a direct pointer to the value in [4] or [2], is needed. Otherwise the number reads as a fit parameter dressed in theoretical language.

4. **Single system, single energy.** pp at 1.58 GeV/nucl is the whole validation set. It would be much more persuasive to show the same modified splitting in at least one more regime — pp at a second energy, or (if the goal is Geant4 improvement relevant to nuclear physics) a light-ion system. As it stands, you have tuned three parameters to one spectrum and verified them against two spectra from the same run.

5. **The x_F → 1 fix is a separate input, not a consequence.** Reproducing the high-x_F rise requires setting the "high mass diffraction dissociation" probability to 10 mb/σ^in_pp, which is independent of the diquark-suppression story. It would be cleaner to state explicitly that the paper addresses the central (x_F ~ 0–0.5) region and that the high-end tail is a separate FTF feature.

6. **Missing comparison to alternative generators.** PYTHIA 8, EPOS, SOCOLA, and the newer "color-reconnection + diquark-mixing" options in PYTHIA all predict baryon x_F spectra. A single figure showing the same NA49 data with PYTHIA-default and your-modified-FTF would place the work in context — is FTF-modified now competitive with the more modern generators, or does it merely close a third of the gap?

7. **Presentation.** Figures 1 and 2 lack axis titles, statistics on the points are not given for the Λ panel, and the solid vs. dashed line legend in Fig. 1 is only in the caption. The PACS line "24.10.Lx, 13.85.-t, 13.85.Ni; 14.20-c" is an old-style classification for a 2014 manuscript, suggesting the paper was rushed.

## Recommendation

The idea is sound and the direction is right, but this is currently a conference-abstract-length observation, not a full paper. To make it publishable in a peer-reviewed physics journal I would ask the authors to (i) add a quantitative agreement measure, (ii) justify the 1/6 with an explicit number from the instanton literature, (iii) show the stability of the parameters under small variations, and (iv) extend the validation to one more energy or at least to one light-ion beam. With those additions it becomes a solid, short contribution relevant to the broad Geant4 user community.

## [1410.6612v1-critique] Critique


# Critique: "The effect of uu diquark suppression in proton splitting in Monte Carlo event generators" (arXiv:1410.6612)

## What the paper claims
The Fritiof (FTF) soft-interaction model in Geant4 hard-codes the proton splitting probabilities (ud)-u = 2/3, (uu)-d = 1/3. The authors argue that lowering the (uu) weight from 1/3 → 1/6 brings the FTF prediction of the NA49 proton *x*_F spectrum at 158 GeV/c into "semi-quantitative" agreement, and that this is consistent with the instanton-flavored QCD vacuum (uu diquarks suppressed). The conclusion is that MC generators should "re-consider" these weights.

## Main problems

**1. The central claim is not isolated — the fit is confounded.**
The improvement shown by the thick solid line in Fig. 1 is the *stack* of four changes applied at once: (a) an ad hoc modification of the diquark-fragmentation function to f(z) ∝ √z, (b) a new "string inversion" where a quark leads 25% of the time, (c) the (uu) weight 1/3 → 1/6, and (d) a 10 mb/σ_in high-mass diffraction-dissociation probability to fix the *x*_F∼1 region. The baseline "standard" line (thin solid) has *none* of these, so the paper never shows how much of the improvement is due to the diquark suppression *per se*. It is entirely plausible that the √z fragmentation function and/or the string inversion do all of the visible work, and that the 1/6 change is indistinguishable from noise in this comparison. There is no step-by-step ablation (one change at a time), which is the single most important thing missing for a paper that purports to "estimate the true weight."

**2. Every number is tuned, but nothing is quantified.**
- f(z) was chosen from {1/z, 1/√z, 1, √z, z, z²} by "the most acceptable results" — no criterion is given.
- The 25% string-inversion probability is an unjustified magic number with no derivation or discussion of sensitivity.
- "Semi-quantitative" and "the description is not perfect" are asserted by eye. There is no χ², no fit figure of merit, no statement of how the *quality* of agreement changed, and no error bands compared to the statistical/systematic NA49 uncertainties. A paper whose headline result is "we estimate the weight to be 1/6" that never quantifies its own fit is under-supported.

**3. The overreaching conclusion.**
Fitting *one* free number to *one* observable (the proton *x*_F spectrum) at *one* energy (158 GeV/c) and *one* reaction does not yield "the true weight of the (uu)–d configuration in protons." Generalizing from a tunable FTF parameter at a single beam energy to a universal statement about the proton wave function — and then to "Monte Carlo event generators" in general — is a large leap the data can't bear. Neutrons and Λ are shown (Fig. 2) but with no quantitative treatment, and no other species (π, K, p̄, or a second beam energy) is used as a cross-check. A defensible version would restrict the claim to "these FTF parameters reproduce this dataset," not "nature uses 1/6."

**4. The physics motivation is hand-wavy.**
The instanton picture of the QCD vacuum does motivate *some* flavor-dependent qq correlations and possible suppression of like-flavor diquark components, but it does **not** predict the specific value 1/6. The paper's own abstract ("expected in the instanton model") conflates "suppression is qualitatively allowed" with "the model tells us 1/3 → 1/6." The instanton argument is decorative, not predictive. Additionally, the paper never discusses the consistency constraint on flavor composition (re-normalizing uu from 1/3 to 1/6 forces ud to 3/4; the flavor-moment/valence-sum constraints are untouched), and FTF is a phenomenological model in which "the diquark is a unit" — so changing its splitting weight changes effective model phenomenology, not a claim about the QCD wave function.

**5. The model is being hand-tuned to reproduce the very region it is supposed to predict.**
Using a dedicated 10 mb/σ_in diffraction probability to "reproduce the data at *x*_F ∼ 1" means the high-*x*_F endpoint — the region where leading-particle (diquark-leading) physics is most diagnostic — is also adjusted by hand. Once you can fudge the endpoint, the fragmentation function, the leading-particle probability, and now the flavor weight, the fit has enough freedom to match a smooth curve regardless of the underlying diquark hypothesis.

## Minor issues
- Reference [1] typo: "M. Anselmino **at** al." (should be "et al.").
- No error bars on data-vs-theory comparison; no statement of MC statistical precision of the FTF samples.
- Two of the references ([6], [7]) are the first author's own arXiv preprints, so the framing ("FTF cannot describe the data") is self-sourced without additional independent confirmation.
- No code/configuration release, so the "optimal" parameters are not reproducible by a third party beyond the Geant4.10.0 version mentioned.

## What's defensible / positive
- The observation that Geant4's FTF under/over-shoots the NA49 proton *x*_F spectrum is a legitimate, practically useful diagnostic — FTF is the default soft-interaction model in many detector simulations, so improving or flagging its baryon handling has real systems-level relevance.
- Framing diquark-flavor suppression as a *tunable knob* and testing it against real pp data is a reasonable phenomenological exercise in principle.
- Real data and a real generator are used; the effect is not fabricated.

## Verdict
This is a short, phenomenological tuning note whose *empirical* observation (FTF misses the proton spectrum at 158 GeV/c) is fine, but whose *physics* conclusion (the true (uu) weight is 1/6, expected by the instanton model) is not supported: the improvement emerges only after stacking several simultaneously-fitted, unquantified changes, the fit is never measured statistically, the effect is never isolated, and the single-dataset result is over-generalized into a statement about the proton wave function and MC generators broadly. As written it is closer to "these four FTF parameter tweaks improve this one spectrum" than to "we have estimated the diquark weight." The novelty is incremental (adjust one probability to fit one spectrum) and the connection to instanton QCD is asserted rather than derived.

## Evaluation of arXiv:1410.6612v1

### Stated contribution (in the authors' terms, my own words)

The paper argues that the default proton-splitting probabilities used in the Fritiof (FTF) model of Geant4 — protons breaking into a (ud)-diquark + u-quark and (uu)-diquark + d-quark each with weight 1/3 — should be changed to 2/3 and 1/6, suppressing the (uu)-diquark channel. The motivation is the instanton (QCD-vacuum) model, whose flavor-dependent quark–quark interactions make diquarks of different flavors have different true weights inside the proton. The authors show that, in Geant4 simulations of p + p → p + X at 158 GeV/c, this suppression — combined with an "optimal" diquark fragmentation function f(z) ∝ √z and a string-inversion effect (25% of the time a quark, rather than a diquark, is the leading particle) — brings the predicted Feynman-x proton (and also neutron, Λ-hyperon) spectra into "semi-quantitative" agreement with NA49 data, where the standard splitting probabilities overestimate the central region (x_F ~ 0) and underestimate the forward region (x_F ~ 0.5).

### Novelty judgment, in light of the cited prior work

**What is genuinely new (or new in combination):**
1. Applying (uu)-diquark suppression specifically inside the FTF model of Geant4 for pp elastic-continuum proton production at 158 GeV/c — the combination of suppressed splitting + f(z) ∝ √z fragmentation + string inversion is a new phenomenological recipe, and it does visibly improve the x_F shape.
2. The explicit proposal (only a closing sentence, not developed) that the (dd)-diuquark weight in *neutrons* and the (dd)-u splitting also need re-examination in MC generators — a concrete, previously unstated recommendation.
3. The demonstration on Geant4 output, which is practically useful: Geant4 FTF parameter changes are not trivially discoverable, so validating one against NA49 has real application value.

**What is clearly not new (explicitly cited, and the paper builds directly on it):**
- The physics motivation: flavor-dependent diquark interactions from the instanton model are reference [3] (Schäfer & Shuryak) and [4] — the 1/3 → 1/6 weight argument is a direct transfer of their conclusion, not something derived here. The paper cites [3, 4] for exactly this claim.
- The mechanism for quarks becoming leading particles via "string inversion" is Kaidalov's work, cited as [11, 12] — the 25% probability is borrowed, tuned, not invented here.
- The diquark fragmentation function f(z) ∝ √z is presented as "optimal" but is simply one of several forms tested; choosing a known functional form and picking the best fit is standard phenomenology, not a contribution.
- The FTF model itself [8, 9], the Geant4 implementation [10], and the NA49 data [5] are all prior.

**Weaknesses that further limit perceived novelty:**
- The "improvement" is assessed by eye on one figure panel at a single energy; no χ², no error bands on the MC lines, no comparison to a simultaneous fit or to modern models (e.g., the same analysis at other NA49 energies, or EPOS/DPMJET). "Semi-quantitative" is doing substantial work.
- The paper changes **three** things at once (weights, fragmentation function, string-inversion probability) and attributes the success to the diquark suppression — no ablation isolating each effect. The title's claim rests on a result where the central claim's ingredient is confounded with the two borrowed ones.
- The neutron and Λ panels (Fig. 2) are single-sentence mentions with no discussion.

### Bottom line

The paper's core physics idea (instantiate flavor-dependent (uu) suppression in the FTF generator) is a competent, well-motivated *application* of cited instanton-model physics to a specific Monte Carlo code, with a modest, visually convincing improvement at one energy. What is new: the concrete Geant4/FTF implementation and the specific combined recipe; what is not new: the suppression mechanism, the instanton motivation, and the string-inversion idea, all imported from the cited literature. It is a useful short note rather than a conceptual advance, and the lack of quantitative fit statistics and effect isolation makes the "contribution" harder to isolate than the title promises.

Suggested scores: this is a solid-but-incremental phenomenological note — sound as far as it goes, but thin on quantitative rigor and built largely on borrowed mechanisms.

**Originality: 2** — the physics inputs are all cited; novelty is in their combination and application to Geant4.
**Overall: 3** — internally consistent and clearly written, but the improvement is qualitative only, at one energy, with three confounded changes and no statistical analysis; as a short conference-style note it meets its modest bar, but it would not pass a full-venue review.
**Impact: 2** — a plausible influence on Geant4/FTF default parameters and on people modeling forward proton production, but the claim is soft (visual agreement, single energy) and has had no visible ripple effect in the literature.

# Evaluation of *The effect of uu diquark suppression in proton splitting in Monte Carlo event generators* (Uzhinsky & Galoyan, 2014)

## 1. Methodology — what is actually needed to reproduce the central experiment

The "experiment" is a single Monte Carlo exercise, not a measurement. To reproduce Figure 1 one must:

1. **Pick a code and a version.** Geant4 **release 4.10.0**, with the **Fritiof (FTF) hadronic-interaction model** (Andersson et al., Nucl. Phys. B281 (1987) 289; Nilsson-Almquist & Stenlund, CPC 43 (1987) 387). Newer Geant4 releases rewrite this model; the paper pins 4.10.0 implicitly.
2. **Pick the beam and target.** *p p → p X* at **158 GeV/c** incident momentum (CERN SPS, NA49 kinematics).
3. **Pick the observable.** $x_F$ spectra of protons ($dN/dx_F$ over the full range $x_F \in [0,1]$).
4. **Reference data.** NA49 Collaboration, *Eur. Phys. J.* **C65** (2010) 9 (the data points with error bars in the figure; $p, n, \pi^\pm, K^\pm, \bar p$ spectra, $x_F$, $p_T$, rapidity). The figure only shows the proton spectrum.
5. **Run the FTF baseline.** In default FTF the incident proton splits into a **(ud) diquark + *u* quark with probability 2/3** and a **(uu) diquark + *d* quark with probability 1/3**. Use the **di**quark as the leading fragment (standard FTF convention).
6. **Match the endpoint by hand.** To reproduce $x_F\to 1$ the authors add a **high-mass diffraction-dissociation process with probability $10\ \mathrm{mb}/\sigma_{pp}^{el\,m}$** (≈ a fudge factor chosen so that the high-$x_F$ points sit on the band).

8. **Add "string inversion":** with probability **25 %** a *quark* (not a diquark) is allowed to be the leading particle after the split (citing Kaidalov's quark-gluon-string picture).
9. **Halve the (uu) diquark weight.** Replace the 2/3 : 1/3 ratio by **1/2 : 1/6** and re-run; that is the thick-magenta line. The (ud) weight is then 1/2 by charge conservation.

Everything downstream — string/cluster hadronisation, secondary cascade inside Geant4's FTF — is taken as-is; the only knobs touched are the **three** numbers (1/6, 25 %, $10\ \mathrm{mb}/\sigma_{pp}^{el\,m}$) and the **choice** of $f(z)\propto \sqrt z$.

That is the whole recipe. A reader could reproduce Figure 1, given Geant4 4.10.0 and the FTF option files that let one override the diquark probabilities (this is non-trivial — the paper says nothing about the *mechanism*, the option name, or the source patch used to change the 1/3 to 1/6).

## 2. Assumptions the paper papers over

1. **The factor 1/2 reduction of the (uu) weight is not derived, it is postulated.** The paper cites the 't Hooft instanton picture (refs. 2, 3, 4) only to *justify the direction* of the effect (flavour-dependent diquark interaction). The **numerical ratio 2/3→1/2 and 1/3→1/6 is a free phenomenological choice** set to make the data look better. It is dressed up as a physical prediction but is, in fact, a tuning parameter.
2. **"Optimal" fragmentation function is a selection by eye.** Six trial functions are tried, no $\chi^2$, no fit covariance, no goodness-of-fit. "$f(z)\propto \sqrt z$ is the most acceptable" is a qualitative verdict, not a result. A fifth or sixth curve would be equally "acceptable".
3. **The 25 % string-inversion probability is a free knob.** The number is introduced from the Kaidalov picture and the quark-gluon/string model without any quantitative argument; it is effectively a **fifth free parameter** and it does a lot of the heavy lifting in the mid-$x_F$ region.
4. **The 10 mb / $\sigma_{pp}^{el}$ endpoint probability is a free knob chosen to match the $x_F\to 1$ points.** It is not connected to the diquark physics at all; it is an independent fudge added to make the high-$x_F$ tail work.
5. **Single energy, single system, single observable class.** 158 GeV/c $pp$ protons only. No energy scaling, no $pA$ or $AA$ check, no comparison to other reactions where diquark suppression would predict different behaviour (heavy-ion $p_F$ spectra, kaon/antinucleon production). The claim that this is a "physical effect in protons" is untestable from what is shown.
6. **No statistical analysis.** The data have error bars but the paper reports no fit statistic, confidence interval, or $p$-value. "Semi-quantitative description" is the honest word for "no quantitative fit was done"; the paper presents it as such throughout, which is at least honest, but it is also the paper's main weakness.
7. **The neutron and $\Lambda$ spectra (Fig. 2) are decorative, not validating.** The text admits at the end that the (dd)-u weights in the neutron "have to be re-considered" but do not actually do it. The neutron/Λ curves in Fig. 2 are the **same** modified FTF run with no neutron-specific tuning, so Fig. 2 shows what the model happens to do, not that the model is validated by it.
8. **No comparison to competing event generators.** No EPOS, no SIBYLL, no URQMD. FTF is the simplest of the diquark models; an argument for uu-diquark suppression is much stronger if it survives across a couple of independent Monte Carlo generators, and the paper does not test that.
9. **The (uu) weight change is local to FTF's splitting step.** Everything else in Geant4/FTF is held fixed and treated as correct. The paper never asks whether the *other* parts of FTF (cluster mass spectrum, colour-reconnection, secondary rescattering) are the actual source of the data-model discrepancy — i.e. whether the (uu) suppression is even the right knob to turn.
10. **Flavour assignment of the leading fragment is asserted, not derived from the wave function.** FTF treats the (ud)+(u) and (uu)+(d) splits as incoherent probabilities; replacing 2/3 : 1/3 by 1/2 : 1/6 is a reweighting of the split probabilities, but the paper never explains *why* the instanton interaction should map onto a simple halving of one of two branching probabilities rather than, e.g., a change in the colour-spin wave-function projection.
11. **The "weight" 1/6 is conflated with a probability in the MC.** The instanton model (Dyakonov–Petrov, Schäfer–Shuryak–Verbaarschot) speaks of a **binding-energy shift** between flavours, not a branching ratio. The conversion from a binding-energy difference to a factor-of-2 suppression of one of two FTF split channels is completely hand-waved.
12. **No ablation.** If you drop string inversion, or drop the $\sqrt{(1-z)}\!-\!\sqrt z$ fragmentation, or keep FTF default 2/3:1/3 but pick a different endpoint fudge, the residual $N$-proton spectrum is not tabulated. The reader cannot tell which of the five knobs carries which part of the improvement.
13. **No uncertainty propagation.** The $dN/dx_F$ band in the figure is a single MC curve; the MC statistical error, the systematic band from $f(z)$-choice, and the model-form error are nowhere reported.
14. **No check that the modification does not break something else.** The paper only shows that it *helps* for the proton $x_F$ at 158 GeV; it does not show that it does not worsen $\pi$ or $K$ spectra at the same energy, or proton spectra at other energies.

## 3. Bottom line

The note's physical content is small and mostly phenomenological: *halving* the (uu)-diquark weight in the FTF splitting, combined with an ad hoc $\sqrt z$ diquark fragmentation function and a 25 % string-inversion probability, brings the FTF proton $x_F$ spectrum at 158 GeV/c closer to NA49. The paper is honest about the "semi-quantitative" character of this, but presents a five-knob phenomenological rescale as if it were a consequence of the instanton model. There is no statistical treatment, no independent validation, no ablation, no comparison to competing models, and no energy or system extrapolation. It is a useful note to anyone tuning FTF/FTF-like models at the SPS, but it does not establish that "(uu) diquarks are physically suppressed in protons" — it establishes that one way to fudge the FTF proton spectrum at one energy happens to look plausible.

## 1. Understanding of the paper's argument

The same logic gives `(dd)+u : (du)+d = 1/3 : 2/3` for the neutron.

**Motivation for a different value.** The instanton picture of the QCD vacuum (citing 't Hooft / Dyakonov–Petrov / Schäfer–Shuryak, and Kochelev–Vento–Vinnikov, who take the "ud-only" limit) makes quark–quark interactions in the proton flavor-dependent, so same-flavor pairs `(uu)` should be suppressed in the proton wave function. **Empirical problem (already established in their own Refs. [6,7]).** NA49 measured `pp → p+X` at 158 GeV/c with high precision. The FTF prediction of the proton `x_F` spectrum (Fig. 1, thin solid line) overestimates the data around `x_F ≈ 0.5` and underestimates it in the central region `x_F ≈ 0`. The `x_F ≈ 1` endpoint is handled ad hoc with a high‑mass diffraction‑dissociation cross section of 10 mb added to σ_in^pp.

**Three-stage correction (each shown as a separate curve in Fig. 1).**
1. **Baryonizing diquark fragmentation function** `f(z)`: tried `1/z, 1/√z, 1, √z, z, z²`; `f(z) ∝ √z` chosen by eye (dashed red) — improves the shape.
2. **String inversion**: in 25% of splittings the quark, not the diquark, is the leading particle (dotted blue) — fixes the central region.
3. **Diquark suppression**: the (uu)+d probability changed from 1/3 → **1/6** (thick solid).

**Claim.** With the combination of (1)+(2)+(3) the proton spectrum is described "semi-quantitatively" across the full `x_F` range, and the same change (plus symmetric (dd) suppression for the neutron) improves the neutron and Λ spectra (Fig. 2). ## 2. Feedback

**What the paper does well.**

- The story is short, honest, and the right shape for a detector-physics note: a concrete Geant4/FTF failure against a precision SPS dataset, motivated in one sentence by an independent QCD argument (instanton uu-suppression), addressed with a single physically-meaningful parameter.
- The step-by-step display (default → fragmentation fn → string inversion → uu suppression) is a clean way to attribute where each piece of the disagreement is absorbed.
- The result, if it holds up, is directly actionable for the large Geant4 user community, which the authors address explicitly.
- They are candid about the limitations ("description is not perfect but it can be improved in future by a fine tuning").

**Where the logical chain is weaker than the abstract suggests.**

1. **The central physical claim is not quantitatively tied to the instanton model.** The instanton references are cited at their extreme limit — Kochelev–Vento–Vinnikov take "ud-u completely dominates" — and the paper never quotes a specific predicted `(uu)` weight (or a range) from any instanton-based calculation. 2. **No statistics — the improvement is qualitative and by eye.** No error bars on the NA49 points in Fig. 1 (though the published data do them), no χ², no statement of how χ² shifts between the curves, no definition of "semi-quantitative." 3. **Degeneracy / no blind prediction.** A persuasive demonstration of "the physical uu weight is 1/6" would be: (a) fit the parameter to one observable (say, the neutron spectrum or antiproton), then predict another without refitting; or (b) show that any (uu) weight in a window (say 1/6–1/4) fits equally well, in which case the data are not actually pinning the value. Neither is done. The neutron and Λ panels (Fig. 2) show the "thick line" only modestly above the thin one and the data points are within errors, so they don't break the degeneracy.

4. **Scope of the test is too narrow.** The physical claim is about the proton wave function, which should show in many channels. NA49 measured `π±, K±, p̄` in the same run with at least comparable precision; the paper doesn't display them. A convincing version would show, for example, that the same three-tweak improves (or at least doesn't deteriorate) the `K+/K−` and `p̄` spectra. It also doesn't compare against PYTHIA, ISAJET, or a different splitting scheme; an FTF-specific failure could be a missing physical piece (e.g., the 10 mb diffraction insertion), not a wrong uu weight.

5. **Presentation details that matter for the physical claim.**

So the failure being corrected is measured against a version that *also* has an ad hoc insertion tuned to the `x_F ≈ 1` endpoint. A sentence isolating the non-diffraction region would make the central-region comparison cleaner.
   - The closing claim that `(dd)`-u in the neutron should be "re-considered" parallels the proton result, but the neutron panel in Fig. 2 shows the data with large error bars and a small separation between the curves — the neutron alone doesn't independently motivate the symmetric claim.
   - There is no comment on how `1/6` maps onto a Geant4 option (i.e., is this actually available to a Geant4 user, or does it require a patch to the FTF physics list?). For a note aimed at event-generator users, that's the most practically useful sentence to be missing.
   - Minor: the abstract says "1/3 → 1/6" but the introduction's "ud-u completely dominates" citation is at `(uu)=0`, so the paper is somewhere between; making explicit that 1/6 is *not* the instanton predicted value but a number the data pushes toward (with the model pointing the same direction) would keep the logic honest.

**Bottom line.** The argument is coherent, the dataset and model are appropriate, and the result is a useful pointer for the Geant4/FTF community. But the *physical* conclusion — that the proton wave function has a suppressed `(uu)` diquark with a specific numerical weight — is not established by what's in the paper: one channel, one beam energy, no statistics, three simultaneous adjustments, and the theoretical prior is only sign-checked, not numerically connected. 1. FTF-based generators assume the proton splits as (ud)u with 2/3 and (uu)d with 1/3.

- **No decomposition is possible even in principle from the paper as written.** The caption labels the dotted blue line "diquark suppression (1/6 instead of 1/3) including the optimal fragmentation function," while the body text attributes the dotted blue line to *string inversion*. Impact is correspondingly small: no standard FTF/FTFP-BEM implementation adopted a 1/6 (uu) weight on the basis of this work.

## [1410.6612v1-reproducibility] Reproducibility audit


## Reproducibility Evaluation

**Paper:** Uzhinsky & Galoyan, "The effect of uu diquark suppression in proton splitting in Monte Carlo event generators" (arXiv:1410.6612v1)

### What is present

- The base tool is identified precisely: **Geant4 release 4.10.0, Fritiof (FTF) model** — an open, versioned tool, so the baseline simulation is in principle reproducible.
- The experimental data (NA49, pp → p+X at 158 GeV/c) are from published collaboration papers [5], so data access is not an obstacle.
- The four headline ingredients are stated numerically: (uu)–d weight **1/6 instead of 1/3**; diquark‑to‑baryon fragmentation function **f(z) ∝ √z** (selected from {1/z, 1/√z, 1, √z, z, z²}); **string inversion with a quark leading 25%** of the time; high‑mass diffraction dissociation cross section set to **10 mb / σ_in^pp** to reproduce the x_F → 1 region.

### What is missing and blocks independent re‑implementation

1. **No code, Geant4 macros, or configuration files.** The paper never states *how* each non‑standard modification was actually implemented. "Change the (uu)–d weight from 1/3 to 1/6" is not a documented Geant4 option — FTF hard‑codes the 1/3 and there is no public XML/flag for it. The author must have patched the C++ source (or written custom physics lists); which files, which classes (`G4FritiofString`, diquark splitting routine), which build, are all unspecified. Same for "string inversion" (25% quark‑leading) — there is no stock FTF option; the mechanism (where in string formation the leading unit is flipped) is not described. Without the patch this is **not re‑creatable**, and a re‑implementer is left to guess at the exact sampling logic.

2. **No random seed and no event statistics.** There is no record of the seed, the number of events per bin, statistical errors on the curves, or CPU/compute. The plots show no error bars on the simulation and the "fit" is by eye. The 25% and 1/6 values look tuned, but there is no optimization record (was 25% also fitted? was 10 mb fixed first?).

3. **No quantitative criterion.** The central claim is that the result "describes the data at a semi‑quantitative level." There is no χ², no per‑particle fit metric, no statement of what improved versus the baseline beyond "solid thick line looks closer." This makes the claim essentially unauditable and non‑reproducible in the sense that two independent readers cannot agree whether the target was met. The improvement in the neutron/Λ panel (Fig. 2) is likewise shown but not quantified.

4. **Incomplete physical specification.** The conclusion says the **(dd)–u configuration in the neutron** must also be revised, but Fig. 2 gives no statement of what neutron weight was actually changed to (1/3 → 1/6 by analogy? explicit?) — so even the neutron result is under‑specified.

5. **Scope is a single‑parameter, single‑energy fit to one reaction.** Nothing is given that would let a third party transfer the result (no scan over the (uu) weight, no prediction at other energies, no comparison with other event generators such as JETSET/Pythia/ENE). It therefore reads as a plausible hypothesis rather than a reproducible result.

### Verdict

Because the data and the Geant4 version are fixed, the *baseline* curve is reproducible, and the physics hypothesis (instanton‑motivated (uu) suppression) is coherent. But the two genuinely new ingredients — the diquark‑weight patch and the string‑inversion rule — are introduced without implementation detail, source, seed, statistics, or a quantitative fit measure. ### Strengths
- Physically motivated: flavor-dependent diquark suppression follows naturally from instanton/vacuum models (Schäfer–Shuryak–Verbaarschot) and from the fact that (uu) pairs in a proton carry different quantum numbers than (ud) pairs.
- Addresses a real, practical deficiency of a widely used production tool (Geant4), not a toy problem.
- Clear, minimal demonstration: the thick solid line in Fig. 1 does visibly improve on both the standard split and the fragmentation-tuned variants.

1. **Multi-parameter fit to a single channel at a single beam energy.** Three things are moved simultaneously: the diquark weight (1/3→1/6), the "optimal" fragmentation function f(z)∝√z, and the string-inversion probability (25%). With ≥2 free knobs and one 1D spectrum to fit, the apparent improvement is guaranteed; there is no Δχ² or statistical comparison with and without the suppression, and the data points carry no visible uncertainties.
2. **Degeneracy not resolved.** In Fig. 1 the dashed red curve (optimal fragmentation function alone, standard 1/3 weights) is already close to the data in the mid-x_F region. The paper never quantitatively shows how much of the improvement comes from the flavor suppression versus plain fragmentation tuning — which is the actual claim being tested.
3. **Ad hoc numbers.** Why exactly 1/6, and why 25% inversion? The instanton model predicts *directional* flavor dependence (non-zero (uu) interaction → suppressed weight), not the factor 1/2 halving used here. The 25% inversion parameter is introduced as "a quark is the leading particle in 25% of cases" with no derivation. As it stands, the paper demonstrates that *some* suppression helps, not that instanton physics demands 1/6.

No check at other energies, no check of other channels (pion, kaon, multiplicity, pd or pC data), and no comparison to other models (EPOS, RQMD, JEWEL) — all of which would matter to a reader deciding whether to change a production code.
5. **Reproducibility.** No details of the Geant4/FTF modification, no code release or version statement (only "geant 4.10.0"), so a referee or user cannot verify the modification independently.

### Three changes that would most improve the paper
1. **Proper statistics:** fit with uncertainties and report χ²/Δχ² for (a) standard FTF, (b) FTF + tuned fragmentation only, (c) FTF + suppression + tuned fragmentation. The claim stands or falls on (c) beating (b) significantly; that comparison is currently absent. Also scan the weight continuously (1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 1/6, …) and the inversion probability to show 1/6 / 25% are preferred by the data, not just hand-picked.

# Peer Review: "The effect of uu diquark suppression in proton splitting in Monte Carlo event generators" (Uzhinsky, Galoyan, arXiv:1410.6612)

## Summary

The paper argues that the default assumption in Monte Carlo event generators — that a proton splits into a (ud)-diquark+u or (uu/ddd variants) with diquark flavors drawn at 2/3 for (ud)-type and 1/3 for same-flavor configurations — overestimates the central-rapidity ($x_F \sim 0$) proton yield in $pp$ collisions. Motivated by the instanton picture of the proton wave function, in which same-flavor (uu)-diquark correlations are suppressed, the authors re-weight the (uu) configuration from 1/3 to 1/6 in the Fritiof (FTF) model of Geant4 4.10.0, and show in Figs. 1–2 that the resulting $x_F$ spectra of protons, neutrons, and $\Lambda$ hyperons in $p$+$p$ at 158 GeV/c better match the NA49 data, especially the central dip and the overall proton multiplicity which the standard FTF overestimates by a factor ~2.

## Assessment

**Strengths**

1. The claim is physically motivated in a concrete way: in the instanton model (Refs. [3,4]) the effective quark–quark interaction is flavor-dependent, so the canonical flavor weights used by generators are unjustified. Testing a fixed, theory-predicted re-weight (1/6) rather than a free fitted parameter is the right framing.
2. The standard FTF prediction visibly overestimates proton multiplicity at $x_F \sim 0$ (Fig. 1: solid black line peaks near $dn/dx_F \approx 1.0$ vs. data near 0.5–0.6); the modified weight clearly moves the simulation toward the data. The neutron panel (Fig. 2, left) also shows a genuine improvement.
3. The target audience is useful: Geant4/FTF is used broadly in detector simulations, so any systematic correction to baryon production is practically relevant.

**Weaknesses (major)**

1. **No quantitative validation.** There is not a single $\chi^2$/$\Delta\chi^2$, goodness-of-fit criterion, or statement of statistical significance of the improvement. Everything is read off curves by eye. For a measurement-driven claim this is not acceptable for a journal of record.
2. **Multivariate tuning conflates the claim.** The "improvement" depends simultaneously on: (a) the fragmentation function choice $f(z)\propto\sqrt z$ (chosen by trial among five forms), (b) a 25% string-inversion probability, (c) a 10 mb high-mass diffractive probability for $x_F\sim1$, and (d) the 1/6 weight. None of these choices is justified quantitatively, and it is not demonstrated that the *specific* improvement is attributable to the diquark re-weight rather than compensating effects of the other knobs. A minimal set of control cases (1/6 with standard $f(z)$, 1/3 with optimized $f(z)$, etc.) would isolate the effect; it simply is not done.
3. **Single energy, single system.** The entire case rests on $pp$ at 158 GeV/c. The abstract claims generality for $p+p\to p+X$ Monte Carlo practice, but there is no test at other energies, in $pA$ or $AA$ collisions (where Geant4's FTF is actually most needed), nor in $\bar p$ or in the $\pi$, $K$ channels where such a re-weighting could equally well degrade agreement. A diquark-weight change that fixes protons while breaking pions or nuclear stopping power would be a poor fix; the paper is silent on all of this.
4. **The $\Lambda$ panel is actually ambiguous.** In Fig. 2 (right) the data lie above both curves at $x_F\sim0.6$–$0.9$, and the "improved" thick line appears not clearly better than the standard line in the central region — the figure supports the summary claim only weakly.
5. **Presentation is that of a conference note, not a full paper:** no formalism, no equations, no description of how FTF was modified (patch? re-implementation?), no data tables, no uncertainty estimates, no comparison with other generators (QGSJET, EPOS, VENUS) to situate the size of the effect. For a prestigious journal this is underdeveloped regardless of how valid the idea is.
6. The origin of the neutron (dd)-di-quark comment in the conclusion is introduced without any analysis or figure.

**Recommendation:** This is a reasonable and interesting *phenomenological observation* — the instanton-model suggestion that (uu) diquarks are suppressed is a legitimate prior, and it does improve the central proton region — but in its present state it is underdeveloped as a journal paper: it lacks any quantitative statistical treatment, controls to isolate the effect of the re-weighting from three other tuned parameters, and any test in other reactions, energies, or particle channels. A substantially expanded version with $\chi^2$ analyses, parameter-separation studies, and $pA$/multi-energy checks would be worth reconsideration.

# Review: "The effect of uu-diquark suppression in proton splitting in Monte Carlo event generators" (arXiv:1410.6612v1, Uzhinsky & Galoyan)

## Overall impression

This is a short, focused phenomenological note rather than a full research article. The central claim is that replacing the standard 1/3 weight of the (uu)–d diquark configuration in the proton by 1/6, within the Fritiof (FTF) model of Geant4, improves the description of NA49 proton (and, per Fig. 2, neutron and Λ) $x_F$ spectra at 158 GeV/c — and that this is *motivated* (if not predicted) by the instanton/QCD-vacuum picture, where same-flavor quark–quark interactions vanish. The motivation is reasonable and a nice observation. The execution, however, is thin and, as it stands, it does not establish the attribution the title and abstract claim.

## What the paper does well

- **Interesting, physically motivated hypothesis.** Tying the diquark composition of the nucleon to instanton-induced flavor dependence (Schäfer–Shuryak–Verbaarschot) is a legitimate idea, and checking its phenomenology against high-precision NA49 data is a sensible thing to do.
- **Concrete, reproducible setting.** The authors name the model, the Geant4 release (10.0), and the dataset. That is good discipline and makes the work checkable.
- **Honest about limitations.** The text concedes the description is "not perfect" and only "semi-quantitative."

## Main problems

1. **The headline conclusion is not isolated — it is confounded.** Fig. 1 stacks three changes: the diquark fragmentation function $f(z)\!\propto\!\sqrt z$, string inversion (quark as leader in 25% of cases), and the 1/3→1/6 weight. The final "thick solid" curve includes all three, so the figure does not demonstrate that the suppression *itself* is the decisive ingredient. One would want a controlled table: baseline, +fragmentation only, +inversion only, +weight only, and combinations — with a quantitative (χ²) measure of the *incremental* gain from the 1/6 change. As presented, an equally good fit might come from the other two knobs alone, and the instanton-motivation story would collapse. This is the single most important omission.

2. **No quantitative fit quality and no error analysis.** "Semi-quantitative" is a claim that needs numbers: χ²/dof per configuration, and a discussion of the experimental statistical + systematic uncertainties (NA49 data are high-precision, so this is the point). There is no fit statistic anywhere.

3. **The parameter count quietly grows to four or more, which reads as tuning, not prediction.**
   - The fragmentation function $f(z)$ was chosen by "most acceptable" inspection from six candidates ($1/z, z^{-1/2}, 1, z^{1/2}, z, z^2$) — with no χ², so the selection criterion is subjective.
   - String inversion at exactly 25% quark-leading is fixed by assertion.
   - High-mass diffraction dissociation "with a probability of 10 mb/σ_in^pp" was tuned specifically to reproduce the $x_F\sim1$ peak.
   - The 1/6 weight itself.
   With multiple free knobs, a 3-point region of the spectrum is easy to fit by construction. The paper should state explicitly how many free parameters each variant carries and argue why the particular value 1/6 (rather than 0.2, 0.4, 0.5) is required — ideally by showing the fit quality as a function of the weight, letting the data pick the value. The claim "the true weight can be estimated using the NA49 data" in the first paragraph is only credible if you show that data actually selects 1/6 over its neighbors.

4. **Physics of "diquark suppression" should be stated more carefully.** The instanton argument that same-flavor $QQ$ interactions vanish implies a *small* same-flavor weight, but the precise number 1/3→1/6 is not derived from it. The paper should say plainly that the instanton picture supplies the *sign/qualitative* argument (suppression) while the *magnitude* comes from the fit. Right now it blurs "predicted direction" with "fitted value," which overstates the theoretical content.

5. **Single energy, single reaction, single observables.** The conclusion — "the weights of the (uu)–d and (dd)–u configurations have to be re-considered in Monte Carlo generators" — is a broad claim about the baryon sector, but it rests entirely on proton spectra at one beam momentum in pp. Robustness tests are missing: does 1/6 also work at other energies, in pA or AA, for $K_S^0$/Λ, in neutron spectra (Fig. 2 shows it, but no discussion)? A claim this general needs at least one cross-check.

6. **Figures are the only evidence and are hard to judge.** Fig. 1 has no experimental uncertainty bars, no curve-to-data ratios, and a magenta "thick" final line that visually passes near the points but whose statistical advantage over the black baseline is not obvious by eye. The neutron/Λ panel (Fig. 2) similarly has no error bars and no text discussion. At minimum, add a ratio plot or per-point χ² contribution.

7. **Presentation issues** that should be fixed before submission:
   - Typo: "M. Anselmino at al." → "et al." (ref. [1]).
   - "10 ( mb)/σ_in" in the body is garbled — it should be a clean fraction, e.g. $10\,\mathrm{mb}/\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{pp}$, and the physical meaning (a branching probability? an effective cross section?) should be explained so a reader can't misinterpret it.
   - The 25% string-inversion number and the fragmentation-function choice get one sentence each; neither is justified.
   - No discussion of how this relates to the existing Fritiof/VISI/shocker literature on baryon production, only two of the author's own earlier notes ([6,7]). It reads as somewhat self-referential.

## Concrete asks before this is submittable

1. Add a controlled comparison table with χ²/dof for: baseline; +best $f(z)$; +inversion; +weight 1/6; and full combination. Make the *incremental* Δχ² of the suppression explicit.
2. Plot fit quality vs. the diquark weight; show whether 1/6 is a genuine minimum.
3. Add data error bars and a ratio subplot to both figures; discuss neutron/Λ in text.
4. At least one independent consistency check (different energy, different reaction, or a baryon-antibaryon ratio) before making the general "reconsider MC generators" claim.
5. Fix the "et al." typo and clean up the garbled diffraction-dissociation probability.

## Verdict

A reasonable and well-motivated observation, but it is currently an under-documented fitting exercise being presented as a physics result. Because the improvement is not isolated from the other tuned ingredients, there is no χ², and the value 1/6 is shown to be a data-selected minimum rather than by-eye, the paper does not yet substantiate its own headline claim. With the controlled comparison and a couple of cross-checks it could become a solid, useful short paper.
