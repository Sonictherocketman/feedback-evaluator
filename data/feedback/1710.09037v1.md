## [1710.09037v1-general-review] General Review


I've read the full 4-page proceeding (arXiv:1710.09037v1, ICNP-style proceedings: Shen & Lin, "Decay behaviors of the Pc hadronic molecules", Oct 2017; summarizing Shen et al., Nucl. Phys. A 954 (2016) 393 and Lin et al., Phys. Rev. D 95 (2017) 114017). Here is my review.

## Summary of the argument

The two 2015 LHCb pentaquark candidates, Pc⁺(4380) and Pc⁺(4450), are interpreted as hadronic molecules sitting just below the D̄Σ\*c (≈4387 MeV) and D̄\*Σc (≈4461–4464 MeV) thresholds. The authors compute the strong decay patterns of the possible assignments — Pc(4380) as S-wave D̄Σ\*c(3/2⁻) or D̄\*Σc(3/2⁻), and Pc(4450) as D̄\*Σc(3/2⁻ or 5/2⁺, the latter being a P-wave molecule) — by estimating the Pc–(meson–baryon) coupling via compositeness and meson-exchange triangle diagrams with Gaussian/dipole regulators. Two claims are drawn from the results:

1. A **discriminator**: R = Γ(D̄\*Λc)/Γ(J/ψp) is ~10 if Pc(4380) is D̄Σ\*c but ~1 if it is D̄\*Σc (Eq. 3).
2. A **width-based preference**: with Λ0=1 GeV, Λ1=2 GeV (Table 2), totals of 130.6 / 64.0 / 128.7 / 35.0 MeV make "Pc(4380) = D̄Σ\*c(3/2⁻), Pc(4450) = D̄\*Σc(5/2⁺)" the picture "more favorable" to LHCb's widths.

## Strengths

- **Physically coherent framework.** The threshold-proximity argument (7 MeV binding for 4380 against D̄Σ\*c, ~11 MeV for 4450 against D̄\*Σc) is the standard and sensible molecular rationale, and the spin-parity bookkeeping is consistent (S-wave D̄B molecules give J^P = L⊗(S_B), parity −; the 5/2⁺ case is correctly treated as a P-wave system).
- **The D̄\*Λc / J/ψp ratio is a genuinely useful experimental proposal** — a specific, observable, assignment-sensitive prediction that the community did not have spelled out this way at the time.
- **Table 2 is internally consistent** (all columns sum to the quoted totals), and the rejection of Pc(4450) as D̄\*Σc(3/2⁻) on widths (128.7 vs 39±21 MeV, i.e. several σ) is statistically meaningful.
- **Hindsight check:** the preferred assignment survived LHCb's 2021 re-analysis (Pc(4380) compatible with 3/2⁻, Pc(4450) 5/2⁺ preferred) and the 2021 widths (≈146 / ≈29 MeV) fit the predicted 130.6 / 35.0 MeV *better* than the 2015 numbers did. This is real, if modest, validation.

## Criticism

1. **The central "more favorable" claim is statistically weak.** For Pc(4380) the comparison is 130.6 vs 64.0 MeV against LHCb's then-value 205±18±86 MeV: both assignments sit within about 0.9σ and 2.1σ of the central value. At that systematics level, declaring D̄Σ\*c "more favorable" over D̄\*Σc exceeds what the data support; the authors would have been stronger presenting it as "consistent, with a mild pull." (The 4450 argument is much stronger and should be foregrounded.)
2. **No uncertainties anywhere.** Every width in Table 2 is a bare number; the only error statement is "we only estimate the order of the ratio results, since there are many variable factors cannot be determined." The headline discriminator (R~10 vs R~1) therefore has no stated error band. Given the sensitivity of loop amplitudes to Λ0 (0.5–1.2 GeV) and the unquantified coupling assumptions, a factor-10 claimed separation with unknown ~factor-2–3 error bars leaves the "1" and "10" regimes far from cleanly disjoint. A sensitivity table (R vs Λ0, Λ1) is not shown in the proceeding.
3. **Weakly bound state + hard cutoff is the Achilles' heel of the method.** The binding energies are ~7–11 MeV, so the bound-state momenta are of order √(2μB) ≲ 100–150 MeV, while the regulator is allowed to move from 0.5 to 1.2 GeV — deeply outside the molecular wave function, where the effective field content (elementary component, other open channels, chiral effects) is unconstrained. The compositeness-based first-vertex coupling is never quoted, so the reader cannot assess how the D̄\*Λc dominance in the D̄Σ\*c case depends on it. The 5/2⁺ P-wave vertex is worse: its derivative coupling "will depend on some parameters which are included necessarily to make the loop integration convergent," and the authors reuse the form-factor cutoff Λ0 for that scale "for simplicity" — an arbitrary move that should at least carry its own scan.
4. **Parent isospin is never specified.** D̄(I=1/2) ⊗ Σc,Σ\*c(I=1) gives I = 1/2 or 3/2 for the molecule, and the predicted D̄Λc vs D̄Σc (and πN, ρN) yields depend on it through Clebsch–Gordan structure. An open channel like D̄Λcπ (7.5 MeV) also implicitly fixes a charge/isospin convention. This omission weakens the "decay pattern" comparison, since the *ratio* of open-charm to light-final-state branches is isospin-dependent.
5. **Incomplete hypothesis space.** LHCb (2015) also allowed J^P = 3/2⁺ for Pc(4380). A 3/2⁺ molecule (P-wave D̄Σ\*c or D̄\*Σc) is a legitimate competitor and is never computed; neither is 5/2⁻ for Pc(4450). The "disentangle the nature" claim therefore covers only the S/P-wave subset the authors find convenient. (This gap mattered later: 3/2⁺ remained experimentally competitive for Pc(4380) through the 2021 LHCb measurement.)
6. **The main competing dynamical picture is absent.** The unitarized coupled-channel results of Xie–Liu–Oset (2016–17), in which a *single* attractive D̄\*Σc / D̄Σ\*c interaction dynamically generates both the narrow and the broad structure, was available and directly challenges the "two separate two-body molecules" framing. Citing only the meson-exchange lineage (and a handful of diquark papers) makes the comparison look one-sided for what is explicitly a phenomenological disambiguation exercise.
7. **A cheap, decisive cross-check is skipped.** In the preferred picture Br(J/ψp) for Pc(4380) is only ~2.7/130.6 ≈ 2%. LHCb's measured Λb → Pc⁺K⁻(→J/ψp)K⁻ yield constrains Br(Pc → J/ψp) × Br(Λb → PcK) and would have been a strong consistency test of the dominant D̄\*Λc branch; it is not confronted with the data.
8. **The production section is decorative.** "Large cross sections" and "much larger than J/ψp" appear with no numbers, no energies, and no acceptance discussion — all deferred to Ref. [15]. In a document whose selling point is experimental guidance, this should carry at least an order of magnitude and a proposed beam energy.
9. **Provenance and presentation.** This is a proceedings note for work already published in Refs. [14, 15]; it adds essentially no new results, which is normal for the genre but means every quantitative claim should at least carry the error analysis of the journal papers — it doesn't. Minor defects: Fig. 1 caption says "(I): a D̄Σc molecule" where the drawings (and context) are D̄Σ\*c; "antiqurk," "residual theorem" (residue), "dependance," "in more favorable"; Eq. (3)'s notation Γ(Pc(4380) → D̄Σ\*c → D̄\*Λc) is compressed to the point of being ambiguous.

## Bottom line

The physics is sensible within the meson-exchange EFT framework, the tables are self-consistent, and the preferred assignment (4380 ≈ D̄Σ\*c 3/2⁻, 4450 ≈ D̄\*Σc 5/2⁺) has historically fared well — later LHCb spin-parity/width results and Lattice QCD threshold studies corroborate it. But the argument as written overstates its statistical leverage (a <1σ width pull on Pc(4380) presented as a preference), quantifies nothing, omits the parent's isospin and two viable spin-parity alternatives, and does not engage the competing coupled-channel picture that was published a year earlier. The D̄\*Λc/J/ψp ratio proposal is the document's most valuable contribution and remains an open, practical experimental test; the rest is a fair but under-supported summary of the authors' journal papers.

**Strengths.**

2. The total-width argument is coherent: `130.6 MeV` (for the `3/2⁻ D̄Σ_c*` picture of `P_c(4380)`) sits comfortably inside LHCb's `205 ± 18 ± 86 MeV`, and the `3/2⁺` `D*Σ_c` `P_c(4450)` at `35 MeV` is plausible. That consistency is a fair, if modest, selling point.

1. **It is a summary, and its main claims live elsewhere.** The derivations, the effective Lagrangians, the couplings, and the Appendix B non-relativistic heavy-quark-spin treatment are all deferred to Refs. 14 and 15. As it stands the proceeding is close to a results-and-plot note: the reader cannot audit the `R ~ 10` claim from this document alone. For a standalone publication this is a real deficiency; it is more acceptable for a conference abstract.
2. **Parametric uncertainties are under-quantified.** Two independent cutoffs (`Λ_0` = 0.5–1.2 GeV, `Λ_1` = 1.5–2.4 GeV) and compositeness-estimated couplings dominate the error budget, yet Table 2 reports a single point (`Λ_0 = 1.0`, `Λ_1 = 2.0`). The text does not show how `R_I` responds across the full `Λ_0` range — if `R_I` drifts from ~5 to ~50, "~10" is not a robust statement. The method transfers; the numerical comparison does not.

**Verdict.** A competent, focused note whose most defensible contribution (a clean `D*Λ_c`/`J/ψ` discrimination ratio to test the molecule hypothesis) is worth keeping. It should not, however, be read as a self-sufficient result: the physics is carried by Refs. 14–15, and the quantitative claims need explicit uncertainty propagation over `Λ_0`, `Λ_1`, and the compositeness couplings before the "order-of-magnitude" statements are trustworthy.

# Critique: "Decay behaviors of the P_c hadronic molecules" (Shen & Lin, 2017)

## Summary

This four-page conference proceeding (PO) summarizes the authors' work (essentially refs. [14] and [15]) on treating the 2015 LHCb P_c⁺(4380) and P_c⁺(4450) candidates as S-wave (and one P-wave) D̄Σ*_c / D̄*Σ_c hadronic molecules. In a meson-exchange "triangle" framework with Gaussian regulators, compositeness-condition couplings, and an off-shell form factor, they compute partial widths into nine final states. The headline claims: (i) the ratio R = Γ(D̄*Λ_c)/Γ(J/ψp) is ~10 for a D̄Σ*_c molecule but ~1 for a D̄*Σ_c molecule, so measuring it in e⁺e⁻ data would disentangle P_c(4380)'s composition; (ii) total widths favor 3/2⁻ D̄Σ*_c for P_c(4380) and 5/2⁺ D̄*Σ_c for P_c(4450); (iii) future searches for the states in D̄*Λ_c / D̄Λ_c production in γp and πp should be large.

## What is done competently

- The internal bookkeeping is consistent: the individual rows in Table 2 sum correctly to the quoted totals (130.6 / 64.0 / 128.7 / 35.0 MeV), and the open/closed channel pattern (short bars) is consistent with the 2015 masses versus the thresholds.
- The width analysis against the 2015 LHCb numbers is fair: 64 MeV and 128.7 MeV are clearly in tension with Γ(4380) ≈ 205 ± 86 MeV and Γ(4450) ≈ 39 ± 19 MeV, while 130.6 and 35.0 MeV sit near center, so the preferred assignment follows as claimed (within 2017 knowledge).
- The proposed experimental discriminant (the D̄*Λ_c vs J/ψp branching ratio at BESIII/Belle II) is a sensible, testable prediction in spirit.

## Main problems

**1. Isospin is never addressed anywhere — and this is a real, not cosmetic, gap.** The LHCb candidates are produced in Λ_b⁰ → P_c⁺K⁻, an I = 0 environment, so I(P_c) = 0. But a naive D̄ ⊗ Σ*_c molecule has light-quark isospin 1/2 ⊗ 1 = 1/2 ⊕ 3/2 — no I = 0 component exists — and the J/ψp final state (p is I = 1/2, J/ψ is I = 0) is itself I = 1/2, so an I = 0 parent cannot decay into it by strong interactions. Conversely, the set of channels in Table 2 is self-consistent only under an I = 1/2 assignment, which contradicts the production. The paper silently computes Γ(J/ψp) for "the" P_c, and includes ρN (1.6 MeV) and, for P_c(4450), D̄Σ*_c (8.9 MeV) open-channel widths that are individually forbidden for an I = 0 parent. Whatever convention ref. [15] used (fixed charge channels, implicit I mixing) is invisible here, and the central discriminant in Eq. (3) is built directly on the J/ψp channel. This tension (widely discussed in the 2015–17 literature on these states) deserves at least a paragraph; its total absence is the paper's most damaging omission for the claim it is making.

**2. The motivation rests on a measurement later overturned.** The 2015 LHCb single-channel analysis that supplies P_c(4380) with a ~200 MeV width, and the "3/2⁻ + 5/2⁺ with opposite parities" spin preferences, was superseded by the LHCb Nature 2020 two-channel reanalysis (five states, P_c(4312)–P_c(4700), different masses/widths; the broad "4380" proved largely a kinematic fitting artifact). A substantial part of this work — explaining "the large decay width of P_c(4380)" and disentangling P_c(4380)'s composition — therefore addresses a structure that, as framed here, no longer exists. As a 2017 snapshot it is understandable; as a paper, its central premise did not survive.

**3. Parameter dependence is asserted, not shown.** The numbers carry two form-factor cutoffs (Λ₀ ∈ [0.5,1.2] GeV, Λ₁ ∈ [1.5,2.4] GeV), plus a third, ad hoc reuse of Λ₀ as the momentum-form cutoff for the P-wave 5/2⁺ vertex ("for simplicity"). The 5/2⁺ width (35 MeV, the one result supporting the P_c(4450) assignment) is precisely the quantity most sensitive to that P-wave cutoff convention, yet no scan or uncertainty band is given — "shown in Ref. [15]". Values like 110.4, 17.7, 35.0 MeV carry false precision for a model with three arbitrary scales, compositeness-condition couplings that themselves depend on Λ₀, and no reported compositeness fractions. Also internally: Eq. (3) claims R_I ≈ 10, but Table 2 at the quoted cutoffs gives 110.4/2.7 ≈ 41 — an "order of magnitude" that is really 1.6 orders; the loose wording ("only estimate the order") covers it, but the PO should show it, not hide it.

**4. Physical plausibility of the disfavored assignment is not argued.** Treating P_c(4380) as D̄*Σ_c places it ~81 MeV below that threshold — binding energy far larger than the ~5–20 MeV scale expected for these molecules. That is arguably a more direct argument against the D̄*Σ_c(4380) option than the width alone, and the paper never comments on it; it simply reports the smaller width as unfavorable.

**5. Self-containedness.** The framework section is essentially two sentences deferring everything — Lagrangians, couplings, compositeness, production cross sections — to refs. [14,15], the latter being the authors' own journal version of this same calculation. For a PO that is normal, but the result is a document whose only new content relative to [15] is the ratio-R discussion in Eq. (3) and the one-table summary. Nothing is reproducible or checkable from this document alone.

**6. Presentation.** Typos: "after the time-part integration has been done with the residual theorem" (residue), "antiqurk", "comositions", "dependance", "P_c(4380) ... in more favorable", the odd phrasing "useful suggestions and the effects on this work" in the acknowledgments. More substantively, Fig. 1 is carelessly labeled: in part (I) the blobs/final baryons in several lower-row panels (e.g. the η_c p / ρN / ωp channels, where panels show η and N or ω with mismatched baryons) do not match the caption's channel list; the figure needs a redraw-and-proof read pass. The ratio discussion of the Pc(4450) D̄Σ*_c row (8.9 vs 0.5 MeV between the 3/2⁻ and 5/2⁺ columns) is also mentioned in no sentence of the text.

## Verdict

An internally consistent, if thin, conference summary of the authors' own PRD paper, whose main quantitative discriminant (the J/ψp-based R ratio) sits on an isospin footing the paper never examines, and whose central physical target (a broad P_c(4380)) was removed by the 2020 LHCb reanalysis. Acceptable as a 2017 PO on its own historical terms; in its present, publication-ready form it would not support the claims it makes.

I read the full text (4 pages) of arXiv:1710.09037, *"Decay behaviors of the P_c hadronic molecules,"* by C.W. Shen and Y.H. Lin (Key Laboratory of Theoretical Physics, ITP/CAS).

## Stated contribution, in my own words

This is a short conference proceeding that summarizes the authors' program for telling apart the two LHCb hidden-charm pentaquark candidates, P_c(4380) and P_c(4450), by computing their strong hadronic decays under the assumption that they are loose meson–baryon molecules. Concretely the authors:

1. **Assume** P_c(4380) with J^P = 3/2⁻ is an S-wave molecule of either D̄Σ*_c(2520) or D̄*Σ_c(2455), and P_c(4450) with J^P = 5/2⁻ or 5/2⁺ is a D̄*Σ_c molecule.
2. **Compute partial widths** into many open channels using effective heavy-pion/kaon/vector Lagrangians, triangular one-meson-exchange (triangle) diagrams, and Gaussian form factors at both vertices, with cutoffs Λ₀ (0.5–1.2 GeV) and Λ₁ (1.5–2.4 GeV) scanned.
3. **Argue discrimination**: the ratio R = Γ(D̄Σ*_c → D*Λ_c)/Γ(→ J/ψ) is ~10 for a D̄Σ*_c assignment but ~1 for a D̄*Σ_c assignment, so a future measurement of the D*Λ_c/J/ψ branching ratio would fix the nature of P_c(4380); P_c(4380)'s large width (414 MeV) favors the broad D̄Σ*_c candidate.
4. **Use widths to fix P_c(4450)**: the 5/2⁺ assignment gives a predicted total width of ~35 MeV, matching LHCb's narrow 44 MeV, while 5/2⁻ gives ~129 MeV; so P_c(4450) is favored to be a 5/2⁺ molecule.
5. **Predict production**: large photoproduction/πp cross sections for D*Λ_c and D̄Λ_c final states through s-/u-channel P_c exchange (in γp, πp).

## Novelty judgment against the authors' own citations

**Almost none — this proceeding is substantially a summary of the authors' own prior publications, and that is said plainly in the text.** The abstract announces "our recent work," and the two decisive references are their own: Ref. [14] (Shen, Guo, Xie, Zou, *Nucl. Phys. A* 954:393, 2016, "Disentangling the hadronic molecule nature of the P_c(4380)") and Ref. [15] (Lin, Shen, Guo, Zou, *Phys. Rev. D* 95:114017, 2016, "Decay behaviors of the P_c hadronic molecules"). The framework section explicitly defers to [14,15] for the Lagrangians and couplings, and the production cross-sections are stated to be "calculated in Ref. [15]."

**Not new (carried over from cited work):**
- The molecular interpretation of the P_c states: Ref. [8,9,10,11] (Chen–Liu–Zhu; Roca–Nieves–Oset; He; Lü–Dong), and molecular predictions from before discovery [12,13] (Wu–Molina–Oset–Zou).
- The quark-model / diquark alternatives and kinetic/narrow-width arguments: Refs. [2,3,4,5,6,7].
- The R_DΣc* ≫ R_D*Σc (~10 vs ~1) channel-ratio result used to discriminate P_c(4380): Ref. [14], explicitly cited as also containing the "details" of the spin-symmetry argument.
- The entire effective-Lagrangian + triangle-diagram decay machinery: Refs. [14,15].
- The production-cross-section estimates: Ref. [15].

**What is (marginally) new in the 4 pages themselves:**
