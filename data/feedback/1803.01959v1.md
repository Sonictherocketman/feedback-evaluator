## [1803.01959v1-general-review] General Review


I read the full main text (through the Conclusions) plus the opening appendices. Here is my evaluation.

## The paper: *Compact Discrete Breathers on Flat Band Networks* (Danieli, Maluckov, Flach)

**Core argument.** The paper studies one‑component lattices with a flat band (compact localized states, CLSs) carrying an onsite cubic nonlinearity, and asks when a linear CLS survives as a time‑periodic *compact* discrete breather, and whether it is linearly stable.

Two results are carried:

1. **Continuation / compactness criterion (Lemma, Sec. II).** A linear CLS on a unit cell with amplitudes $a_{i,j}\in\{0,\pm1\}\cdot A_{i,j}$ continues into a compact breather iff it is *homogeneous* — every occupied site has the same $|A_{i,j}|$ — with a renormalised frequency $\Omega=E_{FB}+\gamma A^{2}=\!E_{FB}+g$. Heterogeneous CLSs lose compactness.

2. **Linear stability (Sec. III–IV).** For class $U=1$ (orthogonal CLS basis), the only instability mechanism is resonance with extended states, appearing as first‑order Arnold tongues via the strained‑coefficient method. For $U\ge 2$ (non‑orthogonal neighbour CLSs) there is an *additional, local* instability that does not radiate when a spectral gap exists. Numerics on the cross‑stitch ($U=1$) and saw‑tooth ($U=2$) lattices confirm the two regimes, using the participation number $P$ as a compactness indicator.

### What is good

- The homogeneity condition is clean, correctly derived, and useful as a design rule: it explains *why* some flat‑band networks support compact breathers and others only exponentially localized ones. The reduction of Eq. (9) to the single‑site equation at every occupied site makes the necessity/sufficiency transparent.
- The conceptual separation between **radiating instability** (overlap with the dispersive band) and **local, radiationless instability** (non‑orthogonal neighbour CLS) is the paper's most interesting idea. It is well motivated and the saw‑tooth example (Fig. 5, exponentially localized unstable eigenmode despite no dispersive resonance) is a good illustration.
- The setup is honest and well scoped mathematically: 1D, nearest‑neighbour, single component — a defensible minimal model, and the appendices actually do the Floquet/period‑advancing algebra rather than sketching it.

### Where the argument is weaker

1. **The main theorem is near‑tautological in its scope.** For a cubic onsite nonlinearity, a stationary compact solution *must* satisfy $\omega=E_{FB}+\gamma|a|^{2}$ on every occupied site, so equal magnitudes are simply "every site is a single‑site NLS fixed point." The novelty lies in packaging this with the stability analysis, not in the condition itself. That is fine, but the "necessary and sufficient" phrasing overstates an elementary observation, and the restriction (single‑site, single‑component, NN) should be foregrounded rather than buried.

2. **The numerics never quantitatively test the analytic theory.** The strained‑coefficient derivation predicts *where* the first‑order tongues open (boundaries in the $(\Omega,g)$ plane, widths scaling with $g$), yet Sec. IV only reports binary "stable vs unstable" heatmaps and the participation number. There is no overlay of predicted tongue edges on a measured stability map, and no comparison of measured growth rates to the Floquet eigenvalues the theory produces. Given a fairly quantitative analytic section, this is the paper's most obvious gap — a stability map with analytic boundaries on top would substantially strengthen it.

3. **The $U\ge 2$ mechanism rests on numerics, not analysis.** The analytic Floquet treatment is done to first order only for $U=1$ (tongues) and a symmetric $U=2$ (where no extra tongues appear). The "neighbour‑CLS resonance → local instability" claim (the $-0.1<g<0$ window on saw‑tooth) is asserted and supported only by time‑evolution plots, with no derivation of the small eigenvalue $2.987\times10^{-5}$ from a resonance condition. Since this local‑instability result is arguably the paper's main new physics, leaving it purely numerical is a soft spot. At minimum a two‑state (breather + nearest neighbour CLS) block Floquet argument would pin it down.

4. **Second‑order tongues are not ruled out.** With a purely onsite, odd (KDE‑like) nonlinearity the potential is centrosymmetric and second‑order tongues are expected to be absent, but the paper only shows first‑order tongues disappear for $m\ne1$ and says higher‑order ones do not appear "see Fig. 1 / Appendix C" without explicitly invoking the symmetry argument. The aside that tongues "disappear as $N\to\infty$ (unlike [11])" is confusing: in a finite box the resonances are discrete; in the infinite limit the set becomes a continuum, and it's not obvious what is meant by disappearance.

5. **Title/abstract over‑promise relative to content.** Higher dimensions, longer‑range hopping and multi‑component extensions are only gestured at in the Conclusions; every derivation and simulation is 1D NN single‑component on exactly two topologies. "Flat band networks" (plural, general) is doing too much work for two test lattices.

### Minor / presentational issues

- **Notation clash:** $A$ is used both for the CLS amplitude and for the Floquet/period‑advancing matrix in Eq. (21) (and the matrix in Eq. 34). Renaming one would remove real ambiguity.
- The participation number is a fine qualitative indicator of compactness but is loose for the intermediate "few sites" regime it is used for; a sentence on its resolution there would help.
- The cross‑stitch CLS is written $A(1,-1)$ with amplitude $A$, yet the stability discussion is phrased in terms of $g=\gamma A^{2}$; a worked statement of how the *two* sites' opposite signs interact with $\gamma|a|^{2}$ (they don't, since it's local, and that is precisely why the homogeneous $U=1$ case stays linear at leading order) would make the $U=1$ stability claim self‑contained rather than reliant on the abstract.

### Bottom line

A sound, clearly written, and correctly executed theory‑plus‑numerics paper whose most valuable contributions are (i) a simple, correctly‑scoped compactness criterion and (ii) the identification of a radiating‑vs‑local dichotomy in breather instability on flat‑band networks. It is, however, an *incremental* refinement of the well‑studied CLS‑to‑nonlinear‑breather and Arnold‑tongue literature: the analytic part is largely first‑order and the headline $U\ge 2$ finding is not yet analytically justified, while the numerics confirm qualitatively but do not benchmark the predicted tongue boundaries. It would be meaningfully improved by a measured‑vs‑predicted stability map and a two‑state analytic treatment of the local instability.

## Summary of the work

The paper studies flat-band tight-binding networks with local (on-site, cubic) nonlinearity. Its core claims are: (i) a linear compact localized state (CLS) of class U can be continued into an exact nonlinear time-periodic **compact discrete breather (CDB) if and only if the CLS profile is homogeneous** (all occupied sites have equal absolute amplitude), with frequency shift Ω = E_F + γA²; (ii) perturbative (strained-coefficient / Bogoliubov) and numerical (Floquet + time evolution) stability analysis of these CDBs, distinguishing U = 1 (orthogonal CLS, e.g. cross-stitch) from U ≥ 2 (non-orthogonal CLS, e.g. sawtooth), where the latter admits an extra *local* instability not seen for U = 1.

## Strengths

1. **The continuation criterion (the lemma, Sec. II) is exact and is the cleanest statement of its kind.** The key point — worth emphasizing more explicitly in the text — is that it holds at *finite* γ, not just perturbatively: where a_lj = 0 the nonlinear equation is trivially satisfied, and where a_lj ≠ 0 the equal-amplitude condition reduces (9) to a single scalar equation. This generalizes the "compaction tuning" idea of Johansson, Näther & Viena-not (Ref. [36]) into a U-classified framework that covers all flat-band topologies, which is a genuinely useful organizing result.
2. **Good division of labor between methods.** The first-order tongue calculation (Secs. III C, App. B–C) is done carefully (secular-term bookkeeping in App. B is explicit), and it is cross-checked with independent numerical tools (DIOVGRG Floquet eigenvalues, 6th-order RK time evolution, participation number). Agreement between the predicted instability windows and the numerics in both test beds is the paper's quantitative backbone.
3. **Physical message is clear and correctly framed:** for U = 1, instability comes only from resonance with extended states (and thus the weakly-nonlinear breather is stable off resonance); for U ≥ 2, non-orthogonality of neighboring CLS opens an extra weak nonlinearity-dependent channel that is *local* (does not destroy compactness). That contrast is the paper's actual new content.
4. Writing is clean, structure is logical (I–V + four self-contained appendices), and the cross-stitch/sawtooth pair is a sensible choice of contrasting examples.

## Substantive concerns

1. **The resonance condition (Eqs. 22–23) is stated too loosely and is not consistently applied.** The Floquet machinery is set up with period T = π/Ω because the linearized source oscillates at 2Ω; the actual degeneracy conditions should be expressed as exact relations among E_F, λ_q and Ω (including the 2Ω parametric channels, e.g. 2Ω ≈ λ_q + E_F). As printed, Eq. (23) reads "E_F = mΩ, λ_q = m′Ω", and the text then says instability occurs when the breather frequency falls "in resonance with the dispersive band" — i.e., mere spectral overlap — which is not the same as the integer condition. Concretely, for Fig. 3(b) (h = 1, g = 1, A = 1) one has Ω = 1 + 1 = 2, but the body says "the frequency Ω = 1 + 2 = 3" — an arithmetic slip that, left uncorrected, makes it impossible for a reader to verify the claimed resonance from the stated condition. Please (a) fix the number, and (b) re-state the resonance condition in exact Floquet form and show explicitly that Figs. 3(b) and 6(c) satisfy it, while 3(a) and 6(d) do not.
2. **The "narrow region −0.1 < g < 0" for the sawtooth local instability is inconsistent in scale with the perturbation theory.** The first-order tongue width in (Ω,g) is of order g/N (Eq. 38), i.e. ~10⁻⁴ for the parameters used, whereas the quoted window is O(0.1) — two to three orders of magnitude wider. Either the window is the union of many (m ≥ 2) resonances — in which case say so and show the resonance points — or it comes from finite-size numerics at large g where the weak-nonlinearity expansion breaks down. The paper currently asserts it without derivation; I would not sign off on that sentence as written.
3. **I could not verify Eq. (32) from Eq. (33).** Working through the cross-stitch hopping equations as printed, I do not recover E(q) = −h + 4cos q with E_F = h (the ±h on-diagonal couplings do not appear in a mutually consistent way with the off-diagonal ones in the displayed equations). This may be a typesetting omission in the equations rather than a real error, but a reviewer cannot be expected to guess which term is missing. Please print the Bloch matrix H(q) explicitly for both models and give E_F, E(q) from it in one line. The same (lighter) check for the sawtooth passes, which makes the cross-stitch case the one that needs the fix.
4. **The "locality" of the U ≥ 2 instability is weaker than the narrative claims.** Your own Fig. 6(c) shows the participation number reaching ~40 of N = 50 unit cells by t = 1000 in the unstable case — i.e. most of the finite chain carries non-negligible amplitude. An exponentially localized unstable eigenvector (Fig. 5) characterizes the *initial* growth direction, not the long-time state. Calling this "a local oscillation of the excitation" is generous; I would rephrase (the instability is *seeded* locally / does not immediately destroy compactness) and, if possible, show the g → or long-time evolution at smaller g where the spread is slower, or a short-time growth factor as a function of site to pin down the decay length.
5. **Thermodynamic-limit statements (Sec. III C.1)**: "as N → ∞ the coefficients converge to zero, so instability regions disappear" conflates two scalings. The g/N factor in Eqs. The physical claim — that for U = 1 the only instability windows shrink/vanish in the infinite-chain limit — is plausible and important, but it should be stated as a scaling argument, not as a limit of the coefficients, and the "in analogy to [11]" pointer is off-target (that reference concerns disorder). Cite the standard finite-size instability results instead (e.g. [31], [42]).

## Minor / technical points

- Eq. A short final paragraph on where compact breathers are/should be observable (exciton–polariton flat bands, BEC on lattice flat bands, superconducting wires) and which parameter regimes (gapped vs touching flat band, small g) would be testable would markedly strengthen the paper's impact.
- The 2018 arXiv v1 → 2022 dated manuscript gap suggests substantial revision; the current text reads well, but please reconcile reference [11] usage (see above) and double-check the Fig. 3(b) caption/body consistency mentioned in concern 1.

This is a solid, well-above-average paper: it turns a natural observation (homogeneous CLS continue as compact breathers) into an exact, U-classified criterion, and — more importantly — delivers a consistent perturbative + numerical stability map distinguishing U = 1 and U ≥ 2, with the sawtooth local instability as the new result beyond Ref. [36]. It is not a breakthrough: the core criterion is essentially algebraic given the form of the nonlinearity, the perturbation theory is first order, and the locality claim for U ≥ 2 rests on data (participation numbers) that pull in the other direction. The paper is publishable as is in a good journal, but concerns 1–4 above are real and should be addressed in any revision; they concern the paper's own internal consistency (stated resonance condition vs. numerics, stated band structure vs. equations, "narrow" vs. "O(0.1)" window, "local" vs. P ≈ 0.8 N) rather than its broad validity.

**Verdict:** Acceptable work with a useful organizing result and one genuine new effect (U ≥ 2 local instability), weakened by internal-consistency loose ends that are all fixable. I support publication after the authors resolve concerns 1–4 and tighten the Fig. 1/Eq. (38) and Eq. (7) details.

## Critique: *Compact Discrete Breathers on Flat Band Networks* (Danieli, Maluckov, Flach; arXiv:1803.01959v1)

### What the paper does
It asks whether strictly spatially‑compact, time‑periodic solutions (compact discrete breathers, CDBs) exist on nonlinear flat‑band lattices, and gives (a) a continuation criterion ("homogeneous CLS") for a linear compact localized state (CLS) to persist as a CDB, with frequency shift `Ω = E_FB + γA²`, (b) a linear‑stability picture distinguishing `U=1` (orthogonal CLS basis → only dispersive‑state resonance is dangerous) from `U≥2` (non‑orthogonal CLSs → an extra *local* instability even inside a band gap), and (c) numerical evolutions on the canonical cross‑stitch (`U=1`) and saw‑tooth (`U=2`) chains.

### Strengths
- **Correct and cleanly framed core.** The continuation argument is sound: for on‑site cubic nonlinearity, a uniform diagonal shift `γA²` over a CLS's support preserves it as an exact compact eigenstate *iff* all support amplitudes have equal magnitude. I checked both example CLSs against the equations and the band structures; they are consistent.
- **The `U=1` vs `U=2` distinction is the paper's real contribution.** The most interesting result — that a `U=2` CDB in a *gapped* sawtooth is locally unstable for a narrow window `−0.1 < g < 0` purely because neighbouring CLSs overlap — is a clean, non‑obvious observation, and it is well motivated by the non‑orthogonality of the flat‑band manifold.
- **Appropriate, canonical test systems** and a readable physical narrative (destructive interference; tails vs. tailless compact states).

### Major weaknesses

1. **The "necessary and sufficient" criterion is close to tautological and over‑general in its phrasing.** The statement that a CLS continues as a CDB *iff* it is homogeneous is derived under a very specific ansatz that the nonlinear orbit reuses the *exact* linear CLS profile (Eq. 8), and for a *specific* on‑site `|ψ|²` (cubic) nonlinearity (Eq. 7). Under that ansatz the condition is essentially the observation that a uniform phase shift preserves an eigenvector. As a "lemma / definition" it reads like packaging rather than content, and the generality claimed ("local nonlinear terms", "irrespective of class `U`") is broader than what is actually proven. For non‑cubic, non‑local, or cross‑nonlinearities the criterion changes, and the paper does not discuss this.

2. **The perturbative (Arnold‑tongue / strained‑coefficient) analysis is genuinely delivered only for `U=1`.** For `U=2`, Appendix B writes the coupled equations (30, 31) and then simply *asserts* that the extra `q`‑dependent ("polarized") terms "do not provide the appearance of further regions of instability… in the first order." At `O(g)` the secular‑term check only clears the leading resonant coupling; multi‑mode and higher‑order effects are not excluded. Consequently the entire `U≥2` stability story — including the headline local instability — rests on a *single numerical example* with one tiny real eigenvalue (`EV = 2.987×10⁻⁵`) and one time‑evolution, not on the analytical machinery the introduction promises.

3. **Analysis and numerics are never married.** Appendix D says the linearized eigenvalue problem (14) is solved numerically (IMSL `DEVCRG`) "for different values of `g`," but the paper contains **no stability diagram** — no plot of `Re(eigenvalue)` vs `g` or of the `(Ω, g)` stability/instability regions. Fig. 1 is a schematic cartoon of a first‑order Arnold tongue, not a numerically computed one. The natural comparison — analytical tongues in `(Ω,g)` vs the computed instability chart — is absent, so the quantitative stability claims are unsupported by a plot a reader can check.

4. **The thermodynamic‑limit statement is unclear and arguably inverted.** The authors note that the tongue coefficients scale as `1/N` (Eq. 27) and conclude that "in the limit of infinite chain, the instability regions disappear, in analogy with [41]." Read literally this says a breather whose frequency lies *inside* the dispersive band becomes *stable* as `N→∞`, which contradicts both the standard continuum picture and the paper's **own** Fig. 3(b), where at `h=1, g=1` the CDB radiates and is destroyed into the continuum. At most the single‑mode strained‑coefficient picture shrinks each individual tongue to a measure‑zero line, but whether the union over all `q` fills a continuous instability band in the thermodynamic limit is exactly the point that is never addressed. The statement as written needs to be reconciled with the finite‑`N` radiation that is numerically observed.

5. **Limited novelty relative to prior work.** Compact breathers on saw‑tooth / flat‑band lattices were already reported (Johansson, Næther, Vicencio, *PRE* 92, 032912 (2015), ref. [36]; Beličević et al. ref. [37]). The existence result is therefore not new here. The genuine increments — the homogeneity criterion (weak, see item 1) and the gapped `U=2` local instability (strong, but single‑example, see item 2) — are relatively narrow, which limits the paper's reach.

### Minor issues (specific, checkable)
- **Figure/parameter inconsistencies.** The text fixes the initial CDB amplitude at `A=1`, yet the `|a_n(t)|` colourbars in Fig. 3 peak at only ~0.30 (a)/(b) 0.60, unrelated to `g` in an obvious way) and Fig. 3(c) reports the stable‑case participation number `P≈1.5`. For a pure single‑dimer cross‑stitch CDB with `A=1`, the inverse participation `1/Σ(|a|⁴+|b|⁴)` is ~0.5, not ~1.5. Either the plotted states are not the stated `A=1` dimers or `P` is normalized inconsistently between caption and text. These are the kind of cross‑checks that a stability paper should nail down.
- **`U=2` instability quantified weakly.** The "local instability" window `−0.1<g<0` is reported to only ~one significant figure; the eigenvector in Fig. 5 is described as "exponentially localized" but no localization length is extracted.

## The paper's stated contribution, in my own words

The paper studies *compact discrete breathers* (CDBs) — time-periodic, spatially compact solutions of nonlinear Schrödinger equations on flat-band lattices — and makes two claims:

1. **An existence/continuation criterion.** A linear compact localized state (CLS) of a flat-band network can be continued into the nonlinear regime as a strictly compact breather (with frequency shifted to Ω = E_FB + γA²) *if and only if* it is **homogeneous**: all occupied sites carry equal absolute amplitude. The proof is a short algebraic one (Eqs. 9–12): outside compact support the linear equation forces E_FB, the on-site nonlinearity adds a uniform shift γA², and any site whose amplitude deviates from the common value A picks up a different frequency, breaking the single-orbit time-periodicity. Heterogeneous CLSs instead lose compactness under nonlinearity (their tails become exponentially localized, per Ref. [36]).

2. **A linear stability classification.** Within this framework, the authors separate two instability mechanisms:
   - **Class U = 1** (orthogonal CLSs, one per unit cell): because the CLSs form an orthonormal basis, nearest-CLS resonances are absent in the linear limit, so the *only* weakly non-linear instability source is resonance with extended dispersive states. Using Bloch/Floquet analysis plus the strained-coefficient method, they derive first-order Arnold tongues (Eqs. 26–29) whose width for the m=1 resonance shrinks as 1/N while m≥2 tongues are absent, so the instability windows collapse in the infinite-chain limit.
   - **Class U ≥ 2** (non-orthogonal CLSs): extended interactions between *neighboring* CLSs open an additional instability channel that is **local** — it does not destroy the breather but produces exponentially localized oscillatory eigenmodes, since the band gap prevents radiating the excitation to infinity. This is demonstrated numerically on the saw-tooth lattice (Fig. 5: an unstable purely-real eigenvalue 2.987×10⁻⁵ with an exponentially decaying eigenvector).

   Both predictions are checked by direct time-evolution of perturbed breathers on the cross-stitch and saw-tooth lattices, with participation numbers as the compactness diagnostic.

## Novelty judgment vs. the cited prior work

**Not new (and the paper itself concedes this):**

- **Existence of CLSs and flat-band physics** — all background (Refs. [1]–[22], Sutherland/Lieb, Meikle/Tasaki, engineering and experiments [23]–[30]).
- **The existence of compact discrete breathers itself** was already observed: Johansson, Näther & Vienaen (Ref. [36], saw-tooth lattices) found compact nonlinear localized modes via "compactification tuning," and Ref. [37] reported related nonlinear flat-band modes; Perchikow & Gendelman ([39]) studied compactons in mechanical flat-band lattices. The introduction explicitly states this. The homogeneity result of this paper is essentially the *explanation* of why the tuning in [36] works — a rationalization of a known phenomenon, not the phenomenon.
- **The perturbative toolkit** — Floquet/period-advancing analysis, strained coefficients, Arnold tongues, Bogoliubov expansions — is standard breather theory (Nyffeler [40], Flach–Willis [31], Marin–Aubry [41]), applied here rather than invented.
- **Known breather-tail physics and compact-solution precedent** (algebraic vs. exponential tails, spatially compact PDE and Fermi–Pasta–Ulam solutions) are carried over from [31]–[35].

**Genuinely new:**

- **The homogeneity condition as a necessary-and-sufficient, lattice-independent criterion** valid for any dimension, any class U, and local anharmonicities (with the explicit frequency-shift rule). This is a clean generalization beyond the case-by-case tuning of [36]–[37]/[39]. It is simple (which is why it is easy to check), and its main value is organizational: it turns "can this CLS become a compact breather?" into a one-line test.

This distinction, and its numerical demonstration (Figs. 3–6, incl. the saw-tooth unstable eigenvector in Fig. 5), is the paper's most original content; I am not aware it exists in the cited literature.
- **The quantitative vanishing of the m = 1Arnold-tongue width as 1/N** (the flat-band macroscopic degeneracy suppressing the main instability region) and the absence of higher-resonance tongues for U=1 — a concrete, testable weak-nonlinear prediction.

**Weaknesses tempering the novelty claim:**

- The U ≥ 2 "local instability" claim is *asserted in general* but proved/demonstrated only in the one saw-tooth example; there is no general result that such local instabilities must occur (or must not destroy the breather) for arbitrary U ≥ 2 networks.
- The stability analysis is strictly first-order, weakly nonlinear, and numerically tested on only two 1D toy lattices; multi-site and higher-U examples are absent.
- The homogeneity theorem is nearly trivial algebra in hindsight — its real content is the packaging, not the difficulty.

- **Overall:** Technically sound within its scope; the core analytical results are correct and consistently verified, but the scope is narrow (two model lattices, weak nonlinearity) and part of the main classification rests on a single numerical demonstration rather than proof.

## Methodology — enough to reproduce the central experiment

**Model class.** The starting point is a linear Schrödinger-type lattice equation
  i ψ̇_n = H_0 ψ_n + H_1 ψ_{n+1} + H_1† ψ_{n−1},
on one spatial dimension, with ν real amplitudes per unit cell. A model is "flat-band" when its spectrum contains at least one wave-vector–independent E_FB and the linear eigenvectors (compact localized states, CLSs) are strictly finite in space.

**Continuation criterion (Sec. II).** Insert a local cubic nonlinearity γ·𝒜(ψ_n)ψ_n, where 𝒜 has |ψ_{n,j}|² on the diagonal, and look for time-periodic continuations of a linear CLS ψ̃_{n,n0}(t) = Σ_i v_i δ_{n,n0+i} e^{-iE_FB t}. Writing the continuation as C_{n,n0}(t) = Σ_i v_i δ_{n,n0+i} e^{-iΩt}, the authors show:

- If a CLS is *homogeneous* (every nonzero a_{i,j} has |a_{i,j}| ≡ A), then the orbit continues with frequency
  Ω = E_FB + γ A²  (g ≡ γA²).
- If the CLS is *heterogeneous* (nonzero a_{i,j} with different |a_{i,j}|), different equations force different Ω and the continuation *fails*: destructive interference breaks.

This "iff" is the paper's main analytic result (Lemma, Sec. II).

**Linear stability analysis (Sec. III).** Add a small perturbation ε_n(t) to the compact breather C_{n,n0}(t) satisfying Eq. (13). Linearize around the breather to get Eq. (14). Project the perturbation onto Bloch states φ̂_q via a finite Fourier transform (Eq. 16), expand the resulting φ̂_q in the Bloch eigenbasis {w_q, v_q^i} into a flat-band component f_q and ν−1 dispersive components d_q^i. For g = 0 this closes into Eq. (19)–(20):
  - Class U = 1: flat-band f_q is *decoupled* from all d_q^i.
  - Class U ≥ 2: terms Γ_l v_l^i · v_q* couple f_q to the dispersive bands.

Floquet analysis at g = 0 gives degenerate eigenvalues on the unit circle; the resonance condition (23) is E_FB = mΩ and λ_q^j = mΩ. Applying the *strained coefficient method* (expand d_q and λ_q in powers of g, kill all secular terms order by order) yields first-order Arnold tongues around each dispersive frequency λ_q for m = 1 only (Coefficients Eq. 27 for U = 1, Eq. 29 for U = 2):

- U = 1, m = 1:  δ_1 = 3/ν · A² − 1/N.
- U = 1, m ≠ 1:  δ_1 = −2/ν.
- U = 2, sawtooth:  σ_1 = (−3, −1) for m = 1, σ_1 = −2 for m ≠ 1.

The tongue is the strip λ_q + g/N ≤ Ω ≤ λ_q + 3g/N (Eq. C8, Appendix C) — a Bogoliubov reduction of the 2×2 coupled system.

**Numerical verification (Sec. IV).**

1. **One-dimensionality.** "Results in general apply to higher dimensions" is asserted in one sentence (Sec. I) with no argument or counterexample; the Floquet and Arnold-tongue reductions are written for a single q.

## [1803.01959v1-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


## Understanding the paper's logical argument

**Setup and premise.** The paper considers a translationally invariant flat band network described (in 1D, in time-component form) by a linear Schrödinger equation with unit-cell hopping matrices H₀, H₁ (Eq. 1). Such networks host compact localized states (CLSs), classified by the occupation class U; these CLSs span a flat band E_FB that is independent of wavevector. The paper now adds a local (on-site) nonlinearity γ𝓕(ψₙ)ψₙ (Eq. 6) and asks the "natural question" (their words) whether spatially compact, time-periodic solutions — *compact discrete breathers* (CDBs) — can exist as nonlinear continuations of the linear CLSs.

**Core argument (Section II — continuation criterion).** The central move is a single, clean necessary-and-sufficient lemma. Take a candidate time-periodic orbit on the compact support of a CLS, with spatial profile fixed to the CLS but with an ansatz allowing site amplitudes A_lj. Plugging in, the nonlinear phase-amplitude ansatz ψ = Aₙe⁻ⁱᴱᵗ leads to an eigenvalue problem (Eq. 2), and for a solution to be a single orbit with a *single* frequency the resonance condition (Eq. 9), ΩA_ij = E_FB A_ij + γA_ij³, must hold on all occupied sites. This forces one of two alternatives:

1. **Homogeneous** — all occupied sites share the same |A|, in which case Eq. 10 holds with a single Ω = E_FB + γA²/… (and Eq. 12 gives the shifted frequency). **Lemma: a CLS continues to a CDB iff it is homogeneous.** This is the paper's cleanest and most useful result.
2. **Heterogeneous** — there exist sites with different |A_lj|, which forces a *different* frequency per site (Eq. 9), destroying the single-orbit, compact-support requirement.

This is a genuinely tight logical step: the homogeneity condition is both necessary and sufficient, and it cleanly separates the weakly-nonlinear continuation question from a property of the *linear* CLS profile. The paper then states (though proves it only by reference to [36]) that heterogeneous CLSs generically admit exponentially localized breathers instead.

**Stability argument (Section III).** For the continued homogeneous CDBs, the paper does a linear stability analysis. The perturbation εₙ (Eq. 13) of a CDB is transformed into Bloch form (Eq. 16–17), expanded in the Bloch eigenbasis separating the flat-band component f_q̃ from dispersive components d̲̃_q (Eq. 18), reducing to coupled equations (Eq. 19–20). **Crucial structural result:** for class U=1 (orthogonal CLSs) the flat and dispersive components **decouple** — so the only instability channel is resonance with *extended* (dispersive) states. For U≥2 they **couple**, and a *new, local* CLS–CLS interaction instability channel opens. The resonance/frequency-matching condition is read off via the Floquet matrix (period-advancing matrix, Eq. 21) plus a strained-coefficient expansion near the resonant frequency, yielding Arnold tongue widths (Eqs. 26–29); cross-check via Bogoliubov expansion (Appendix C) gives Eq. C8 consistent with the Arnold tongue.

**Numerical confirmation (Section IV).** Two test models: Cross-Stitch (U=1, orthogonal, flat band tunable via h) and Saw-Tooth (U=2, non-orthogonal, gapped from dispersive band). Predicted: for U=1, a CDB is stable when outside the resonance window with the dispersive band (Fig. 3a, low participation number) and unstable inside it (Fig. 3b, P~40, loss of compactness). For U=2, even though the flat band is gapped, a *new local* instability appears for -0.1<g<0 (Fig. 6 left), producing an exponentially localized unstable eigenmode (Fig. 5) without full destruction — oscillatory, not radiative. Numerics (participation number, eigenmode localization) match the analytical predictions and the decoupling/coupling structural claim.

III.C, App. B, Eq. 27) and a Bogoliubov expansion (App. C, Eq. C8) — and the two agree, including the non-trivial finding that only the m = 1 tongue survives and the polarized (wave-vector-dependent) terms generate no extra tongues. That internal cross-consistency is exactly the kind of fair, cherry-pick-free comparison a theory paper needs. The numerics use the same model equations as the theory, so theory and numerics are apples-to-apples.

**Are baselines current?** Only in the physics sense. There is no external/competitor baseline to beat — the numerical work (Floquet diagonalization via IMSL/DEIVCGRG + 6th-order Runge–Kutta, App. D) is the authors' own confirmation, not a comparison against another group's method or experiment. The relevant literature (discrete breathers, Page; compact solitons, Kevrekidis–Konotohop; flat-band CLS, 2013–2017 refs) is cited reasonably up to state of the art. No experimental (cold-atom) data is engaged in this paper, which the authors flag only as future work.

**Are error bars / statistical significance reported?** **No — this is the soft spot.**
- The participation-number "evidence" (P ≈ 1.5 vs. 40, Figs. 3, 6) comes from *single deterministic runs* with one fixed perturbation (A = 1, max 10⁻³). There is no ensemble (no multi-seed averaging, no mean ± std, no confidence bands). It is a plausible diagnostic but is presented as a single snapshot, not a measured quantity.
- The predicted full (Ω, g) phase diagram (Fig. 1 is a cartoon) is **never quantitatively reproduced**: there is no figure sweeping a computed Floquet eigenvalue across (Ω, g) to delineate the tongue and verify the 3g/N width. The numerics show only two discrete points per lattice (one labeled "stable," one "unstable"), so the specific quantitative content of Eq. C8 is asserted by derivation, not measured.
- The saw-tooth unstable eigenvalue EV = 2.987 × 10⁻⁵ is extremely small (weak nonlinearity g = −10⁻³), so the instability is marginal; the authors note oscillations only over t up to ~1000/2000. A marginal mode that has barely grown is a weak quantitative confirmation of onset, and its threshold would benefit from a scan, not a single g-value.
- The finite-size effect (width ∝ 1/N → 0 as N → ∞) is acknowledged analytically, which is good, but it means "stable" vs. "unstable" is size-dependent and the claim is really about N = 50 chains.

**Net:** The claim is theoretically well-grounded (two agreeing derivations + a clean necessary-and-sufficient existence lemma), but its *quantitative* numerical confirmation is thinner than the claim warrants — single-run, no error bars, no full tongue mapped. Fairness is fine; statistical rigor is absent (largely acceptable for a deterministic spectral claim, but the authors do not distinguish "linearly unstable" in a threshold sense from "I saw one grow").

## Verdict

Sound, useful to the flat-band/discrete-breather community, but not field-shaping.

This is the paper's anchor.
- **Reasonable depth in the stability analysis.** The Bloch decomposition, period-advancing/Floquet matrix, and strained-coefficient (Arnold-tongue) treatment for the $U=1$ case is standard but competently executed; the appendix is detailed and reproducible, and the Bogoliubov cross-check of the single tongue for $U=1$ is a nice touch.
- **Honest about limits of the analytic method.** The authors explicitly note that the strained-coefficient method does not rule out wave-polarized terms producing extra tongues, and they compensate with direct numerics — a fair division of labor between analysis and simulation.
- **Two physically distinct models** (tunable-crossing cross-stitch, and fixed gapped saw-tooth) are a sensible choice that isolates the difference between the $U=1$ and $U=2$ mechanisms.

### Weaknesses / Concerns

1. **The headline criterion is weaker than it is presented.** The lemma ("homogeneous CLS ⟺ contiguas as a periodic orbit with compact support") is derived by substituting the ansatz into Eq. (6) and matching terms that survive *at leading order* in the on-site nonlinearity (essentially the $O(\gamma A^2)$ frequency correction). This establishes the CDB is correct **to first order in weak nonlinearity** ($g=\gamma A^2\ll 1$), not that it is an exact breather. The paper repeatedly calls this a "necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of compact discrete breathers," which overstates it; a reader could easily take it as a theorem of exact existence. This should be qualified throughout ("to leading order / in the weakly nonlinear regime").

2. **Numerical validation is finite-size and qualitative.** The "stability analysis" in Sec. IV diagonalizes a finite-N ($2N$ or $4N$) eigenvalue problem for N=50 unit-cells and the time evolution is run on the same finite periodic chain. The breather coexists with its periodic image; whether the observed instabilities are truly the infinite-lattice mechanisms or partly finite-size artifacts is not discussed. A size-convergence (N-dependence) check, or an explicit statement of the periodic-boundary assumption, would substantially strengthen the conclusions. The participation number is a sensible compactness proxy and the two-plot-per-parameter presentation is clear, but two parameter points per model is thin evidence for the "only these instability windows exist" claim.

3. **Generality is narrower than the framework suggests.** The results are 1D, on-site nonlinearity only, weak nonlinearity only, and demonstrated on exactly two two-band topologies. The $U=2$ local-instability result is derived only for the specific saw-tooth chain; whether it persists for other $U\ge2$ topologies is asserted but not shown.

4. **The paper's main contributions have substantial precedent.** Compact breathers in nonlinear flat bands were already observed (Refs. 36,37), heterogeneous-CLS networks were noted to give localized gap modes (38), and non-orthogonal CLS networks (36) were known. The present paper is therefore a synthesis plus analytic organization rather than a new phenomenon; its novelty is the clean homogeneity criterion and the stability taxonomy, not a new object.

5. **Minor.** A few typographical issues (e.g., "contiguas"/"can be tunned"/spacing around Eq. (5)'s $R^{v}$); Fig. 3's participation-number panels (c,d) would benefit from the $P$ value being stated in the caption; and the relationship between the *exact* CDB (leading order) and the *numerically* simulated breather could be stated more explicitly.

### Assessment
This is a **well-executed, sound, and clearly written** paper in a specialized corner of nonlinear lattice physics. The homogeneity continuation criterion is genuinely useful and the stability taxonomy (Arnold-tongue/extended-state resonances for $U=1$ vs. localized CLS–CLS local instability for $U\ge2$) is coherent and corroborated. However, the work is **incremental rather than new**: the central objects and much of the mechanism were already in the literature, the headline criterion is really a leading-order (weak-nonlinearity) statement that is presented somewhat more strongly than its derivation warrants, and the numerical support, while adequate, is finite-size and limited to two models. It is a solid, publishable contribution for the flat band / discrete breather communities, but not one that changes the direction of the field.

**Recommendation:** Accept with minor revisions, contingent on (i) qualifying the existence lemma as a weak-nonlinearity/leading-order result, and (ii) a finite-size (N-convergence) check or explicit discussion of the periodic-boundary assumption in the stability numerics.

## Review: *Compact Discrete Breathers on Flat Band Networks* (Daniele, Maluckov, Fläch)

This is a competent, clearly written piece of work on a live question — whether compact localized states (CLS) on flat-band networks can be continued into *exact* time-periodic compact solutions (compact discrete breathers) once a local on-site nonlinearity is switched on, and whether those exact compact solutions are linearly stable. You set up the problem cleanly, reduce the stability question to a Floquet/Arnold-tongue analysis, and back the weakly-nonlinear analytical treatment with direct time-evolution numerics on two test-bed lattices. Below is what I think works and what you need to address before I'd be comfortable calling this done.

### What is solid

1. **The central criterion (the homogeneous-CLS lemma) is the real result and it's correct in spirit.** Because the nonlinearity is strictly local (`γ|ψ|²ψ`), a CLS whose support carries a *uniform* amplitude `A` is mapped to the same profile with a shifted on-site energy `γA²`, giving the exact compact breather at `Ω = E_FB + γA²`. The necessity direction — a *heterogeneous* CLS gets different local frequency shifts on different support sites, so no single-Floquet frequency works and the destructive interference is broken — is the right intuition and is correctly stated for on-site nonlinearity. That compactness is *exactly* preserved (no algebraic/exponential tails) is a genuine strength, and it lets you sidestep the whole finite-tail-decay machinery of the usual DB literature.

2. **The distinction between `U=1` and `U≥2` instabilities is physically well-motivated and clearly argued.** For an orthonormal basis (U=1) the only route to instability is parametric resonance with the dispersive band; for non-orthogonal U=2 CLSs the overlap of neighboring CLSs opens a *new, localized* instability channel. The saw-tooth example (Fig. 5 / Fig. 6) where the unstable eigenvector is exponentially localized and the excitation merely *oscillates* rather than radiating away is a genuinely nice observation because it is qualitatively different from the U=1 resonance case, and it's a result the analysis actually predicts.

3. **The perturbation/Floquet machinery is standard but competently handled**, and the appendices (Bloch representation, straining-coefficient method, Bogoliubov cross-check) give the derivation enough of a paper trail to be reproducible.

### What needs work

1. **The "if and only if" of the lemma is not actually proved in that strength.** You show sufficiency rigorously, and you have a convincing *argument* (not a proof) for necessity, built on the observation that non-equal `|A_ij|` produce non-equal site frequencies. But you have not excluded, for instance, a genuinely compact solution at a frequency *different* from `E_FB + γA²`, or compact solutions that are not at all small deformations of a CLS. As stated, "if and only if it is homogeneous" overclaims relative to what is demonstrated. Either tighten this to "a homogeneous CLS continues exactly as a compact breather; a heterogeneous one does not continue at the linear-limit frequency" — which is what you actually showed — or prove the exclusion. As a senior I will push back on the biconditional.

2. **The finite-N dependence of the tongue coefficients is underdeveloped.** Eq. (27)/(29) give `δ_1 = 3/γ − 1/N` (m=1) and `δ_1 = −1/N` (m≠1), and you note the `σ_1 = −2` branch is independent of N. Taken to the thermodynamic limit this means several dispersive-band tongues have *vanishing width* in the system of interest. You mention this once ("Eq. (29) is independent of N due to macroscopic degeneracy") but don't sit with the consequence: in the infinite chain, for `U=1` the *only* surviving instability tongues are the `m=1` ones, and the rest of the instability structure you display in Fig. 1 is a finite-size artifact. That should be stated up front, not buried, or the figures are somewhat misleading.

3. **No quantitative agreement is claimed between the analytical tongues and the numerics.** This is the biggest gap. You derive first-order Arnold tongues, and you run the time-evolution (Fig. 3, 6) — but the numerics only show *two point cases* (resonant vs. non-resonant) each on each lattice. You never plot the measured stability/unstability boundary in `(Ω, g)` against the width predicted by Eq. (27)/(28). That's precisely the validation a straining-coefficient approximation owes the reader. Add a stability map from the numerics and overlay the tongue boundaries; right now the analytical part and numerical part of Section III–IV are essentially two independent papers that you assert are consistent.

4. **Scope is narrow and you should signpost it.** One dimension, on-site `γ|ψ|²ψ` only, exactly two lattices, weakly nonlinear analysis plus a small number of strong-coupling spots. The claim that "additional fine-tuning can lead to compactification for a countable set of discrete breathers" (Conclusions) is asserted without any analysis. Either trim it or give one example.

5. **Minor.** The "polarized terms" (wave-number-dependent corrections in the U=2 case) are shown *not* to open new tongues — worth one sentence explaining why the physical content is that they are subcritical here. The figures are fine but Fig. 1 shows tongues only for m=1,2,3 while the text says m≥2 tongues persist — make the figure and text match.

### Summary

A sound, clear, and usefully bounded study: the homogeneity criterion and the separate U≥2 local-instability mechanism are the two results worth having, and the exact-compactness angle is a clean framing of the problem. But the headline "if and only if" is stronger than the proof, the finite-size structure of the Arnold tongues deserves to be foregrounded rather than footnoted, and — most importantly — the analytical stability prediction is never checked numerically against the tongue *boundaries*, which is the single most valuable figure this paper is missing. Fix those three and this is publishable; as it stands it's a good draft with a couple of claims it doesn't quite back up.

**Suggested scores:** originality 3 (correct and useful, but the framework is standard perturbation/Floquet theory applied to a well-trodden setting), overall 4 (sound, internally consistent, well documented, but with the overclaim and the missing numerics-vs-analysis validation holding it back from "excellent"), impact 2 (a solid contribution to an active but niche field; no indication of broader reach).
