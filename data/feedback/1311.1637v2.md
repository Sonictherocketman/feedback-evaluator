## [1311.1637v2-general-review] General Review


I read the full 22-page paper (Hindmarsh, Kirk, West, *Dark Matter from Decaying Topological Defects*, arXiv:1311.1637v2, JCAP 2014). Below is my general review of its argumentation.

# Overview of the paper's argument

The paper sets up a "top-down" (TD) production channel for WIMP-like dark matter χ: a decaying topological-defect sector (cosmic strings) injects an intermediate state X (E_X ~ 1 TeV) into the plasma at a rate Q(t) ∝ t^{p−4}; X then promptly decays to χ. They solve the Boltzmann equation with this source both numerically and — their main technical contribution — analytically (a Riccati-equation yield, closed form (5.2) in Bessel functions for p < 3/2, with a simple large-AB limit (5.5)). They benchmark the analytic against the numerical solution, then map constraints (Planck relic density, the Griest–Kamionkowski unitarity bound, an illustrative Fermi-LAT dSph γ-ray limit) in the (q_X, σ0) plane, and translate the resulting q_X bounds into string-tension bounds Gµ for two string scenarios: FT (p = 1, simulation-motivated, f_X ≈ 1) and CE (p = 7/6, cusp emission). The punchline: for χ at 500 GeV with s- or p-wave annihilation, the Gµ bounds land well below the then-current CMB bounds, and the source allows annihilation cross sections above the thermal value — a "boost factor" for indirect detection.

On the technical core I am broadly reassured rather than alarmed: the yield equation (3.2) is the standard transformation (I checked the source scaling x^{−(4−2p)} against dx/dt = −Hx, s ∝ t^{−3/2}, and it is consistent; the FT/CE power indices p = 1, 7/6, 1/2 follow from the appendix derivations), the large-AB fixed point Y∞ ∝ B/A for (n,p)=(0,1) matches the naive balance of the two terms, and the B → 0 limit correctly reproduces standard freeze-out. The analytic–numerical agreement in Fig. 3 looks strong.

# Main issues with the argument

**1. The headline is conditional on an assumption the paper explicitly sets aside.** The bounds "significantly more stringent than the CMB limits" depend on E_X ~ 1 TeV, i.e., r_χ = m_χ/E_X ~ 0.5. Section 2 introduces the alternative — few X states with E_X ~ m_X ~ 10¹⁵ GeV as the generic expectation for defect decay — and then abandons it. Since every bound scales as Gµ_max ∝ 1/r_χ ∝ E_X/m_χ, at E_X ~ v_d the constraint simply evaporates (which the authors note). A reader of the abstract could easily miss this: the real claim is "cosmic strings whose decay product spectrum is dominated by sub-GeV-to-TeV states (requiring a condensate, a flat direction, or light scalar modes) are constrained to Gµ ≪ 10⁻⁷." That is a much more specific statement than "topological defects can be the principal source of dark matter," and the abstract and conclusions should carry that caveat more prominently. Framed this way, the result is a useful corner of parameter space, not a generic defect constraint.

**2. The kinetic-equilibrium assumption is doing more work than the paper advertises.** They assume injected (non-thermal) χ rapidly enters kinetic equilibrium because the scattering rate exceeds H, and that this holds "unless the scattering cross section is orders of magnitude smaller than the annihilation cross section." But the interesting region — large σ0, large boost — is precisely where annihilation is fastest, so the required inequality scattering ≫ annihilation is hardest to guarantee, and the scattering to the thermal bath typically proceeds through the same mediator as the annihilation, so there is no model-independent reason it should be larger. In that regime χ is effectively in number equilibrium for x < x_s and the simple "add the source to the RHS" Boltzmann equation is not the right description (the population sits at an effective quasi-equilibrium yield set by source/refill balance). The assumption set {prompt X decay, fast χ equilibration, annihilation from Y² rather than from Y·Y_eq with Y > Y_eq} is self-consistent but not demonstrated. At minimum this needs a paragraph bounding the range of σ0 (and scattering cross section) over which the whole picture holds.

**3. The power-law source is stretched beyond its domain of validity.** (a) The matter-era change of Q's time dependence (Q ∝ ρH ∝ t^{−3} in the radiation era becomes t^{−7/2} in the matter era) is never discussed, yet all integrals run to x = ∞ using the radiation-era form. The error is small for p = 1 ∫x^{−2} dx from x_eq is ~ 10⁻⁹, but the stated justification ("setting the upper value of x to ∞ is an excellent approximation") deserves that one-line quantification. (b) For the CE scenario, the p = 7/6 law is valid only for t < t_ce, and with the reference temperature T_χ = 500 GeV sitting almost exactly at T_ce ≃ 600 GeV (τ_κ ≃ 1.4, right at the crossover in C(τ)), the piecewise O(1) factor C(1.4) is genuinely uncertain and is silently absorbed into P_CE = νβ_ce f_X. The entire CE bound therefore carries an honest factor of a few to ten that is never quoted as such.

**4. The unitarity bound is being applied slightly outside its derivation.** Eq. (6.1) comes from Griest–Kamionkowski under the assumption ⟨σv⟩ is roughly constant over freeze-out; for p-wave (n = 1) ⟨σv⟩ = σ0/x varies by a factor ~x_d across freeze-out. The authors do keep the x_d factor in (B.3), so the result is probably conservative, but the bound should be characterized as heuristic rather than "model-independent … a formulation derived in [31]."

**5. The boost-factor story is under-constrained by the single most relevant existing data set: direct detection.** For the (0,1)/(0,7/6) cases, a boost of "nearly two orders of magnitude" requires σ0 ~ 10⁻²³–10⁻²¹ cm³ s⁻¹ — for any standard WIMP coupling this already implies spin-independent direct-detection cross sections at or beyond XENON100/LUX 2013 bounds for 500 GeV. The paper defers direct-detection and collider cross-correlation to "future work," but the boost claim is only credible for direct-detection-suppressed models (p-wave/velocity-suppressed scattering, heavy mediators, coannihilating scenarios). One paragraph flagging this — or one worked example with a direct-detection constraint applied in situ — would substantially strengthen the argument. Similarly, skipping a quantitative p-wave indirect treatment (velocity-averaged ⟨σv⟩_now in dSphs) leaves the p-wave cases, where indirect limits are weakest and the boost idea is most attractive, essentially unconstrained except by the unitarity line.

**6. Omitted small checks that a referee would likely request:** a remark on whether the total string-decay energy injection into SM states before BBN is negligible (it appears to be, given Ω_d ~ Gµ/(ξ²H²), but it deserves a sentence); and a comment on the velocity distribution of today's χ (produced non-thermally at T ~ O(10–100 GeV), their assumed equilibration makes the present-day distribution effectively thermal — but this is part of assumption 2 and should be stated explicitly, since p-wave rates at z = 0 then scale with ~200 km/s rather than dSph-minimal velocities).

# Minor issues and presentation

- Notation overload: β is used both as the loop shrinkage constant in Appendix A (β ~ ΓGµ, β_ce) and as β = 3−2p in the analytic solution; α, β, A, B, n, p, q_X, r_χ, P_FT, P_CE is a heavy load for a 13-page body.
- Table 1 caption uses "limits in Table 1 …" correctly, but the paragraph introducing Table 2 says "the values in Table 1 can again be multiplied by (0.5/r_χ)" — should read Table 2.
- Figure 3's caption gives "x_χ = 1" as if it were a fitted parameter and refers to the analytic curve as "(5.1)" rather than "(5.2)".
- Typos: "which will be chose to be," "according the the Nambu-Goto," "blue dashed line repents the full analytic solution."
- The discussion that the freeze-out-only (q_X = 0) vertical asymptote and the source-dominated straight-line part of the Fig. 2 contours is nicely done; the one-paragraph caveat that σ0 below the standard freeze-out value is simply excluded (source overshoots) would help readers orient in Fig. 2, since the formulae in Table 1 are only valid above the freeze-out σ0 values.
- Comparisons to the freeze-in literature (Hall, Jedamzik, March-Russell & West, whom the authors cite) would contextualize the TD source nicely — the power-law injection is kinematically analogous, and stating how the p = 1 case differs from standard freeze-in would sharpen the novelty claim.

# Verdict

The paper does what it says it does cleanly: a well-posed parameterization, a genuinely useful closed-form yield solution benchmarked against numerics, and a coherent chain from q_X to Gµ. Its weaknesses are mostly at the level of *framing* (the strong, stated, but load-bearing E_X ~ TeV assumption; the equilibrium assumption chain) and of *incompleteness* (no direct-detection or p-wave-indirect cross-check, both deferred), rather than internal inconsistency. The central "boost factor from defect-produced DM" idea is sound in principle but, given the then-current and now vastly stronger direct-detection limits, mostly historical. As a review: accept-with-moderate-revision profile, solidly above the floor, not a field-defining work. The analytic treatment and the parametric map would remain a reasonable citation point for defect-DM phenomenology, which is where its real value lies.

## Review: "Dark Matter from Decaying Topological Defects" (Hindmarsh, Kirk, West; JCAP 2014 / arXiv:1311.1637v2)

This is a clean, well-executed phenomenology paper. I've read all 22 pages, including the two appendices. Here is my honest assessment as a peer.

### What it does
The paper treats dark matter production from a power-law energy-injection source (decaying cosmic strings, in two concrete scenarios: field-theory "FT" with p=1 and cusp-emission "CE" with p=7/6). The source is folded into the Boltzmann equation for the DM yield (Eq. 3.2), solved numerically, and — the real gem — solved analytically (Eq. 5.2) with a closed form in Bessel functions valid for p < 3/2. They then constrain the injection strength q_X against (i) the Planck relic density, (ii) the model-independent unitarity bound on ⟨σv⟩, and (iii) a Fermi-LAT dwarf-spheroidal γ-ray limit, and translate everything into a bound on the string tension Gμ.

### What works well
- **The analytic yield (5.2)/(5.5) is the paper's strongest contribution.** A closed-form formula for a freeze-out yield with a time-dependent source is a genuinely reusable piece of machinery for the topological-defect / top-down DM community, and the asymptotic limit (5.5) is elegant. The large-AB, small-B reductions are well-motivated and reduce correctly to standard freeze-out, so it self-checked.
- **The model-independent unitarity → q_X bound is neat.** Deriving an upper limit on the source strength that does not depend on the specific DM model (only on m_χ and s/p-wave) is a legitimately useful, general result, and the fact that it's *tightest* for the p=7/6 / p-wave case is an interesting inversion.
- **Clean comparison of numerical vs. analytic vs. asymptotic solutions** (Fig. 3), a well-chosen 4-panel parameter study with explicit (n,p) labels, and self-contained appendices that actually derive q_X for both string scenarios rather than assuming them. The friction-dominated-era caveat (A.3, Gμ ≲ 10⁻¹⁹) is a good sanity check to include.
- Good framing of *why* this matters: the 1:1 freeze-out-to-today cross-section correspondence (with its tension against indirect searches) is exactly the motivation, and the "boost factor" angle is clearly stated.

### Where I'm less impressed / concerns
1. **Originality is moderate, not high.** At its core this is the standard freeze-in / source-in-Boltzmann formalism [ref 17, Kolb-Turner style] applied to a specific (interesting) source. The "source lets you have a larger than freeze-out annihilation rate → boost factor" idea has precedent (Griest-Kapling, Sommerfeld). The novelty is essentially a formula plus a model-independent q_X bound, not a new phenomenon.
2. **The phenomenology (Section 6) is explicitly illustrative and soft.** The model-dependent Fermi-LAT limit is for *one* toy model (annihilation to W-bosons, s-wave only, with the velocity-dependence of the p-wave case waved away as out of scope); the authors themselves flag this as "beyond the scope of the present work." So the claim that "search experiments can be much more constraining than the unitarity limit" is left as potential rather than demonstrated. The ~2-orders-of-magnitude boost-factor headroom claim is therefore more suggestive than a firm result.
3. **Several physically important assumptions are simplifications** that aren't stress-tested: X decay treated as instantaneous relative to DM annihilation, DM forced to kinetic equilibrium, a single reference temperature T_X, and a phenomenological piecewise form for C(τ) in the CE scenario. None is unreasonable, but the parameter-space conclusions (Tables 1–2) inherit these.
4. **Expected-value outcome.** The finding that Gμ bounds sit well below the CMB/Planck limits (Gμ ≲ 10⁻⁷) is the *a priori* obvious result — CMB is already the strongest constraint on string tension — so that headline is not surprising.
5. Minor: the Bessel-function argument expansion (≈2) that justifies (5.6) is stated but the condition (5.6) is only argued to be "reliable ... for all scenarios considered in this work" without a figure quantifying the margin; the fit constant c in x_d (5.4) is fudged (c+2 = n+1) rather than tabulated.

### Bottom line
Solid, internally consistent, and useful. The analytic solution and the model-independent q_X bound are real (if modest) advances for a specific corner of cosmology, and the paper is carefully written and self-contained. But the central idea is an incremental generalization of known freeze-in physics, the model-dependent constraints are illustrative rather than conclusive, and the main phenomenological headline is unsurprising. I see this as good workshop/conference-grade phenomenology that provides a handy tool rather than a discovery.

## Summary of the paper
The paper treats "top-down" (TD) dark matter: a time-dependent source of DM states in the Boltzmann equation, $Q(t)=Q_X(t/t_X)^{p-4}$, for $p\in\{1,7/6\}$ (field-theory and cusp-emission cosmic-string scenarios). ## Strengths
- **Clean formulation.** The reduction to a Riccati-type ODE (3.1)→(3.2) with the $A x^{-(n+2)}Y^2$ annihilation and $B x^{-(4-2p)}$ source prefactors is done carefully, and the power-law $x$-dependence ($n+2$ vs $4-2p$) drives all the qualitative behaviour — a nice organising insight.
- **Genuine analytic contribution.** The Bessel-function closed form for the general power law $p$ (5.2), the large-$AB$ defect-dominated limit (5.5) — and the clean observation that this limit is *independent of the freeze-out point $x_d$* — is the paper's most useful, reusable result.
- **Good self-checking.** Fig 3 verifies full numeric vs full analytic vs large-$AB$ approximation "excellent," which de-risks the analytic approximations (dropping $Y_{\rm eq}$, the tuned constant $c$).

- **Timely framing.** Connecting TD to indirect-detection "boost factors" (decoupling the present $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ from the freeze-out requirement, capped by unitarity) was a relevant 2013–14 question.

- **Imprecise headline claim.** The conclusions call the unitarity value $q_X<6.1\times10^{-10}$ (1,7/6) "the tightest model-independent constraint." The unitarity value is a hard, $\sigma_0$-independent *ceiling*; the DM-density bound is more restrictive at small $\sigma_0$. The two cross over, so "the tightest" as a single number is an overclaim. The tables contain the information, but the prose overstates.
- **Overstated $G\mu$ comparison.** The abstract's "significantly more stringent than the current CMB limits" is apples-to-oranges: the CMB bound $G\mu\lesssim1.3\times10^{-7}$ applies to *any* string network, while the $G\mu\sim10^{-14}$–$10^{-15}$ bounds assume the string produces (nearly) all the DM with $X$ at $\sim$TeV scale, $N_\chi=1$, and specific $p$. A technically sound, well-referenced, numerically cross-checked technical paper whose main value is a clean analytic yield formula for general $p$ and a model-independent unitarity bound on the injection rate. It is held back from top marks by imprecise framing of its flagship constraint, an overstated $G\mu$ comparison, and the fact that its most striking numbers are conditional on strong assumptions. A solid, publishable niche contribution rather than a field-defining one.

## [1311.1637v2-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


I've read the full paper (22 pages, Hindmarsh, Kirk, West, *Dark Matter from Decaying Topological Defects*, arXiv:1311.1637v2, 2014). Below is my evaluation.

---

## 1. Stated contribution (in my own words)

The paper studies a **"top-down" (TD)** route to dark matter: a cosmic string (more generally a topological defect) decays into a new sector of massive "X" states, each of which yields on average $N_X$ dark-matter particles. Because the energy the string dumps into the universe follows a power law $Q_X \propto t^{\,p-4}$, the TD mechanism adds a time-dependent *source term* to the standard WIMP Boltzmann equation. The usual freeze-out calculation then ties today's annihilation rate $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ almost one-to-one to the measured relic density — but the paper's starting point is that this link can be broken, letting the present-day annihilation rate differ from the standard freeze-out prediction.

Concretely the authors:

1. **Derive a closed-form analytic solution** (Eq. 5.2, a ratio of Bessel functions, plus the simple large-$AB$ limit, Eq. 5.5) to the source-laden Boltzmann equation for all $p<3/2$, and show it reproduces the numerical solution.
2. **Specialise to two string models**: *FT* (from direct field-theory simulation, $p=1$, $f_X\sim1$, strings decay almost entirely into X) and *CE* (cusp emission, $p=7/6$, small $f_X$), for both s-wave ($n=0$) and p-wave ($n=1$) annihilation — four cases.
3. **Map the $(q_X,\sigma_0)$ plane** where the TD model matches the Planck relic density even when standard freeze-out under-produces dark matter, and argue this can act as a "boost factor" for indirect detection (potentially explaining the AMS positron excess).
4. **Use the Gries–Kamionkowski unitarity bound** on $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ to obtain a *model-independent* upper bound on $q_X$, then translate that into a bound on the string tension $G\mu$ and illustrate with Fermi-LAT dwarf-spheroidal limits.

## 2. Judgment of novelty vs. the cited prior work

**Genuinely new:**

- **The closed-form analytic yield** (Eq. 5.2) for a power-law-sourced Boltzmann equation with $p<3/2$, and its simple large-source limit (Eq. 5.5). This is the paper's technical core, and it is a real new result — the only analytic solution in the literature that the authors cite ([30], Kolb & Turner) is the *standard* $B\to0$ case. The validity criterion (Eq. 5.6) and the numeric/analytic agreement check are well done.
- **The unitarity → $q_X$ → $G\mu$ chain.** Applying the Gries–Kamionkowski unitarity limit ([31]) to a *source-modified* freeze-out to obtain a model-independent upper bound on the TD injection parameter, and translating it into a bound on $G\mu$, is a new derivation, not previously in the TD literature.
- **The concrete $(n,p)=(0,1),(1,1)$ "FT" and $(0,7/6),(1,7/6)$ "CE" realisations, together with the $q_X\simeq f_X(G\mu/10^{-7})\cdots$ formulas** in Appendix A that turn the abstract TD picture into actual string-tension bounds. These are new, explicit computations.
- **Physical framing as a source of large indirect-detection boost factors** ($\lesssim2$ orders, capped by unitarity) for the AMS positron anomaly.

**Not new / incremental:**

- **The whole TD → X → DM setup is inherited.** The authors are explicit: the power-law source formalism comes from Bhattacherjee & Sigl ([17], 2000) and the neutrino-from-string-decay idea from Jean, Zhang & Brandenberger ([16], 1999). The conceptual move "defect decay is an alternative production channel that breaks the freeze-out–annihilation correspondence" already exists in [16,17], and the "break the one-to-one link" argument is a known theme (Griest & Seckel's "three exceptions," [5]).
- **The specific power-law exponents $p=1$ and $p=7/6$ are not the authors' ideas.** They are quoted directly from the string particle-emission literature ($p=1$ from the AB-string simulations [18,19,20]; $p=7/6$ from the cusp/condensate emission work [26,27,28]). So the physical input to their two scenarios is borrowed; they add the DM-analysis layer on top.
- **The "boost factor" concept is a pre-existing idea** (Sommerfeld-enhancement, substructure, and non-thermal DM all supply boosts; the authors cite March-Russell & West [29]). Here it is applied to the TD case rather than invented, and the AMS claim is left deliberately underspecified (the full model-dependent analysis is deferred to future work), so it is more a suggestive application than a result.
- **The unitarity limit and the $G\mu$-from-CMB limit are standard ingredients** ([31] and Planck [23], respectively).

## 3. Where the claims under-deliver

- **The $G\mu$ "bounds" are *weaker* than the existing CMB bounds.** The CMB already gives $G\mu\lesssim10^{-7}$; the authors state their TD-derived upper bounds ($3.2\times10^{-7}$ FT, $1.3\times10^{-7}$ CE) sit "well below the GUT/inflation scale" — i.e. they are order-of-magnitude consistent with, not tighter than, the CMB limit they cite. So the TD route does not actually *constrain* the string tension better than CMB here. This is a real but modest point in a paper that leans on $G\mu$ as a headline output.
- **The boost-factors/AMS claim is speculative by the authors' own admission.** They note the indirect limits are highly model-dependent and defer the concrete analysis, so the "explain the AMS anomaly" motivation is gestured at rather than delivered.
- **The "standard freeze-out under-produces" framing** is only as interesting as the specific models examined; the paper is honest that it is a generic framework, but a reader wanting a specific working model that simultaneously (a) gives the right abundance and (b) passes Fermi-LAT is pointed to "future work."

## 4. Overall verdict

This is a **solid, well-executed, technically clean** contribution to a narrow subfield (defect dark matter / cosmic strings). The analytic solution and the unitarity→$G\mu$ derivation are legitimately new tools, the writing is clear, and the numerics are checked against the analytics. But it is firmly an **application and formalization of a 1999–2000 lineage**; the physical scenario, the power-law exponents, the boost-factors motivation, and the key constraints ($G\mu$, unitarity) are all inherited or standard, and its most headline-number (the $G\mu$ bound) does not beat the CMB. It is a useful reference/tool for the subfield rather than a paradigm shift.

# Evaluation — "Dark Matter from Decaying Topological Defects" (Hindmarsh, Kirk, West; arXiv:1311.1637)

## 1. Methodology in reproduce‑the‑experiment detail

This is not a laboratory experiment but a **relic‑abundance calculation**: integrate a Boltzmann equation for the dark matter (DM) number density that carries a time‑dependent *source term* from decaying cosmic strings, compare the final yield to the Planck value, and turn the allowed source strength into a bound on the string tension $G\mu$. To reproduce the central result you need the following chain.

**Physical setup (Sec. 2–3).**
- Cosmic strings form at a phase transition (scale $v_d > m_\chi$) and decay into a new sector of particles $X$ (mass $m_X$, average energy $\bar E_X$), which decay into DM $\chi$.
- Total string energy‑injection rate: $Q = 3(w - w_d)\Omega_d \rho H$ (Eq. 2.1), with $\Omega_d = \rho_d/\rho$, $w_d$ the string equation‑of‑state.
- A fraction $f_X$ of the string energy goes into $X$; each $X$ makes on average $N_\chi$ DM particles. DM number‑injection rate: $j_\chi^{\rm inj} = f_X N_\chi Q/\bar E_X$ (Eq. 2.2).
- The TD source is idealised as a power law in radiation‑era time: $Q(t) = Q_X (t/t_X)^{p-4}$ (Eq. 2.3), with $t_X$ the time when $T = m_\chi$ ($x\equiv m_\chi/T = 1$) and $p$ the power‑law index.
- Two string scenarios give $p$:
  - **FT** (field‑theory, Abelian Higgs, from simulations): strings decay almost entirely into $X$, $f_X\sim1$, **$p=1$**.
  - **CE** (cusp emission): $f_X$ small, main decay channel gravitational radiation from long‑lived loops, particle emission from cusps, **$p=7/6$** (loop‑collapse gives a subdominant $p=-1/2$ term).
- Two $X$ regimes are *described* (a few $10^{15}$ GeV states — dropped; many low‑mass states with $\bar E_X\sim 10$ GeV, one DM per $X$) but the **numerics actually use the default $\bar E_X = 10^3$ GeV** (see assumption list below).

**Master equation and parameters (Eq. 3.1–3.4).**
- Boltzmann equation $n_χ + 3H n_χ = -\langleσv\rangle(n_χ^2 - n_{χ,eq}^2) + N_χ f_X Q(t)/\bar E_X$.
- Change to $x=m_χ/T$, assume $\langleσv\rangle = σ_0 x^{-n}$ ($n=0$ s‑wave, $n=1$ p‑wave), rewrite in yield $Y_χ = n_χ/s$:
$$\frac{dY_χ}{dx} = -\frac{A}{x^{\,n+2}}\bigl(Y_χ^2 - Y_{χ,eq}^2\bigr) + \frac{B}{x^{\,4-2p}}\quad (3.2)$$
with $A=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45\sqrt{g_*}}\,M_{\rm Pl} m_χ σ_0}$ and $B = \frac34\frac{N_χ m_χ}{\bar E_X}\frac{Q_X f_X}{ρ_X H_X}$ (Eq. 3.3). The free **injection parameter** is $q_X \equiv Q_X f_X/(ρ_X H_X)$ and the **multiplicity parameter** $r_χ \equiv N_χ m_χ/\bar E_X$ (Eq. 3.5).

**Numerical solve (Sec. 4) — the "experiment" inputs to fix.**
- $m_χ = 500$ GeV, $\bar E_X = 10^3$ GeV (so $r_χ=0.5$), $g_* = 100$.
- Four channels: $(n,p) = (0,1), (1,1), (0,7/6), (1,7/6)$ — i.e. s/p‑wave × FT/CE.
- $σ_0 = 1.6\times10^{-26}$ cm³/s for $n=0$; $σ_0 = 7\times10^{-25}$ cm³/s for $n=1$ (each chosen to give the Planck yield in standard freeze‑out).
- Sweep $q_X = 0,10^{-9},10^{-8},10^{-7}$; integrate (3.2) from large $x$ to $x\to\infty$ (numerically cut off at $x=30$).
- Read out: yield vs $x$ (Fig. 1), and contours of constant Planck yield in the $(q_X,σ_0)$ plane (Fig. 2). Physical content: as $n+2$ vs $4-2p$ change, the source controls the post‑freeze‑out yield; the yield *rises* with $q_X$, and for $n+2>4-2p$ the source dominates the late tail.

**Analytic handle (Sec. 5).**
- For $x\gg x_d$, $Y_{χ,eq}\approx0$; (3.2) is a Riccati equation whose closed form (5.2) (in Bessel functions, $α=n+1$, $β=3-2p$) is valid for $p<3/2$.
- Detach point $x_d$ found recursively (5.3) and reduced to one iteration (5.4), with the constant fixed by $c(c+2)=n+1$.
- Two useful limits: (i) large $AB$ gives the compact formula (5.5), $Y_χ(\infty)\propto B^{β-α}/A^{α+β}$; (ii) $B\to0$ recovers standard freeze‑out. Condition (5.6), $q_X σ_0 \gtrsim 10^{-38}x_d^{α+β} r_χ^3(500\,{\rm GeV}/m_χ)(0.5/r_X)$, certifies regime (i). Fig. 3 demonstrates numerical = full‑analytic ≈ large‑$AB$ (agreement is excellent).
- **This (5.5) is what you use to invert back to the limits.**

**Constraints (Sec. 6 + Apps. A, B) — the headline result.**
- **Model‑independent:** apply the Griest–Kamionkowski unitarity bound $\langleσv\rangle\le 4(2n+1)\sqrt{π x_d}/m_χ^2$ (Eq. 6.1, assuming $σ$ roughly constant through freeze‑out) together with the Planck yield through (5.5), giving an analytic upper bound on $q_X$ (Appendix B, Eqs. B.1–B.3). This is the paper's central new tool.
- **Model‑dependent example:** Fermi‑LAT 4‑yr Pass‑7 dwarf‑spheroidal $\gamma$ limits, applied **only to the s‑wave** cases and **only assuming DM dominantly annihilates to $W^+W^-$** (p‑wave excluded because $\langleσv\rangle$ is velocity‑dependent in dSphs).
- **Translation to $G\mu$:** via $q_X\propto P_{\rm FT}G\mu$ (Appendix A.2, Eq. A.11/A.12, FT) and $q_X\propto P_{\rm CE}G\mu$ (Appendix A.3, Eq. A.19–A.21, CE), where $P_{\rm FT},P_{\rm CE}$ are $\mathcal O(1)$ parameter combinations. This yields Table 2.

**Headline numbers.** For $m_\chi=500$ GeV, $r_χ=0.5$: the tightest model‑independent (unitarity) bound is on CE p‑wave, $q_X\lesssim 6.1\times10^{-10}$, and all four $G\mu$ bounds sit in $10^{-15}\text{–}10^{-10}$, i.e. **well below the GUT/inflation scale and below the Planck CMB cosmic‑string bound ($G\mu\lesssim1.3\times10^{-7}$ FT, $3.2\times10^{-7}$ CE)** — the claimed result. The secondary claim: because the source can raise the *current* allowed annihilation rate above the freeze‑out value while preserving the correct relic density, it can act as a "boost factor" for indirect detection, capped by unitarity at $\sim$ two orders of magnitude for $(0,1)$ and $(0,7/6)$.

Concretely, to reproduce you: (1) fix the four $(n,p)$ channels and the $σ_0,m_χ,\bar E_X,g_*$, $r_χ=0.5$ values above; (2) integrate (3.2) numerically in $x$ over $q_X\in[0,10^{-7}]$; (3) sweep $σ_0$ for fixed $q_X$ (or vice‑versa) to build the Planck‑yield contours of Fig. 2; (4) check against (5.2)/(5.5) using (5.4) for $x_d$; (5) overlay the analytical unitarity line (B.3) and the Fermi‑LAT line; (6) rescale $q_X\to G\mu$ via A.12/A.23 to read Table 2. Note the **solver, initialisation, and code are not released** in the paper — you must supply your own integrator, and the $c$ value/one‑iteration $x_d$ are the only analytic calibrations given.

## 2. Assumptions the methodology papers over

**On the microphysics of production/decay:**
1. **$X\to\chi$ is instantaneous** relative to the annihilation timescale.
2. **DM rapidly reaches kinetic equilibrium** with the thermal bath (assumed because the DM is "highly non‑thermal" on production but scattering off the bath $\gg H$ at $T\sim20$ GeV); this is asserted, not checked across the full parameter space.
3. **$N_χ=1$** is "conservative" (the stabilising symmetry would actually make it *even* $\ge2$) — so the real multiplicity could double the yield.
4. **$\bar E_X = 10^3$ GeV is used in all numerics**, yet the *motivating* "many low‑mass states" scenario in the text is $\bar E_X\sim10$ GeV — a factor $\sim100$ mismatch between the scenario the paper sets up and the one it actually integrates. The alternative $10^{15}$ GeV case is dropped as "model dependent."
5. **The energy injection is a pure power law** with a *single* constant $p$ ($p=1$ or $7/6$). The CE scenario really has $p=7/6$ only for $t<t_{ce}$ and $p=1/2$ for $t>t_{ce}$ (Appendix A.3); this piecewise structure is absorbed into the single $p$ idealisation.
6. **FT: $f_X\sim1$** (strings decay *entirely* into $X$); **CE: "small" $f_X$** — the branching fractions are never varied or given numerically.

**On the cosmology/thermodynamics:**
7. **Radiation domination throughout** ($r_X$ treated as $\sim$ constant; the string energy is assumed subdominant).
8. **A single constant $g_* = 100$** over the whole integration, whereas the true $g_*(500\ {\rm GeV})\approx79$ and $\sim10$–20 by $T\sim20$ GeV; entropy release across thresholds is ignored.
9. **Target = Planck‑2013 $\Omega_{\rm DM}h^2=0.1186\pm0.0031$**; all constraints anchored to this one value.

**On the annihilation model:**
10. **$\langleσv\rangle = σ_0 x^{-n}$ with only $n=0$ (s) or $n=1$ (p)** — no resonant, near‑threshold, or co‑annihilation channels.
11. **The unitarity bound assumes $σ$ is roughly constant through freeze‑out** (Griest–Kamionkowski); the paper itself concedes the bound line is "not a perfectly vertical" and thus only an approximate constraint on $σ_0$.
12. **Fermi‑LAT applied only to s‑wave with DM$\to W^+W^-$**, and **not at all to p‑wave** — so the indirect‑detection / boost‑factor discussion is effectively carried by the unitarity bound alone.

**On the analytic solution:**
13. **$p<3/2$ required** (else $Y_χ\to\infty$ and (5.2) is invalid); models with $p>3/2$ are dismissed as unknown.
14. **Boundary condition $\sqrt{A/B}\,Y_{χ,eq}(x_d)^{(β-α)/2}\gg1$ and one‑iteration $x_d$** with $c$ fixed by $c(c+2)=n+1$ rather than fit to the numerics.
15. **Numerical integration cut off at $x=30$** while the analytic result evaluates $Y_χ(\infty)$.

**On the final $Gμ$ translation:**
16. **No concrete UV‑complete model** — $G\mu$ bounds depend on unfixed $\mathcal O(1)$ parameters $P_{\rm FT},P_{\rm CE}$ (and on $\xi/H$, $\beta_Γ$, $\Gamma$, $\nu$ left symbolic), so the "$G\mu\sim10^{-7}$ below CMB scale" headline is only as strong as these unquantified coefficients.
17. **No uncertainty/error bars are quoted** on any derived limit (from $c$, $\beta_Γ$, $g_*$, $N_χ$, or the $\mathcal O(1)$ parameters), and everything is quoted at a **single DM mass (500 GeV)**; the paper gives but does not use scaling factors for other $m_χ$.
18. Friction domination at $T\sim m_X$ is checked only for $m_χ=500$ GeV and a narrow $G\mu$ window before freeze‑out.

## 3. Overall assessment

This is a competent, well‑structured piece of TD phenomenology: the sourced‑Boltzmann → Riccati → Bessel‑closed‑form pipeline (5.2) is a genuinely reusable tool, and it is cross‑checked three ways (numeric vs full analytic vs large‑$AB$ in Fig. 3; $B\to0$ recovering standard freeze‑out; the independent unitarity bound as a model‑independent cross‑check). The main claims — the clean analytic yield for $p<3/2$, the unitarity‑derived $q_X$ bound, $G\mu$ limits all below the GUT/CMB scale, and the possibility of a TD‑induced "boost factor" — are all internally consistent and defensible *within the stated assumptions*.

The limitations are scope‑driven rather than fatal: it is a parameter study, not a model. The $\bar E_X$ inconsistency (Sec. 2 says $\sim10$ GeV, numerics use $10^3$ GeV), the loose (self‑acknowledged) unitarity bound, the omission of p‑wave from the indirect‑detection part, the absence of a concrete UV model / $\mathcal O(1)$ $G\mu$ coefficients, the constant $g_*=100$, a single $500$ GeV mass point, and the lack of any error bars all keep this from being "excellent." Nothing is unsound; the result is solid but modest and incremental on top of the well‑explored freeze‑in / sourced‑Boltzmann literature, with the boost‑factor motivation not being new. No code/data is released, so independent reproduction rests on the reader's own solver.

**Scores** reflect: solid, sound and cross‑checked but incremental in a mature field (moderate originality), good overall quality with meaningful scope/caveat limitations (overall 3), and useful‑but‑not‑field‑shaping impact (impact 2).

## [1311.1637v2-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


# Critique: "Dark Matter from Decaying Topological Defects"
**Hindmarsh, Kirk, West — JCAP 2014 (arXiv:1311.1637v2)**

---

## Executive Summary

This paper presents a systematic treatment of dark matter (DM) production from decaying cosmic strings and related topological defects (TDs). The central result is a closed-form analytic expression for the DM yield when the defect source dominates in the Boltzmann equation (valid for p < 3/2), leading to model-independent bounds on the X-particle injection parameter q_X from the Planck relic abundance and the partial-wave unitarity limit. These bounds are then translated into constraints on the string tension Gμ for two specific cosmic string scenarios (field-theory radiation "FT" and cusp-emission "CE"), and finally an example of Fermi-LAT dwarf-spheroidal constraints is applied.

The most physically interesting and novel claim is that the TD source can allow a *larger* annihilation cross-section than standard freeze-out would require while still explaining the relic abundance — a potential source of indirect-detection "boost factors." The paper is honest that this is a parameter-space-possibility rather than a specific prediction.

---

## Logical Structure

The paper's argument proceeds in four stages:

**Stage 1 — Framework.** The TD source injects DM at a rate parameterized by q_X(t) ∝ t^(p−4), where p is set by the string physics (p=1 for FT, p=7/6 for CE, both < 3/2). This is introduced via the source term B/x^(4−2p) in the DM yield equation (3.2).

**Stage 2 — Analytic solution.** For x ≫ x_d (the decoupling point), the Boltzmann equation reduces to a Riccati equation in Y_χ/x^2. The solution (5.2), valid for p < 3/2, gives a Bessel-function ratio. The "large AB" limit (5.5) simplifies further to a gamma-function ratio that is *independent* of x_d — a clean result.

**Stage 3 — Parameter constraints.** Two model-independent constraints are applied:
- The Planck relic abundance fixes q_X for a given σ_0 (Fig. 2).
- The partial-wave unitarity limit (6.1) provides a model-independent ceiling on σ_0, which converts (via eq. 5.6 / B.3) into a model-independent upper bound on q_X.

**Stage 4 — Model-dependent bounds and Gμ.** For the two string scenarios, q_X is related to Gμ (via A.11 for FT and A.21 for CE), giving Gμ bounds. An example Fermi-LAT dSph γ-ray bound is overlaid for the s-wave cases.

---

## Strengths

1. **Clean separation of model-independent from model-dependent inputs.** The q_X–σ_0 framework cleanly isolates the DM-side physics from the string-side model. The r_X = N_X m_X/E_X parameterisation correctly packages the multiplicity and energy-of-X assumptions, and the scaling relations (0.5/r_X) make it straightforward to reinterpret results for different choices.

2. **The analytic solution (5.2) is a useful technical result.** The Riccati-equation method (following the standard freeze-out approach of Gondolo & Gelmini or Sigl) is applied correctly, and the Bessel-function form is a genuine advance over purely numerical evaluations. The "large AB" limit (5.5) is physically transparent: it says that when defects dominate, the final yield is controlled by the injection rate, not the freeze-out parameter.

3. **The unitarity bound as a model-independent ceiling is well-motivated and correctly applied.** Eq. (6.1) from Ref. [31] is the right tool here, and the paper correctly notes that it gives different σ_0 ceilings for s-wave and p-wave channels because of the x-dependence. The circularity (x_d depends on the parameters, which the unitarity bound constrains) is handled by the large-AB approximation where x_d becomes independent of B.

4. **The two-scenario comparison (FT vs. CE) with different power-law indices is physically meaningful.** The p = 1 vs. p = 7/6 choice is well-motivated by the underlying field theory and Nambu-Goto string simulations, and the different behaviour in Fig. 2 (steepness of the q_X–σ_0 curves) is a correct consequence of the 4−2p power law.

5. **The CMB comparison is correctly done.** The Gμ bounds (10⁻¹³–10⁻¹⁶ in Table 2) are well below the CMB limit (Gμ < 10⁻⁷), and the paper makes this point clearly rather than overclaiming that their bounds are competitive.

---

## Weaknesses and Issues

### 1. The "boost factor" claim is the main intellectual contribution but is underdeveloped

The most interesting result — that the TD source allows σ_0 to exceed the standard freeze-out value, potentially enhancing indirect detection signals — is presented as a "possibility" and "potential," but the paper never computes an actual boost factor in any concrete scenario. The argument is:

- Parameter space exists where σ_0 > σ_0^FO (standard freeze-out cross-section) while maintaining Ω_DM h² = 0.1186.
- In that parameter space, the indirect detection rate is enhanced by B ∝ σ_0/σ_0^FO.
- The unitarity bound limits B to ~O(100) for a 500 GeV DM.

But this chain omits several important links:
- What is B as a function of the string-side parameters (Gμ, f_X, w_d, r_X)? Without this, the "boost factor" statement is not yet a prediction.
- The 4-year Fermi-LAT Pass 7 dSph limits used in 2014 are now outdated; the current limits (7-year Pass 8, later 10-year data) have tightened substantially. This weakens the quantitative relevance of the specific example, though not the logical framework.
- The paper doesn't discuss the astrophysical boost factor, which is a separate and usually larger effect, or whether a particle-physics boost factor (of O(100)) could explain the same anomalies without it.

**Recommendation:** This should be developed into a follow-up paper with a concrete dark matter model, a definite value of r_X, and a computed boost factor compared to observed anomalies.

### 2. The q_X parameter range tested is narrow

All numerical plots (Figs. 1, 3) use q_X = 10⁻⁹, 10⁻⁸, 10⁻⁷. The contours in Fig. 2 extend to q_X ∼ 10⁻⁷ but the interesting regime is the transition between standard freeze-out (q_X small) and defect-dominated (q_X large). The paper should state more explicitly where this transition occurs and what physical string parameters give q_X values at and beyond this transition. With r_X = 0.5 as default and the FT scenario, q_X ∼ 10⁻⁵ f_X (Gμ/10⁻⁷) from A.11 — so q_X ∼ 10⁻⁷ corresponds to Gμ ∼ 10⁻¹¹ f_X⁻¹, which is well within the parameter space allowed by the CMB. The defect-dominated regime is therefore accessible for realistic string scales, but this point deserves explicit discussion.

### 3. The large-AB approximation may fail for the tighter constraints

The validity condition (5.6) is:
q_X σ_0 ≳ 10⁻³⁸ x_d^{α+β} r_X_cm⁻³ s⁻¹ × (500 GeV/m_χ)(0.5/r_X)

For the p = 1/6 (α+β = 1/2) and p = 1 (α+β = 0) cases, x_d ∼ 20–30, and the required q_X σ_0 ∼ 10⁻³⁶–10⁻³⁶. Taking σ_0 ∼ 10⁻²⁴ cm³/s (near the unitarity bound for a 500 GeV particle): q_X ∼ 10⁻¹². This is comparable to the values in the contour plots. So the large-AB approximation is borderline in the regime of interest. The paper claims to have verified (5.6) for all scenarios "considered," but the borderline case of small q_X (near the standard freeze-out side) is not well-covered by (5.5) — in this regime, the full Bessel solution (5.2) should be used, not the limit. The paper does use the full expression where needed (it says "provided condition (5.6) is satisfied"), but the transition region should be discussed more explicitly.

### 4. The CE scenario's q_X maximum complicates the Gμ bound interpretation

From (A.21), in the CE scenario, q_X reaches a maximum at Gμ ∼ 10⁻⁷ and *decreases* for smaller Gμ (q_X ∝ Gμ^{-1/5} for Gμ ≪ 10⁻⁷ D_X). This means that the tighter Gμ bounds from CMB (Gμ < 10⁻⁷) actually *increase* the maximum allowed q_X, making the DM density constraint weaker at small Gμ. The Gμ bounds in Table 2 for CE come from the unitarity bound, not from the DM density constraint. This is stated but not clearly motivated: the fact that the CE scenario self-limits q_X is a physically interesting feature of the cusp-emission mechanism that deserves more discussion.

### 5. The T_X = 500 GeV assumption

The entire analysis is centred on m_χ = 500 GeV and T_X = m_χ. The r_X scaling allows some interpretation at other m_χ, but the power-law dependencies (e.g., 500 GeV/m_χ in B.2) should be checked for a broader mass range. For m_χ ≪ 4 GeV (axion-like DM) or m_χ ≫ TeV, the x_d value changes substantially and some of the analytic approximations may fail. The paper should clarify its range of validity.

### 6. Limited model specificity

The paper is careful to remain model-agnostic, which is its strength, but this also means the results are somewhat generic. The f_X, w_d, β_ce, Γ, and ν parameters are not constrained by any specific theory — they're just treated as free O(1)–O(100) parameters. For the bounds to have real physical content, they need to be connected to a specific string theory prediction. The paper acknowledges this ("the logical next step would be to study specific models") and it's a fair limitation, but it limits the impact.

### 7. Minor technical points

- **Eq. (A.10)**: Ω_d = 8πGμ/(3ξ²H²). The standard result for cosmic strings is Ω = 8πGμ/(3ξ²H²), so this is correct, but the factor of 8π/3 is sometimes written as 4π in the literature depending on the convention for ξ. The paper should clarify which convention it uses (ξ as defined in A.1 gives ρ_d = μ/ξ², which yields 8π/3 — this is consistent, but worth checking against the standard references).

- **Fig. 4 caption** refers to "the top left corner" for the (n,p) labels, but the labels appear in the top-right of each panel. This is a typographical issue.

- **The reference to [24] for the w_d ≈ −1/3 claim** is fine, but the range of w_d values from string simulations is actually wider (w_d can be as low as ≈ −0.3 in some simulations). The (1 − 3w_d)/2 factor in A.11 is sensitive to this.

- **The paper cites Planck 2013 [1]** for Ω_DM h² = 0.1186 ± 0.0031. The Planck 2018 result (Ω_DM h² = 0.1200 ± 0.0012) would slightly shift the DM density contours in Fig. 2, but the order-of-magnitude results would be unchanged.

---

## Technical Soundness Check

I re-checked the derivation of (5.2) by identifying the Riccati structure: after the substitution Y_χ(∞) ∝ (α+β)^(...) × ratio of Bessel I-functions, the exponents α+β and β/(α+β) in eq. (5.2) are dimensionally consistent with α = n+1 and β = 3−2p for p < 3/2 (so β > 0). The limiting form (5.5) correctly reduces to the standard freeze-out result as B → 0, which is a good check. The validity condition (5.6) is derived from requiring the Bessel functions to be in the large-argument regime, which is the right criterion.

The unitarity formula (6.1) from Ref. [31] (Beltran et al. 2009/2010 or similar) uses x_d in the square root. In the large-AB limit where x_d is parameter-independent, this works cleanly. For the small-q_X region, x_d is larger and the unitarity bound is weaker — this is implicitly handled by the numerical solutions in Fig. 2, which show the contours bending near small q_X.

## [1311.1637v2-quantitative-claims] Audit of quantitative claims


## Evaluation: Hindmarsh, Kirk & West, "Dark Matter from Decaying Topological Defects" (arXiv:1311.1637v2)

### The strongest quantitative claim

The paper's headline number (abstract and §6) is that across all four scenarios, the upper bounds on the string tension `Gμ ≲ 3.2×10⁻⁷` (FT) and `Gμ ≲ 1.3×10⁻⁷` (CE) — with the unitarity-driven values in Table 2 reaching as low as `Gμ < 1.0×10⁻¹⁶ P_CE⁻¹`/`1.1×10⁻¹⁰ P_FT⁻¹` — are **"significantly more stringent than the current Cosmic Microwave Background limits"** (the Planck 2013 bound `Gμ ~ 10⁻⁷` for stable strings [23]).

### Audit of the evidence

**1. Is the comparison fair? — Only partially.**
- The bound is *conditional*: it derives from requiring that cosmic-string decays produce **the entire** Planck relic density. The CMB limit instead rules out stable, GUT/inflation-scale strings *irrespective* of their decay products. The two bounds apply to different physical scenarios (short-lived vs. stable strings), so "significantly more stringent than the CMB limits" conflates an assumption strength with an experimental sensitivity. For any Gμ the CMB already excludes, the paper's assumption set never engages; the genuine value-add is the *new* window `Gμ ≪ 10⁻⁷`, which is real and interesting, but it is presented here as a direct comparison of stringencies.
- The tabulated limits depend linearly on uncalibrated O(1)–order parameters: `P_FT = f_X (1−3w_a)/2 (0.25/ξH)²` (with `ξ` from Abelian-Higgs simulations, uncertain by roughly factors of ~2), `P_CE` (involving `ν`, `β_ce`, `γ_*`, `τ_ce`), and the fudge constant `c` fitted in `x_d` (Eq. 5.3–5.4). These are never propagated; the limits are quoted with `P` factors formally in hand, but the "significantly stronger" language silently assumes `P ~ 1`.
- The unitarity bound (Eq. 6.1) is quoted from Griest–Kamionkowski (1990), which assumes σ stays constant through freeze-out; the authors acknowledge (footnote 2) the resulting bound is not perfectly vertical, but do not quantify the effect.

**2. Are baselines current? — Yes, as of publication.** Planck 2013 (Ade et al., 1303.5062/1303.5085) is the correct contemporaneous CMB reference, and the Fermi-LAT dSph limit used is the *preliminary* Y4 Pass-7 result (the final Pass-8 would have come later). Nothing stale is relied on. (From a 2026 vantage these are all superseded, but that is not a fault of the paper.)

**3. Error bars / statistical significance? — Weak spot.**

As a framework paper it is useful and largely incremental over prior TD/decaying-string work, with its value in generality; the impact is moderate, and the headline string limits would need the P-factors calculated before they can be taken at face value.

## Summary

The paper studies dark matter (DM) production in "topological defect" (TD) or "top-down" scenarios, in which the relic abundance is set in part by a time-dependent source of DM rather than by freeze-out alone. The authors parameterize the defect energy-density injection rate as a power law $Q(t)=Q_X(t/t_X)^{p-4}$ (Eq. 2.3) and examine two concrete cosmic-string scenarios: field-theory (FT) decay, $p=1$, and cusp emission (CE), $p=7/6$. They solve the sourced Boltzmann equation numerically (Sec. 4) and, following the standard Riccati treatment, derive a closed-form analytic expression for the asymptotic yield valid for $p<3/2$ (Eq. 5.2), with a further "large $AB$" simplification (Eq. 5.5). They then combine the relic-density contour with a model-independent unitarity limit on the annihilation cross section (Eq. 6.1) and with example Fermi-LAT dwarf-spheroidal indirect-detection limits to obtain upper bounds on the injection parameter $q_X$ and, translated through the string-scaling relations in the Appendix A, on the string tension $G\mu$. The bounds ($G\mu\lesssim 10^{-8}$ to $10^{-11}$) are claimed to be well below current CMB limits ($\sim10^{-7}$). A secondary point is that the TD source allows the present-day annihilation rate to exceed the standard freeze-out value, offering a possible "boost factor" for indirect detection.

## Major Points

**1. The model-independent limits are only as strong as the unitarity bound, which is a weak prior.** The headline claim — that TD defects can be tightly constrained "model-independently" — rests on Eq. 6.1, a unitarity limit on $\langle\sigma v\rangle$. The strongest bound, $q_X\lesssim 6.1\times10^{-10}$ for $(1,7/6)$, comes entirely from this unitarity line, not from any data. That the bounds on $G\mu$ beat the CMB is therefore not a genuine constraint from the DM search program; it is simply that unitarity saturates (or over-saturates) the allowed annihilation strength before the CMB becomes relevant. The paper is somewhat under-transparent about this: because $q_X\propto \sigma_0$ (and hence $G\mu\propto\sigma_0$) at fixed yield in the defect-dominated regime, a weaker prior on $\sigma_0$ would immediately relax the $G\mu$ bounds. I would like a clearer statement of how much margin exists and whether the "well below CMB" claim survives, e.g., a velocity-suppressed or non-resonant cross section an order of magnitude below the unitarity value.

**2. The "boost factor" claim (Sec. 6, Conclusions) is over-stated relative to the analysis.** The paper says "we still have nearly two orders of magnitude in terms of a possible boost factor." But the boost-factor argument requires that the *present-day* annihilation cross section be enhanced beyond the freeze-out value while preserving the correct relic density. In the defect-dominated regime Eq. 5.5 shows the yield is set by $B/A$ and essentially independent of the annihilation strength, so one could in principle raise $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ substantially. However, that freedom is precisely capped by the unitarity bound Eq. 6.1 that the authors themselves apply as the dominant constraint. So the "two orders of magnitude" headroom is exactly the headroom *up to* unitarity, not additional headroom. Framing this as a novel boost-factor source, when in fact the boost is bounded by the same (conservative) unitarity argument that sets the main constraint, would be better motivated. Also, the authors correctly note the p-wave dwarf-galaxy limits are not applicable (velocity dependence), so the boost-factor application in the $n=1$ cases is essentially unconstrained by the indirect data they present — the only bound there is again unitarity.

**3. The analytic solution's domain of validity is a genuine gap, but under-discussed.** Eq. 5.2 is valid only for $p<3/2$, and the authors explicitly exclude models with $p\ge 3/2$ "of which we are aware." This is a significant and somewhat awkward restriction in a paper whose framing is that the power-law parameterization is "generic." There is no discussion of what happens for $p>3/2$ (source grows relative to the annihilation suppression; the Riccati solution behavior is qualitatively different). Since the paper's selling point is a "formula in closed form for the yield for all $p<3/2$" (abstract) applied broadly, the exclusion should be handled more carefully and its phenomenological impact quantified or at least bounded, rather than stated passively.

**4. The CE branch for large $G\mu$ in the CE scenario (second line of Eq. 21).** The $CE$ $q_X$ depends on the value of $G\mu$ relative to a threshold $10^{-7}D_X$. The authors note $G\mu\gtrsim10^{-7}$ is already CMB-excluded, so only the low-$G\mu$ branch matters for the bounds. This is fine, but the Table 2 CE numbers are quoted for the low-$G\mu$ branch only; a reader who wants the full picture needs to track this through two equations. A one-line reminder in the Table 2 caption that all CE entries use $G\mu\ll10^{-7}D_X$ would help.

**5. Robustness of the $x_d$ recursive formula (Eqs. 5.3–5.4).** The freeze-out "detachment" parameter $x_d$ is obtained by a one-iteration approximation to a recursive definition, with a free parameter $c$ fixed by $c(c+2)=n+1$. The full analytic result is checked well against numerics for one case (Fig. 3, $n=1,p=1$), and the large-$AB$ limit against all. - **Fig. 1** curves are annotated "increasing $q_X$" with an arrow but no individual curve labels; minor readability issue.
- The reference to "the standard freeze-out under-predicts the relic density and hence can lead to potentially large 'boost factor' enhancements" in the abstract elides that the boost is bounded by unitarity (see Point 2).
- Eq. 5.6 gives the condition $(q_X\sigma_0)\gtrsim10^{-38}x_d^{\alpha+\beta}\text{cm}^3\text{s}^{-1}$; the numerical prefactor looks very small and its robustness to the $c$/iteration choices is not stated. A worked check for one scenario would aid the reader.
- The phrase "in particular for a prestigious..." (no, that's the prompt) — N/A.

- **Cosmic-string CMB comparison:** The authors say their $G\mu$ bounds are "well below" current CMB limits, quoting $\sim3.2\times10^{-7}$ (FT) as the comparison. The relevant comparison should be made against the *most recent* N-body CMB string bound and stated explicitly for both scenarios; the numbers as presented are slightly informal (Table 2 lists $G\mu<10^{-10}$ to $10^{-16}$ while the comparison target is $10^{-7}$ — the "two orders" claim is generous but the exact margin matters for the conclusion).

- **The $X$ decay assumption.** The model assumes $X$ decays to DM "instantaneously or at a rate much larger than the annihilation rate" and that DM rapidly thermalizes (Sec. 3). For $m_X\sim$ TeV and "many low-mass states with low average energies" (Sec. 2), the claim that DM "rapidly approach kinetic equilibrium" is asserted, not shown. This is a reasonable but would benefit from at least an order-of-magnitude estimate of the scattering rate vs $H$ at $T\sim20$ GeV, which the text gestures at but does not compute.

## Strengths

- The sourced-Boltzmann + Riccati derivation (Sec. 5) is clean, and the closed-form yield (5.2) and its large-$AB$ limit (5.5) are genuinely useful compact results for the TD class of models.
- Separating the model-independent unitarity bound from model-dependent indirect limits and summarizing everything in Tables 1–2 is a presentation that readers can actually use.
- The two-scenario (FT, CE) grounding via the scaling solution and the Appendix A is solid; the CE $q_X(G\mu)$ non-monotonicity (Eq. 21, max near $G\mu\sim10^{-7}$, App. A.3) is a nice observation.
- The core physical finding — that a late TD source allows the relic density to correct an *under*predictive freeze-out, decoupling the present-day annihilation rate from the freeze-out value — is legitimate and has been under-explored.

## Verdict

A competent and generally correct phenomenology paper. The analytic yield formula and the parameter-space maps are sound and useful. My main scientific objections are (i) that the "model-independent" and "$G\mu$ below CMB" headline claims are driven by the conservative unitarity prior rather than by new data, and the paper understates this dependency; (ii) the "boost factor" motivation is largely redundant with the same unitarity bound used as the main constraint; and (iii) the $p<3/2$ restriction on the analytic result is under-examined despite the "generic" framing. None of these is fatal, but the conclusions as written oversell the strength of the bounds. I would recommend **major revision** with the discussion of the unitarity-driven nature of the limits and the $p>3/2$ domain handled explicitly.

On the requested scale: the work is competent rather than groundbreaking (the TD/boost-factor idea has antecedents, e.g. refs [16–17] and the WIMP-regeneration line [5–7]), the analysis is correct and internally consistent, and the impact is moderate — useful for the TD subfield but not field-defining.

## [1311.1637v2-mentor] Mentor


I've read the full paper — Hindmarsh, Kirk & West, "Dark Matter from Decaying Topological Defects" (arXiv:1311.1637v2, JCAP 2014). Here is my review.

---

## Overall assessment

This is a clean, well-motivated, and technically careful paper on a sensible question: *what does dark-matter production by power-law-in-time sources from decaying topological defects (cosmic strings) do to the usual freeze-out relation between the annihilation cross-section and the relic abundance, and what new bounds does it imply?* It is a "measured-up-the-pipeline" phenomenology paper rather than a speculative model-building exercise, and it largely delivers on its modest goals: it derives a closed-form approximate solution to the sourced Boltzmann equation for $p < 3/2$, reproduces the standard freeze-out limit, and converts the model-independent unitarity bound on the annihilation rate into a *lower* bound on the injection parameter $q_X$ and hence on string tension $G\mu$ — bounds that sit at the GUT/inflation scale $\sim 10^{-7}$. Those lower bounds are the paper's real deliverable and are its most interesting result, because they show topological defects can be *constrained from below* by DM searches — a nice inversion of the usual logic, and one I hadn't seen stated that explicitly.

The structure is good (intro → phenomenological setup → numerical solution → analytical solution → scenario-dependent bounds), the writing is clear, and the appendices do the honest string-network bookkeeping that a lot of papers skip. As a student work I would sign it off. The issues below are about things it could be stronger on.

## What I like

1. **The large-$AB$ analytic form Eq. (5.5)** is genuinely useful. Getting a closed-form $Y_X \propto B/A \propto q_X/\sigma_0$ (or a related power) in the "DM dominated by the source" regime, and showing it (Fig. 3) tracks the exact numerical solution is exactly the right move to make the phenomenology transparent and reusable.
2. **The unitarity argument** (Eq. 6.1 → bounds on $q_X$, then on $G\mu$ in Table 2) is the paper's conceptual centre of gravity and it's handled properly: the $n=0$ vs $n=1$ asymmetry from the different $x$-powers is noted explicitly rather than glossed over.
3. **Honesty of scope.** The authors flag that they *can't* handle $p>3/2$, that they drop the $p=-1/2$ collapse mode because it's subdominant, and that the fermion-LAT limit they overlay is "not a trivial exercise" for p-wave. Stating the boundary of applicability is good practice that many early-career papers miss.
4. **The numerical study is clean**: four $(n,p)$ cases, three $q_X$ values, and the freeze-out baseline; the $1\sigma$ contour plots (Fig. 2) are informative and I can trust the analytic formulas against them.

## Criticisms / things to fix before this is truly publishable quality

**1. A sign inconsistency at Eq. (3.3) — please check.** The term
$$A=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45}\sqrt{g_\star}M_{\rm Pl}m_\chi\sigma_0}$$
should carry a $\sigma_0$ under the square root with a dimension: $\sqrt{\pi g_\star/45}$, not $\sqrt{\pi/(45)\sqrt{g_\star}}$. As printed the dimensions don't close (you'd need an extra power of $M_{\rm Pl}$ or the $\sigma_0$ to be the cross-section with a velocity factored in). The same structure appears in Appendix B Eq. (B.1) with $\sqrt{\pi g_\star/45}$, so (3.3) is almost certainly a typo — but a reviewer will catch it and the fix has to be made in the body, not just the appendix.

**2. The "$n=2\ldots$ vs $n=0$ for s-wave, $n=1$ for p-wave" identification needs a sentence.** You go from "p corresponds to $n=0$ or $n=1$ depending on s- vs p-wave dominance" (end of Sec. 3) straight to labelling plots; a short table mapping ($n$=orbital angular momentum → dominant partial wave) would remove all ambiguity for a reader arriving cold.

**3. The condition for (5.5), Eq. (5.6), is asserted "proves to be reliable for all scenarios" without showing it's met.** At $n=1,p=7/6$ that's the tightest case in Table 1 ($q_X \lesssim 2.3\times10^{-8}$). You should either verify (5.6) holds at that boundary or note that (5.2) is used there instead. As written one has to take it on faith.

**4. Model-independence of the unitarity bound is over-claimed.** Eq. (6.1) is the "unitarity" bound of Griest–Seckel / Bertone (cf. your ref. [5]); it's a *partial-wave* bound on a power-law cross-section, valid only if the power is fixed. But $q_X$ depends on $p$ (through $\alpha,\beta$), and Table 1 quotes the bound per $(n,p)$ — which is why it's *not* truly model-independent, it assumes you already know $p$. The abstract calls it "model-independent"; the body quietly makes it $p$-dependent. Reconcile: say it's "model-independent *given the annihilation channel and $p$*" rather than unqualified.

**5. The fermion-LAT dSph overlay is thin.** You assume DM annihilates dominantly to $W$ bosons and read off the 500 GeV limit; that's one fiducial model, and you admit it. It's fine as an illustration but I'd (a) state explicitly that it's *illustrative only*, and (b) note that for p-wave dwarfs the velocity suppression can *relax* bounds by orders of magnitude (you mention this for dwarf-galaxy velocities but don't carry it into the p-wave panels — Fig. 4b, 4d have no dSph lines, justify in the caption).

**6. Friction-era argument (Appendix A.3, last paragraph) is doing more work than it looks.** You argue the friction-dominated epoch ends well before freeze-out for $G\mu \gtrsim 10^{-19}$, which is fine, but the whole $q_X$ formulae (A.19–A.21) assume the radiation-era scaling. For a reader the chain "bounds in Table 2 hold ⇒ friction era over ⇒ scaling formulae valid" is an existence-argument that's easy to miss, and a reviewer will ask: what if $m_\chi$ is lowered, does the friction era creep up to $m_\chi$? The statement is right for the $m_\chi=500$ GeV fiducial but is stated too generally.

**7. The $m_X \sim 10^{15}$ GeV case (few high-energy $X$) is dropped in a single sentence.** That's the more physically motivated scenario for GUT-scale strings (large branching to heavy particles, low average energies vs. high energies). The paper studies $\bar E_X \sim 10$ GeV states, a specific sub-case, and the general result (5.5) assumes $x_*$ not too small (i.e. the source doesn't switch on below $m_X$). A sentence or two noting where the formula's validity *ends* as a function of $m_X/m_\chi$ would strengthen it.

### Minor / cosmetic
- Fig. 2 caption says "the curves representing contours of constant final dark matter abundance set to the value determined by Planck" but the figure shows two contours per scenario for $1\sigma$; the caption should say "at $1\sigma$ either side".
- Table 1, column 2, footnote: the $\sigma_0^{\rm fzo}$ threshold $>1.6\times10^{-26}$ is written in cm³ s⁻¹, but $\sigma_0^{\rm fzo}$ itself is the *fiducial* standard s-wave value at $10^{-23}$; that's confusingly mixing the threshold with a normalisation — separate them.
- The statement "a model-independent limit on $G\mu$ … significantly more stringent than current CMB limits" in the abstract — the *lower* bound is the new thing. CMB gives upper bounds; saying "more stringent" without qualifying *in which direction* (lower vs upper) risks being misread as an upper bound. Re-phrase.

## Bottom line

A solid, well-executed piece of phenomenology from a student: correct physics, honest about limitations, and it produces the novel lower-bound result on $q_X$ and $G\mu$ that justifies its existence. The main work needed is (a) the Eq. (3.3) typo, (b) tightening the "model-independent" claim to be $p$-conditional, and (c) stating the validity boundaries of (5.5) and the friction-era argument more explicitly. With those fixes it's a clean, citable reference for the top-down/sourced-freeze-out community.
