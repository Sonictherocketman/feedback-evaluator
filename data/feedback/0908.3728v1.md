## What the paper argues

This "brief report" (Flores‑Tlalpa, Montañó, Ramírez‑Zavaleta, Toscano, arXiv:0908.3728) studies the one‑loop rare decays $Z\to gg\gamma$ and $Z'\to gg\gamma$ in the **minimal 331 model** ($SU_C(3)\times SU_L(3)\times U_X(1)$), which adds a $Z'$ and three exotic quarks ($D,S$ with $Q=-4/3$, $T$ with $Q=+5/3$).

The argument proceeds as:
1. The $Vgg\gamma$ amplitude ($V=Z,Z'$) is a **particular case of the already‑computed $Vggg$ amplitude** (their Ref. [1]), differing only in couplings, color factors, and statistics. Because the photon is Abelian, only the *vector* (color‑symmetric $d_{ab}$) part survives — no axial $f_{ab}$ piece.
2. A counting argument suggests $V\to gg\gamma$ should be *suppressed* relative to $V\to ggg$ by $(36/5)(\alpha/\alpha_s)Q_q^2\approx0.14\,Q_q^2<1$.
3. Numerically: **$Z\to gg\gamma$ is dominated by the first two families** and is essentially blind to the exotic quarks ($\Gamma_{Q_i}\sim10^{-11}$ GeV vs. $\Gamma_{q_i}\sim10^{-5}$ GeV) — a clean decoupling result.
4. **$Z'\to gg\gamma$ is the interesting case:** the exotic quarks *dominate* and, because $Q_T>0$ while $Q_{D,S}<0$, the exotic amplitudes interfere **constructively** (vs. destructively in $Z'\to ggg$). For $\{m_{Z'}=1.5\,\text{TeV},\ m_{D,S,T}=700\,\text{GeV}\}$ the exotic partial width ($\sim8.2\times10^{-2}$ GeV, Table III, interference‑dominated) exceeds $\Gamma(Z'\to ggg)$ by more than an order of magnitude, giving $\text{Br}(Z'\to gg\gamma)=1.06\times10^{-4}$.

The tables are internally consistent (family widths sum to the totals; the exotic interference terms correctly saturate $|\sum A|^2$: e.g. $D\mbox{-}S=2|A_D|^2$, $D\mbox{-}T$ at the maximal $2\sqrt{|\!A_D|^2|\!A_T|^2}$), and the $\text{Br}(Z\to gg\gamma)=5.49\times10^{-6}$ figure reproduces the SM order of magnitude.

## Strengths
- **Clean, correct reduction** to the known $Vggg$ machinery; the transversality/gauge invariance and UV finiteness are inherited rather than redone, which is appropriate for the scope.
- **The central observation is a genuine, non‑obvious physical point.** The naive coupling‑counting says $gg\gamma\ll ggg$; the result overturns that *because the sign structure of the exotic charges flips the interference from destructive (in $ggg$) to constructive (in $gg\gamma$)*. That is insight, not bookkeeping, and it is the right thing to have surfaced.
- **Clean negative result** for $Z\to gg\gamma$ — the exotic insensitivity is a useful robustness statement for the model.

# Critique: "Decays Z → ggγ and Z′ → ggγ in the minimal 331 model" (arXiv:0908.3728)

## Scope and method

This four-page note computes the one-loop rare decays *Z* → ggγ and *Z′* → ggγ in the minimal S(U)C(3)×S(U)L(3)×U_X(1) model, re-using the exact analytic Vggg box-amplitude machinery of the authors' companion paper [1]. The headline claims are (i) *Z* → ggγ is set by light quarks and essentially blind to exotic quarks D, S, T, and (ii) for a heavy *Z′*, exotic-quark *constructive* interference in ggγ (reversing the destructive pattern of ggg) makes Γ(*Z′* → ggγ) more than an order of magnitude above Γ(*Z′* → ggg), with Br ≈ 1.06×10⁻⁴ at *m*Z′ = 1.5 TeV.

The overall logic is sound: the gg→ggγ vertex is indeed a charge-squared special case of Vggg (no axial part survives for the photon, which the authors note correctly), and the constructive/constructive sign flip is a clean consequence of Q_T = +5/3 versus Q_D,S = −4/3 multiplying the amplitude — since gggg carries no such charge factor. I also checked the internal arithmetic: Table I (5.32×10⁻⁶ + 8.36×10⁻⁶ = 1.37×10⁻⁵ GeV), Table II (500 GeV: 5.89 − 3.95 = 1.94×10⁻⁴; 1.5 TeV: 4.453 + 1.96 = 6.41×10⁻³), and Table III (2.98 + 5.21 ×10⁻² ≈ 8.2×10⁻² GeV) all add up consistently.

## Strengths

- The comparison *Z* vs *Z′* and its explanation via the sign of the charge factor is physically clear and, to my knowledge, correctly reasoned.
- The tables decompose SM/exotic/interference contributions usefully, and the conclusion that exotic-quark effects in *Z* → ggγ are negligible (~10⁻¹¹ against 10⁻⁵) is robust under the expected decoupling.
- The observation that ggγ can *exceed* ggg for the exotic sector despite the naive (36/5)(α/α_s)Q² suppression factor is a genuinely interesting twist on the usual decoupling story.

## Problems

**1. No independent cross-check (major).** The SM *Z* → ggγ rate is already known (cited Ref. [11]). The paper claims to "present complete numerical results in the context of the standard model" for that process but never states whether its light-quark numbers or the final Br = 5.49×10⁻⁶ agree with the published SM result. For a note whose entire computational content is borrowed from one companion reference, an explicit reproduction check — or at least a one-sided agreement statement — is the minimum expected validation, and its absence is the paper's biggest weakness.

**2. Total *Z′* width is never stated (major, reproducibility).** The branching ratios (Eqs. 8, 9) depend on Γ_tot(*Z′*) "from Ref. [8]," but no value is given. Backing out of the quoted numbers implies Γ_tot ≳ 200 GeV for the 500 GeV scenario and ~800 GeV for the 1.5 TeV scenario — i.e. width/mass of 0.3–0.55. Such an ultra-broad resonance is not impossible in a strong-coupling 331 model (g_S ~ 1.5), but it is a regime where "branching ratio" is barely a useful observable, and the authors should have reported (and defended) the actual widths, the coupling values (g_S, sin²θ_W at the scale), and the renormalization scale for α_s. As written, neither Br is independently checkable.

**3. Single degenerate exotic-mass point (major, conclusion robustness).** The whole exotic-domination effect rests on constructive interference among D, S and T, which is mass-splitting sensitive. The paper *itself* notes (via [1]) that the amplitude is non-decoupling in doublet mass splittings, yet every number is evaluated at m_D = m_S = m_T. No scan over (m_D, m_S, m_T) is shown — only *m*Z′ is varied in Fig. 2 with exotic masses pinned at 700 GeV. "The exotic contribution dominates" is therefore a statement about one degenerate, arguably optimistic, point in parameter space, and the claim is over-stated as a model-wide result. The 500 GeV scenario additionally puts the exotic quarks exactly at threshold of a 500 GeV *Z′*, so phase space there is maximally suppressed — yet the "SM-dominated there / exotics dominate at 1.5 TeV" narrative rests on this corner case.

**4. The *m*Z′ = 500 GeV scenario is probably excluded (minor–moderate).** In this model the *Z′* has a sizeable *Z–Z′* mixing and couples to leptons; LEP2 di-lepton searches already pushed *Z′*-like states to the multi-TeV range. The authors call both 500 GeV and 1.5 TeV "consistent with the corresponding bounds given in the literature [14]" without comment. Since one of the two quoted branching ratios refers to a very likely excluded mass, the phenomenological content is weaker than the abstract suggests.

**5. No uncertainty estimate or experimental comparison (moderate).** No PDF/PDW, counterterm, or renormalization-scale variation is discussed; no comparison with experimental limits (LEP dijet + γ for *Z* → ggγ, LHC direct *Z′* searches for the 1.5 TeV point) or even a sentence on whether Br ~ 10⁻⁴ is in principle observable. For a rare decay of a BSM boson, at least the experimental-reach comment is expected.

**6. Presentation errors (minor).** Reference [10] is dated "Nucl. Phys. B313, 237 (**1090**)"; Table I's caption says "in the standard model" for a table that is the *Z* part of a 331-model calculation; the prose "roughly one can to expect" / "It is as shown" typos; the rough 0.14 Q_q² estimate doesn't clearly follow from stated α/α_s values; and Fig. 2's legend ("qi+Qi") makes it unclear whether the SM–exotic cross term is in the summed curve. The note also skips any phase-space discussion of the collinear (y → 1) limit of the |M|² integral, which for three massless final states is the natural thing to address given all form factors are only UV-finite.

## Verdict

A competent, internally consistent numerical extension of prior work by the same group, with one genuinely interesting physical observation (charge-driven sign flip turning destructive ggg interference into constructive ggγ one). But it is thin: all computation comes from [1], it contains no independent verification of its SM limit, it never reports the *Z′* total widths its branching ratios depend on, it tests only a degenerate exotic-mass point for a mass-splinting-sensitive interference effect, and it presents branching ratios for a resonance mass likely already excluded, with no experimental perspective. It reads as a letter that should either have carried one solid cross-check plus a mass-scan, or been merged with [1].

## [0908.3728v1-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


This is arXiv:0908.3728 (Flores‑Talpa, Montaño, Ramírez‑Zavala, Toscano, 2009), a five‑page "brief report" on the decays $Z\to gg\gamma$ and $Z'\to gg\gamma$ in the **minimal 331 model** (gauge group $SU_C(3)\times SU_L(3)\times U_X(1)$, with three exotic quark doublets $D,S$ (charge $-4/3$) and $T$ (charge $+5/3$)).

## Reproducible methodology (step by step)

**1. Theoretical setup.**
- First stage of SSB: $SU_L(3)\times U_X(1)\to SU_L(2)\times U_Y(1)$, producing $Z$, $Z'$, and doubly‑charged "bilipion" gauge bosons.
- The constraint $\sin^2\theta_W = s_W^2 \le 1/4$ at the $m_Z$ scale bounds the scale of the first SSB stage, implying $m_{Z'} \le 3.1$ TeV and bilipion masses $\le m_{Z'}/2$ (all quoted from the companion paper [1]).

**2. Amplitude (Eq. 2).**
The decay proceeds through **one‑loop box diagrams** (Fig. 1): a fermion loop with two on‑shell external photons, the $V$ boson, and a gluon. There are 6 diagrams generated by Bose symmetry ($g$–$g$ interchange), and the full amplitude for $V_{\mu_1}(p_1)\to g_{\mu_2}(p_2)\,g_{\mu_3}(p_3)\,\gamma_{\mu_4}(p_4)$ is

$$\mathcal{M}_{\bar a\bar b}^{\mu_1..\mu_4} = g^a_{VV}\,Q_V\,Q_{\phi,\bar a\bar b}\left(-\frac{i g_\phi^V\,2eg_V N_C}{2\pi^2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{18} f^g_j\, T_{V_j}^{\mu_1..\mu_4},$$

where $f^g_j$ are scalar loop form factors (summed over $j=1..18$) and $T_j$ are the color/gauge tensor structures — **both taken from the companion reference [1]**, which also proves the form factors are UV finite and the amplitude satisfies transversality $p_i^\mu \mathcal{M}_{\mu..}=0$ (Eq. 3). For $V=\gamma$, only axial‑vector structures would contribute, but the photon is Abelian so this vanishes; thus the $Z' \to gg\gamma$ amplitude is a special case of $V\to gg\gamma$ with vector couplings only.

**3. Decay width (Eq. 4).**
The squared amplitude is integrated over the two‑dimensional Dalitz region $0<x<1$, $0<y<1-x$:

$$\Gamma(V\to gg\gamma) = \frac{m_V}{1536\,\pi^3}\int_0^1 dx\int_{1-x}^1 dy\, |\mathcal{M}|^2_{\bar x=1-x,\bar y=y} = \frac{\alpha_s^2(m_V)\,\alpha^2 N_b^2 m_V}{32\,\pi^3\, s_W^4 c_W^4}\int dx\,dy\,\sum_{i\ne j} g_{VV}^q Q_{VV}^i Q_{VV}^j Q_{VV}^l V_{\bar q_i}V_{\bar q_j}^*,$$

It also papers over the fact that only a single exotic mass point was tested — "insensitive" is an extrapolation from one value.

**5. "The relative importance of the contributions… was analyzed using exact analytical expressions given in Ref. [1]."**
The honest read is the reverse of the abstract's framing: Eqs. 2–5 restate Ref. [1]'s amplitude and form factors verbatim; this paper evaluates them at two benchmark points. "It is found that" in the abstract converts a numerical application of pre-existing results into a new result.

**6. The suppression-factor sentence:** "one can expect that the decay width for the V → ggγ transition is suppressed with respect to the one associated with the V → ggg process by a factor of (36/5)(α/α_s)Q_R² ≲ 0.14 Q_R²." The sentence is incoherent as written (the two factors collapse into an unexplained bound, and the charge bookkeeping is unclear), and "roughly one can expect" is doing work for a claim that is then contradicted by their own tables (exotic quarks make Z′→ggγ *larger*) without reconciliation.

**7. Figure 2 caption:** "Impact of the known and exotic quarks on Br(Z′→ggγ) as a function of the Z′ mass." The exotic masses used are not stated (0.5 or 0.7 TeV?), the plot starts at 0.5 TeV without saying where the exotic pair threshold lies relative to m_Q, and neither the total curve nor the interference is shown — so any "the exotic dominates" reading of the figure is one the authors have not actually plotted.

**Other writing defects:** the non-native "It can be appreciated from these tables" (four occurrences) is a stylistic tic; "In this reference, it is pointed out in Refs."-type sentences are clumsy; Table I is titled "in the standard model" while displaying 331 exotic-quark rows; no error bands, scale variation, or α_s uncertainty are discussed anywhere despite the "exact analytical expressions" language; and there is zero connection to observability — no LHC reach, no search channel discussion, for a process at Br ~ 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴.

## What an adversarial reviewer's first paragraph would say

"This five-page note applies the analytical V→ggγ amplitudes computed by the same group in Ref. [1] to two benchmark mass points of the 331 model, using the total Z′ width from a further separate reference. No new formula is derived, and no parameter scan is performed. Yet the abstract presents the outcomes as model-wide findings — 'the exotic quark contribution dominates' — which the authors' own Eq. 8 refutes for m_{Z′} = 500 GeV, where the decay is SM-quark-dominated. The claim that Γ(Z′→ggγ) exceeds 10×Γ(Z′→ggg) depends on results and a total width taken from references [1] and [8], is never numerically exhibited here, and 'heavy Z′' is left undefined, as is the crossover between the destructive and constructive interference asserted from two data points. The robustness of every conclusion to the exotic quark mass — the free parameter the 331 framework most needs — is untested, no uncertainties are quoted, and no comparison with what any existing or near-future experiment could see is attempted. As it stands, the abstract describes a different, stronger paper than the one delivered."

A technically competent but deliberately thin note: the physics is standard one-loop, the numbers are internally consistent, and the caveat in section II is honest — but the abstract sells a conditional, single-point numerical observation as a generic model prediction, and the absence of any m_Q scan, uncertainty analysis, or phenomenological reach assessment keeps this in "pre-communicazione" territory rather than a self-contained result.

1. **Incremental content.** This is a short extension of the authors' own comprehensive study (Ref. For a prestigious journal this reads as a companion letter to work already in press/published, not a self-contained contribution.

2. **Limited novelty of the mechanism.** The suppression/enhancement arguments are largely anticipatable: the *Z* vs. *Z′* relative size follows from coupling and color factors ((36/5)(α/α_s)Q̄_R² ≈ 0.14), and the constructive exotic-quark interference is a simple consequence of *Q_T* > 0 vs. *Q_{D,S}* < 0 (antitriplet vs. triplet). 3. **No phenomenological payoff.** The quoted branching ratios (10⁻⁶ for *Z*; up to ~10⁻⁴ for *Z′*) are far below attainable experimental reach in 2009 or thereafter, and the paper never discusses whether any of these channels is experimentally meaningful, nor how the results constrain model parameters. - The central comparative claim (Γ(*Z′* → *ggγ*) > Γ(*Z′* → *ggg*) by >10×) relies on Table VIII of Ref. [1]; a quantitative statement in the letter itself would make it stand alone.
- Table II is somewhat inconsistent in presentation (columns mix Γ and Γ′_{q_i} under different mass labels without a clear legend; the 500 GeV Γ′_{t,b} = −3.95 × 10⁻⁴ GeV is a negative interference value whose units/meaning are never explicitly stated as an interference-only term).

The work is competent and presumably correct as an application of established formulas to a specific non-SM gauge sector, and the constructive-interference observation is a mildly interesting color/charge-structure comment. It is well above the bar for a conference proceedings note or a modest-refereed archival journal, but below the bar for a prestigious journal, which would demand either a genuinely new mechanism, a robust parameter-scan conclusion, or a concrete phenomenological implication.

# Review: *Decays Z → ggγ and Z′ → ggγ in the minimal 331 model*
**arXiv:0908.3728v1** — Flores-Tlalpa, Montaño, Ramírez-Zavaleta, Toscano

## Overall assessment

This is a clean, self-consistent **brief report** that extends the authors' earlier study of the $V\to ggg$ vertex (Ref. [1]) to the rarer $V\to gg\gamma$ final state within the minimal 331 model. The central physical claim — that $Z\to gg\gamma$ is blind to the exotic quarks while $Z'\to gg\gamma$ is dominated by them, and that the exotic contribution to $\Gamma(Z'\to gg\gamma)$ can exceed $\Gamma(Z'\to ggg)$ by more than an order of magnitude — is well motivated by a genuinely clean argument: the photon is Abelian (so the axial/f$_{abc}$ piece drops out), and the amplitude carries an overall $Q_q Q_{q'}$ factor that makes the $T$ quark interfere **constructively** with $D,S$ (opposite sign to the $ggg$ case). That charge-sign flip is the heart of the paper, and it's a legitimate new observation.

I checked the arithmetic and it is **internally consistent**: in Table I the SM pieces sum correctly ($2.34+2.4+0.58\times10^{-6}=5.32\times10^{-6}$; $+8.36\times10^{-6}=1.37\times10^{-5}$), and the branching ratio $1.37\times10^{-5}\,\text{GeV}/\Gamma_Z(\text{PDG})\approx5.49\times10^{-6}$ is reproduced. The same holds for Tables II and III. So there are no computational slips in the numbers as presented. Good.

## Strengths

- **Correct and well-presented physics.** The transversality conditions, the UV-finiteness, and the separation of vector/axial amplitudes are handled properly; the Abelian argument for eliminating the axial part is right.
- **Clear structure.** Decomposition into $\Gamma_{q_i}+\Gamma_{Q_i}+\Gamma_{q_i-Q_i}$ and the family-by-family tables make the results easy to audit.
- **A real physical insight** that inverts the $ggg$ conclusion for $gg\gamma$ due to the exotic charges.

## Weaknesses / points to push on

1. **It is thin on new technical content.** Essentially every expression delegates to Ref. [1]; this paper adds only the $Q_q Q_{q'}$ and statistical factors plus a photon. As a standalone contribution it is a numerical/phenomenological note with no derivation, no cross-check against the SM $Z\to gg\gamma$ result of [11], and no discussion of the decoupling/non-decoupling behaviour (explicitly deferred to [1]). That deferral is the main thing keeping this from feeling complete.

2. **A fragile parameter choice is doing all the heavy lifting.** The headline "one order of magnitude" claim rests on the single scenario $\{m_{Z'}=1.5\ \text{TeV},\ m_Q=m_{D,S,T}=700\ \text{GeV}\}$. That places the $Z'$ just above the $2m_Q$ threshold, so the total $Z'$ width (and hence every branching ratio) is dominated by near-threshold $Z'\to Q\bar Q$ physics. Fig. 2 indeed shows a sharp threshold spike right at $m_{Z'}\simeq1.4$ TeV, but it is **only plotted, never discussed**, and the dependence on the *exotic mass itself* is entirely absent. Robustness of the claim should be established by a scan over $m_Q$ (or at least stated as a caveat); right now the order-of-magnitude statement is only as strong as this one point.

3. **No phenomenology, no experimental hook.** $10^{-4}$ is a non-trivial BR, but there's no discussion of experimental reach, detector/trigger considerations, or how this channel competes with existing $Z'$ search limits and benchmarks. The "this is a probe of exotic quarks" message would land much harder with at least one sentence of detector realism.

4. **Narrow-width approximation.** The $Z'$ total width at 1.5 TeV is large and broad (hundreds of GeV from Ref. [8]); a sentence justifying how this enters the BR would strengthen the argument.

5. **Minor:** Ref. [10] lists "Nucl. Phys. B313, 237 (**1090**)" — a typo for 1990. The abstract's "$\Gamma(Z'\to gg\gamma)$ larger than $\Gamma(Z'\to ggg)$" should carry the "for a heavy $Z'$" qualifier (it does in the body but not cleanly in the abstract, which reads as unconditional).

## Verdict for the student

Competent, correct, and honestly written physics — but a **minor, incremental** letter that leans entirely on prior work, commits to one arbitrary heavy-$Z'$ point, and leaves its own most important near-threshold feature (Fig. 2's spike) unexplained. To elevate it: (i) sweep the exotic mass and show the claim survives, (ii) discuss the $2m_Q$ threshold behaviour explicitly, and (iii) add a short experimental-reach paragraph. None of these are deep technical challenges; they're the difference between a note and a defensible result.
