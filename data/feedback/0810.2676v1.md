## [0810.2676v1-general-review] General Review


# Review of: "Dynamical simulations of polaron transport in conjugated polymers with the inclusion of electron-electron interactions" (Ma & Schollwöck, 2008)

## Summary of the Argument

The paper presents dynamical simulations of polaron transport in a one-dimensional conjugated polymer chain under a smooth turn-on of an external electric field. The model combines the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) Hamiltonian with the extended Hubbard model (EHM), treating the π-electrons quantum-mechanically via time-dependent density-matrix renormalization group (TDDMRG) and the lattice backbone classically via molecular dynamics. The central questions addressed are: (1) what is the relationship between stationary polaron velocity and applied electric field? (2) how do on-site (U) and nearest-neighbor (V) electron-electron interactions modify polaron transport compared to the standard SSH model?

The main findings are: an ohmic-like linear regime for v_p(E₀) between 3 and 9 mV/Å; a maximum drift velocity of ~3V_S (well above the sound speed); polaron dissociation above ~10 mV/Å; monotonic suppression of transport by increasing U; and a non-monotonic effect of V (small V suppresses, large V favors transport).

---

## Critique

### Strengths

1. **Methodological soundness of the approach.** The combination of adaptive TDDMRG for the quantum electrons with classical MD for the backbone is well-motivated by the large mass mismatch and has been validated in other 1D quantum many-body settings referenced in the paper. Going beyond Hartree-Fock to include correlation effects at the TDDMRG level is a genuine improvement over previous SSH-EHM/UHF work (Di et al.).

2. **Physically meaningful improvement over SSH-only models.** The threshold field for dissociation (~10 mV/Å) is substantially closer to the experimental value (~15 mV/Å) than the SSH-only predictions (6 mV/Å from Rakhmanova & Conwell, 3.5 mV/Å from Johansson & Stafström). This is a concrete and important result demonstrating that electron-electron interactions are not a quantitative nit but a qualitative ingredient.

3. **Systematic parameter study.** The decomposition of the U and V effects (Section III.C, III.D) and their combined study (III.E) gives a structured picture of the parameter space, and the connection to polaron width W_c provides a unifying physical narrative.

4. **Clear physical interpretation.** The central narrative — that polaron transport velocity is governed by the delocalization extent of the defect — is coherent and is supported by multiple independent observables (charge width, bond-order parameter, velocity).

---

### Weaknesses and Missing Elements

#### 1. Critical methodological details are omitted

The TDDMRG bond dimension D is never stated, nor is the Trotter step size or the truncation error monitor. Without these, the reader cannot assess whether the electronic correlations are adequately captured, especially at the strong-correlation parameters (U = 4.0 eV) where correlation lengths grow. This is arguably the single most important omission for assessing reliability. If D was kept fixed across all parameter values, the results at large U may be in the under-resolved regime.

#### 2. Finite-size and boundary effects

The chain contains N = 100 sites and the polaron starts at sites 30–31. At v_p ≈ 0.5 Å/fs for 160 fs, the polaron center moves ~80 sites. The right-hand boundary is never far from the polaron at later times, particularly at high fields where the charge accelerates rapidly. No discussion is given of boundary effects, nor of convergence with N. Periodic boundary conditions were presumably not used (they would be inconsistent with the external-electric-field term), but this is not made explicit. The readers are left without assurance that the "stationary" velocity is not an artifact of a finite chain or of the polaron approaching the edge.

#### 3. The "ohmic" terminology is imprecise

True ohmic behavior in a solid refers to a linear current–field relationship in a many-particle system. Here one observes a single quasi-particle reaching a steady drift velocity under a constant force, with the "friction" supplied by phonon emission. This is more analogous to saturated drift velocity (e.g., hot-electron physics in semiconductors) or to a single-particle Langevin problem. Calling the regime "ohmic" risks misleading readers into thinking a macroscopic linear I–V characteristic has been established in the SSH-EHM model. A more precise description would be "linear drag regime" or "steady drift."

#### 4. Insufficient discussion of what limits the maximum velocity

The authors note that v_p reaches ~3V_S and that this is "well above" the speed of sound, but do not analyze the physical mechanism setting the upper bound. In the SSH-only model, Basso & Conwell found v_max ≈ V_S (the sound speed as a natural upper limit), while Johansson & Stafström found ~4V_S in a non-adiabatic treatment. The fact that the present work gives ~3V_S sits between these, but no physical discussion is offered of whether the limit is (a) the point at which phonon emission can no longer keep up with the electron, (b) the onset of the Cherenkov-like radiation of phonons by a superluminal soliton, or (c) something else entirely. Given that the EHM modifies the electronic dispersion and the polaron width, the sound-speed analogy is less direct and deserves a paragraph of interpretation.

#### 5. The V-dependence study at U = 0 is artificial and under-discussed

The authors explicitly state (p. 15) that setting U = 0 "is not realistic." The non-monotonic V effect is the most physically interesting finding in the paper, yet it is established in an unphysical parameter regime and then only lightly extended to finite U (Fig. 13). The claim that large V "favors transport" via "electron-hole attraction" is intuitive but not quantitatively tested — for example, does the crossover point V ≈ 1.2 eV shift with U? The combined U–V study at fixed E₀ = 3 mV/Å (Fig. 13) shows v_p decreasing with U for all V, but no 2D map or contour plot is provided. A more complete exploration of the (U, V) plane, particularly at the physically relevant values, would substantially strengthen the paper.

#### 6. No energy or entropy analysis

The work-energy balance is never discussed. The field does work on the charge; the excess energy must go into phonons (the oscillatory tail) and into internal excitation of the charge. Quantifying the fraction of the polaron's kinetic energy stored in the radiated phonon wake would illuminate the mechanism of velocity saturation and provide a direct comparison to experimentally measurable quantities (e.g., Raman shifts from incoherent phonon scattering). The "tail" (Fig. 2) is identified only qualitatively as inertia; a Fourier analysis of its oscillation spectrum would connect it to the phonon density of states and could reveal whether the velocity saturation is tied to a specific phonon emission channel.

#### 7. Limited temporal analysis of the transient regime

The initial acceleration phase (first ~20–30 fs) is shown in figures but never analyzed. How does the acceleration scale with E₀? Is there a characteristic time constant (a "polari­nic relaxation time")? For the ohmic region, one would expect v(t) ∝ at initially and then v → v_ss; extracting the acceleration and the relaxation time as a function of E₀ would sharpen the claim of linearity. The 40-fs time-step used in the figures (Figs. 1, 2, 6, 7) is coarse for resolving the transient.

#### 8. Disagreement with prior work is treated too briefly

The discrepancy between the present threshold (~10 mV/Å) and Johansson & Stafström's (~3.5 mV/Å) is attributed solely to the absence of electron-electron interactions in their work. However, other methodological differences exist: they used the standard SSH with a specific chain length, a different definition of "dissociation," a different initial condition (possibly a pre-formed polaron vs. a Gaussian pulse), and possibly a different field-turn-on protocol. A more careful comparison, ideally reproducing the SSH-only results with the present TDDMRG+MD setup and showing the effect of adding EHM terms, would make the comparison definitive rather than suggestive. The same applies to the Rakhmanova & Conwell comparison (6 mV/Å) and the Di et al. UHM/UHF comparison (9.5 mV/Å).

#### 9. The role of the polaron initial condition

The polaron is initialized as a static, adiabatic solution (imposed at t = 0 by reflection symmetry at sites 30–31). The authors do not discuss the sensitivity of results to the initial condition — for instance, does a polaron initialized with some velocity (a non-stationary state) reach the same steady velocity? Are there dynamical instabilities for fields below the "threshold" if the initial condition is perturbed? The question of whether the observed threshold is a true thermodynamic phase transition or a dynamical crossover is not addressed.

#### 10. Minor issues

- Typo in the abstract: "We find that **the the** on-site Coulomb interactions" (double "the").
- The phrase "we adopt a newly developed real-time simulation method" (p. 5) is vague. What is "new" about it? If it is the specific TDDMRG+MD coupling scheme, a sentence describing the key novelty would help the reader.
- The field profile in Eq. 4 is a half-Gaussian with a smooth turn-on, but the turn-on width T_W = 25 fs and center T_C = 30 fs are taken from Fu et al. without justification for the present parameters. A small convergence test (T_W = 10, 25, 50 fs) would confirm that the results are not sensitive to the turn-on profile.
- Figure 4 shows only ~10 data points and the "linear region" is identified by eye. A linear fit with error bars would make the claim more quantitative.
- The paper does not discuss the effect of the Gaussian turn-on on the extracted "stationary" velocity — is it measured at the end of the 160-fs window, or does the velocity still show a small secular drift? Fig. 3 shows a slight curvature, especially at later times, which could affect the extracted slope.

---

### Overall Assessment

The paper makes a methodologically reasonable and physically motivated contribution: it demonstrates that including electron-electron interactions via TDDMRG in polaron transport calculations shifts the prediction for the dissociation threshold in the direction of experiment and reveals a non-monotonic V effect not captured by UHF. The central physical picture (delocalization controls velocity) is clear and supported.

However, the paper is limited by (a) the absence of key methodological validation (TDDMRG bond dimension, finite-size convergence, turn-on convergence), (b) a somewhat narrow parameter-range study that is explicitly stated to be "not for any specific polymer," and (c) the lack of deeper physical analysis of the velocity saturation mechanism and the energy balance. The results are convincing within the stated assumptions but lack the depth of physical interpretation and methodological validation that would elevate the work from "demonstration that the method works and gives reasonable numbers" to "understanding of the physics of polaron transport with correlations."

The impact is moderate: it provides a useful numerical benchmark for the SSH-EHM model and a qualitative argument for why electron-electron interactions must be included in future polaron transport studies, but the findings (U suppresses, V has non-monotonic effect, threshold ~10 mV/Å) do not open new directions of research. The TDDMRG+MD combination, while solid, is not the main novelty — it was already established by Zhao et al. [48]. The physics content is incremental within the established SSH/EHM framework.

**Suggested major revisions before acceptance:**
1. State the TDDMRG bond dimension and truncation error.
2. Add a finite-size convergence test (N = 60, 100, 150).
3. Justify or test the field turn-on profile dependence.
4. Quantify the "ohmic" regime with a linear fit and error bars.
5. Add a paragraph on the physical mechanism of velocity saturation (phonon radiation, Cherenkov criterion).
6. Discuss the energy balance (work done by field, phonon excitation, polaron internal energy).

# Peer review

**Paper:** Ma & Schollwöck, *Dynamical simulations of polaron transport in conjugated polymers with the inclusion of electron–electron interactions* (arXiv:0810.2676v1).

Overall this is a competent, well-organized computational study. It combines the adiative-TDDMRG treatment of the π-electrons with classical MD for the backbone to treat polaron transport in the SSH + extended-Hubbard model, and draws a coherent physical picture. There is one real methodological gap (no convergence/error analysis for an approximate method) and a few points where the claims outrun the evidence. Details below.

## What the paper does well

1. **Right tool for the problem.** Replacing the unrestricted Hartree–Fock level (Di et al. [23]) with TDDMRG so that genuine electron correlation is included is a genuine and useful step. The payoff is visible: where UHF predicts a non-monotonic stationary velocity with a local extremum at U ≈ 2V, the correlated calculation gives a clean monotonic decrease in U. Attributing that discrepancy to the neglected correlation (and citing static studies [34,35,36,37] that show UHF over-polarizes / over-localizes) is a reasonable and defensible interpretation, and is the paper's most valuable conceptual point.

2. **Useful threshold result.** The computed dissociation field (~10 mV/Å for U = 2.0 eV, V = 1.0 eV), sitting between the SSH-only values (6 mV/Å, Rakhmanova & Conwell; 3.5 mV/Å, Johansson & Stafström) and the experimental ~15 mV/Å [50], is a clean illustration that e–e interactions matter for charge-carrier thresholds.

3. **Consistent delocalization story.** Linking velocity to polaron width W_c (Eq. 8), and explaining the non-monotonic V dependence via V-induced charge polarization (Fig. 11–12) at large V is physically sensible and internally consistent. The contrast between the monotonic-U, non-monotonic-V polaron and the earlier charged-soliton result [49] is a nice touch and makes sense (more delocalized defect, lower peak charge density).

4. Clear figures and an honest statement that U = 0 in the V study is unphysical and done only to isolate V's effect.

## Issues and concerns

1. **No TDDMRG convergence or uncertainty analysis — the main gap.** For an approximate, variational/desired-truncation method the paper should at least report the bond dimension, the discarded norm (truncation error), and a demonstration that results (velocity, W_c, threshold) are insensitive to these choices. Nothing of the sort is shown. Equally, every result appears to be a single deterministic realization; there are no error bars, no ensemble, no finite-T. At 100 sites with a single polaron, this should be flagged explicitly. I'd want a convergence check before trusting the *quantitative* velocity values and the precise 10 mV/Å number.

2. **The supersonic regime is asserted, not analyzed.** v_p reaches ~3 v_S (Fig. 4; 0.46 vs. 0.15 Å/fs). A charge defect moving at three times the lattice sound speed should generically radiate phonons (a Cherenkov-like channel); the fact that the polaron stays coherent for 160 fs deserves a real analysis, not just the note that an oscillatory "tail" carries away the extra energy. The classical MD of the backbone does in principle allow that radiation, but the paper never quantifies it (e.g., phonon spectrum behind the defect, or a plot of the defect width/stability vs. field), so the stability of the supersonic polaron is effectively hand-waved. This is the regime of greatest physical interest and it is the least rigorously treated.

3. **"Good agreement with experiment" is over-claimed.** 10 vs. 15 mV/Å is a ~30% underestimate. "Closer and more consistent than the SSH-only values" is fair and should be said; "good agreement" is stronger than the data support.

4. **Sound-speed formula is misprinted.** You write v_S = √(4K/Ma)/2 = 0.15 Å/fs. A uniform 1D chain with spacing a and bond constant K has v_S = a√(K/M) ≈ 1.22 × √(21/1349.14) = 0.152 Å/fs, which does reproduce the *number* you quote — but the *expression* as typeset evaluates to √(K/Na)·…≈0.11. The number is right; the formula is not. Please correct the expression (a√(K/M)) so a reader can reproduce it. Also clarify your convention: with two sites per SSH unit cell, is 0.15 defined from the site spacing a or the unit-cell length? A one-line statement removes the ambiguity.

5. **Scope limits not emphasized.** Single chain, single polaron, coherent (effectively 0 K) dynamics, no disorder, no ensemble. For organic electronics the relevant regime is finite T and doped ensembles. I'd like a sentence or two stating that the conclusions are intended as the coherent, single-defect limit and may not carry over directly to thermal transport.

6. **Small matters.** Abstract/figure typos ("the the," "polaron center postion" in the Fig. 5 text). The polaron center position is extracted by a Fourier phase (Eq. 6–7); this is reasonable but you should note where it can fail — precisely the high-field dissociation case, where the charge and distortion split (Figs. 5–7) and the single-winding-number phase becomes ill-defined.

## Verdict

A solid, publishable study that applies the correct method to the problem and adds real (if modest) new content: the correlated vs. UHF difference, the non-monotonic V dependence, and a threshold closer to experiment than SSH-only work. It is not a methodological breakthrough — TDDMRG for this model was already established (Zhao et al. [48]) — so the novelty is incremental-to-moderate. The two things that hold it back from being a strong 5 are the absence of any convergence/uncertainty discussion for an approximate method, and the under-analyzed supersonic regime. Fix the sound-speed expression, temper the "good agreement" claim, and add a TDDMRG convergence check, and it becomes a clearly good paper.

# Critique

**Paper:** *Dynamical simulations of polaron transport in conjugated polymers with the inclusion of electron‑electron interactions* — H. Ma & U. Schollwöck (arXiv:0810.2676, RWTH Aachen).

## What the paper does
It simulates a single polaron on a 100‑site chain (SSH + extended Hubbard + time‑dependent uniform electric field). The π‑electrons are evolved with adaptive TDDMRG and the C‑H backbone with classical MD, iteratively. They track the polaron center (charge and bond‑order), extract a "stationary" velocity, and map it against field strength and against the on‑site repulsion *U* and nearest‑neighbor interaction *V*. Main results: acceleration then a quasi‑steady velocity; an "ohmic" region (~3–9 mV/Å at U=2.0, V=1.0 eV); supersonic motion (~3 V_S); and polaron dissociation at E₀ ≳ 10 mV/Å, which they argue brings the threshold closer to the experimental ~15 mV/Å than previous SSH‑only (6 or 3.5 mV/Å) or UHF‑EHM (9.5 mV/Å) calculations. *U* monotonically suppresses transport; *V* is non‑monotonic.

## Strengths
- **Well‑motivated gap.** EHM+SSH polaron dynamics beyond mean field (correlations) was a genuine open item, and doing the *real‑time* version with a moving backbone and applied field is a coherent extension of Di et al ([23], UHF) and Zhao et al ([48], TDDMRG but less complete).
- **The cross‑method narrative is the paper's best feature.** Comparing SSH‑adiabatic, SSH‑nonadiabatic, UHF, and TDDMRG thresholds, and attributing the improvement (10 vs 6 vs 3.5 mV/Å) to the inclusion of e‑e interactions/correlations, is a reasonable and clean argument.
- **Method is appropriately matched to the problem.** Adaptive TDDMRG is the right tool for 1D correlated real‑time dynamics at affordable cost; treating the slow lattice as a coupled classical bath is a defensible approximation.
- The *V* sweep and the *U*, *V* combined study (Figs 10–13) add value beyond the single‑point field sweep.

## Weaknesses

**1. The experimental comparison is weaker than it is advertised.** The authors themselves state U=2.0 eV, V=1.0 eV are "not the standard values for any special kind of polymers," chosen only to "show how polaronic transport will generally evolve." That means they did *not* fit *U,V* to trans‑polyacetylene and then independently predict the dissociation threshold. Reporting "good agreement with experiment" (10 vs 15 mV/Å) with deliberately un‑fit, generic parameters is optimistic — the improvement over SSH could be attributed to the addition of the full EHM terms as much as to the correlation treatment. The threshold is a single number with no uncertainty, on a single event.

**2. Substantial numerical detail is missing for a paper whose selling point is the numerical method.** Nowhere is the maximum bond dimension (truncation), the truncation error, the time step, or the MD integrator given. There are no convergence tests and no error bars anywhere (the data sets in Figs 4, 8, 10, 13 are only a handful of points). For a TDDMRG study, reporting the truncation quality is essential to trust that "correlations" — not a too‑small bond dimension — is responsible for the shift in the threshold.

**3. The classical lattice is treated too casually.** With K=21 eV/Å² and M=1349 eV·fs²/Å², the acoustic phonon quantum is ≈ ℏω = 0.658·√(21/1349) ≈ 0.08 eV — several times k_BT at room temperature. A purely classical backbone with no zero‑point or quantum phonon effects, over a 160 fs window, is not justified and the limitations (no phonon quantization, no temperature/noise/ensemble averaging) are not discussed. Real polaron motion is thermally activated and stochastic; a single trajectory at T=0 is a coarse proxy.

**4. The headline "supersonic" result is under‑analyzed.** A polaron carrying a classical lattice distortion at ~3 V_S ought to be limited by phonon emission (a Cherenkov/Cherenkov‑like drag) — and the paper actually *shows* the signature (the oscillatory "tail" of monomers that absorbs energy). But it never identifies this as the momentum/energy‑dissipation mechanism, does no energy balance, and doesn't connect the tail dynamics to the speed limit. As written, "moves faster than the speed of sound" reads as a curiosity rather than a physically understood regime.

**5. The "center position" extraction is ad hoc.** Eqs (6)–(7) define the polaron center through an arctan/wrap formula that is essentially a phase estimate of the staggered pattern, never validated against the simpler moment definition. Meanwhile the width W_c (Eq 8) uses a *first moment* X_c, not the arctan center — so the center used to compute velocity and the center used to compute the width are subtly inconsistent. The "ohmic" fit is visual, over ~7 points.

**6. Simulation length/boundaries.** The "stationary" velocity is read from ≤160 fs on a finite chain; the polaron is near the right edge by the end (Fig 6 shows charge running off the chain). Whether the velocity is genuinely stationary or merely pre‑edge‑effect is not established, and boundary conditions (periodic vs fixed for the lattice) are not fully specified.

**7. Modest novelty.** The qualitative physics (U localizes and suppresses; correlations enlarge the defect width vs UHF; a local extremum of v_p in U is a UHF artifact) was largely anticipated by [23] and static studies [34–37]. The paper's contribution is the consistent correlated real‑time treatment, not a new mechanism.

## Verdict
A competent, internally consistent computational study that closes a real gap (correlated real‑time EHM+SSH polaron dynamics under a field) and makes a credible, well‑framed case that electron‑electron interactions matter. Its central claim is somewhat softened by the self‑admittedly generic parameter choice, and it is held back by the absence of the convergence/numerical detail its emphasis on TDDMRG calls for, by an under‑discussed classical‑lattice approximation, single‑event statistics, and by shallow analysis of its most striking results (supersonic motion, ohmic regime). Solid work for the subfield, but not a breakthrough.

## [0810.2676v1-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Paper under review

**"Dynamical simulations of polaron transport in conjugated polymers with the inclusion of electron‑electron interactions"** — Haibo Ma & Ulrich Schollwöck (RWTH Aachen), arXiv:0810.2676, cond-mat.mtrl-sci, Oct 2008 (21 pp.).

## Stated contribution, in my own words

The authors run real‑time simulations (up to 160 fs) of a single polaron traveling along a model 100‑site conjugated‑polymer chain under a smooth‑turn‑on electric field, using a combined Su–Schrieffer–Heeger (SSH) + extended‑Hubbard (EHM) model: the π‑electron wave function is evolved with adaptive TDDMRG, the classical lattice backbone with molecular dynamics. The purpose is to give a full picture — at the correlated, beyond‑mean‑field level — of how the on‑site interaction **U** and the nearest‑neighbor interaction **V** each affect polaron transport. The key reported results: (i) the polaron accelerates, then cruises as a coupled charge‑plus‑deformation entity, with a linear ("ohmic") v–E region at 3–9 mV/Å for U = 2.0 eV, V = 1.0 eV and a maximum speed ~3× the sound speed; (ii) above ~10 mV/Å the charge outruns its lattice distortion and the polaron dissociates; (iii) vp decreases *monotonically* with U (consistent with prior UHF and Zhao et al.'s TDDMRG work, but unlike the authors' own companion finding that *charged solitons* are non‑monotonic in U); (iv) vp is *non‑monotonic* in V — small V localizes the defect and slows it, but at larger V the induced neighboring electron–hole attraction delocalizes it and speeds it up; and (v) with both interactions, the monotonic U‑suppression is stronger for larger V.

## Novelty judgment against the cited prior work

**What is genuinely new:**

1. **A correlated (beyond‑SCF) treatment of the *extended* Hubbard model in polaron *dynamics*.** This is the real gap this paper fills. Zhao et al. [48] ran the same MD+TDDMRG machinery but only with on‑site U. Di et al. [23] included V, but purely at the unrestricted Hartree–Fock level. Nobody among the cited works had V *and* electron correlation simultaneously.
2. **The non‑monotonic V dependence (minimum near V ≈ 1.2 eV, recovery at larger V) and its mechanism** — electron–hole attraction at neighboring sites promoting hopping — directly contradicts Di et al.'s UHF result that V monotonically suppresses velocity and that a local extremum sits at U ≈ 2V. The authors attribute the discrepancy to electron correlation (citing [34–37] for the systematic under‑localization by mean‑field), which is a substantively new physical claim, not just a new number.
3. **Contrast between polarons and charged solitons in the U dependence** (monotonic vs. non‑monotonic) versus the authors' own companion paper [49] — a clean, new qualitative distinction with a proposed physical rationale (charge peak height/delocalization).
4. **New quantitative parameters in the correlated model**: the dissociation threshold (~10 mV/Å, sitting between Di et al.'s UHF 9.5 and the experimental ~15 mV/Å [50]), the linear region boundaries, and the width–velocity correlation (Wc decreasing with U and for small V).

**What is *not* new:**

A competent, well‑executed application paper that extends an existing technique (its own and Zhao et al.'s) to an existing model. The genuinely new content is the correlated V‑dependence study, whose non‑monotonicity overturns the UHF prediction — a clean but narrow contribution; everything else (method, model, qualitative scenarios, U‑suppression) is inherited from the cited literature.

## A. Methodology, at reproduction level

**Model.** The system is a 1D chain of N = 100 π-electron sites with Nₑ = 99 π-electrons (net charge +1 relative to one electron per site, i.e., a hole polaron; S = ½). The total time-dependent Hamiltonian is H(t) = H_el + H_E(t) + H_latt.

**Electronic part (extended Hubbard + SSH, Eq. 2):**

H_el = −Σ_{n,σ} t_{n,n+1}(c†_{n+1,σ} c_{n,σ} + h.c.) + (U/2)Σ_{n,σ}(n_{n,σ} − ½)(n_{n,−σ} − ½) + V Σ_{n,σ,σ′}(n_{n,σ} − ½)(n_{n+1,σ′} − ½),

with hopping linearized in the bond stretch: t_{n,n+1} = t₀ − α(u_{n+1} − uₙ).

**Lattice part (Eq. 3):** H_latt = (K/2)Σₙ(u_{n+1} − uₙ)² + (M/2)Σₙ u̇ₙ², evolved by classical Newtonian MD; forces on the displacements come from the instantaneous quantum electronic energy.

**Field (Eqs. 4–5):** uniform axial field with a smooth half-Gaussian turn-on, E(t) = E₀ exp[−(t−T_C)²/T_W²] for t < T_C, E₀ for t ≥ T_C, with T_C = 30 fs, T_W = 25 fs (borrowed from Fu et al. [18]). The field energy couples site-by-site: H_E(t) = |e| Σ_{n,σ}(n a + uₙ)(n̂_{n,σ} − ½)E(t).

**Fixed parameters (standard trans-polyacetylene, ref [22]):** t₀ = 2.5 eV, α = 4.1 eV/Å, K = 21 eV/Å², M = 1349.14 eV·fs²/Å² (≈ 13 amu, one CH monomer per site), a = 1.22 Å. Model sound speed v_s = √(4K/M)·a/2 = 0.15 Å/fs.

**Setup and initial condition.** The polaron is centered on the bond between sites 30 and 31 by imposing a constraint of reflection symmetry about that midpoint on the initial state. The chain is then evolved 160 fs.

**Dynamics scheme.** A "newly developed" iterated scheme: at each step, the π-electron matrix-product state is evolved by adaptive TDDMRG under H_el + H_E at the current displacements, while the displacements are advanced by classical MD. The paper gives no algorithmic detail and defers everything to refs [48] (Zhao et al., PRB 78, 035209 — which does the same thing without V) and [49] (a companion paper, *still "submitted"* at the time of this v1).

**Observables and analysis.**

2. **π electrons only, one site per monomer**, free charge |e| per site, background neutrality at one electron per site (hidden in the −½ shifts). No σ–π mixing, no next-nearest-neighbor hopping, no on-site potential, no higher-order phonon terms; linear t(u) only.

12. The 160 fs simulated window is orders of magnitude below ps–ns experimental transport times; "stationary" is an extrapolation from a short interval with no fitting protocol reported.

**Numerical/methodological assumptions:**
13. **The MD + adaptive-TDDMRG coupling algorithm is entirely in refs [48, 49],** one of which was unpublished at submission; all convergence knobs (time steps, χ, truncation) are unspecified, and A-DMRG's known norm/entanglement drift over 160 fs is never quantified.
14. **Boundary conditions unstated**, while the center-of-mass formula implies periodic wrapping. But the paper is more "demonstration with borrowed parameters" than a quantitative method: the central numbers live on an explicitly non-physical (U, V) pair, the method section defers all reproducible content to two external papers (one unpublished), and no numerical uncertainty is reported anywhere.

Originality: an extension of Zhao et al.'s existing EHM+SSH+TDDMRG machinery (adding V and a U–V sweep), on an established method — solid but incremental. Impact: a modestly useful study inside a narrow subfield (1D real-time charge-carrier dynamics); it neither shaped the DMRG community nor the organic-transport community.

## Understanding of the paper's logical argument

The paper (Ma & Schollwöck, arXiv:0810.2676, 2008) sets out to study **the dynamics of a polaron defect in a 1D conducting polymer under an applied external electric field**, treating both electron-photon coupling and electron-electron (e-e) interactions simultaneously.

The argument runs as follows:

1. **Motivation / gap in the literature.** Polaron dynamics in conjugated polymers has usually been studied with the Su–Schrieffer–Heeger (SSH) model alone. That model captures π-electron–lattice coupling but **ignores e-e interactions entirely**, even though the statics literature on conjugated polymers shows e-e correlations matter. Prior SSH-only studies also disagree with each other (Basko–Conwell: polaron speed cannot exceed the sound speed *v_s*; Rakhmanova–Conwell: adiabatic, threshold field ~6 mV/Å; Johansson–Stafström: non-adiabatic, peak ~4*v_s* at ~3.5 mV/Å) and with experiment (~15 mV/Å). So both the model and the method should be improved.

2. **Model & method.** The combined Su–Schrieffer–Heeger + extended Hubbard (EHM) Hamiltonian is used, with on-site *U* and nearest-neighbor *V* interactions, plus a smoothly turned-on uniform external field *E(t)* (Gaussian pulse). The π-electron part is evolved quantum-mechanically with the **adaptive time-dependent DMRG (TDDMRG)**; the lattice part with **classical molecular dynamics**. This hybrid is taken from the same authors' recent work (Zha et al., ref. 48) and is described as "newly developed." Realistic PA parameters are used for the SSH part; *U* and *V*=2.0/1.0 eV are chosen to fall in the "weak-interaction" regime of conjugated polymers.

3. **Results structure (three questions are asked, in order):**
   - **(a) *General picture of transport* (Sec. III.A):** Figs. 1–3 show that the polaron (charge density peak + dimerisation pattern) moves rigidly as a single quasiparticle; the two "centers" track each other over 160 fs, with only a transient acceleration followed by a nearly constant velocity. An oscillatory lattice "tail" is left behind, attributed to lattice inertia and energy/momentum conservation — the tail is the physical damping mechanism.
   - **(b) *Stationary velocity vs field* (Sec. III.B):** Fig. 4 shows a **nearly ohmic linear region for 3–9 mV/Å**, a sub-linear regime below that, and **polaron dissociation above ~10 mV/Å** (Figs. 5–7) in which the charge outruns the lattice distortion by many sites. The 10 mV/Å threshold agrees with Di et al.'s UHF result (9.5 mV/Å) and is quantitatively closer to experiment (15 mV/Å) than the SSH-only values.
   - **(c) *Influence of e-e interactions* (Secs. III.C–E):**
     - **U alone** (Fig. 8): *v_p* decreases *monotonically* with U; the polaron width *W_c* shrinks from 10.10 (U=0) to 7.62 sites (U=4 eV), corroborating a localization argument (Fig. 9). The contrast with the authors' *earlier* result for charged **solitons** (non-monotonic in U, ref. 49) is explained by the polaron being more delocalised than a soliton.
     - **V alone** (Fig. 10, Figs. 11–12): the velocity is **non-monotonic** in *V*, with a shallow minimum near *V*≈1.2 eV. Small *V* localizes the defect and reduces *v_p*; large *V* produces strong charge polarisation and the induced e–h attraction across near-neighbour sites favours hopping and increases *v_p*.
     - **U and V together** (Fig. 13): *v_p* still decreases monotonically with *U* for fixed *V*, but the *rate* of suppression increases with *V* (large-charge-polarisation defects are more sensitive to U). The authors attribute the UHF prediction of a local minimum at U≈2V to the missing electron-correlation physics of the UHF method; TDDMRG gives a monotonic curve instead.

4. **Conclusion (Sec. IV).** The combined SSH+EHM model with TDDMRG+MD gives a coherent, internally consistent picture: transport of a polaron, an ohmic regime, super-sonic velocities at high field, a dissociation threshold, and a qualitatively and semi-quantitatively reasonable agreement with experiment. The qualitative trends are linked to a single measurable quantity — the static polaron width *W_c* — i.e., the mechanism is "delocalisation ↔ mobility".

### Strengths

- **Problem choice is well motivated.** Combining SSH + EHM and asking specifically how *V* (not just *U*) affects polaron *dynamics* is a genuine gap the paper fills.
- **Method is a real advance for real-time dynamics.** Coupling TDDMRG to classical MD is a clean way to push polaron *dynamics* past the mean-field level; the authors are honest that this is a "newly developed" method and cross-reference prior TDDMRG applications (magnetisation, spin–charge separation, ultracold atoms) to establish credibility.

1. **Arbitrary *U* and *V*.** The authors acknowledge this but don't go further: there is no parameter sweep or fit to a *specific* real polymer to demonstrate whether the qualitative conclusions (ohmic region, non-monotonic *V*) survive a realistic choice. The conclusion "large *V* favours polaron transport" is a strong claim that should ideally be tied to a named material.

2. **The classical-lattice approximation is not discussed.** Treating the backbone as purely classical in a coherent, low-temperature polaron problem is a known approximation with real consequences (zero-point motion, phonon occupation, phonon quantum back-action). The paper offers no estimate of how large the error is. This is probably the single biggest unaddressed assumption.

7. **The non-monotonic *V* result is explained qualitatively (e–h attraction / charge polarisation) rather than quantitatively.** A small additional analysis — e.g., the induced charge polarisation as a function of *V*, or the width of the central charge-density peak directly — would make the argument more solid.

8. **Missing comparison with other correlated methods.** No direct comparison to, e.g., real-time DMRG without MD, or exact diagonalisation on short chains (N ≤ 16) with identical *U, V* where TDDMRG can be checked quantitatively. That would be the clean check that the observed monotonic-*U* / non-monotonic-*V* trends are genuinely correlation effects and not numerical artifacts of the MD+TDDMRG hybrid.

9. **Minor writing issues.** A "Haibo Ma" vs "Hai-Bo Ma" inconsistency (author byline vs. metadata), reference [49] is "submitted" (not citable), no temperature is mentioned anywhere, and "nearly all relevant electron-phonon and electron-electron interactions are fully taken into account" in the abstract is a bit strong — only 1-NN e-e interactions are included.

10. **The tail-oscillation is qualitatively described but never quantitatively characterised** — its amplitude, energy, and field-dependence are the natural diagnostic of energy transfer from charge to lattice, and they are not given.

### Overall assessment

A solid, methodologically interesting paper from a strong group, addressing a genuine gap (e-e interactions in *dynamical* polaron transport) and giving results that are closer to experiment than prior SSH-only calculations. The physical narrative is clean and internally consistent. The main limitations are (i) the unexamined classical-lattice approximation, (ii) the lack of any parameter set tied to a real material, (iii) the thin analysis of the most striking observations (super-sonic regime, tail, dissociation), and (iv) missing convergence / benchmarking diagnostics. It is a clear, well-executed study but incremental in conceptual novelty — it extends the *method* rather than proposing a new physical picture of polaron transport.

## [0810.2676v1-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


## Evaluation

**Paper:** Ma & Schollwöck, "Dynamical simulations of polaron transport in conjugated polymers with the inclusion of electron-electron interactions" (arXiv:0810.2676, 2008).

**What the paper claims:** Using TDDMRG for the π-electrons combined with classical MD for the SSH backbone (a combined SSH + extended Hubbard model), they find (i) a polaron accelerates then settles to a nearly constant velocity as a charge+deformation entity; (ii) an "ohmic" region for 3–9 mV/Å with super-sonic velocities; (iii) polaron dissociation at E₀ ≳ 10 mV/Å; and (iv) that U monotonically suppresses, while large V promotes, transport — which they take as evidence that electron-electron interactions are *vital* for a realistic description.

The stationary velocity changes by ~15–20% across the U and V ranges shown, so the dissociation threshold itself is plausibly strongly parameter-dependent — but the paper never maps the threshold over (U, V) space. The "10 mV/Å" figure could shift substantially, and the claim of closeness to 15 mV/Å rests on one unscanned point.

3. **"Close" is doing a lot of work.** 10 vs. 15 mV/Å is a 33% discrepancy — hardly "in good agreement" for the central numerical claim of the paper. Meanwhile the only *independent* quantitative comparison, Di et al.'s 9.5 mV/Å (ref. 4. **Confounders are not ruled out.** The SSH-only benchmarks differ from this work in more ways than just the absence of e–e interactions: Rakhmanova & Conwell used an adiabatic (Born–Oppenheimer) treatment, Johansson & Stafström a different non-adiabatic scheme and setup, and all use different chain lengths, boundary conditions, and field profiles. A zero-temperature, 100-site, single-polaron, defect-free quantum simulation also doesn't capture the physical conditions of a macroscopic breakdown measurement (temperature, disorder, multiple carriers), so even the category being compared is mismatched.

In short: the paper's headline justification for the entire model extension ("the inclusion of electron-elec­tron interactions is vital") is built on a single-parameter-point number compared, without sensitivity analysis and without controlling for confounds, across a 33% gap to an experimental value whose material and regime differ from the simulation. The simulations themselves (Figs. 1–7) are internally consistent, but this inferential leap is not supported by its data.

## Secondary cracks (not the weakest, but real)

- **Mis-labeled "ohmic" region:** in Fig. 4 the slope of v_p(E) *decreases* from 3 to 9 mV/Å (roughly 0.046 vs. 0.012 Å/(fs·mV/Å) — the "ohmic" window is actually where the curve flattens). No fit, error bars, or residuals are shown.

- **Finite-size/finite-time:** at 120 fs the highest-field trajectories put the defect within ~15 sites of the end of a 100-site chain; no discussion of boundary or finite-size effects on the dissociation threshold.

## Verdict

A competent, methodologically sound numerical simulation on an established model, whose main interpretive overreach — the benchmark comparison used to declare e–e interactions "vital" — is not supported by the data presented. Incremental extension of the same group's prior TDDMRG/MD work (they had already done SSH+Hubbard by TDDMRG [48] and solitons [49]); limited field impact, with the conclusion itself (correlation matters in conjugated polymers) already long established.

The paper studies polaron transport in a single conjugated-polymer chain under a smooth, then constant, external electric field. 4. **The headline parameters are arbitrary, which weakens the quantitative comparisons.** You note that $U=2.0$ eV / $V=1.0$ eV are not tied to any real polymer.
