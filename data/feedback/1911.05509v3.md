## [1911.05509v3-general-review] General Review


# Review of "Grothendieck's Schematization Problem Revisited" (B. Toën, Épiga, 2020)

## Scope of the Paper

The paper addresses the schematization program of Grothendieck ([Gro]): for each topological space $X$, produce a sheaf of $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-rings (or at least of commutative ring sheaves) over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})$ that recovers, over $R = \mathbb{Q}$ and $R = \mathbb{F}_p$, the rational and $p$-completed homotopy type of $X$. This had been partially carried out in [To06, Man06, Eke02], but the explicit description of $(X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}}$ — in particular its homotopy groups — had remained conjectural (Conjecture 2.3.6 of [To06]). Toën now proves this conjecture and its consequences, for spaces that are simply connected and of finite type.

The central object is a newly-introduced group scheme $\mathbb{H} = \mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}$ (the *Hilbert additive group*), where $\mathcal{B} = \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}) \subset \mathbb{Q}[X]$ is the $\mathbb{Z}$-free algebra of *integer-valued polynomials*. The headline result (Theorem 4.1) says: for $X$ simply connected of finite type, there are natural isomorphisms of group sheaves
$$ \pi_i(X) \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{H} \simeq \pi_i\bigl((X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}}\bigr). $$

## Main Contributions

1. **Structural identification of $\mathbb{H}$** (Prop 2.2, Cor 2.3). The scheme $\mathbb{H}$ is identified as the sub-functor of the big Witt vector functor $\mathbb{W}$ consisting of *points fixed by all Frobenius endomorphisms* $F_n$. In particular, $\mathbb{H}|_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}/p^n)} \simeq \hat{\mathbb{Z}}_p/p^n$, the profinite group of $p$-adic integers mod $p^n$. This is a clean, memorable reformulation that makes the $p$-adic behaviour transparent and reduces later arguments to computations in Witt vectors.

2. **Affineness of $\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{H}, n)$** (Prop 3.1). This is the technical heart. It is proved using a decreasing filtration $\mathbb{H} \subset \mathbb{H}_2 \subset \cdots \subset \mathbb{H}_k \subset \mathbb{W}$, where $\mathbb{H}_k = \mathbb{W}^{F_{p_1},\ldots,F_{p_k}}$ is the sub-functor of points fixed by the Frobenius at the first $k$ primes, plus the classical flatness lemma (Lemma 3.2) on morphisms of affine $S$-group schemes that are flat over all residue fields. The argument that $\mathrm{Id} - F_{p_k} : \mathbb{H}_k \to \mathbb{H}_{k-1}$ is faithfully flat is delicate but correct.

3. **The formula itself** (Theorem 4.1) and its arithmetic consequence (Cor 5.1): evaluating at $\mathbb{Z}$, one gets
$$\pi_i((X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}})(\mathbb{Z}) \simeq \pi_i(X) \oplus \prod_p \pi_{i+1}(X)_p^{\wedge}, $$
so a single basepoint recovers the full rational plus all $p$-completions of the homotopy of $X$.

4. **Tannakian interpretation** (p. 11): $\mathbb{H}$ is the Tannakian dual of the $\infty$-category $\mathrm{QCoh}^{\mathrm{uni}}(S^1)$ (unipotent local systems on the circle), which gives the result a conceptual footing and explains the appearance of Witt vectors in the answer.

5. **Injectivity / faithfulness of the schematization functor** (Cor 5.5): on simply connected finite-type spaces the functor is injective on objects and on equivalence classes of objects, and admits a natural retraction.

## Strengths

* Solves a 14-year-old conjecture of the author, and does so with a clean answer that was not anticipated: the identification $\mathbb{H} = \mathrm{Fix}(F_n, n>0) \subset \mathbb{W}$ is a genuinely new conceptual entry.
* The Tannakian formulation (p. 11) is a satisfying unification and should guide future work, especially in the direction of the universal HKR theorem [MRT19].
* The arithmetic formula of Cor 5.1 is a strikingly concrete consequence that a reader can verify in low examples.
* The paper is honest about its limits: Remark 4.5 acknowledges that the $\mathbb{H}$-module structure on the right-hand side of Theorem 4.1 is derived only via the isomorphism itself and is not yet known to exist naturally; Section 5 distinguishes carefully what the functor is faithful to and what it is not.
* Appendix B (fpqc topos) is a good self-contained resource on the limit-taking results that are used throughout.

## Critique

### 1. Scope: only simply connected finite type, and only the *underlying group sheaf* structure

Thereturning to the original program is the principal weakness. Grothendieck's program was never restricted to simply connected finite type, and his vision was of a full $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring object, not just a sequence of group sheaves. The paper:

* Excludes all non-simply-connected spaces (where the fundamental $\infty$-groupoid / $\pi_1$ action adds a substantial new ingredient);
* Gives only $\pi_i$ for each $i$, with no information on $k$-invariants, Postnikov towers, or the action of Steenrod / Adams operations. The isomorphism is in the category of sheaves of groups, not of $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-groups or connective spectrum-valued sheaves.

The paper should say this more prominently in Section 4 or in a dedicated "Limitations" paragraph. As it stands, a reader who reads Theorem 4.1 in isolation might believe they have a full algebraic model of homotopy theory.

### 2. The ind-affine structure of $\mathbb{W}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$

The proof of the affine-ness (Prop 3.1) requires checking that $\mathrm{Id} - F_{p_k} : \mathbb{H}_k \to \mathbb{H}_{k-1}$ is faithfully flat, and the argument is conducted over each residue field $k$. Over $\mathbb{F}_p$ one uses the decomposition
$$ \mathbb{W}_{\mathbb{F}_p} \simeq \prod_{p|hn} \mathbb{W}_p^{\infty}, $$
which is an *ind-scheme* decomposition, not a finite one. The paper does not discuss at what level of "ind-affineness" the flatness statements are being made, or how the finite affine level of $\mathbb{H}_k$ interacts with the infinite product. This is a point that would benefit from an explicit remark — either that the argument is being carried out component-by-component on a fixed "level" of the ind-scheme and then assembled (with justification), or that the assertion is actually to be interpreted as a statement about fpqc sheaves rather than about representability of the map itself.

There is no obvious error here, but the gap between the informal statement (a morphism of affines is fidedèly flat) and the rigorous level at which it needs to be phrased is not closed in the current text.

### 3. Comparison with prior work could go deeper

The introduction sketches the relation to [Man06], [Eke02], [MRT19] but the comparison is qualitative. In particular:

* **Mandell [Man06]** works in the category of $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-algebras, where the functor is not fully faithful. This paper works in the smaller category of affine (cosimplicial) ring sheaves, where one can prove more — but at the cost of losing the full algebraic structure. A precise statement of which properties are transferred and which are not would be valuable; the introduction makes a gesture at this (the normalization functor is not faithful) but does not give a clean criterion.
* **Ekedahl [Eke02]** computes $H^*(K(\mathbb{Z}, n))$ via a different complex ($B^{\otimes^n}$ with $B$ = the ring of functions on $\mathbb{C}$). The paper mentions that the two answers are very close but the relationship is not made explicit. A short lemma showing that the two complexes are quasi-isomorphic (or differ by a contractible summand) would close the loop.
* **Universal HKR [MRT19]**: the HCK filtration on the filtered circle is mentioned as an analogue of the natural filtration on $\mathbb{H}$, but the connection is left entirely to future work.

### 4. The Tannakian statement is half-developed

The formula
$$ R \mapsto \operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega \otimes R) \simeq \mathbb{H}(R) $$
is elegant, but the paper does not pursue the natural general question: is there a "Tannakian" group scheme $\mathbb{H}_X$ that dualises $\mathrm{QCoh}^{\mathrm{uni}}(X)$, and does $\pi_i(X) \otimes \mathbb{H}_X$ recover the higher homotopy of the schematization? A single paragraph explaining why this is hard (or what the obstruction is) would be more useful than the brief mention.

### 5. Missing explicit examples

The only worked example in the paper is Cor 5.2 (fpqc cohomology of $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}, M \otimes \mathbb{H})$ for a finite abelian $M$). A section with two or three more concrete computations would greatly help the reader. For instance:
* Compute $(\mathbb{S}^1 \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}}(\mathbb{Z})$ explicitly (this should be $\mathbb{Z} \oplus \prod_p \hat{\mathbb{Z}}_p$, up to conventions).
* Compute the fpqc cohomology with coefficients in $\mathbb{H}$ (rather than $M \otimes \mathbb{H}$) — this would give the $\mathbb{H}$-valued points, which should be related to $\mathrm{Hom}(S^1, S^1)$.
* Give the formula for a space with nontrivial $\pi_2$, to test the "Postnikov" side.

### 6. Language and accessibility

The paper is entirely in French (with a short English abstract). Since the intended readership — commutative algebraic geometers, homotopical algebraists — is largely francophone and English-speaking, the language choice significantly limits the immediate readership. The notation is dense (many symbols for the same kind of object: $\mathbb{H}, \mathbb{H}_k, \mathbb{H}_{Z/p^n}$, $\mathbb{W}, \mathbb{W}_{\mathbb{F}_p}$, $\mathbb{W}_p^{p^\omega}$, $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$, $\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_p$, $\mathbb{A}$), and a notation table at the end would be helpful.

### 7. Derived algebraic geometry perspective

The paper works entirely in the classical (non-derived) world. In the derived formulation, one would expect the sheaf $\mathbb{H}$ to be replaced by a derived ring $\mathbb{L}\mathbb{H}$ (perhaps derived free over $\mathbb{Z}$), and the isomorphism Theorem 4.1 to hold at the level of derived homotopy sheaves. The paper does not discuss this at all. A short remark, even speculative, would indicate whether the derived statement is expected to hold and what the main technical obstacle would be.

## Summary

This is a solid and important contribution: it closes a conjecture that had been open for over a decade, identifies the right structural object (the Frobenius-fixed points in Witt vectors), and produces a clean arithmetic formula (Cor 5.1). The Tannakian interpretation is a genuinely new and useful way to view the result.

The limitations are: (i) the result is only for simply connected finite type spaces, not the full Grothendieck program; (ii) only the underlying group sheaf structure is captured, not higher homotopical structure (k-invariants, $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring structure); (iii) the technical treatment of the $\mathbb{F}_p$-fibres of $\mathbb{W}$ is a little hand-wavy about the ind-affine level; (iv) the comparison with [Man06] and [Eke02] is more suggestive than precise; (v) the paper would benefit substantially from more worked examples.

For an expert reader working on schematization, this is an essential paper. For a broader algebraic-geometric audience, the technical prerequisites (derived $\infty$-categories of sheaves, affines on the big fpqc site, Eilenberg–Moore for $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-rings) place a substantial bar on entry.

# Review: B. Toën, "Grothendieck's schematization problem revisited"

## Summary

The paper proves Toën's 2006 conjecture (Conj. 2.3.6) on the homotopy groups of the schematization (affinization) of a simply connected, finite-type space X over Spec(Z):

> **πi((X⊗Z)uni) ≃ πi(X) ⊗ H**

where **H** is the "Hilbert additive group" — Spec(B) of the ring of integer-valued polynomials B = Z[(X n)] — equivalently the Cartier dual of the formal group Ĝm, and identifiably the subfunctor of the big Witt vector group W fixed by all Frobenius endomorphisms (Prop 2.2, via Elliott's duality for binomial rings).

From this the author derives: faithfulness and conservativeness of the schematization functor on the homotopy category of simply connected finite-type spaces; a functorial retraction of the adjunction unit X → (X⊗Z)uni(Z); an explicit adèlic decomposition of that unit involving free loop spaces in the p-adic completions ((X⊗Z)uni(Ẑp) ≃ X^{S¹}_p); fpqc cohomology computations H^i_fpqc(Spec Z, M⊗H) (zero in degree >1, M⊗Ẑ in degree 1); a rigidity/stability statement over Spec Ẑp; and a Tannakian interpretation: H is the Tannakian dual of unipotent local systems on S¹.

**Answer to a central question of Grothendieck's program, answered negatively:** the homotopy sheaves of the schematization are *not* O-modules; they are naturally H-modules. The proposed answer to Grothendieck's question is thus: the "true structural sheaf" for schematization over Z is the Hilbert sheaf H, with its canonical map H → O.

## Strengths

1. **The main theorem is exactly the right one and the answer is surprising.** That the natural "structural group" is H (integer-valued polynomials / Witt fixed points) rather than a familiar group like Z's Malcev completion or O itself, is a genuinely new and clean answer — one that unifies the char 0 fiber (Ga) and the char p fiber (constant pro-étale Ẑp) in a single flat affine group scheme over Z.

2. **The technical apparatus is handled with care.** The key inputs are (a) the fiberwise flatness criterion for affine Z-morphisms (Lem 3.2, presented as unreferenced with a self-contained proof — the Tor¹-contradiction argument is correct); (b) the fact that fpqc sheafification commutes with countable limits and lim¹ in the fpqc topos (App B, non-trivial and correctly proved via infinite fp-flat tensor products and repleteness); these are the lemmas a reader must trust, and the proofs check out.

3. **The Postnikov induction (Thm 4.1) is correct.** The reduction to Eilenberg–MacLane sheaves (Lem 4.2) via Künneth-type arguments in cosimplicial C-algebras and the Eilenberg–Moore theorem (App A, re-proved and slightly generalized, the proof I verified is standard and sound) is well organized. The passage to infinite X via lim¹-vanishing (Cor B.2) is justified. The functoriality of the resulting isomorphisms is explicitly tracked in Rem 4.3, which is a detail many authors would omit.

4. **The section 5 computations are correct and elegant.** The adèlic square Z ↪ Q ⊕ Ẑ → A is homotopy cartesian with a short exact sequence, the "free loop" statement (X⊗Z)uni(Ẑp) ≃ X^{S¹}_p is consistent with Cor 5.1 (the πi-terms πi(X)⊺ ∏p π_{i+1}(X)∧p match those of a free loop space), and I cross-checked Cor 5.2: applying Cor 5.1 to X = K(M,n) recovers H^0 = M and H^1 = M⊗Ẑ exactly. The Frobenius-fixed-points lemma (Lem 5.3) for the factorization through X_p is a neat device.

5. **Honesty about scope.** The author explicitly flags what is *not* proved: comparison with Mandell's E∞-formalism is deferred (the claimed equivalence of mapping spaces is a conjectural consequence, not a theorem), the H-module structure is only known through the constructed isomorphism and not directly (Rem 4.5), and the filtered-circle / HKR-filtration story is left open.

## Weaknesses and concerns

1. **The headline "solution to Grothendieck's schematization problem" is overclaimed.** What is proved is a theorem about *one particular functor* ((−⊗Z)uni, proposed by the author in 2006) on *one particular subcategory* (simply connected, finite type). Grothendieck's program in *Pursuing Stacks* (134) concerns arbitrary homotopy types, the role of π₁, and the full relative theory. Nothing here addresses non-simply-connected spaces or the general base R. A more modest abstract ("a major step toward" / "an answer in the simply connected finite-type case") would be appropriate. The author partly hedges in the body, but the abstract's "nous proposons comme une solution" is stronger than the theorems support.

2. **The adjunction is not shown to be the H-linear map.** Rem 4.5 concedes that the isomorphism πi(X)⊗H ≃ πi((X⊗Z)uni) is built through the proof machinery, and that the natural map πi(X) → πi((X⊗Z)uni) (from the unit of the adjunction) is *not* verified to coincide with it. For a "solution" to a *universal property* program, this matters: the stack (X⊗Z)uni is canonical, but the identification with πi(X)⊗H is. A canonical, functorial, H-linear description of the unit map is what would fully answer Grothendieck's vision, and its absence is a real (if disclosed) gap.

3. **The comparison with Mandell is a substantive missing piece, presented a bit generously.** The remark on p.1 that Thm 4.1(1) plus [Man06] "imply" that the map C*(X,Z) → (mapping spaces in E∞) is an equivalence for simply connected finite-type X is asserted without proof, and the author himself notes the compatibility of the two retractions is "pas immédiate." This is a theorem that the algebraic-geometry community would want, and its absence should be stated as a conjecture with a proof sketch or omitted from the summary of main results.

4. **Minor technical exposure in Prop 3.1's induction.** The inductive step ("les champs affines sont stables par limites homotopiques... donc par récurrence K(Hk,n) est affine") takes as its base case K(W, n) with W the full big Witt group. This rests on a [Toë06] result that K(G,n) is affine for flat affine group schemes G (via the Hochschild presentation), but it is not re-proved or pinpoint-cited, and a reader working from this paper alone would need to verify it. It is likely true and standard in that framework, but the proof as written has a slight implicit gap.

5. **The Tannakian interpretation (after Cor 3.5), while charming, is underdeveloped.** The statement "H is the Tannakian dual of unipotent local systems on S¹" is sketched in dense prose without a careful statement (the use of A = C*(S¹,Z) as an E∞-algebra, the fiber of D(Z)→D(R), etc.). It deserves either a full statement with a clean proof or relegation to a remark.

6. **Scope restrictions are only partially compensated.** The "finite type" hypothesis is used essentially and the results do not extend (by the methods here) to, e.g., K(Q, n) or infinite type. Given that rational homotopy of arbitrary nilpotent spaces is classical, the finite-type restriction is understandable, but it limits how far this can be said to recover the classical theory in Grothendieck's program, and the author does not discuss what would be needed to relax it.

## On the soundness

I did not find any error. Spot-checked: the Tor¹ argument in Lem 3.2 (correct), the App B proof of exactness of countable products in the fpqc topos (the infinite tensor product B = ⊗ᵢ Ai built as a filtered colim is flat over A, and the epi/mono checks are standard — correct), the Eilenberg–Moore proof in App A (standard, uses a truncation argument in the D(S)-module framework; sound), the Postnikov recursion and the lim¹ argument in Thm 4.1 (the constant system πi+1((Xn⊗Z)uni) for n ≥ i correctly kills lim¹), and the adèlic computation in Cor 5.1 (cross-checked against the free-loop space formula — consistent).

## Verdict

A solid, well-documented, expertly crafted paper that proves a long-standing central conjecture in the schematization program and gives a new, somewhat surprising answer to a direct question posed by Grothendieck — replacing the expected O-module structure with H-modules, where H has a clean independent description (integer-valued polynomials / Witt fixed points / Cartier dual of Ĝm). The technical lemmas are genuinely useful to the field (Lem 3.2, App B). The limitations — simply connected, finite type only; non-canonical identification of the H-module structure with the adjunction map; deferred comparison with Mandell; overclaimed "solution" language in the abstract — are real but honestly disclosed.

**Recommendation: publish** (small revision requested for the abstract's overclaim, a precise citation/proof of the base case in Prop 3.1, and either a proof sketch or conjecture-status for the Mandell-comparison statement).

---
**Originality:** the identification of H as the right answer to Grothendieck's structural-question is genuinely new and surprising in its form; the methods (binomial ring / Witt fixed points) are a fresh technical lens on the program. Not groundbreaking beyond the field, but a substantial original result: **4**.

**Overall soundness and quality:** no errors found in the proof chain as far as I can verify; the technical apparatus is careful; but the scope is narrow, the canonicality issue with the adjunction map (Rem 4.5) is a disclosed but real weakness, and the framing slightly outruns the theorems: **3.5**, rounded up given cleanliness and honesty → **4**.

**Net:** For a journal editor, this manuscript's language and exposition would pass without revision. The prose is polished, the argument is transparent and signposted, and the scholarly context is carefully drawn. ## [1911.05509v3-critique] Critique


# Critique: *"Grothendieck's schematization problem revisited"* — Bertrand Toën, Épigda 4 (2020), Art. 14

## Summary of the work

The paper resolves Conjecture 2.3.6 of Toën's 2006 "Champs affines," a long-standing open item in Grothendieck's *Pursuing Stacks* program for a schematic homotopy theory. The schematization of a space $X$ (over $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z$) is the affine field $(X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}\simeq \mathbb R\mathrm{Spec}(C^*(X,\mathbb Z))$. While Toën had characterized its fibers in characteristic $0$ (rationalization) and over $\mathbb F_p$ (mod-$p$ completion), the explicit homotopy groups of the integral field were conjectural. The theorem now proved gives, for $X$ simply connected of finite type,
$$\pi_i\big((X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}\big)\simeq \pi_i(X)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb H,$$
where $\mathbb H$ is the **Hilbert additive group** — the affine group scheme whose coordinate ring is the Hopf algebra of integer-valued polynomials $\mathcal O(\mathbb H)$, with $\mathbb Z$-basis the Hilbert polynomials $\binom X n$. Two auxiliary structural results: (i) the unit $X\to (X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}(\mathbb Z)$ admits a functorial retraction preserving the induced homotopy maps; (ii) the schematization is faithful/fully faithful on homotopy and injective on homotopy classes of objects.

## Strengths

1. **Resolves a genuine open conjecture in a foundational program.** This is not an incremental fix: Conjecture 2.3.6 was left open in the originating 2006 paper and the integral fiber was the genuinely missing piece of the story. Giving a clean, explicit closed form is the central value of the paper.

2. **The identification $\mathbb H\simeq\mathbb W^{F}$ (the subfunctor of simultaneous fixed points of the Frobenius $F_n$ on Witt vectors, Prop. 2.2) is the conceptual heart and is elegant.** The further Tannakian reinterpretation — $\mathbb H$ as the Tannakian dual of local nilpotent systems on $S^1$ — is a compelling, non-obvious motivation for why *this* field, and not an $\mathcal O$-module, is the right answer. This reframes Grothendieck's question negatively and precisely: the homotopy groups of the schematization do **not** carry an $\mathcal O$-module structure; they carry a natural $\mathbb H$-module structure with $\mathbb H\hookrightarrow\mathcal O$ a morphism of fields. That is a sharp and satisfying conceptual outcome.

3. **Appropriate, well-controlled proof technique.** The central theorem is proved by recursion on the Postnikov tower using the Eilenberg–Moore theorem (Appendix A) and fpqc-descent for the limit functors involved (Appendix B). The two appendices make the paper essentially self-contained on its foundational lemmas — including Proposition B.1 (the fpqc faisceautization functor commutes with countable limits) and the self-proved flatness criterion (Lemma 3.2), neither of which had a readily citable reference in this generality.

4. **Nicely ties together several threads.** The result connects Toën's affine-field schematization, Mandell's $E_\infty$-algebra results, Ekedahl's integral homotopy models, and the universal-KR / filtered-circle work of Moulinos–Robalo–Toën (MRT19) via $(S^1\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}\simeq K(\mathbb H,1)$ and the HKR-type filtration. Corollary 5.2 gives an explicit computation of $H^*$ of $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb H)$ that is a useful byproduct.

## Weaknesses and open gaps

1. **Strong restriction to simply connected finite-type spaces.** $X\in\mathrm{Top}_{\geq1}^{\mathrm{tf}}$ is a substantial hypothesis. Grothendieck's program is more general in spirit, and the paper does not indicate what (if anything) is known for non-simply-connected or non-finite-type spaces, nor whether the proof breaks at a specific step. This is the most significant limitation on the "solution" framing.

2. **Functoriality of the $\mathbb H$-module structure is left open (Remark 4.5).** The author concedes that although $\pi_i((X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}})$ is a natural $\mathbb H$-module, it is *not* shown that this structure is directly/naturally induced by the unit morphism $\pi_i(X)\to\pi_i((X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}})$ (a "naive" functoriality). This is a real gap for a result claiming to be a *formulated* description of the field — the module structure's naturality in $X$ is precisely part of what one would expect a clean theorem to deliver.

3. **The $\mathrm{CAlg}$ equivalence result is asserted rather than proved in full.** The introduction claims that Theorem 0.1 + [Man06] implies that
   $$\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}}(C^*(X,\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z)\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{CAlg}}}(\cdots)$$
   is an equivalence for $X$ simply connected of finite type — precisely the point that is "more remarkable" given that the normalization $\infty$-functor is *not* generally fully faithful. But the paper neither proves this equivalence in detail nor isolates exactly where full faithfulness is recovered. As stated, it reads as an assertion resting on an external input.

4. **Comparison with Ekedahl [Eke02] is sketched, not sharpened.** Both papers address integral homotopy theories over $\mathbb Z$, and the introduction acknowledges an "overlapping" formula but stops short of a precise statement of how the two models relate globally (the known equivalence is only stated "at least in a local form" via MRT19). A sharper comparison would strengthen the positioning of $\mathbb H$ relative to Ekedahl's integral model.

## The paper's stated contribution, in my own words

The paper continues the schematization program of Grothendieck's unpublished manuscript *Pursuing Stacks*, as set up by the author's own 2006 paper [Toë06]: there is an ∞-functor of "affinization" `(– ⊗ Z)^uni : Top → ChAff` (the ∞-category of affine ∞-stacks over Spec(ℤ)), sending a space X to the affine ∞-stack `RSpec(C^*(X,ℤ))`, and it was shown there that the cases R=ℚ and R=𝔽_p recover rational and p-adic homotopy theory. What remained open, and what this paper resolves, is the **integral case**: the explicit description of the homotopy groups of `(X⊗Z)^uni`, previously only conjectured ([Toë06], conj. 2.3.6).

The contribution is Theorem 0.1/4.1: for a simply connected, finite homotopy type X, the fpqc sheaves of homotopy groups of the affinization are naturally (and functorially in X) determined by a single group scheme ℍ over Spec(ℤ) — the "additive group of Hilbert" — via `π_i((X⊗Z)^uni) ≃ π_i(X) ⊗_ℤ ℍ`. ℍ is identified concretely three ways: by the ring of integer-valued (binomial) polynomials in one variable, by the subfunctor of Witt vectors `𝕄(R)` on which all Frobenii `F_n` are simultaneously the identity, and by the Tannakian dual of the category of ℤ-local systems on the circle. From this, the paper derives: a decomposition of `(X⊗Z)^uni`(ℤ) in rational, p-adic, and adelic pieces; a functorial retraction of the unit `X → (X⊗Z)^uni`(ℤ); injectivity on mapping-space homotopy; a rigidity statement on the pro-étale site (the affinization is the p-adic completion of X); and explicit computations such as `O(ℍ) ≃ ℤ ⊗^L_{C^*(S¹,ℤ)} ℤ` and `H^i_fpqc(Spec(ℤ), M⊗ℍ)`. The author presents all this as "a solution to the schematization problem" over Spec(ℤ).

## Is it novel, given the cited prior work?

**Genuinely new in this paper:**
- **The formula itself** (Thm 4.1): `π_i((X⊗Z)^uni) ≃ π_i(X) ⊗ ℍ`. It was *conjectured*, not known — it is the author's own Conjecture 2.3.6 of 2006, standing for ten years, so this is the resolution of a genuinely open problem.
- **The identification of ℍ**: the integer-valued polynomial ring B (ℍ(R) = Hom(Bin(ℤ),R)), the "simultaneous Frobenius-fixed points" of the Witt vector functor `𝕄`, and the Tannakian reinterpretation (ℍ as the dual of ℤ-local systems on S¹) are all new; none of these appear in [Toë06]. The local description `ℍ|_{Spec(ℤ_(p)^…)} ≃ Ẑ_p` (Cor. 2.3) is a new explicit computation.
- **The derived Hopf-algebra description** `O(ℍ) ≃ ℤ ⊗^L_{C(S¹,ℤ)} ℤ` (Cor. 3.5), obtained by relating the affinization to the neutral local systems on the circle.
- New **consequences**: the adelic-type decomposition `π_i((X⊗Z)^uni)(ℤ) ≃ π_i(X)⊗ℚ ⊕ ⊕_p π_i(X)̂_p` plus a correction term (Cor. 5.1); the pro-étale rigidity statement of Cor. 4.6; the cohomology computation Cor. 5.2; and the functorial retraction of Cor. 5.4 within the affine-∞-stack framework, whose proof is independent of Mandell's.
- The Eilenberg–Moore variant (Appendix A) and the fpqc-topos limit argument (Appendix B: the faiscéautisation functor commutes with countable limits) are standard tools, but their specific application here is new.

**Not new (the paper is honest about all of these):**
- **The entire framework** — affine ∞-stacks, the existence of the affinization functor (as a left adjoint), the U-localization statement, the rational and p-adic identifications — is all [Toë06]. This paper does not extend the machinery, only computes the integral homotopy groups inside it.
- **The existence of a retraction `X → (X⊗Z)^uni` and injectivity on mapping-space homotopy** was already known by Mandell [Man01, Man06] in the `E_∞`-algebraic setting; here it is re-proved independently (via a genuinely different route, using ℍ and the adelic decomposition), and the author explicitly says the two retractions' compatibility is an *open* question, so there is no unified picture, only duplication of the statement.
- **The comparison with Ekedahl [Eke02]** (cylindric complexes computing `H^*(K(ℤ,n),ℤ)`) is merely compatibility — Cor. 3.4 matches Ekedahl's result but does not improve on it.
- **The comparison with [MRT19]** (the filtered circle / universal HKR) is only *suggestive* — the author conjectures that ℍ's filtration underlies the HKR filtration but explicitly defers the proof to "futures" work.
- The original program, the motivating questions (O-modules? homotopy vs algebra?), and the definition of ℍ are all from Grothendieck/[Toë06].

## Judgment

This is a solid, self-contained resolution of a specific, well-posed, ten-year-old open conjecture in a niche area (the Grothendieck–Toën schematization program, a small but real research community: Toën, Mandell, Ekedahl, Moulinos–Robalo). The central identification — that the homotopy sheaves of the affinization are controlled by a concrete, computable group scheme over ℤ, with a clean description via integer-valued polynomials, Witt vectors, and Tannakian duality — is genuinely new mathematics, not a cosmetic reformulation. The two headline "consequences" (retraction, faithful-in-homotopy) are, however, largely already known via Mandell's independent `E_∞` work, and the author is candid about the unproven compatibility. The scope claims are somewhat generous for a "solution to the schematization problem": only the homotopy groups, only for R=ℤ, only for simply-connected finite types are computed — the full structure (space structure, Postnikov, k-invariants, extension to arbitrary base rings) remains open. Overall it is a good but not transformative paper: the proof is clean and the writing careful, but the impact is confined to a specialized program rather than reshaping the broader field.

## 1. My understanding of the logical argument

**Setting (recalled in §1).** The goal is Grothendieck's *schematization* program: to an ordinary space $X$ assign an "affine field" $(X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}$ — the left adjoint of global sections in the category $\mathrm{ChAff}\subset \mathrm{St}_{\mathbb Z}$ of affine fields over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z)$. It is known (Toën 2006) that $(X\otimes\mathbb Q)^{\mathrm{uni}}\simeq X_{\mathbb Q}$ (rationalization) and $(X\otimes\mathbb F_p)^{\mathrm{uni}}$ gives the mod‑$p$ / $p$-adic piece, but for the *global* ring $\mathbb Z$ the homotopy groups of the field were left as conjecture [Toê06, conj. 2.3.6]. One tool that organizes the paper is **Proposition 1.1**: for affine fields, a map $u:X\to\mathcal F$ is an equivalence iff it is a bijection on cohomology $H^i(-,\mathcal O)$. So "a candidate is the schematization" reduces to "it is affine and matches cohomology," which is a tractable test.

**The central new object (§2).** Toën defines the *additive Hilbert group* $\mathbb A:=\mathrm{Spec}(B)$, where $B\subset\mathbb Q[X]$ is the ring of integral‑valued polynomials (the binomial free algebra with basis $\binom X n$). $B$ is a $\mathbb Z$‑Hopf algebra, so $\mathbb A$ is a flat group scheme over $\mathbb Z$; concretely it is the Cartier dual of the multiplicative formal group $\tilde{\mathbb G}_m$. The key algebraic fact (**Prop. 2.2**, via Elliott's binomial‑ring theory) is that $\mathbb A$ identifies with the **subgroup $\mathbb W^F\subset\mathbb W$ of the big Witt‑vector functor consisting of points fixed simultaneously by all Frobenius $F_n$**. This gives a concrete local picture: over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z/p^n)$, $\mathbb A\simeq$ the constant sheaf with fiber the pro‑integer group $\dot{\mathbb Z}_p$ (**Cor. 2.3**), obtained from Hesselholt's product decomposition of the $p$‑typical Witt scheme. So $\mathbb A$ is the right candidate for the schematization of $\mathbb Z$ and a proposed "structural sheaf."

**Making the candidate work (§3).** Three ingredients are needed before the main formula:

- **Descent lemma 3.2** (a special case of the fpqc criterion that flatness of a morphism of affine $\mathbb Z$‑schemes can be checked fiberwise over fields; Toën reproduces the proof, citing no reference) plus the tower $\mathbb A=\varprojlim \mathbb A_k$ (fixed points of more and more Frobeni) yields that $B$ and each $\mathbb A_k$ are flat over $\mathbb Z$.
- **Lemma 3.3**: $K(\mathbb A,n)\simeq\mathrm{holim}_k K(\mathbb A_k,n)$, where the $\lim^1$‑term vanishes by the fpqc‑topos limit result (App. B.1).
- Since affine fields are stable under $\mathbb U$‑small homotopy limits/colimits, **$K(\mathbb A,n)$ is an affine field** (**Prop. 3.1**).

Then **Cor. 3.4** uses the criterion of Prop. 1.1: the map $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb A(\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ gives $K(\mathbb Z,n)\to K(\mathbb A,n)$, and this is the schematization of $K(\mathbb Z,n)$ — i.e. it is an isomorphism on $H^i(-,\mathcal O)\to H^i(K(\mathbb Z,n),\mathbb Z)$ (verified by a Hochschild complex for $n=1$ and by induction in $n$ via the $K(\mathbb A,n\!-\!1)$‑classifying decomposition of $C^*$-cohomology). Along the way **Cor. 3.5** identifies $\mathcal O(\mathbb A)\simeq\mathbb Z\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{C^*(S^1,\mathbb Z)}\mathbb Z$ (concentrated in degree 0), and §4 gives a Tannakian reading: $\mathbb A$ is "the Tannakian dual of the local systems on $S^1$."

**The main theorem (§4).** **Theorem 4.1**: for $X$ simply connected of finite type,
$$\pi_i(X)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb A\;\simeq\;\pi_i\big((X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}\big)$$
as group sheaves. Proof by induction on the Postnikov tower of $X$:

- **Eilenberg–MacLane bases** (Lemma 4.2): for a finitely generated abelian $\pi$, $(K(\pi,n)\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}\simeq K(\pi\otimes\mathbb A,n)$. $\pi=\mathbb Z$ is Prop. 3.1; $\pi=\mathbb Z^m$ by Künneth; general finitely generated $\pi$ uses the classifying/Bockstein square $K(\pi,n)\to *$ over $K(\mathbb Z,n+1)$ together with a new **Eilenberg–Moore theorem for cosimplicial commutative algebras (App. A.1)** to show $C^*(K(\pi,n),\mathbb Z)\simeq C^*(K(\mathbb Z,n),\mathbb Z)^{\otimes_{\cdot}}$.
- **General $X$**: the same cotensor trick applied stage by stage up the Postnikov tower; finally $X=\mathrm{hocolim}_n X_n$, $C^*(X,\mathbb Z)=\mathrm{colim}_n C^*(X_n,\mathbb Z)$, and the fpqc sheafification commuting with $\varprojlim_n$ and with vanishing $\lim^1_n$ (Cor. **Consequences (§5).** Using the cartesian square $\mathbb Z\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}=\prod_p\dot{\mathbb Z}_p$ (base changed to $\mathbb Q\to\mathbb A_{\rm ad\ell}$) one decomposes the field $(X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}(\mathbb Z)\simeq X_{\mathbb Q}\times_{X_{\mathbb A_{\rm ad\ell}}}\prod_p X^{S^1}_p$, and Thm. 4.1 gives:

This is the explicit **negative answer** to Grothendieck's question of whether the homotopy groups of the schematization should be $\mathcal O$‑modules.

## [1911.05509v3-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


I read the paper (Toën, *Grothendieck's schematization problem revisited*, Epiga 4 (2020) No. 14) carefully, following the whole proof chain from the Hilbert group §2 through the affineness of K(ℋ,n) (§3), the central Theorem 4.1 (§4), and the injectivity/rigidity corollaries (§5), including the two appendices.

## The claim that must hold

The paper's main conclusion — Conjecture [Toë06, 2.3.6] — is Theorem 4.1: for a simply connected type-finite space X, the sheaves of homotopy groups of the affinitisation satisfy
  πᵢ((X⊗ℤ)ᵘⁿⁱ) ≃ πᵢ(X)⊗ℋ
(a canonical isomorphism of *sheaves of abelian groups* over Spec ℤ, hence after global sections πᵢ((X⊗ℤ)ᵘⁿⁱ(ℤ)) ≃ πᵢ(X)⊗ℋ(ℤ)).

## The single weakest link

The whole proof is built as: EM-theorem (App. A) + Postnikov/recursion + limit lemmas (App. B) → Lemma 4.2 (the EM base case for K(π,n), π finite abelian) → Theorem 4.1. The one step where the conclusion stops being entailed by the data is the **final sentence of Lemma 4.2** (p. 12): after the homotopy-cartesian square of affine schemes produces (via the long exact sequence in homotopy) a *short exact sequence*
  0 → πᵢ⊗ℋ(R) → πᵢ((K(π,n)⊗ℤ)ᵘⁿⁱ(R)) → Ext₁(πᵢ₊₁(X), ℋ(R)) → 0,
Toën asserts these terms glue to the *isomorphism* πᵢ ≃ πᵢ⊗ℋ "by virtue of the long exact sequence in homotopy and the fact that ℋ(R) is a torsion-free group (as a subfunctor of W(R)), which implies [Ext is zero]". 

Everything else in the paper — the affineness of K(ℋ,n) (Prop. 3.1, Lemma 3.2 with the self-contained fibre-flatness criterion, Lemma 3.3 with the fpqc-limit computation), the EM theorem (App. A), the ℕ⁺-lim lemmas (App. B) — is proved inside the paper from the definitions. The torsion-freeness of ℋ(R) for *every* commutative ring R is **not**: it is imported from [Eli06, Theorem 9.1] via the embedding j: ℋ ↪ 𝕎 of Prop. 2.2 (so ℋ(R) ≤ (1+T R[[T]]), the multiplicative group of formal power series), and then used as a bare parenthetical "car sous-groupe de W(R)". Concretely:

1. It is the **only external fact the proof leans on** — the rest is self-development from [Toë06].
2. It is **load-bearing**: if ℋ(R) had one element of order m (e.g. over R = ℤ/p²ℤ, a mixed-characteristic base never treated in §§2–3), Ext¹(π,ℋ(R)) ≠ 0 for some finite π, and the extension term survives: Theorem 4.1 collapses to an exact sequence, not an isomorphism, and with it Cor. 5.1, the decomposability (2)–(4) of Theorem 0.1, and the "no ℚ̂-module structure, only ℋ-module structure" answer to Grothendieck.
3. The paper never checks the mixed-characteristic case itself: §2.3's Cor. 2.3 only gives explicit descriptions around fixed primes (ℍ_{ℤ/pⁿ} ≃ Ẑ_p/pⁿ and the characteristic-0/characteristic-p points), so a reader trusting only this document is left to take [Eli06] on faith for the global torsion-freeness.

Why this is the *weakest* and not *just* a weakness: every link downstream of it (Postnikov recursion, the lim¹-annihilation via Cor. B.2, the injectivity theorems, the H¹(Spec ℤ, M⊗ℋ)-vanishing in Cor. 5.2) follows legitimately from Lemma 4.2 as stated; and every link upstream (affineness of ℋ, the ℋₖ-descending sequence in Prop. 3.1, the EM theorem) is proved. The entailment chain is genuinely valid up to — and *entirely dependent on* — that single externally-sourced torsion-freeness fact, which the paper asserts in a clause rather than proving.

Secondary observation (not the main link): the paper is otherwise well-executed and the EM theorem (App. A) and the fpqc topos facts (App. B) are reproduced carefully, so I do not doubt the rest of the argument.

## Overall assessment

Originality: 3. It proves (a real, previously conjectural) explicit global formula πᵢ((X⊗ℤ)ᵘⁿⁱ) ≃ πᵢ(X)⊗ℋ and identifies ℋ (integer-valued-polynomial/Hilbert) as the coefficient object — genuine new content — but inside the (−⊗R)ᵘⁿⁱ framework already set up in [Toë06], and with a parallel result for the E_∞-algebra functor already in [Man06]; the novelty is completion + global (ℤ-global) rigidity and the injectivity/fidelity statements, not a new theory.

Overall: 3. The argument is, as far as one can audit from the document, sound and cleanly organised, with a self-sufficient appendix treatment of its two hardest technical tools; it is docked for the single load-bearing external dependence (torsion-freeness of ℋ via [Eli06]) that the argument never internalises — that is the step where the data in the paper, standing alone, fall just short of entailing the headline isomorphism.

Impact: 3. It settles a stated conjecture in a long-running program (Grothendieck's schematisation) and yields clean structural consequences (ℋ-module structures, a faithful ∞-functor Top₍s.c., tf₂₎ → ChAff, the p-complete rigidification over Spec Ẑ_p, and the predicted bridge to [Eke02] and [MRT19]), but the program itself remains specialist, and Manin's concurrent [Man06] carries most of the E_∞-side novelty, so the field effect is real but contained.

## [1911.05509v3-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


# Critique of Writing and Framing: Toën, "Grothendieck's schematization problem revisited" (Épigale 4:14, 2020)

## Overclaims beyond the evidence

**1. The abstract: "we propose as a solution to the schematization problem."**
This is the single boldest claim in the paper, and it is the least supported. The actual result is Theorem 4.1: for *simply connected spaces of finite type*, the fpqc sheaf of homotopy groups of $(X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\text{uni}}$ is isomorphic to $\pi_i(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{H}$. That is a homotopy-group computation on a narrow slice of the category. Grothendieck's schematization program — as the author himself describes it on page 2 — aimed for "une approche purement algébrique à l'homotopie," a full algebraic theory of all homotopy types over $\mathbb{Z}$, including the fundamental groupoid, for arbitrary spaces, over arbitrary rings. Calling a computation in the simply-connected finite-type case "a solution" stretches the language far beyond what is delivered. A fairer phrasing would be "a step toward a resolution" or "an important partial answer within the schematization program."

**2. "Nous aimons penser à $\mathbb{H}$ comme le *vrai faisceau structural*" (p. 3).**
The paper shows that $\mathbb{H}$ appears in the homotopy groups and that the old $\mathcal{O}$-module answer to Grothendieck's question is negative. But it does not prove that $\mathbb{H}$ is *the* right or unique algebraic structure. The word "vrai" (true) implies a uniqueness or optimality theorem that does not exist in the paper. More damning, the author's own Remarque 4.5 (p. 13) concedes: "Nous ne savons cependant pas comment démontrer que les faisceaux $\pi_i((X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\text{uni}})$ possèdent une structure naturelle de $\mathbb{H}$-modules de manière directe." The $\mathbb{H}$-module structure is a byproduct of the proof's construction, not something shown to be intrinsic to the sheaf. Claiming $\mathbb{H}$ as "the true structural sheaf" while simultaneously admitting one cannot prove the structural property independently is an internal tension the paper does not defuse.

**3. "Ceci est tout à fait remarquable" (p. 3).**
Calling one's own observation — that the E∞-normalization comparison map for $(X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\text{uni}}$ is an equivalence while the naive normalization is not — "quite remarkable" is a degree of self-praise that an external reviewer would find uncomfortable. The observation is certainly interesting, but "tout à fait remarquable" reads as if the author pre-emptively framing the significance on the reader's behalf. The evidence (the theorem itself) makes the point; the adjective does not.

**4. The speculative HFR/spectrum paragraph in the Introduction (p. 3–4).**
The last paragraph of the introduction connects to [MRT19] (Sagave–Tabuada's filtered circle) and HFR filtrations, suggesting that "$(X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\text{uni}}$ pour tout espace $X$" carries a natural filtration generalizing the HFR filtration. The author hedges with "Cela suggère" and "fera l'objet d'investigations futures," but the paragraph is placed in the introduction and reads as a substantive finding rather than a conjecture. In fact, the author explicitly states "Ce point particulier n'est pas traité dans ce travail." Placing a purely speculative connection in the final paragraph of the introduction, with no theorem number or conjecture label, inflates the apparent scope of the paper.

**5. The "negative answer" framing (p. 3).**
The introduction frames the $\mathbb{H}$ result as "répond par la négative" to Grothendieck's $\mathcal{O}$-module question. But the actual result is richer than a negative: the homotopy groups are not $\mathcal{O}$-modules but $\mathbb{H}$-modules, where $\mathbb{H} \simeq \mathbb{W}^F$ is itself an affine flat group scheme of nontrivial structure (Witt vectors, Frobenius, p-adic integers as fibres). Stating the result as a "negative answer" undersells it and distorts the balance. A more accurate framing: "the correct structure is not an $\mathcal{O}$-module but rather the scheme of groups $\mathbb{H}$, a fixed-point subscheme of the Witt vector group."

**6. Scope restrictions buried in theorem statements, not the abstract.**
Every major result requires $X$ to be "simplement connexe et de type fini." The abstract and introduction do not flag this restriction; one only encounters it in the hypotheses of Theorems 0.1, 4.1, 5.5. For a paper claiming to address "the schematization problem," the omission of this fundamental restriction from the abstract is a significant framing gap. An honest abstract would read: "…for a simply connected space $X$ of finite homotopy type, we prove…"

---

## What an adversarial reviewer would write in their first paragraph

> This paper proves a conjecture of the author from [Toë06] computing the homotopy groups of the schematization "$(X \otimes \mathbb{Z})^{\text{uni}}$" as $\pi_i(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{H}$, where $\mathbb{H}$ is the Cartier dual of the formal completion of $\hat{\mathbb{G}}_m$. While the computation is interesting and the proof is self-contained, the framing significantly overstates the result. The abstract claims the schematization functor is "proposed as a solution to the schematization problem," yet the theorem applies only to simply connected spaces of finite type — a severe restriction that is not mentioned in the abstract. Moreover, the author himself acknowledges (Rem. 4.5) that the $\mathbb{H}$-module structure on the homotopy sheaves is not shown to be intrinsic to the sheaf, but is an artifact of the construction: "Nous ne savons cependant pas comment démontrer … de manière directe." This undermines the central conceptual claim that $\mathbb{H}$ is the "vrai faisceau structural." Finally, the extensive connections to Ekedahl [Eke02] and Manin [Man06] are noted as "très proches" but with "différences de contextes," and it is never precisely stated which results here are genuinely new in content rather than in method. The paper would benefit from a more measured abstract that specifies the scope of applicability and from separating the proven theorem (homotopy-group formula) from the speculative applications (HFR filtrations, universality statements) that are not established.

## Summary of the writing's strengths (for balance)

The paper is well-organized, the proof strategy (Postnikov descent + affine-space machinery) is clearly laid out, the appendices on Eilenberg-Moore and the fpqc topos are appropriate for self-containedness, and the comparison with [Man06], [Eke02], [MRT19] is honest about what is shared and what is independent. The core technical contribution — the computation itself, the descent argument via the $\mathbb{H}_k$ tower, the use of the fpqc topos — is solid. The overclaims are in the *framing* layer: the abstract, the title-level "solution" language, and the speculative introduction paragraph. The mathematics is good; the marketing of the mathematics is overstated.

## Scores

- **Originality (3):** The core idea — identifying the correct algebraic structure via the Cartier dual of $\hat{\mathbb{G}}_m$ and computing $\mathbb{H}$'s fibres as p-adic integers — is a genuine and interesting observation. However, the overall framework (affine spaces, schematization) is the author's own from 2006, and many ingredients (Witt vectors, Ekedahl's computations, Manin's results) are pre-existing. This is a significant computation within an established program, not a new paradigm.

- **Overall paper (3):** The mathematics is correct (presumably, being in a published journal), well-organized, and self-contained. The proof of the main conjecture is nontrivial. However, the overclaiming in the abstract, the admitted gap regarding the intrinsic nature of the $\mathbb{H}$-module structure, and the severe scope restriction (simply connected, finite type) prevent this from being a stronger score. The speculative final introduction paragraph dilutes the paper's clear contribution.

- **Impact (3):** The result resolves a specific conjecture and corrects the expected algebraic structure ($\mathbb{O}$-modules → $\mathbb{H}$-modules). It opens a new line of connection between arithmetic (Witt vectors, p-adic completion) and algebraic homotopy theory. However, the scope restriction means it does not immediately answer the broader question of Grothendieck's program, and the intrinsic-structure gap limits how far one can build on it. Likely to be cited as the definitive computation within its scope, but unlikely to be field-shaping at a deeper level.

## [1911.05509v3-reviewer-verdict] Reviewer verdict


# Review: B. Toën, *Le problème de la schématisation de Grothendieck revisité* (EPiGA, Vol. 4 (2020), 21 pages)

## Summary

The paper gives an explicit description of the integral schematization (affine-ification) functor X ↦ (X ⊗ ℤ)^uni over Spec(ℤ), answering for simply connected spaces of finite type the conjecture [Toë06, Conj. 2.3.6]: the homotopy groups of (X ⊗ ℤ)^uni are the fppf sheaf π_i(X) ⊗_ℤ ℍ, where ℍ is the (additive) Hilbert scheme, i.e. the functor R ↦ ℤ[R] of integer-valued polynomials. The proof identifies ℍ as the simultaneous fixed-point subfunctor of the Frobenius endomorphisms on the big Witt scheme (Prop. 2.2), proves that ℍ embeds in ℚ[X] via the fixed primes and reduces to a flatness criterion (Lemme 3.2), and then builds the result by Postnikov recursion using an Eilenberg–Moore theorem (Appendix A) and a careful analysis of denumerable limits in the fppf topos (Appendix B). The paper additionally: computes ℍ_{ℤ̂_p/pⁿ} ≃ Ẑ_p/pⁿ (Cor. 2.3) and the p-adic rigidity result (Cor. 4.6); produces the adjunction retract (Cor. 5.4) and hence faithfulness on equivalence classes of objects and injectivity on homotopy classes of morphisms (Cor. 5.5, answering a central question of [Gro] in the integral setting); identifies the cohomology H*_fppc(Spec(ℤ,M ⊗ ℍ)) (Cor. 5.2), matching Ekedahl's [Eke02]; and proves a Tannakian statement (ℍ ≃ 𝕫 ⊗^𝕻_{C(S¹,ℤ)} ℤ, Cor. 3.5) identifying ℍ as the Tannakian dual of the nilpotent local systems on S¹.

## Evaluation

**Strengths.**

1. The result is a genuine answer to a named open conjecture at the intersection of Grothendieck's *Pursuing Stacks* program and Ekedahl's minimal models in integral homotopy theory. The identification of ℍ — a humble ring of integer-valued polynomials — as the "true structural ℱ-structure" controlling integral schematization is an elegant and non-obvious insight, and the identification ℍ ⊂ 𝕎 (fixed points of Frobenius) is a beautiful piece of machinery that unifies the p-adic and generic fibers.
2. The paper is self-contained in a useful way: the Eilenberg–Moore theorem (App. A) and the fppf-toos limit theorems (App. B, including the new fact that the fppf fpqc site is *réplete*, Cor. B.2) are stated and proved independently of their use, making the paper a resource in its own right.
3. The comparison of consequences (Cor. 5.5 (1)–(2)) is exactly the kind of answer Grothendieck demanded, and Cor. 5.1's decomposition of the homotopy of (X⊗ℤ)^uni(ℤ) in terms of π_i(X) and p-adic completions of π_{i+1}(X) is a concrete new computation.
4. The proof of Prop. 2.2 via the duality between binomial rings and ℱ-Fröbenius-fixed Witt vectors is short, transparent, and general enough to be reusable.

**Weaknesses.**

1. The functoriality of the isomorphism in Thm. 4.1 is only in a remark (Rem. 4.3), and the central "Ceci est tout à fait remarquable" comparison with [Man06] (the natural map Map_{C𝔸ℱ}(𝒞(X,𝕫,𝕫),𝒥) → Map_{𝔼∞}(𝒞(X,𝕫,𝕫),𝒥) being an equivalence) is asserted in the introduction but never formalized as a theorem in the body. A key consequence of the main theorem is thus left as rhetoric.
2. The Tannakian statement on p. 11 ("le schéma en groupes ℍ est le dual de Tannaka des systèmes locaux niptropes sur S¹") is the punchline of a substantial argument (the last page of §3) but is left as a prose summary, not a theorem statement.
3. Scope. The hypothesis "simply connected of finite type" is essential to the Postnikov argument (it forces the cohomological dimension hypotheses for Eilenberg–Moore in degree 0–1). The paper does not discuss what happens for spaces with torsion-free π₁, infinite type, or K-theory spectra — a paragraph on the natural limitations would orient the reader.
4. The interaction with filtered Hodge theory and [MRT19]'s filtered circle is claimed ("ceci sera l'objet d'investigations futures") without a single formal conjecture; the H-module structure of §4 (Rem. 4.4/4.5) is an honest "we don't know" — better to state it as a conjecture with its expected consequences.

## Recommendation

**Accept** (the venue EPiGA is high level; the result solves a real conjecture with clean, reproducible machinery). Score **8/10**.

**Three changes that would most improve the paper:**

1. **Promote two pieces of prose to theorems.** First, state and prove explicitly: the isomorphism of Thm. 4.1 is functorial in X, the adjunction morphism and its retract form a homotopy idempotent in Top_{≥1}, and consequently Map_{CAlg}(𝒞(X,𝕫,𝕫),𝒥) → Map_{𝔼∞}(𝒞(X,𝕫,𝕫),𝒥) is an equivalence. Second, make the Tannakian identification of p. 11 a numbered theorem ("ℍ ≃ 𝕫 ⊗^𝕻_{QCoh^𝕻(S¹,ℤ)} 𝕫"), with the argument currently scattered in §3 collected as its proof. These are the two results a reader coming from [Eke02] or [MRT19] needs to cite, and currently they are not citable.

2. **Add a "Comparison and limitations" section.** (a) State precisely what is shared with and what is independent of Mandell's E_∞-based construction [Man06], and pose explicitly the open question the author gestures at: are the two retraction morphisms (the one from §5.4 here and the one in [Man06]) mutually compatible? (b) State as a conjecture (with the expected H-module statement) that the HKR filtration of [MRT19] on ℍ is that induced by the degree filtration on integer-valued polynomials. The introduction already makes both claims informally; a formal, citable version of the relationship to the two strongest competing approaches is the paper's highest-leverage contribution and is currently not there in full.

# Peer review — "Grothendieck's schematization problem revisited" (B. Toën)

## Scope and context

This is a note in the style of *Pursuing stacks* (SGA 4½): it concerns one precise, self-contained thread of Grothendieck's program to algebraise the homotopy groups of an infinite-loop type, for the affine base $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z)$ over the big site of affine schemes. The central achievement is the proof, for simply connected spaces of finite type, of the conjecture [Toë06, conj. 2.3.6], namely a *concrete formula*

$$\pi_i\big((X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}\big)\simeq \pi_i(X)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb H$$

for the homotopy group *schemes* of the $\mathbb Q$-schematisation, where $\mathbb H$ is introduced as a new affine group scheme ("the additive group of Hilbert"), together with a functorial retraction $X\to(X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}(\mathbb Z)$ and injectivity-on-homotopy-groups results for the ∞-schematisation $(-\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}:\ Top_{\ge1}^{\mathrm{tf}}\to\mathrm{ChAff}$.

The paper is written in French in two languages (French title/body, English summary) in the classical style. It is a 20-page note; the arXiv identifier is 1911.05509v3, and it is a 3-page note (not the full article). This is the EPiGA version (2020).

## What works well

**Soundness / correctness.** The argument is clean and the proof is largely self-contained:

1. **The group scheme $\mathbb H$.** Its definition as $\mathrm{Spec}(B)$ where $B\subset\mathbb Q[X]$ is the ring of integer-valued polynomials (the free binomial ring on one generator) is standard, but its embedding $j:\mathbb H\hookrightarrow\mathbb W$ as the simultaneous fixed point of the Frobenius $F_n$ (Prop 2.2) is elegant and immediately recovers the p-adic description $\mathbb H_{\mathbb Z_{(p)}/p^n}\simeq \hat{\mathbb Z}_p$ (Cor 2.3) via Hesselholt. This identifies the abstractly-defined $\mathbb H$ with the previously-mysterious "true structural sheaf" — a genuinely satisfying identification rather than another ad hoc choice.

2. **Platitudes of affineness.** The proof that $K(\mathbb H,n)$ is affine by a clever argument through the decreasing sequence of fixed point subgroups $\mathbb H_k\subset\mathbb W$ and a fibre-by-fibre flatness criterion (Lemma 3.2) is a nice touch; it handles both positive and zero characteristic coherently via Hesselholt's $\mathbb W_{\mathbb F_p}\simeq\prod_{p\mid n}\mathbb W^\infty_{p'}$ decomposition.

3. **The main inductive argument (Thm 4.1).** The Postnikov recursion on $X$ plus the Eilenberg–Moore theorem (Appendix A) for the homotopy-cofiber of $K(\mathbb Z,n)\to K(\mathbb Z,n+1)\simeq K(\mathbb Z,\dots)$ yields the formula cell-by-cell. The treatment of the general case $X=\mathrm{holim}_n X_n$ via a "the pre-sheaf is constant past $n=i$" argument using Cor. B.2 (which proves the limit-1 vanishing of the fpqc sheafification) is standard but careful.

4. **The $H^1$ calculation.** Cor. 5.2, $H^1_{\mathrm{fpc}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z),M\otimes\mathbb H)\simeq M\otimes\check{\mathbb Z}$ (with $\check{\mathbb Z}:=\prod_p\hat{\mathbb Z}_p/\mathbb{Z}$), is a nice closed-form consequence and the paper explains how it follows from Thm 4.1 through the universal coefficient / Kunneth argument. (One small point: the paper uses the notation $\check{\mathbb Z}$ for the *non-rationalised adelic quotient*, not the usual $\mathbb{A}$; the reader must keep straight which $\mathbb A$-related ring is meant on page 14, where $\check{\mathbb A}\subset\check{\mathbb A}$ appears — this is a notational trap but it does not affect the validity of the proof.)

5. **Injectivity.** Corr 5.5 is sharp: it says the ∞-schematisation is injective on equivalence classes of objects and on mapping *spaces* (homotopy), but not fully faithful. The construction of $R:\mapsto r_Y\circ f\circ i_X$ as a retraction works and the conservation argument using Cor. 5.2 to deduce that $r$ preserves equivalence classes is the correct non-trivial step.

**Positioning and comparison.** The introduction is honest and useful. The author explicitly distinguishes his result (1)–(4) from the $E_\infty$-algebra results of Mandell [Man06], and notes that the comparison between the two retractions of [Man06] and [Toë06] is not treated here. That the relation to [Eke02] and [MRT19] is explained, including the observation that the filtered circle of [MRT19] is a particular case of the natural filtration on $\mathbb H$, is a good signal of how the paper sits on the landscape.

**Presentation.** The notation is consistent within each section, the lemmas are properly isolated, and the appendices (Eilenberg–Moore, fpqc topos properties) are short, correct, and clearly flagged as "we do not know of a reference").

## Limitations and weaknesses

The review must be balanced. Several limitations should flag itself in the body (and the author flags most of them honestly):

- **The restriction "simply connected of finite type" is essential to the argument as written**, but is it *the* optimal domain of definition? The Postnikov recursion breaks down on the first Postnikov stage precisely because $\pi_1(X)\otimes\mathbb H$ is not abelian. The author gives no indication of how to handle non-simply-connected spaces. This is a real limitation, not merely a scope question — one of the central motivations of Grothendieck's program is non-abelian homotopy. The paper is honest about this point: it is presented as "the global case for a particular focus", and that is a fair description.

- **The functoriality claim on $\pi_i(X)\otimes\mathbb H$ (Rem. 4.3) is verified**, but the natural $\mathbb H$-module structure on $\pi_i((X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}})$ (Cor. 4.4) is asserted without proof. The author explicitly says he does not know how to do it in a way independent of the proof (Rem. 4.5). This is a small but real gap: the formula (1) of Thm 4.1 is an isomorphism of sheaves, but the *module structure* is left implicit. For a paper that purports to give a "solution" to a problem, the module structure is arguably part of the answer (it is what distinguishes this from $\pi_i(X)\otimes\mathbb A$ as a sheaf).

- **The compatibility question between the retraction of [Man06] and that of [Toë06] is left open** (page 3). The author concedes this but it is in fact essential to claiming a "solution". Two independent retractions $r_1,r_2$ from $(X\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}$ to $X$ that need not be the same mean the answer is only "solution up to choice".

- **Cor. 4.6 / the $\check{\mathbb Z}$ description.** The proof uses "standard dévissage de Postnikov" without giving the details. Also, the description $\prod_p X_p^{S^1}\to X_p$ as *evaluation at the basepoint of $S^1$* is asserted without reference to a definition of $X_p^{S^1}$ as the space of free loops; the reader must re-derive this from the construction of $X_p$.

- **Comparison with [Eke02] and [MRT19].** The author acknowledges that the *global* version of $(S^1\otimes\mathbb Z)^{\mathrm{uni}}\simeq K(\mathbb H,1)$ has been obtained by [MRT19] and that his proof requires a "slightly more delicate study". But the comparison is not made with any explicit statement showing the *functorial compatibility* of the two formulae. The paper is positioned as solving the same problem as [Eke02]/[MRT19] in a *global* setting over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z)$, which is its real contribution.

- **Language.** A French paper in a prestigeuous journal is unusual and a barrier to the wider reader; the English summary at the top is short and doesn't reproduce the key formula. (Not a fault of the paper itself, but a practical limitation.)

- **No new results on the non-abelian case**, no new results on the derived Morita theory of affine ∞-schemes, and no comparison with Lurie's *Higher Algebra*. This is a focused note and the title ("revisited") makes no over-reaching claim, but a reader expecting a *complete solution* to the schematization problem will not get one; they will get the *rational and p-adic local* pieces and the identification of $\mathbb H$, which is the central step toward the full solution, not the solution itself.

## Verdict

This is a well-constructed, self-contained, and genuinely useful note. The main results (Thm 4.1, Cor. 4.4, Cor. 5.2, Cor. 5.5) are correct, the proofs are honest about their gaps, and the comparison to [Eke02] and [Man06] is fair. The identification of $\mathbb H\hookrightarrow\mathbb W$ via the Frobenius action (Prop 2.2, Cor 2.3) is the single most valuable contribution: it replaces an abstractly-defined group scheme with a concrete arithmetic object.

The work is solid but narrow. It solves the *global* version of [Eke02]'s result over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z)$ for simply connected spaces of finite type, not Grothendieck's full program.

## Scores

- **Originality: 4.** Identification of $\mathbb H$ as the fixed point of all Frobeni on $\mathbb W$ is genuinely new. The use of the decreasing fixed-points sequence $\mathbb H_k$ to prove affineness is a novel device. The functorial retraction and the corollary Cor. 5.5 are new even in the presence of [Man06]. But the overall framework (affine ∞-schemes, Eilenberg–Moore, Postnikov recursion) has all the machinery from [Toë06]/[Lur09] doing the heavy lifting, so the *new* ingredients are at the level of clever use rather than new foundational objects.

- **Overall soundness: 4.** The proofs are correct and self-contained. Small gaps are flagged by the author (Rem 4.5). I could not identify any error on close reading. The notational choice for $\check{\mathbb A}$ / $\check{\mathbb Z}$ in p. 14 and the "dévice" of Cor 4.6 could be tightened but do not affect validity.

- **Impact: 3.** The paper settles a conjecture of [Toë06] that had stood for ~14 years, and the identification $\mathbb H\simeq\mathbb W^F$ is a result that subsequent work (in particular the development of [MRT19] toward an "integral" or "global" HKR theorem) can build on. But the result is *necessary, not sufficient*, for solving the schematization problem as Grothendieck posed it; it does not resolve the non-simply-connected case, and the comparison with the $E_\infty$ picture of [Man06] is left open. The work is a solid increment in a multi-decade program, not a field-shaping result on its own.

# Review: "Grothendieck's schematization problem revisited" (B. Toën, EPiGA 4 (2020), Art. 14)

## Overall assessment

This is a clean, well-written note whose single main job — proving the author's own 2006 conjecture ([Toën06, Conj. 2.3.6]) — it carries off convincingly. The headline result, Theorem 0.1/4.1, gives an explicit closed formula for the homotopy-groups of the *affinization* (schematization) functor over Spec(ℤ): for a simply connected finite homotopy type $X$,

$$\pi_i((X\otimes\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}}) \simeq \pi_i(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{H},$$

where $\mathsf{H} = \mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{B})$ is the group scheme of (integrated) Hilbert polynomials, i.e. the free binomial ring on one generator. The rest of the note's value lies in what this buys: the identification of $\mathsf{H}$ as the simultaneous fixed-point subgroup $\mathsf{W}^F\subset\mathsf{W}$ of the big Witt vectors (Prop. 2.2), the affinization statement $K(\mathbb{Z},n)\rightsquigarrow K(\mathsf{H},n)$ (Cor. 3.4), and the injectivity/retraction properties of the schematization functor (Cor. 5.4–5.5), plus a decomposition of $(X\otimes\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}}$ into its $\mathbb{Q}$-, $\mathbb{A}$-, and $p$-adic pieces (Cor. 5.1).

## What is done well

- **The core identification is the right idea and cleanly expressed.** Expressing $\mathsf{H}(R) = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Bin}(\mathbb{Z}),R) = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},\mathsf{W}(R)^F)$ and thus $\mathsf{H}\simeq\mathsf{W}^F$ ties an otherwise mysterious group scheme to something with an explicit fibre description (Cor. 2.3: $\mathsf{H}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^n}\simeq \hat{\mathbb{Z}}_p/p^k$). Having $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_p$ show up as the fibre above the $p$-adic locus is a satisfying and useful concrete handle, and explains why $S^1$ maps to $K(\mathsf{H},1)$ with the expected fibre.
- **The proof architecture is sound and honest about its dependencies.** Theorem 4.1 is reduced to Eilenberg–MacLane spaces (Lem. 4.2) via Postnikov recursion, and Lem. 4.2 is where the real work happens — it reduces to $\pi=\mathbb{Z}/m$ and applies the Eilenberg–Moore quasi-isomorphism (App. A) to a homotopy-cartesian pullback square. The recursion and the passage $X\simeq\mathrm{holim}_n X_n$ at the end (using App. B) are handled correctly.
- **Good scholarly hygiene.** Lemme 3.2 (the pointwise-then-global flatness criterion, a Grothendieck-style statement) is flagged as "classical, with no known reference" and a self-contained proof is supplied. The author is careful to mark, point by point, which statements overlap with Manin [Man06] and with Ekedahl [Eke02]/[MRT19], and to say plainly where the proofs differ and where they would need extra work to make the two retraction constructions compatible (p.3). That level of candour is exactly right for a paper this size.

## Things to fix / tighten before this is airtight

1. **The $\mathsf{H}$-module structure is not fully understood (Rem. 4.5).** You prove in Thm. 4.1 that $\pi_i((X\otimes\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}})$ *has* a natural $\mathsf{H}$-module structure, but the isomorphism with $\pi_i(X)\otimes\mathsf{H}$ is built through an explicit construction, and you do not know whether the canonical map $\pi_i(X)\to\pi_i((X\otimes\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{uni}})$ is itself an $\mathsf{H}$-module map. You flag this yourself, but it is a live gap: a reader can reasonably ask whether the identification in (1) of Thm. 0.1 is *canonically* $\mathsf{H}$-linear or only after choosing a construction. If this is genuinely open, say so more prominently — it is arguably a second main open problem, not a footnote.

2. **Functoreality is asserted "by observation" (Rem. 4.3).** The claim that the isomorphisms of Thm. 4.1 are functorial in $X$, via $c^{-1}\circ b\circ a^{-1}$, is stated rather than proved. The three morphisms are each claimed functorial by construction, but you should at least point to the specific commutativity diagrams (the $X\to X_i\leftarrow K(\pi_i,i)$ diagrams) that make $a$, $b$, $c$ compatible with maps of spaces. As written this is a one-line hand-wave that a careful referee will push on.

3. **The Eilenberg–Moore theorem (App. A) is re-proven "à la Toën" on the fly.** The remark that the $\mathcal{U}$-smallness condition needed for its application to $X\to Y\leftarrow Z\simeq\mathrm{holim}$ is not discussed in any detail. You should confirm (in one sentence) why the type-finiteness hypotheses of (1)–(2) of Thm. A.1 are satisfied in the two specific applications (Lem. 4.2 and the Postnikov step at the top of p.12). Right now it is taken for granted.

4. **Scope of the title vs. content.** "Grothendieck's schematization problem revisited" over-promises; the note resolves the global formula in the simply-connected finite case over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})$ (and, via base-change, the pointwise $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{F}_p$ cases already known from [Toën06]). The general (non-simply-connected, higher-torsion) case and the local (over $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$) case are out of scope. That is fine, but the introduction should set this boundary explicitly so a reader doesn't expect the full program to be closed.

5. **Minor.** The phrase "le $O$-module structure" / "$\mathsf{H}$-module structure" for the homotopy *groups* is used in a slightly loose sense on p.3 ($\mathsf{H}$-modules in the fpqc topos over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})$) — spell out once that "module" means module in the $\infty$-category of sheaves of abelian groups, so the reader knows the base-change and global-section arguments in p.5 work.

## Bottom line

A focused, correct, and well-presented proof of a meaningful conjecture, with a genuinely useful concretization ($\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{W}^F$, the corollary decompositions, the retraction/injectivity corollaries). It is not a broad field-shifting paper, and it should position itself as one step in the author's longer schematization program — but within that frame it is exactly the right piece and it is done well. The main weakness is the unresolved functoriality/canonicity of the $\mathsf{H}$-linearity (points 1–2), which is the natural "next question" and currently reads as an underdeveloped edge of an otherwise finished result.
