## [1710.10040v2-general-review] General Review


I've read the full paper (Klein & Suh, *Contact Real Hypersurfaces in the Complex Hyperbolic Quadratic*, arXiv:1710.10040v2, 24 pp.). Below is my evaluation followed by the scores.

## What the paper is about

The **Main Theorem** classifies, for $m\ge 3$, the connected orientable real hypersurfaces $M$ of the non‑compact complex quadric $Q^{m*}=SO_{2,2}/SO_2SO_m$ that are simultaneously (a) contact hypersurfaces and (b) carry constant mean curvature (CMC). $M$ is one of three models:

- (i) a tube of radius $r>0$ around the totally geodesic complex hyperbolic quadric $Q^{m-1*}$;
- (ii) a horosphere whose center at infinity is the class of an $\mathfrak A$‑principal geodesic;
- (iii) a tube of radius $r>0$ around the totally geodesic real hyperbolic space $\mathbb R H^m$.

The proof (Sections 4–5) works entirely through Okumura's contact identity $\varphi S+S\varphi=k\cdot\varphi$ together with the $S^1$‑bundle $\mathfrak A$ of real structures (conjugations) on $Q^{m*}$. The key step, Theorem 5.4, shows that a CMC contact hypersurface has a globally $\mathfrak A$‑principal unit normal $N$ (i.e. $AN=N$ for some section of $\mathfrak A$); this is achieved by proving that three local alternatives for $g(A\xi,\xi)$ force $AN=N$ (one case by an algebraic contradiction, $k=0$). Once $\mathfrak A$‑principality is in hand, Lemma 5.5 shows $M$ has exactly three principal curvatures $\{\alpha,0,\mu\}$, a case split on $k$ relative to $\sqrt2$ identifies the three curvature profiles, and a focal‑submanifold (Jacobi‑field) argument pins down each profile to a uniquely classified totally geodesic submanifold.

## Assessment of the argument

The proof is internally consistent and largely correct. I checked the delicate points:

- The 3‑case argument of Theorem 5.4 is sound. Case (1) is clean: (5.14) forces $A_zN_z$ to be a second unit normal, hence $\pm N_z$. Case (2) is a legitimate continuity/upper‑semicontinuity step. Case (3) correctly reduces to $k=0$, a contradiction.
- The "if" direction of the iff is properly handled: Propositions 3.1 and 3.2 don't merely show the tubes are Hopf — they verify the *contact* condition directly in items 4 ($S\varphi+\varphi S=\sqrt2\tanh(\sqrt2r)\,\varphi$ and $=\sqrt2\coth(\sqrt2r)\,\varphi$), and the horosphere ($k=\sqrt2$) satisfies $\alpha=\mu=\sqrt2$, hence the contact identity with $k=\sqrt2$. So the contact hypotheses on both sides of the iff are genuinely checked.
- The curvature identifications in Lemma 5.5 match the models in §3 (e.g. $k>\sqrt2\Rightarrow\alpha=\sqrt2\tanh(\sqrt2 r),\ \mu=\sqrt2\coth(\sqrt2 r)$, exactly the tube around $\mathbb R H^m$ of Prop 3.2).

There is no clear error. The main weaknesses are of a different kind, discussed below.

## Critique

1. **Limited novelty — this is a re‑proof of an already‑published classification.** This is the central point. The same authors' 2015 paper [2] (*Contact hypersurfaces in Kähler manifolds*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. **143**, 2637–2649) has *already* classified contact hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature in **both** $Q^{m}$ and $Q^{m*}$, and its statement in the Introduction (pp. 2–3) gives precisely the same list of models for $Q^{m*}$. The present paper is, by the authors' own framing, an application of the $\mathfrak A$‑bundle method from [15] to *re‑derive* the $Q^{m*}$ half of [2]. So the theorem is not new; the contribution is methodological — it ports the "fundamental geometric structures" technique of [15] to the non‑compact complex quadric. That is worth doing as a template for other rank‑2 symmetric spaces, but it does not advance the classification itself. The title reads as if it announced a new classification, which overstates what is new.

2. **The duality transfer is asserted, not proved.** The final identification of the focal submanifold in Cases 1 and 3 invokes "the classification theorem for totally geodesic submanifolds in the complex quadric [5,6] … applied to the non‑compact dual $Q^{m*}$." This compact→non‑compact dual step carries all the real classification content of the theorem, yet it is a one‑sentence appeal. For a paper whose stated aim is a careful, self‑contained re‑derivation, the most substantial external fact is left least justified. A short lemma that the Cartan/Killing‑type duality sends the classified compact totally geodesic types bijectively to those in $Q^{m*}$ would close this.

3. **The $\mathfrak A$‑principal/continuity steps are hand‑wavy in places.** In case (2) of Theorem 5.4 the passage "by continuity reasons" that $\lim z_n$ is $\mathfrak A$‑principal is stated without making explicit which continuity (the set $\{A:AN=N\}$ is discrete/finite per fibre; upper‑semicontinuity of "$\exists A$" is what is actually used). Similarly, throughout Theorem 5.4 the argument mixes an *arbitrary local section* $A$ of $\mathfrak A$ with the fact that equations must hold *for all* $A\in\mathfrak A_z$; the dependence on the chosen local trivialization is never explicitly untangled, even though several equations (5.15)–(5.18) depend on fixing a section and differentiating it. This does not break the argument, but a careful reader has to reconstruct that the pointwise equations are section‑independent. Tightening this would strengthen the paper.

4. **The "if and only if" packaging obscures the hypothesis structure.** The theorem lives entirely inside the class of *constant‑mean‑curvature* hypersurfaces (CMC is a premise on both sides), yet the Introduction/Abstract phrasing ("a new proof of the classification of contact hypersurfaces with CMC" and "$M$ is a contact hypersurface iff …") reads as a bare equivalence between contact and congruent‑to‑model. The CMC premise should be foregrounded in the statement; as written a reader could misread it as a classification of *all* contact hypersurfaces.

5. **Notation/terminology inconsistencies (minor but recurring).**
   - Title and body use "complex hyperbolic **quadratic**" rather than the standard "quadric"; the authors use "quadric" for the compact $Q^m$ and "quadratic" for the dual, an asymmetry that is gratuitous.
   - Both $s$ (the matrix generator of the involutive automorphism, §2) and $S$ (shape operator, §4–5) appear; $r$ is used for both tube radius and, near the end, as the $r$ in $F(p)=\gamma_p(r)$. Neither is confusing in context but neither is flagged.
   - The $J$ in the $G$‑action on $\mathfrak A$ and the complex structure $J$ are the same, yet in the curvature formula ($R(X,Y)Z$, p. 6) the real‑structure operator also appears as $A$ alongside $J$; readers must hold the block‑matrix model of §2 in mind throughout.

6. **No new quantitative or structural insight is extracted.** Given that the model hypersurfaces and their curvatures were already in [2], the paper adds no corollary, converse estimate, or example — e.g. it does not discuss rigidity of the horospheres, the $m$‑dependence, or how the $\mathfrak A$‑isotropic/principal dichotomy of §2 interacts with the CMC condition beyond what the classification forces. A few such remarks would raise the value of an otherwise pure re‑derivation.

## Overall judgment

A correct, carefully executed, and reasonably self‑contained article in classical differential geometry. The mathematics is sound and the proof of the Main Theorem is complete (both directions of the iff, both for the abstract claim and for the model hypersurfaces). It is a perfectly respectable geometry journal paper. Its significance is, however, real but small: it is a second proof — by a now‑standard in‑house method — of a classification that these same authors already published, with a couple of load‑bearing steps (dual transfer of totally geodesic submanifolds; the continuity/local‑section arguments) left somewhat compressed. I would accept it for publication but would not expect it to be widely cited for results (which predate it); its lasting value is as a clean worked example of the $\mathfrak A$‑bundle technique on a non‑compact rank‑2 space.

**Paper:** *Contact Real Hypersurfaces in the Complex Hyperbolic Quadric* — S. Klein & Y. Jun Suh (arXiv:1710.10040v2, 2017).

### Summary

The paper proves (more precisely, *re-proves*) the classification of connected orientable real hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature (CMC) in the complex hyperbolic quadric $Q^{m*}=SO_{2,m}^*/SO_2SO_m$ ($m\ge 3$) that are contact hypersurfaces. The biconditional is: $M$ is contact iff it is an open part of (i) a tube around the totally geodesic complex hyperbolic quadric $Q^{m-1*}$, (ii) a horosphere centered at an $\mathfrak A$-principal geodesic, or (iii) a tube around a totally geodesic real hyperbolic space $\mathbb{R}H^m$. The machinery is the $S^1$-bundle $\mathfrak A$ of "fundamental geometric structures" (real structures/conjugations) on $TQ^{m*}$, the Okumura relation $\phi S+S\phi=k\,\phi$, and a chain of Codazzi-based tensor identities. The core analytic step (Theorem 5.4) is that CMC forces the unit normal to be $\mathfrak A$-principal; once that holds, the principal curvatures are computed (Lemma 5.5) and the tube/focal-submanifold construction identifies $M$ in the three cases distinguished by $k$ versus $\sqrt2$.

### Strengths

1. **Correct and well-executed.** I traced the logical spine end-to-end and found no inconsistencies. The logical flow — contact ⇒ automatically Hopf (Eq. 5.1 makes $\xi$ a principal direction); CMC ⇒ $\alpha$ constant (5.2); CMC ⇒ $\mathfrak A$-principal normal (Thm 5.4); then three-principal-curvature structure (Lemma 5.5) and the three-case Jacobi-field/focal-set argument — is sound and standard for this community. The three subcases at the end (pp. 21–24) check out against the explicit principal-curvature tables of Propositions 3.1/3.2: $k<\sqrt2$ (tube around $Q^{m-1*}$), $k=\sqrt2$ (no focal set ⇒ horosphere), $k>\sqrt2$ (tube around $\mathbb{R}H^m$), with the principal curvatures matching the tube tables under the correct normal orientation.

2. **Intrinsic setup.** Section 2's development of $\mathfrak A$ as an $\mathrm{Ad}(G)$-invariant $S^1$-subbundle of $\mathrm{End}(TQ^{m*})$, the "A-principal/A-isotropic/vector" trichotomy, and the curvature tensor via the Kobayashi–Nomizu formula is clean, self-contained, and mirrors the compact-dual treatment — good for readers coming to the dual for the first time.

3. **Clarity and craft.** Notation is introduced carefully, the lemmas are cleanly numbered and each does a visible job, and the heavy computations (Lemmas 5.1–5.3) are at least organized by the underlying identity (Eq. 4.1) that they all unwind.

### Weaknesses

1. **The result is not new — the paper states this itself.** The abstract: "We give a *new proof* of the classification." The classification already appeared in the first authors' [2] (PAMS 2015), and the method is that of the second author's compact-quadric paper [15]. So the *object of interest to the field* (the classification statement) is known; what is new is only the *proof technique* in a single ambient space (the non-compact dual). There is no discussion of why this proof is preferable to the one in [2] — is it shorter, more intrinsic, more generalizable, or does it unlock a next step? Without such a trade-off argument, the "new" in "new proof" reads as a transplant for its own sake. This is the paper's central limitation and the dominant factor in its scoring.

2. **Incremental within the authors' own program.** This is the non-compact-dual counterpart of [15], and the final member completing the rank-2 list alongside [2] and the 2-Grassmannian results. That is legitimate completion work, but readers who know the complex-quadric paper will see most of Section 5 as a sign-flipped, re-derivation of a template they have essentially read before. The genuine "new" work compresses to (a) Section 2's intrinsic $\mathfrak A$ for $Q^{m*}$ and (b) the case analysis of Thm 5.4 / Lemma 5.5 / the three final cases.

3. **The proof is largely a long verification.** Lemmas 5.1–5.3 are pages of contracted tensor identities. The one conceptually interesting move — that constant mean curvature forces $N$ to be $\mathfrak A$-principal, via the case-contradiction argument (pp. 19–20) — is non-trivial and is the real heart of the paper, but it arrives after a large amount of mechanical expansion that is hard to follow on a first read and is, in the companion-paper context, repetitive.

4. **Minor notational inconsistency.** In the Main Theorem, part (iii) refers to "the *n*-dimensional real hyperbolic space $\mathbb{R}H^n$," but the ambient space is $Q^{m*}$ and the development/proof (Section 3; p. 24) concerns $\mathbb{R}H^m$ ("totally real *m*-dimensional real hyperbolic space"). The theorem should read $\mathbb{R}H^m$; "n" is a carryover slip.

### Recommendation

This is a solid, correct, well-written paper in differential geometry of symmetric submanifolds. It is the kind of incremental but rigorous result appropriate for a good specialist venue in submanifold/contact geometry. It is not a contribution that changes a result or a method for the broader field; a reader should go to [2] for the classification and to [15]/[11] for the framework, and use this paper as the self-contained, dual-space version. A short section positioning the new proof against [2] would meaningfully improve it.

This is legitimate work, and the 𝔄-principal/𝔄-isotropic language does give a genuinely more conceptual framework than the compact paper, but there is no new theorem of independent interest.

The mathematics is careful and I found no error on inspection; the contributions lie in the unified framework for rank-2 Hermitian symmetric spaces rather than in new results. The impact on the field is modest — it completes the CMC classification program for the four rank-2 families (Q^m, Q^{m*}, G_2, G_2^*) and gives a model for similar duals, but it does not open new directions.

# Evaluation: "Contact real hypersurfaces in the complex hyperbolic quadric" (Klein & Suh, arXiv:1710.10040v2)

## Stated contribution, in my own words

The paper classifies **contact real hypersurfaces of constant mean curvature (CMC)** in the **complex hyperbolic quadric** \(Q^{m*}=SO^*_{2,m}/(SO_2 SO_m)\) for \(m\ge3\) — the non‑compact dual of the complex quadric \(Q^m\). The classification is that such a hypersurface is congruent to an open part of one of: (i) a tube of radius \(r>0\) about the totally geodesic complex hyperbolic quadric \(Q^{m-1*}\); (ii) a horosphere whose center at infinity is an \(\mathfrak A\)-principal geodesic class; or (iii) a tube of radius \(r>0\) about the totally geodesic real form \(\mathbb R H^m\).

**Crucially, this classification result is *not* the contribution.** The authors state plainly that they are giving a **new proof** of a result "first shown in [2]" (Berndt & Suh, *Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.* 2015). The actual contribution is methodological: they port the proof technique of Suh's [15] — which worked from the Okumura characterization equation \(S\phi+\phi S=k\,\phi\) (Eq. 1.2/5.1), where \(k\) is forced constant for \(m\ge3\) — from the **compact** complex quadric \(Q^m\) over to the **non‑compact dual** \(Q^{m*}\).

## What is genuinely new, concretely

1. **The geometry of the \(S^1\)-bundle \(\mathfrak A\) of real structures (conjugations) on \(Q^{m*}\), in Section 2.** This is the most substantive new content. For \(Q^m\), these real structures could be identified with the shape operators of complex hypersurfaces in a complex space form (Reckziegel [11], Klein [5]). In the non‑compact dual that interpretation breaks — the authors explicitly note the real structures in \(\mathfrak A\) "cannot be interpreted as the shape operator of a complex hypersurface in a complex space form." So they have to rebuild the scaffolding from scratch: the splitting \(T_pQ^{m*}=V(A)\oplus JV(A)\), the notions of \(\mathfrak A\)-principal vs. \(\mathfrak A\)-isotropic vectors, and the curvature tensor formula (p. 6) in terms of the fundamental structures \((g,J,\mathfrak A)\). These formulas are new.

2. **New computational lemmas specific to \(Q^{m*}\)**: Lemmas 4.1–4.3, 5.1–5.4, 5.5 (the Codazzi‑based identities and the principal‑curvature eigenvalue calculations, including the three-case branch \(k\gtrless\sqrt2\)). Theorem 5.4 (normal \(\,\mathfrak A\)-principal if \(H\) constant) is a genuinely new intermediate result, though it is a lemma *in service of* the re-proof, not claimed standalone.

3. **A new proof** by this Okumura-based method, giving the three cases via Jacobi-vector-field / focal-manifold analysis (pp. 22–24).

## What is *not* new

- **The Main Theorem itself** — already proved in [2]; this is a re-derivation.
- **The contact-hypersurface machinery** (Sasaki [12], Okumura [10], Blair [3]) — standard.
- **The rank‑2 Hermitian-symmetric-space setup** (transvection group, isotropy, Cartan decomposition) — standard (Knapp [7], Kobayashi–Nomizu [8]).
- **The use of the \(S^1\)-bundle of real structures as a "fundamental geometric structure"** was already used for the quadric \(Q^m\) in [15] and [11]/[5]; here it is adapted, not invented.

## Novelty judgment

## Methodology (reproduction guide)

**Stage 1 — Model the ambient space and its "fundamental geometric structures" (Sec. 2).**
- Write $G = SO_{2,m}$, $K = SO_2 SO_m$, $p_0 = eK$, with $Q^{m*} = G/K$ via the involutive automorphism $\sigma: g\mapsto sgs^{-1}$, $s = \mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,1,\dots,1)$. For the tube of radius $r$: the normal Jacobi operator $\tilde R_N$ has eigenvalues $0$ and $-2$ with identified eigenspaces; integrating the Jacobi equation along normal geodesics gives the principal curvatures **0, $-\sqrt2\tanh(\sqrt2 r)$, $-\sqrt2\,\mathrm{coth}(\sqrt2 r)$** (signs flipped in Propositions 3.1/3.2 when $N$ points outward), with eigenspaces $J(V(A)\oplus\mathbb{R}N)$, $V(A)\oplus\mathbb{R}N$, $\mathbb{R}JN$, multiplicities $m-1$, $m-1$, 1, and the defining relation $S\phi + \phi S = \pm\sqrt2\tanh/\mathrm{coth}(\sqrt2 r)\cdot\phi$ (i.e. the contact constant $k = \sqrt2\tanh(\sqrt2 r) < \sqrt2$ or $k = \sqrt2\,\mathrm{coth}(\sqrt2 r) > \sqrt2$).

**Stage 3 — General CMC contact hypersurface: force the normal to be $\mathfrak{A}$-principal (Secs. 4–5).**

4. **Theorem 5.4 (the core step):** for a CMC contact hypersurface, take Eq. 5. Lemma 4.3 (available once $N$ is $\mathfrak{A}$-principal): $\tilde\nabla^{\mathrm{End}}_X A = 0$ for $X\in\mathcal{C}$ and $ASX = SX$ on $\mathcal{C}$.
6. **Lemma 5.5:** any non-Reeb principal curvature $\sigma$ solves the constant-coefficient quadratic $2\sigma^2 - 2k\sigma + \alpha k - 2 = 0$ (Eq. 5.23); at most two such roots $\lambda, \mu$ exist with $\mu = k - \lambda$; one must be **zero** (else $S$ would commute with $A$ on all of $J$-invariant $\mathcal{C}$, contradicting $JA = -AJ$). Hence $\alpha = 2/k$, $\mu = k$, and three cases: $k < \sqrt2$ ($\mu = \sqrt2\tanh\sqrt2 r$), $k = \sqrt2$ ($\alpha = \mu = \sqrt2$), $k > \sqrt2$ ($\mu = \sqrt2\,\mathrm{coth}\sqrt2 r$), with $r>0$ in the first and third cases. These match exactly the curvature data of the tubes computed in Stage 2.

**Stage 4 — Reconstruct $M$ from its focal data (Proof of the Main Theorem).**
- Let $F: M \to Q^{m*}$, $p \mapsto \gamma_p(r)$, be displacement in the normal direction at distance $r$ (exists in the $k\ne\sqrt2$ cases since the curvature data fixes an $r$). Compute $dF_p(X) = Z_X(r)$ via explicit Jacobi fields: $Z_X(r) = (\cosh(\sqrt2 r) - \tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt2}\sinh(\sqrt2 r))E_X(r)$ on the $-2$‑eigenspaces of $\tilde R_N$, $Z_X(r) = E_X(r)$ on the $0$‑eigenspace.
- **Case 1 ($k<\sqrt2$):** $Z_X(r) = 0$ exactly on the Reeb direction, so the focal manifold $P = \mathrm{im}\, dF$ is an $(m-1)$-dimensional $J$-invariant, $\mathfrak{A}$-invariant (hence $\mathfrak{A}$-principal), totally geodesic submanifold ($S_{\eta_p}Z_X(r) = Z'_X(r) = 0$ is verified term-by-term). 3. **Heaviness for the pay-off.** Much of Sections 4–5 is mechanical Codazzi/Gauss contraction that will be recognized verbatim from the compact case. For a note of this scope the introduction is also long — a fair fraction is surveying [5], [6], [14], [1].
4. **Refractory dependency on [15]** ("submitted for publication") as the methodological source is a soft spot that a referee may flag.

### Verdict
This is a competent, sound, and well-organized incremental contribution: a faithful transfer of an established method to the non-compact dual of the complex quadric, confirming a previously known classification and completing a small piece of the rank-2 Hermitian-symmetric-space classification program. It is well written and rigorous enough that the algebra is hard to fault, but it advances the field only marginally and its main theorem is a re-proof. As a student deliverable this is solid work; as a standalone advance it is a solid "note," not a breakthrough — I'd tighten the framing of what is new and motivate the Theorem 5.4 cases explicitly before submission.
