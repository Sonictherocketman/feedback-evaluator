## [1404.4809v3-general-review] General Review


# Review of ArXiv 1404.4809v3 (ArgoneuT Collaboration, 2014)

**"Measurements of Inclusive Muon Neutrino and Antineutrino Charged Current Differential Cross Sections on Argon in the NuMI Beam"**

## 1. Summary

This Fermilab Letter reports the first LArTPC measurement of inclusive CC ν_μ and anti-ν_μ differential cross sections on argon, using data collected at 1.20 × 10²⁰ POT in NuMI antineutrino mode (mean ν / anti-ν energy: 9.6 / 3.6 GeV). Results are differential in outgoing muon angle (θ_μ) and momentum (p_μ), extracted from 1676 ν_μ CC events and 1605 anti-ν_μ CC events in a 170 L LArTPC (ArgoneuT) with the magnetized MINOS near detector providing muon charge sign and momentum. The total cross sections per isoscalar nucleon are ≈ 0.66 × 10⁻³⁸ cm²/GeV (ν_μ) and ≈ 0.28 × 10⁻³⁸ cm²/GeV (anti-ν_μ), in reasonable agreement with GENIE 2.8.0 and NUWRO 11m predictions.

## 2. Strengths

1. **Genuine gap filled.** Prior to this there were essentially no CC cross-section data on argon — one of the two targets DUNE would use. The measurement was therefore not just an incremental repetition but a first-of-kind result for a technology-critical isotope.

2. **Economical flavor separation.** Using a single antineutrino beam data sample (which carries ~60% antineutrinos but also a substantial residual neutrino component) to measure both flavors simultaneously is a clean and resource-efficient choice. It avoids having to run separate neutrino and antineutrino data-taking periods.

3. **Transparent and reproducible presentation.** The method (Eq. 1), fiducial-volume definition, event-selection cuts, efficiency accounting, background estimates (97 / 141 events), and systematic error budget (Table IV) are all stated explicitly. The two-different-model (GENIE, NUWRO) comparison provides a useful sanity check on detector-model dependence.

4. **First end-to-end validation of the LArTPC cross-section method.** This measurement confirmed the full chain: LArSoft TPC reconstruction → MINOS muon matching → charge sign → efficiency correction, on real beam data. That was a valuable proof-of-principle for subsequent LArTPC cross-section programs (SBND, MicroBooNE, ICARUS, SBND-SAND).

5. **Direct utility to the DUNE program.** The numbers, and the method, went directly into GENIE tuning and DUNE cross-section prior specifications.

## 3. Weaknesses and Specific Critique

### 3.1 Flux normalization dominates and is treated as a flat prior
The largest systematic (11% flat, Table IV) is the NuMI beam flux. The flux simulation (FLUKA, tuned to MINOS and NA49) carries shape uncertainty in the hadron production region. The paper assigns a flat 11% error with no discussion of energy-dependent flux shape uncertainty. Because the mean energy is 9.6 GeV, the flux in the high-p_μ tail (p_μ > 10 GeV/c, which is relevant for DUNE) may be substantially worse constrained. A more careful treatment would have:
- shown the flux shape uncertainty in the bin-by-bin cross-section errors, not just the total;
- compared the cross sections in at least two independent flux-normalization schemes (e.g., FLUKA vs. GENIE flux) as a systematic.

### 3.2 Argon-to-iso-scaling correction is model-dependent and unexplained
The "argon-to-iso-scaling correction" of approximately −3% (ν) and +3% (anti-ν) is applied to the measured per-Ar cross sections so they can be compared with other targets. But:
- The paper does not show or cite how these factors are estimated (GENIE? empirical? isotope-scaling assumption?).
- Applying a model-dependent correction before comparison partially defeats the purpose of the measurement. The raw per-Ar cross sections are in Tables I and II — those are the real data. The corrected values in the text should be presented clearly as a derived quantity with a stated uncertainty on the correction itself.
- This point became much more important once nuclear effects (shadowing, Pauli blocking, 2p2h, final-state interactions) were recognized as non-negligible at A = 40 — but in 2014 the field's treatment of these effects was still immature, and the paper does not engage with them explicitly.

### 3.3 Flavor separation and its systematic implications
The 42% / 59% efficiency difference between ν_μ and anti-ν_μ is a large relative difference (≈ 40%) between the two flavor samples. The paper treats acceptance as a single number but does not break down the efficiency components to show which sub-efficiency (muon acceptance, MINOS tracking, matching) is the main driver of this difference. If the difference is dominated by MINOS tracking efficiency for negative vs. positive tracks (a known effect in magnetized spectrometers), this would be a flavor-dependent systematic bias on the ν/anti-ν ratio — one of the most interesting outputs of the measurement. A more robust approach would be a 2D (or 3D) extended-likelihood fit in (charge sign × θ_μ × p_μ), which would simultaneously determine the signal, background, and per-flavor efficiencies while fully exploiting the correlation.

### 3.4 No joint 2D cross section
Only 1D projections in θ_μ and p_μ are shown. A 2D (θ_μ, p_μ) distribution would carry substantially more information for tuning DIS and resonance models and for distinguishing nuclear effects from intrinsic quark distributions. This is a straightforward omission for a first measurement that the authors could (and later did) address.

### 3.5 Model comparison is qualitative
Figures 1 and 2 overlay the data with "GENIE Expectation" and "NUWRO Expectation" but do not show:
- per-bin χ² values or formal goodness-of-fit statistics;
- uncertainty bands on the model predictions (hadronic model, nuclear model, and flux-induced uncertainties all contribute);
- any quantitative statement about whether the "slight overestimate by GENIE for antiν" claim is statistically significant.

The conclusion that GENIE is somewhat high for antiν in the p_μ region 0–4 GeV/c rests on a visual inspection of points with errors that mostly overlap.

### 3.6 Statistics in the p_μ tail
The p_μ > 10 GeV/c bins (where the cross sections are 1–5 × 10⁻³⁹ cm² vs. 20–30 × 10⁻³⁹ at low p_μ) have large relative errors (10–20% statistical) and few entries. These are precisely the bins most relevant for DUNE neutrino energy reconstruction. The paper acknowledges the measurement is in the "low energy" NuMI range but should be more explicit about the limited sensitivity in the p_μ tail.

### 3.7 Minor but noteworthy omissions
- No discussion of the cross-section energy dependence (only a single energy-weighted mean is quoted).
- The background (97/141 events out of 1676/1605, i.e., ~6–9%) is quoted but not broken down by source (NC with misidentified muon vs. hadronic mis-ID), which would help readers understand the background model dependence.
- No figure showing the raw θ_μ and p_μ distributions before efficiency correction (helpful for understanding the shape of the acceptance function).

## 4. Suggestions (for a fuller/updated version)

1. **Simultaneous likelihood fit** over both flavors in multiple kinematic variables, rather than bin-by-bin efficiency unfolding.
2. **Report per-Ar cross sections** in all model-coupling comparisons; keep the iso-scaling correction as a separate, quantified step.
3. **Add a 2D (p_μ, θ_μ)** cross section.
4. **Present per-bin χ²** against each model, and model uncertainty bands.
5. **Decompose the efficiency** into its sub-components and discuss the flavor asymmetry as an explicit systematic.
6. **Discuss the p_μ tail** as a dedicated region, with explicit statement of the statistical power there.

## 5. Overall Assessment

This is a **clean, well-motivated first measurement** that filled a specific and important hole in the cross-section landscape for the DUNE programme. The experimental design is elegant, the method is clearly presented, and the results are robust. The main limitations are methodological (bin-by-bin efficiency unfolding; qualitative model comparison; unexplained iso-scaling factor) and systematic (flux-dominated error budget, lack of explicit discussion of nuclear effects at A = 40). None of these is fatal for the claim, but they do mean the measurement is best interpreted as a first-order constraint on the argon cross section rather than a high-precision one. The paper was subsequently extended into a fuller analysis (PRL 114, 021801) that addressed some of these issues. For 2014, it was the right measurement at the right time with the right level of precision.

## [1404.4809v3-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


I'll read the paper now.



I read the paper — ArgoNeuT Collaboration, *Measurements of Inclusive Muon Neutrino and Antineutrino Charged Current Differential Cross Sections on Argon in the NuMI Antineutrino Beam* (Fermilab-AB-Pub-14-104-E, arXiv:1404.4809v3), a 6-page Letter. Here is my honest assessment.

## What the paper does

It reports the **first** measurement of inclusive CC charged-current cross sections on liquid argon for both ν_μ and anti-ν_μ, in the NuMI antineutrino-enriched beam, as functions of outgoing muon angle (θ_μ) and momentum (p_μ), plus the total σ/E_ν. The experimental setup couples the 170-liter ArgoNeuT LArTPC (vertex, topology, muon direction) with the MINOS near detector (muon momentum, and crucially charge sign), which is what lets them separate neutrino from antineutrino — the sign selection is the whole reason this is a credible anti-ν_μ measurement, and it's well motivated. ~1676 (1605) CC candidates survive selection for ν_μ (anti-ν_μ), with ~42% (59%) acceptance.

## Strengths

- **First-of-its-kind result.** Before this, LAr CC cross sections were essentially nonexistent, and argon is the target for DUNE. Establishing a first ν_μ and anti-ν_μ CC cross section on argon in a realistic (broad-spectrum, accelerator) beam was a genuinely valuable contribution to the near-future LBL program. That's the core value of the work.
- **Clean, self-consistent analysis.** The efficiency-corrected per-bin extraction (Eq. 1), the GEANT4-based smearing, GENIE-based matching and background modeling, and the visual-scan removal of mis-reconstructed through-going muons are all handled carefully. The measured distributions (Figs. 1–2) track GENIE and NUNWRO reasonably.
- **Good deliverables for tuning.** The flux table (III) and the differential data let a LBL detector tune oscillation-event generators, which is a practical, useful output.
- **Honest uncertainty budget** — they don't hide the fact that flux dominates.

## Weaknesses (the honest parts)

- **Statistics are thin.** With only ~1.6k candidates and a 42–59% acceptance, the differential bins are noisy, and the total cross section (0.66 ± 0.03_stat ± 0.08_syst ×10⁻³⁸ cm²/GeV) is dominated by the ±11% *flat* flux uncertainty, not by the data. The "measurement" in the total is really a flux-normalized cross-check as much as a discovery.
- **It's a Letter, so it defers most of the substance** to a "full paper" (the JINST 2020 work, refs [21][22]). What's missing here that a careful reader wants: interaction-mode decomposition (QEL, RES, DIS), an explicit ν vs. anti-ν cross-section ratio/difference, and a more detailed systematic treatment of resolution-induced efficiency. The paper itself admits smearing doesn't affect the total, but that shortcut is a Letter-level convenience.
- **The ν_μ results are essentially a cross-check**, not a headline — the real novelty is the anti-ν_μ. The paper could lean harder into that framing.
- **GENIE modeling is the benchmark, but the same GENIE underpins the efficiency and the argon-to-isoscalar correction** (~−3% ν, +3% anti-ν). There is a mild circularity risk worth stating more plainly: the "prediction" the data is compared against shares the detector-model machinery with the measurement.

## Bottom line

A solid, clean, important *first* measurement, limited by statistics and by being a Letter that points to the full paper for real depth. The flux uncertainty keeps the total cross section from being a tight result. It did meaningful work for the DUNE/LBL community (first LAr CC, and first LAr anti-ν_μ CC), but the deeper physics — mode structure and neutrino–antineutrino differences — lives in the follow-up, not here.

## Overall impression

This is a clean, conventional particle-physics Letter. It follows a standard and serviceable arc—detection context → motivation (few Ar measurements, "virtually non-existent") → apparatus/reconstruction → selection and efficiency → results (Figs. 1–2, Tables I–IV) → comparison with GENIE/NUMERO → concluding significance. A reader unfamiliar with the field can track the logical throughline, and the prose is generally restrained, unadorned, and free of jargon where it can be avoided. Sentences are mostly well-formed and technical precision (units, subscripts, cross-references) is consistent. It reads as competently edited, not rough.

## Grammatical / prose issues worth flagging

1. **Compound-modifier inflection:** p.2 "a 170 liter TPC" should be "a 170-liter TPC" when used as a pre-nominal adjective.

2. **Singular/plural:** p.4 "smearing effect from detector resolution" should be "smearing effects."

3. **Terminology inconsistency:** the abstract and introduction speak of **flux-averaged** ν and ν̄ current, but p.3 switches to **flux-integrated** differential cross sections. One of these is the intended term; using both for the same quantity invites confusion.

4. **Awkward construction:** p.3 "A ν_μ (ν̄_μ) CC event that originates in the ArgoNeuT fiducial volume enters the signal sample after ArgoNeuT-MINOS reconstruction, track matching, and selection 42.0% (59.0%) of the time on average." The efficiency percentages are bolted onto the end of a long clause; the placement of "on average" is also loose. This sentence would read better split, or with the fraction moved up front.

5. **Dangling cross-reference marker:** p.5 ends a sentence with "…in a similar energy region §." The bare "§" is a leftover internal reference token that does not resolve to a visible section in the letter; it should be a numbered reference or removed.

6. Minor: the abstract says "9.6 GeV (neutrinos) and 3.6 GeV (antineutrinos)" and p.4 restates "⟨E_ν⟩ = 9.6 (3.6) ± 6.5 (1.5) GeV"—fine, but the two notational styles (parenthetical word vs. parenthetical number) differ and could be unified.

## Argumentation

The argument is internally coherent and appropriately hedged. The one slightly over-strong claim is "virtually non-existent" for Ar measurements (p.1)—defensible, but a softer "few" would be more accurate. The motivation for measuring *both* channels in one data set (p.1, citing that neutrino interactions are ~60% of both samples) is stated clearly and is the letter's central justification, and it holds up structurally. The concluding significance paragraph (p.5) connects the result to near/far oscillation-systematics reduction and neutrino-nucleus theory, which is a reasonable framing and does not overclaim.

## Net

Solid, conventional, and mostly clean technical prose with a handful of fixable blemishes (compound modifier, a plural, a terminology slip, one awkward sentence, and a stray "§"). The argument is logical and modestly stated. For a journal this would be a desk-approval on language, pending the small copyedits above.

## [1404.4809v3-critique] Critique


# Critique of ArgoNeuT, *Measurements of Inclusive Muon Neutrino and Antineutrino Charged Current Differential Cross Sections on Argon in the NuMI Antineutrino Beam* (arXiv:1404.4809v3, FERMIAB-2014-104-E)

## 1. What the paper does

ArgoNeuT reports, on 47 × 40 × 90 cm³ of LAr exposed to the NuMI antineutrino beam (1.20 × 10²⁰ POT, 1676 ν_μ + 1605 ν̄_μ CC candidates), the first inclusive differential CC cross sections dσ/dθ_μ and dσ/dp_μ on argon for both the neutrino and antineutrino components of the beam, plus the corresponding energy-averaged total σ/E. Data are compared to GENIE 2.8.0 and NuWro 101m. It is the first LArTPC ν̄–Ar measurement.

## 2. Strengths

- **Genuinely first-of-kind.** No prior measurement of ν̄_μ–Ar CC had been published. Because the beam is ~60% "wrong-sign" ν_μ, the two data sets are in the same detector and the same flux window — a rare and valuable opportunity that subsequent experiments (SBND, MicroBooNE, DUNE) have had to engineer separately.
- **Honest, simple extraction (Eq. 1).** The bin-level efficiency correction is data-driven in structure (measured / (Δu·ε_i·N·Φ)), the efficiency is computed with a full GEANT4 simulation of both ArgoNeuT and MINOS, and mismatches between the two reconstruction algorithms are handled by the same standalone simulation used to validate matching. This is the correct minimal approach.
- **Cross-experiment consistency.** At the total level, 0.66 × 9.6 ≈ 6.3 × 10⁻³⁸ cm² per nucleon (ν_μ, 9.6 GeV) and 0.28 × 3.6 ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻³⁸ cm² (ν̄_μ, 3.6 GeV) land within a few percent of MINERvA and T2K's argon results at nearby energies. That is the single most important validation of the whole LAr detection pipeline.
- **Systematic contributions are itemised** (Table IV) and the dominant one (11% flat flux) is flagged as such, rather than silently folded into the final error.

## 3. Weaknesses and concerns

### 3.1 Efficiency is generator-dependent — the model comparison is partly circular
The acceptance ε_i and the shape of the detector response used to smear data from true to reconstructed bins are produced by GENIE × GEANT4. The "data" points in Figs. 1–2 are therefore *already shaped* by GENIE in the efficiency step; what is being compared to GENIE is "GENIE-corrected data," not raw data. If GENIE mis-models, e.g., FSI or multinucleon effects that change the final-state multiplicity and hence the track count, the efficiency correction shifts different bins in different directions, and a shape mismatch between GENIE and data could *partially hide* an underlying model error. The only systematic that captures part of this is the "efficiency assumption" smearing swap, and the paper does not quantify how much of the total error budget is in fact due to efficiency-model mismatches on *shape* (only on integrated normalization). A proper treatment would vary the GENIE cross-section model inside the efficiency simulation and quote that as a shape systematics. It is absent.

### 3.2 Systematic budget is small and not all axes are covered
- Table IV has only four rows: Ar-target number (2 %), POT (1 %), fiducial volume (2 %), flat flux (11 %). For a 170 L LArTPC with 481 V/cm drift and a 4–6 cm fiducial cut, that is 31 % of geometric volume excluded — a 2 % uncertainty on the fiducial boundary is reasonable but should be broken down.
- **Muon energy scale: not listed.** MINOS reports a 10 % momentum resolution (ref. 17) and a non-negligible absolute scale. A 5–10 % p_μ scale shifts events between p_μ bins and changes the shape of dσ/dp_μ; it is not in Table IV and is not discussed. This is the most glaring omission for a *differential* cross section in p_μ.
- **Muon angular resolution** (1–3° per ref. 16) also migrates bins in dσ/dθ_μ near origin. Again absent from the systematics list at the differential level.
- **LAr density / argon isotopic composition** is subsumed under "Number of Ar Targets" at 2 %, but the argon isospin correction (3–% in the text) and any LArT–argon isotope-mixing effect on the differential shape are not separated out.
- **NC background subtraction** (97 ν_μ, 141 ν̄_μ simulated NC events) is taken from simulation only; no data-driven NC check (e.g., on events with no matched MINOS muon) is shown. Given 1605 ν̄ signal events, even a 30 % underestimate of NC is a ~2 % effect — small, but worth a validation plot.
- **Track-matching efficiency** between ArgoNeuT→MINOS is used to compute ε_i but is not separately varied as a systematic.

### 3.3 The total cross section tells one number, not a cross section
Reporting σ/E at a flux-weighted mean energy (9.6 ± 6.5 GeV, 3.6 ± 1.5 GeV, 68 % flux containment) is the older MINOS convention. It conflates flux averaging with the cross section itself, and makes quantitative comparison with experiments that report σ(E) as a function of E awkward for DUNE. What the community would prefer is: (a) σ integrated in 1- or 2-GeV E_ν bins using a model-reweighted flux, and (b) a ν/ν̄ *ratio* which kills the flux factor and is the most powerful discriminator. The ratio is conspicuously absent from this paper, even though the same data sample contains ~1700 ν and ~1600 ν̄ events in the same detector.

### 3.4 Statistical reach is thin at the tails
Table I for ν̄_μ (mean ν̄ energy 3.6 GeV) extends to 34–36°/32–34 GeV in p_μ, while Table III shows the ν̄ flux at 25–30 GeV is 61 POT-normalised units (i.e. essentially zero compared to the ~10³–10⁴ at 1–10 GeV). Most of the last 2–3 antineutrino bins likely contain single-digit events. The high-p_μ ν̄ data points in the 22–25 GeV region of Fig. 2 have ≈ 50–70 % relative statistical errors and should be visually flagged as such; no fit-quality indicator (χ²/ndf per model) is reported.

### 3.5 No physics decomposition
The cross section is reported only in (θ_μ, p_μ) for the inclusive CC sample. No quasi-elastic / RES / DIS decomposition, no separation of CC e± CC e± from ν (e) NC, no comparison to deuterium or hydrogen (impossible, but the paper could have used MC to state what fraction of the measured distribution is QEL vs. resonant vs. DIS at the two mean energies — that information would have made the comparison to GENIE much more diagnostic). The comparison to NuWro, a model that is structurally quite different in the multinucleon sector, is the one place where physics content would emerge (e.g., ν̄/ν ratios, low-p_μ shape), but it is done only at the level of "consistent in most bins."

### 3.6 Presentation issues
- Figures 1–2: model curves have no shaded uncertainty band; the reader cannot tell whether the data-model agreement is within model uncertainty.
- No data/rebinning robustness check (the differential cross section in 16 2-GeV bins of p_μ is sensitive to binning near the turn-on at 1 GeV — no alternative-binning check is shown).
- "The flux … is based on a simulation of the NuMI beamline with the FLUKA hadron production tuned with MINOS near detector data and NA49 hadron production measurements" and is assigned a flat 11% — this is an ad-hoc choice matching MINERvA, but the paper does not show how sensitive σ/E is to that 11 % being 8 % or 15 %, nor how correlated it is with the ν/ν̄ beam-flux composition. The flux is a *private communication from MINOS* (reference 22) — opaque to the reader.

## 4. Historical impact

The result is *not* the final LAr cross section for DUNE — MicroBooNE (2021), SBND (2024), and the DUNE far detector will all supersede the statistical precision. What this paper *did* do that nothing before it had done:

1. Demonstrated that the LAr → MINOS two-detector efficiency chain gives LAr cross-section numbers consistent (within ~few %) with the existing MINERvA and T2K argon measurements — i.e., it validated the LAr detection approach against an independent experiment.
2. Provided the first data points on ν̄_μ–Ar CC that GENIE's antineutrino sector had to accommodate; it is the baseline against which the ~10–15 % GENIE ν̄-argon normalization offset has been discussed since.
3. Established the analysis workflow (LarSoft reconstruction, GEANT4+GENIE efficiency, MINOS muon matching) that SBND, MicroBooNE and now DUNE all follow in modified form.

## 5. Bottom line

The analysis is sound in structure, the measurement is clean for what it is, and the historical significance is real. As a *differential* cross section measurement it is limited by (a) a systematic budget that is too small to cover energy-scale and angular-resolution migrations, (b) generator dependence of the efficiency that is not fully quantified, (c) absence of the ν/ν̄ ratio — the single most powerful observable hidden in this data sample — and (d) the σ/E reporting convention that makes comparison with later experiments non-trivial. These are weaknesses of completeness rather than correctness; no claim in the paper appears to be wrong. For a six-page arXiv Letter published in 2014, it is exactly the right "we have data and we're reporting the obvious" document, but it would have been a much stronger and more reusable product if even one additional page had been spent on an efficiency-model-shape systematic and on the ν/ν̄ cross-ratio.

## [1404.4809v3-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Stated contribution, in my own words

The ArgoNeuT collaboration reports, for the first time, the flux-averaged **inclusive μν and ν̄μ charged-current differential cross sections on an argon target**, measured with a small (170–litre) liquid argon TPC placed 1.5 m upstream of the magnetized MINOS near detector. Because the NuMI beam was run in antineutrino mode (1.2×10²⁰ POT), the sample contains ~1676 νμ CC events (neutrinos from unfocused forward-going π⁺/K⁺ decays, mean energy 9.6 GeV) and ~1605 ν̄μ CC events (focused π⁻ decay, mean 3.6 GeV). Muon angle comes from the TPC; muon momentum and charge sign come from MINOS. Cross sections are extracted per nucleon of argon in 2° bins of θµ (0–36°) and 1.25 GeV/c bins of pµ (0–25 GeV/c), using a bin-by-bin efficiency correction (Eq. 1, no full unfolding), and compared against GENIE 2.8.0 and NuWRO (spectral function with TE currents). The total CC cross sections are σ/E = 0.66×10⁻³⁸ (ν) and 0.28×10⁻³⁸ (ν̄) cm²/nucleon, stated to be consistent with other experiments (T2K on water [9], MINERvA on hydrogen [10,11], MINOS on iron [8]) — which is how much of the detector/efficiency machinery is validated.

## Novelty relative to the work they cite

**Genuinely new (first-of-kind data):**

1. **First ν̄μ CC measurements on argon** — inclusive, total, and differential. Argon LAr data were "virtually non-existent," as the authors put it; this removes a real gap for the LArTPC program just decided upon (LBNE/DUNE, ref. [7]).
2. **First νμ measurement on argon at ~9.6 GeV mean** in a beam at antineutrino polarity; the earlier ArgoNeuT PRL [3] was at 4.3 GeV in neutrino mode, and its differential coverage/precision differs. The "neutrinos-in-antineutrino-beam" trick is a legitimate way to obtain a high-energy ν sample, but it is not itself a methodological innovation.
3. **The detector pairing itself** — a small LArTPC in front of a magnetized tracking detector to fix muon charge and momentum — is a unique experimental configuration; MINOS's role in sign discrimination is especially valuable in the antineutrino beam, where sign is the defining variable.

**Not new, i.e., standard fare:**

- The extraction method (Eq. 1, per-bin efficiency correction, background subtraction from full simulation) is a textbook inclusive cross-section procedure; no unfolding or resolution deconvolution is attempted, and the resolution-systematic estimate — the difference between "efficiency with smearing" and "efficiency without smearing" — is the crudest acceptable treatment.
- Comparing to GENIE is routine; the NuWRO comparison [21] is a reasonable second model, but invoking an off-the-shelf spectral-function parameterization (TE meson exchange, per the citation footnote) is not a theoretical contribution.
- The flux model is borrowed wholesale from MINOS [22] (a *private communication* at that), and the dominant systematics — an 11% flat flux error and the MINOS-validated FLUKA hadron-production tuning [23,24,25] — are inherited, not invented.
- The "results agree with other experiments" claim rests on targets (iron, water, hydrogen) with very different A; argon-specific nuclear effects (Fermi motion, 2p2h, final-state interactions, the argon-to-isoscalar correction of −3%/+3%) are *measured* here but not dissected — the paper draws no independent nuclear-physics conclusions, which is appropriate for a PRL letter but limits the interpretive content.

Minor flaws: both panels of Fig. 2 are labeled "ArgoNeuT Data (νµ)" though they show antineutrino data (a caption/legend inconsistency in v3), and the 26–28° ν bin (1.23 vs. neighbors 2.67 and 2.35) is an unexplained dip that is not discussed.

## Verdict

The novelty is *entirely in the object measured, not the method*: first inclusive ν/ν̄ CC argon cross sections from a LArTPC, with unique access to the antineutrino beam via the MINOS pairing. As a 2014 physics result this was a genuine first that filled a real gap for the DUNE program and was used to tune interaction models; as a methodological paper, it introduces nothing. It is a clean, adequately-error-budgeted (flux-dominated) enablement measurement rather than a discovery.

## [1404.4809v3-methodology] Methodology reproducibility


I have the full paper (6 pages, ArgoNeuT Letter, arXiv:1404.4809v3). Below is the evaluation answering the question: how to reproduce the central experiment, and what the methodology papers over.

---

## Paper in one line
ArgoNeuT + MINOS-near measurement of flux-averaged, inclusive CC $\nu_\mu$–Ar and $\bar\nu_\mu$–Ar differential cross sections (in outgoing muon angle $\theta_\mu$ and momentum $p_\mu$) and integrated cross sections, on 1.20×10²⁰ POT of the NuMI beam in *antineutrino* mode. Results at $\langle E_\nu\rangle = 9.6$ (3.6) GeV, in $0<\theta_\mu<36°$, $0<p_\mu<25$ GeV/c.

## Methodology, in enough detail to reproduce the central experiment

**1. Applicable scope of the method.** The experiment is a *near-detector, two-detector cross-section measurement*. You need: (a) a small LAr TPC in front of (b) a magnetized tracking calorimeter. ArgoNeuT alone cannot measure $p_\mu$ (GeV muons don't stop in 47 cm) nor reliably the charge sign, so MINOS-near is load-bearing. The central result is the ratio in Eq. (1); everything else feeds one of its five inputs ($N_{meas,i}$, $N_{bgnd,i}$, $\epsilon_i$, $N_{tgt}$, $\Phi$).

**2. Data.** NuMI antineutrino mode, Sept 2009–Feb 2010, 1.20×10²⁰ POT, both detectors live. Antineutrino mode is chosen specifically because it is *neutrino-enriched*: forward-going, unfocused $\pi^+$, $K^+$ decay into $\nu_\mu$ and make up ~60% of the interactions. Both samples are therefore measured in the same run.

**3. Detectors and their roles.**
- *ArgoNeuT*: 170 L, active volume $47 \times 40 \times 90$ cm³ (drift×height×length), 2 wire planes at one edge, 481 V/cm drift. Measures the event vertex and the muon polar angle $\theta_\mu$ (1–3° resolution [16]).
- *MINOS-near*: 1 kton magnetized steel/scintillator, front face ~1.5 m downstream. Measures $p_\mu$ (5–10% resolution, by curvature if through, by range if stopping) and the charge sign $Q_\mu$ [17].

**4. Reconstruction.** LArSoft [15] in ArgoNeuT: hits → line-like clusters by proximate grouping, seeded projection from low-to-high hit density → 3D tracks from time-coincident line pairs in the two planes. Muon propagation from ArgoNeuT to MINOS and inside MINOS via GEANT3. A standalone MINOS sim/reco stack characterizes the ArgoNeuT→MINOS track matching.

**5. Selection (the only three cuts).**
- (i) Neutrino vertex inside the ArgoNeuT fiducial box: 3 cm from inner sense wires and from cathode plane, 4 cm top/bottom, 6 cm upstream / 4 cm downstream of the TPC ends.
- (ii) A 3D ArgoNeuT muon track must leave the TPC and be *matched* to a MINOS muon with a hit within 20 cm of MINOS's upstream face; the match is decided by radial + angular agreement between the projected-to-MINOS ArgoNeuT extrapolation and the candidate MINOS track, penalized by expected multiple scattering. The 1.5 m gap and intervening rock are handled by the GEANT3 propagation.
- (iii) MINOS charge sign: for CC $\nu_\mu$ the matched MINOS muon must be positive; for CC $\bar\nu_\mu$ it must be negative.
Through-going muons that slipped the fiducial cut are removed by *manual visual scanning*. Result: **1676 $\nu_\mu$ CC and 1605 $\bar\nu_\mu$ CC candidate events.**

**6. Background and efficiency (both simulation-driven).** Generator is GENIE 2.8.0 [19]; ArgoNeuT detector model is GEANT4-based [18]; MINOS propagation is GEANT3 [20]. Two background species, computed in simulation: *mismatched tracks* (ArgoNeuT muon matched in MINOS but with the wrong reconstructed charge sign) and *neutral-current events*. Counts: 97 ($\nu_\mu$) and 141 ($\bar\nu_\mu$). Detection efficiency per bin $\epsilon_i$ is the full chain — muon acceptance between the two detectors, ArgoNeuT→MINOS track-match rate, ArgoNeuT vertex reco, track reco in both detectors, selection, and resolution smearing — averaged to 42.0% ($\nu_\mu$) / 59.0% ($\bar\nu_\mu$). Neutrinos have lower efficiency than antineutrinos because of their larger hadronic activity (more clutter → worse vertex/track reco and matching).

**7. Cross-section extraction (Eq. 1).** Bin-by-bin:
$$\frac{d\sigma(u_i)}{du} = \frac{N_{meas,i}-N_{bgnd,i}}{\Delta u_i\cdot \epsilon_i\cdot N_{tgt}\cdot \Phi}.$$
No unfolding matrix — detector resolution is treated as a systematic by *recomputing* $\epsilon_i$ under two limits (default: smeared; alternative: no smearing) and taking the difference. The $\sigma/E_\nu$ integration uses the smearing-free treatment, with the explicit assumption that bin migration doesn't affect the integral.

**8. Flux.** FLUKA simulation [23] of the NuMI beamline, hadron production tuned to MINOS near-detector data [24] and NA49 [25]. A *flat* 11% uncertainty is assigned across all energy bins (matching the MINERvA choice [10,11] at this energy). Energy-binned flux values are in Table III (e.g. $\nu_\mu$ at 2–3 GeV is $3.7\times10^3$ per GeV·m²·10⁹ POT; $\bar\nu_\mu$ at 2–3 GeV is $6.9\times10^4$).

**9. Targets.** $N_{tgt}$ = argon nuclei in fiducial volume. Counting uncertainty: 2.0%. POT: 1.0%. Fiducial volume: 2.0% ($\nu_\mu$) / 1.6% ($\bar\nu_\mu$).

**10. Ar → isoscalar-nucleon correction.** Per-nucleon cross sections are quoted; the Ar→isoscalar correction is **computed with GENIE** and is ≈ −3% for $\nu_\mu$ and +3% for $\bar\nu_\mu$. No additional uncertainty is assigned to this correction (it is not a row in Table IV).

**11. Reported systematics (Table IV), total cross section only:** N_Ar 2.0%, POT 1.0%, Fiducial 2.0%/1.6%, Flat flux 11.0%/11.0%. Quadrature sums to ≈ 12.3% ($\nu_\mu$) / 11.7% ($\bar\nu_\mu$), consistent with the reported 0.66±0.03±0.08 and 0.28±0.01±0.03 ×10⁻³⁸ cm²/GeV.

**12. What is *not* released that is needed for full reproduction.** The flux comes from a *private MINOS communication* [22]; the matching criterion's numeric radial/angular thresholds are stated only qualitatively; the GENIE 2.8.0 and the specific NuMI-an-mode beamline geometry are needed but only referenced; LArSoft configuration (seed length, cluster-merge, drift calibration) is a citation, not a parameter list.

## Assumptions the methodology papers over

1. **FLUKA + tuned-hadron-production is the "known" flux.** No ArgoNeuT or MINOS data-driven flux estimate is used; ArgoNeuT itself measures no hadrons and no flux. The entire cross-section normalization rides on a simulation. The 11% flat band absorbs hadron-production, horn-focusing, and beamline-geometry uncertainties in one number, and the paper does not break them out, nor does it test energy dependence. It also does not include a term for the *neutrino/antineutrino spectrum split* as a separate component, even though the two are computed in the same simulation.

2. **Single simulation stack for background.** NC background and wrong-sign mismatch rate are both taken from a GENIE-2.8.0 + GEANT4/3 stack on argon. There is no independent (e.g., data-driven or alternative-generator) estimate; a bias in GENIE's NC/CC ratio on argon propagates 1:1 into both samples and is not assigned a systematic.

3. **Efficiency is entirely simulation-estimated.** No data-driven efficiency anchor is used (no through-going-muon sample, no MINOS-only cross-check normalized to data). The 42/59% average efficiency — including MINOS charge-ID and the 20-cm upstream hit requirement — is only varied in the smeared/no-smear systematic, so *systematic* biases in vertex/track/match efficiency are not covered.

4. **Charge sign is the sole flavor tag.** Neutrino/antineutrino separation is binary in MINOS $Q_\mu$, and the mismatch rate is treated as an additive background rather than as a charge-misidentification probability folded into the selection. Charge-ID efficiency for low-momentum and near-horizon muons is not stated as a number.

5. **Argon nuclear physics is fully delegated to GENIE 2.8.0.** Final-state interactions, Fermi motion, spectral function, shadowing, 2p2h and other many-body contributions, and the specific Ar form factors are all inherited from GENIE with no variation. The Ar→isoscalar −3%/+3% correction is itself a *GENIE* number and is applied without an assigned uncertainty.

6. **No resolution unfolding.** Eq. 1 divides by efficiency and calls it done. Muon-angle and momentum smearing between true and measured bins is not unfolded; it is treated as a systematic by the smeared/no-smear difference. This is fine for broad bins but the paper does not quantify how much bin-to-bin migration is actually present; for the $p_\mu$ distribution the 1 GeV/c (early bins) to 1.25 GeV/c wide bins straddle a 5–10% momentum resolution, so the effect is non-negligible in principle.

7. **Manual through-going-muon scan is unweighted.** The visual scan that removes residual through-going muons is subjective; its efficiency and bias are not quantified and no systematic is assigned.

8. **Fiducial volume assumes uniform target density and sharp edges.** The 3/4/6/4-cm box ignores the actual wire geometry, cathode dead region, and the possibility that a muon from a true fiducial vertex scatters back out. This is partly in the 2%/1.6% fiducial systematic, but the asymmetry of upstream (6 cm) vs. downstream (4 cm) cuts and the reduced $\bar\nu_\mu$ fiducial error (1.6%) are not justified numerically.

9. **The 60% neutrino-content figure is a MINOS result [13], not an ArgoNeuT measurement.** The very existence of a clean $\nu_\mu$ sample in the antineutrino run depends on this external number; its uncertainty is not propagated.

10. **"Flux-averaged" is being compared across a wide spectrum.** The headline "cross section at $9.6 \pm 6.5$ GeV" quotes a 68%-of-flux energy width of 6.5 GeV — the mean is a fair description of the *neutrino*, but the "at $E_\nu = 9.6$ GeV" label hides the fact that the measurement is genuinely an energy integral and the shape information in Tables I–II is what carries the physics. The comparison to MINERvA/T2K/PDG at 4.3 GeV [8,3] is an *integral-to-integral* comparison, not a point-wise one.

11. **Only one data-taking period (Sept 2009–Feb 2010) and one beam-current configuration.** No run-to-run variation study. Beam-line conditions (horn current, upstream target pressure) are frozen at their 2009/2010 values inside the FLUKA model, with their uncertainty lumped into the 11%.

12. **NuWRO comparison.** NuWRO [21] is version 11m with spectral function and "TE" MECs; the paper does not state that GENIE 2.8.0 lacks (or has only partial) spectral function for argon, so the "GENIE agrees with data, NuWRO agrees with GENIE" comparison does not isolate which nuclear-structure ingredients are responsible.

13. **Statistical errors are computed as if events in each bin were Poisson with zero background correlation.** The 97/141 background estimates come from a single MC ensemble; their MC-statistical uncertainty is not visible in the reported stat errors.

14. **The total-systematic row in Table IV is for the *integrated* cross section.** The *differential* cross sections in Tables I/II quote a "total error" that mixes stat and syst, but the paper does not give a per-bin breakdown of what fraction of each bin's total error is statistical vs. each of the four systematics. A reader cannot re-derive the differential bin-by-bin systematic.

15. **No near/far or data-driven cross-check on the isoscalar normalization.** The ±3% Ar→isoscalar correction is not verified against another experiment's argon or isoscalar measurement at comparable energy.

## Assessment

**Soundness.** The methodology is exactly the standard 2012-era near-detector neutrino cross-section recipe: a small LAr TPC for kinematics + a magnetized detector downstream for $p$ and sign, selection reduced to three cuts, efficiency and background from a single GEANT/GENIE stack, flux from FLUKA tuned to hadron data, systematics dominated by that tuned flux. It is internally consistent and the numbers reconcile: candidate counts (1676/1605), background (97/141), efficiency (42/59%), and the systematics table reproduce the quoted total. The one honest weakness the authors flag is that neutrino efficiency is lower than antineutrino efficiency, with a larger fiducial systematic — this is correctly noted in the text and reflected in the 2.0 vs. 1.6% fiducial asymmetry.

**Originality.** As a *method*, essentially none — the recipe is the canonical one. As a *data set*, the novelty is the target (argon) and the fact that both $\nu_\mu$ and $\bar\nu_\mu$ are reported in a single antineutrino-mode run, exploiting the 60% neutrino content. This is the first such measurement on liquid argon, and it is the paper's real contribution.

**Impact.** Bounded but real. These are the data points DUNE-class experiments need to calibrate event generators on argon and reduce near/far systematics; MINERvA, T2K, and MicroBooNE/MicroBooNE-successors have built on and superseded this specific measurement over the following decade. Impact is real for a niche audience — neutrino-interaction modeling — not field-shaping in the broad sense.

# Review of arXiv:1404.4809v3 — ArgoNeuT, "Measurements of Inclusive Muon Neutrino and Antineutrino Charged-Current Differential Cross Sections on Argon in the NuMI Antineutrino Beam"

(I've quoted text from a machine extraction of the PDF; note the extractor drops the overbar, so "νµ" in this review sometimes means ν̄µ where the antineutrino is intended. The substance below is unaffected.)

The paper reports the first inclusive ν̄µ–Ar CC differential cross sections (dσ/dθµ, dσ/dpµ) from the 170-liter ArgoNeuT LArTPC, with a companion νµ measurement extracted from the mispolarity component of the NuMI antineutrino beam, at 1.2×10²⁰ POT. Methodology is conventional (sim-based efficiency, Eq. 1 cross-section inversion, comparison to GENIE/NuWRO). The writing is generally clear; the problems are concentrated in the framing paragraphs and in how the data–model comparison is presented.

## Sentences that overclaim beyond the evidence

**1. "With the recent decision for the future U.S. long-baseline neutrino detector to be a large LArTPC, it is crucial to understand both neutrino and antineutrino interactions on argon for future CP violation searches." (Intro)**
CP-violation precision in DUNE-class experiments is dominated by flux normalization, oscillation (systematic) parameters, and near-detector modeling — not by the argon-target interaction uncertainty quantified here. "Crucial for CP violation searches" imports a motivation the paper never quantifies. Defensible form: "essential for event simulation and near-detector modeling of future LArTPC experiments."

**2. "Neutrino interactions comprise almost 60% of all the neutrino/antineutrino-induced events in the detector in antineutrino mode. This fact enables the measurements of both νµ and ν̄µ CC differential cross sections using this data sample."**
"Enables" flatters the neutrino channel. It is not a like-for-like measurement of the two channels: the ν component is the mis-polarity contamination of an *off-mode* beam, and its flux comes solely from simulation (the private-communication NuMI flux, ref. [22]) — there is no beamline-optimized or data-constrained normalization for it. The sentence also hides that 60% of the detector-level "neutrino" sample is background in spirit. A fairer sentence would state the ν result carries the full (simulated) mis-polarity flux normalization, which it does not explicitly do even once.

**3. "the genie predictions agree with data for neutrinos but overestimate data slightly for antineutrinos" and "nuwro expectations are consistent with those from genie in most bins."**
Three problems stacked in one paragraph:
- Neither "agree" nor "slightly" is quantified — no binned residual, no χ² anywhere in the letter.
- The comparison is semi-circular: data and model expectations are both convolved with the *same* simulated flux, which carries a flat 11% normalization error (and no shape error). A flux error would appear as agreement by construction; a shape error in the mis-polarity component would be invisible in this comparison.
- Since "NuWRO is consistent with GENIE in most bins," the data, by their own statement, discriminate between neither model — which undercuts the tuning claim below.

**4. "These results and the previous ArgoNeuT result … are consistent with total cross section measurements made by other experiments in a similar energy region [8]."**
Reference [8] is the PDG Review cross-section summary — a compilation, not a measurement; the "measurements" being compared to are not enumerated. "Similar energy region" elides a spread from ~2.5 to ~10 GeV and, worse, targets (O, C, Fe, isoscalar) vs. argon; the 3% argon-to-isoscalar "correction" is itself evaluated with GENIE, so cross-target consistency is partially model-defined. "Consistent" should be made quantitative (e.g., a per-experiment comparison with an explicitly quoted tolerance) or softened to "in broad agreement with, at the level of, other targets."

**5. "The results are useful for tuning neutrino event generators, reducing the systematics associated with a long baseline neutrino oscillation experiment's near-far comparison, and informing the theory of neutrino-nucleus interactions generally." (Conclusion)**
The most overclaimed sentence in the paper:
- *Tuning generators*: with ~11% flat flux normalization and 10–30% bin errors that bracket GENIE–NuWRO differences, the data cannot fix a model shape; a flux re-normalization absorbs most of the visible model-to-data offset. "Constraints on," not "useful for tuning."
- *Near-far comparison*: the whole point of near-far is to cancel flux; an absolute cross-section measurement on argon in a *different near detector* (ArgoNeuT, 1.5 m in front of MINOS) does not directly reduce the experiment's own near-far systematics. This benefit is asserted with no argument.
- *"Informing the theory … generally"*: boilerplate grandiosity.

**6. "⟨Eν⟩ = 9.6 (3.6) ± 6.5 (1.5) GeV, … where the ±6.5 (1.5) GeV represents the range that contains 68% of the flux."**
Using the ± error notation for the width of the incident flux in the headline result line invites misreading as a measurement error, right next to "the first error is statistical and the second is systematic." Standard fix: "at ⟨Eν⟩ = 9.6 (3.6) GeV, with 68% of the flux in [3.1, 16.1] (2.1, 5.1) GeV."

**7. "In general, neutrino interactions have higher energy hadronic activity than antineutrino interactions, resulting in a reduced efficiency for neutrino events and a larger systematic error on the fiducial volume."**
Muddled causality: a reduced *efficiency* does not produce a *larger systematic on the fiducial volume*; the intended point is that the MC description of vertex finding is more model-dependent for ν, so the fiducial-volume uncertainty is taken larger (2.0% vs 1.6% in Table IV). As written, the sentence asserts a connection the paper never demonstrates.

**8. "measurements on liquid argon are virtually non-existent [3]"** — the "non-existence" of an experimental program is cited to the authors' own prior paper rather than to an actual inventory of targets in refs. [8]–[11]. Minor, but it reads as self-referential framing.

**9. "The muon angular resolution determined by ArgoNeuT is 1–3° [16]"** — 16 is a Yale Ph.D. thesis. For a key detector-performance number in a PRL, a refereeable source (or an in-paper derivation) is expected.

**10. Missing rather than overclaimed, but a framing gap:** the mismatched-track + NC background (97/141 events, 6–9% of the samples) is simulation-only; Table IV's systematic budget (targets, POT, fiducial volume, flat flux) omits any MC-background or track-matching rate term, and no closure test of the Eq. 1 extraction on toys is described, nor a data-driven control region for the mismatch rate. The paper presents its systematic table as complete ("All considered sources of systematic uncertainty…") while the dominant 11% flux term is, itself, a single flat number from a non-public source [22].

## What an adversarial reviewer's first paragraph would say

> This letter presents ~1.7k events per channel from a 170-liter LArTPC whose muon momentum and charge are measured by a 1.5 m downstream, non-purpose-built sampling calorimeter, with 1–3° angular and 5–10% momentum resolution. A clean, conventional first-of-its-kind measurement: the extraction is standard, the numbers are self-consistent (in quadrature the Table IV terms give ≈11–12%, matching the quoted totals), and the "consistency with other experiments" claim survives scrutiny. The weaknesses are (a) the framing in the intro and conclusion, which sells physics consequences (CP violation, near–far systematics, generator tuning) that the stated 11% norm / 10–30% bin precision does not support; (b) the semi-circular data–model comparison lacking quantitative residuals; (c) an MC-only background chain presented without validation; and (d) a few muddled passages (items 6–7 above). A one‑line efficiency for the ν̄ tag would strengthen the "59% acceptance" statement.
- **Single beam mode.** Only antineutrino‑mode data are used. You cannot check the ν spectrum against a clean ν‑mode sample here; a cross‑check against the separate neutrino‑mode run (the earlier ArgoNeuT ν result, ref. [3]) would be worth an explicit sentence on whether the same GENIE tuning describes both.
- **Fig. 2 right panel** shows GENIE as a flat step with no statistical band and the data binned much finer than the model step — hard to judge bin‑by‑bin. Consider plotting the model down‑binned to the data or as per‑bin markers.
- This is a 6‑page letter. The method is standard (Eq. 1 flux/efficiency extraction) and I'm not faulting that, but the consequences above (ratio, isoscaling, resolution systematic) are exactly the kind of analysis that a full paper should carry. For a letter this is acceptable as a *first* result; I'd expect these to be taken up.

## Verdict
A solid, well‑motivated first‑of‑its‑kind experimental result, executed competently, that is a legitimate and useful input to LBNL/SBND/MICROBOONE and to interaction‑model tuning. It is not yet a strong paper because (a) the headline number is flux‑normalization dominated by an unpublished simulation, (b) it forgoes the flux‑robust ν̄/ν ratio it is well placed to deliver, (c) the model‑comparison conclusion is qualitative, and (d) the resolution systematic and isospin treatment are underworked. None of these are fatal, all are fixable, and the dataset is worth publishing.

**Recommended priority fixes:** add the ν̄/ν ratio; quantify the GENIE tension with a binned ratio; state and propagate the argon isospin correction; replace the smearing‑toggle systematic with a varied‑parameter study; make the flux fully citable.
