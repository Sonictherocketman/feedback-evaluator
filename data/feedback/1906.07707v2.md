## Review: "Coherent States for the Manin Plane via Toeplitz Quantization"
*Durčevich & Sontz (arXiv:1906.07707v2, math-ph)*

### What the paper does

The authors develop the Sontz theory of Toeplitz quantization (symbols drawn from a possibly non-commutative algebra) in the concrete setting of the Manin plane $\mathbb{C}Q_q(\theta,\bar\theta)$, defined by $\theta\bar\theta=q\bar\theta\theta$. The central, framing idea is to **invert the usual quantization order**: rather than starting with a classical phase space and quantizing it to obtain annihilation/creation operators and their eigenvectors, one starts with a Toeplitz annihilation operator $T_{\bar\theta}$ and *reads off* a "phase space" as the point spectrum (eigenvalue set) of that operator (Def. 3.1). Coherent states are defined as eigenvectors $T_{\bar\theta}\phi_\lambda=\lambda\phi_\lambda$, the recursion for the coefficients is solved explicitly (eq. 3.1), and a radius of convergence $R_w$ controls which complex numbers actually occur as eigenvalues. The rest of the paper transposes the standard coherent-state apparatus — resolution of the identity, time evolution, the Bargmann/coherent-state transform into a generalized Segal–Bargmann space with reproducing kernel, a secondary Toeplitz quantization, Berezin/Glauber upper and lower symbols — into this setting, and closes with a comparison to the (nilpotent) paragrassmann algebra.

### Assessment of the argument

**Genuine strength — the viewpoint.** The "eigenvalues define the phase space, not the other way round" perspective is legitimately interesting in the non-commutative setting, where there is no ambient classical space to quantify. Section 3 is the heart of the paper and it is executed cleanly: the eigenvector recursion (3.1), the explicit solution (3.2), the radius formula for $R_w$ and its worked values ($w_n=n!,|q|=1\Rightarrow R_w=\infty$; $w_n=1,q=1\Rightarrow R_w=1$), and Prop. 3.1 are correct and well argued. Section 5's observation that coherent states evolve into coherent states under $e^{-itN}$ is a nice confirmation that the construction has some of the expected structure.

**Main weakness — the marquee machinery is conditional, not established.** The headline "curious composition of quantization schemes" (SB space of Secs. 6–7, coherent-state quantization of Sec. 8, upper symbols of Sec. 10) is repeatedly gated on the assumption "suppose the resolution of the identity (4.1) holds for some measure $\rho$." But the paper never *proves* existence of such a $\rho$ apart from the classical Gaussian case ($w_j=j!,|q|=1$). Section 4 honestly reduces this to a Hausdorff/Stieltjes moment problem (Theorem 4.1, eq. 4.7) and cites Schmüdgen, but the necessary moment conditions are never shown to be sufficient anywhere except that one example. So the substantial bulk of the paper describes an edifice whose foundation (existence of $\rho$) is left unproved for essentially all cases of interest. This is acknowledged in passing (Remark 6.3, end of Sec. 6, Sec. 7) but is underplayed relative to how confidently the abstract presents the composition as having been carried out. A reader coming for the "composition of quantization schemes" gets, for general weights, a catalogue of *what would hold if* $\rho$ existed, not a construction of $\rho$.

**Patchiness in the creation/annihilation story.** Because the authors multiply the symbol on the *left* (a departure from their earlier work, justified after the fact in Sec. 9 rather than up front), they correctly observe that for $q\neq 1$ the creation operator $T_\theta$ and annihilation operator $T_{\bar\theta}$ are *not* adjoints. They then patch this by redefining the creation operator as $(T_{\bar\theta})^*$ — but Theorem 9.2 (the clean result that this symbol is $\lambda^*$) only holds for **real** $q$. A core interpretation (a natural CCR-like pair) thus silently becomes $q$-dependent and partially breaks for complex $q$. This deserves far more prominent treatment than a remark buried in Sec. 9.

**"Remarkable" results that are less so than claimed.** Theorem 9.1, that the *normalized* lower symbol of $T_{\bar\theta}$ is exactly the identity function and is independent of both $q$ and the weights, is flagged twice as surprising/remarkable. In fact this is essentially a formal consequence of the eigenvector relation together with the authors' own sesquilinear-form convention (linear in the second slot, so $\langle\phi_\lambda,T_{\bar\theta}\phi_\lambda\rangle=\lambda\langle\phi_\lambda,\phi_\lambda\rangle$). It is a consistent and useful sanity check, but it does not validate the scheme, and the emphasis oversells it.

**Domain hand-waving.** Several calculations are explicitly left "formal," with domains set aside (Sec. 9, the $(T_\theta)^\sharp$ computation for the creation operator). For unbounded operators on an incomplete pre-Hilbert space, some of these are not negligible, and at least one (why $T_{\bar\theta}$ as computed on $\mathcal P$ extends to the claimed dense domain $D(T_{\bar\theta})$) is asserted via "standard functional analysis." The $R_w$ boundary behavior — whether boundary points are actual eigenvalues — is also left open (conjectured spectrum = closure of $B_w$).

**The comparison (Sec. 11) is somewhat anti-climactic.** The paragrassmann example yields a nilpotent annihilation operator with a one-point phase space $\{0\}$ and a single coherent state, used essentially to motivate "extreme quantum theory" (Def. 3.3), a definition invoked exactly once. It reads as tacked on to a thin point, and the authors themselves call the outcome "trivial."

**Depth vs. length.** At 24 pages, the paper is long for its genuinely new content: a definition plus eigenvector computation (Sec. 3), a radius-of-convergence analysis, and a (largely conditional) transcription of standard Bargmann/Berezin/Glauber machinery. The physical framing is thin — Planck's constant enters only in the discussion (Sec. 1) that the *Weyl–Heisenberg* algebra, not the Manin plane, is "really" quantum, which is a terminological clarification more than a substantive result. The "surprise" that the Manin plane's phase space is a ball $B_w$ is asserted but not deeply unpacked or tied to the geometry of the $q$-deformation beyond the radius formula.

**Proofreading.** Several errors undercut a polished presentation: "Lesbegue" (for Lebesgue), "Stieljes" (Stieltjes), "n-tples" (n-tuples), "it it exists" (doubled word). The Dirac-notation caveat ("technically does not apply, since we are not in a Hilbert space setting") is worth carrying through more carefully given how much of the argument leans on $|\phi_\lambda\rangle\langle\phi_\lambda|$.

### What would strengthen the paper

1. **Prove existence of the resolution-of-identity measure $\rho$ in at least one genuinely non-Gaussian case** (e.g., a $q$-deformed weight family), or state precisely the Hausdorff/Stieltjes condition under which (4.7) is a determinate/indeterminate moment problem. Right now the SB/secondary-quantization content is conditional throughout.
2. **Move the left-multiplication justification to the front** and confront the $q\neq 1$ non-adjointness of the creation/annihilation pair as a first-class feature, explaining what (if anything) the complex-$q$ case means physically.
3. **Moderate the "remarkable" language** to reflect that $(T_{\bar\theta})^\flat=\lambda$ follows formally from the definition.
4. **Tighten domain arguments** or state explicit lemmas for the extensions from $\mathcal P$ to $D(T_{\bar\theta})$ and to the SB inner product.
5. **Either develop the paragrassmann comparison into its own point or cut it / move it to remarks.**

### Bottom line

A competent, internally consistent piece of work whose central *viewpoint* — reading the phase space off the spectrum of a Toeplitz annihilation operator in a non-commutative setting — is a genuinely fresh angle and is executed well in its core (Sec. 3). Its principal limitation is that the most elaborate of its advertised constructions live behind an unproved existence assumption (the resolution of the identity), leaving general cases conditional, and there is meaningful patchiness (real-$q$ restrictions, domain hand-waving, the adjoint/creation mismatch) plus presentation errors. It contributes modestly but solidly to a niche area.

## [1906.07707v2-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


I have read the full paper (arXiv:1906.07707v2, "Coherent States for the Manin Plane via Toeplitz Quantization" by Đurdević and Sontz). Below is my assessment.

## Summary of the work

The paper develops coherent states for the noncommutative Manin plane ℂQ_q(θ,θ̄) inside the authors' framework of densely defined Toeplitz quantization. Rather than starting from a classical phase space and quantizing, they go in the opposite order: the weights w_n of a sesquilinear form determine a Hilbert space and Toeplitz operators; the annihilation operator T_{θ̄} is then studied, and its point spectrum (the "phase space" B_w) yields coherent states φ_λ as eigenvectors. The paper computes these states explicitly (with a radius of convergence R_w depending on |q| and w_n), studies the resolution of the identity (reduced to a Hausdorff/Stieltjes moment problem for radial measures), the coherent state transform (identifying a generalized Segal–Bargmann space as a reproducing kernel space), time evolution under the number operator, upper and lower (Berezin) symbols, relates the two quantization schemes, and closes with a comparison showing the paragrassmann algebra is an "extreme quantum theory" with a one-point phase space {0}.

## Strengths

1. **A coherent, well-executed research program.** The conceptual move — deriving the phase space from the point spectrum of a quantum annihilation operator instead of quantizing a pre-given classical space — is clearly articulated (Remarks 1–3.2) and consistent. The paper is honest about what its framework buys: a quantum object playing the role of phase space.

2. **Rigorous functional analysis.** Domains of densely defined operators are handled properly (eq. (2.4)), the convergence of the coherent-state series is analyzed via the root test with an explicit R_w formula, and Proposition 3.1 carefully proves eigenvectors lie in the domain. The treatment of when no coherent states exist (the "curious" R_w = 0 case, a trivial phase space) is a nice touch.

3. **The paragrassmann comparison (Section 11) is the conceptual gem.** Showing T_{θ̄} is nilpotent (a single Jordan block), with point spectrum {0} and hence a truly one-point phase space, gives the slogan "paragrassmann algebras are extremely quantum" a precise and defensible meaning.

4. **Clean unconditional results.** The normalized lower symbol of T_{θ̄} is identically the identity map of the phase space, *independent of q and of the weights* (Theorem 9.1, Remark 9.5) — a genuinely pleasant normality check. Likewise Theorem 10.1 (the annihilation operator is the frame quantization of f(λ) = λ) and the reproducing-kernel identification (Corollary 6.1, matching Bargmann's classical observation).

5. **Honest about limitations.** The authors repeatedly flag which theorems are conditional on a resolution of the identity whose existence is an open moment problem, rather than hiding it. That intellectual honesty is commendable even if (see below) it weakens the take-home results.

## Weaknesses and points to address

1. **Two formula discrepancies in the version I read, both of which should be re-checked.**
   - The general formula (2.2) of Theorem 2.1, as printed — $q^{-jn}(w_{n+i}/(w_n w_{n+i-j}))^{1/2}\phi_{n+i-j}$ — at i = 0, j = 1 gives $q^{-n}w_{n-1}^{-1/2}\phi_{n-1}$, whereas the annihilation formula (2.3), which the paper explicitly derives "about which (2.2) says" and uses throughout, is $q^{-n}(w_n/w_{n-1})^{1/2}\phi_{n-1}$. These agree only for w_n = 1. The same mismatch appears for the creation formula (9.1) at i = 1, j = 0: (2.2) yields $w_n^{-1/2}\phi_{n+1}$ but (9.1) says $(w_{n+1}/w_n)^{1/2}\phi_{n+1}$. Both (2.3) and (9.1) are correct in the standard Segal–Bargmann limit (w_n = n!, q = 1: $\sqrt{n}\,\phi_{n-1}$ and $\sqrt{n+1}\,\phi_{n+1}$), so it is (2.2) and the last summation step of its proof that appears to carry an error (the factor $w_{n+i-j+j}$ vs. a $w_{n-j}$-type factor). Since Theorem 2.1 is foundational for the rest of the paper, this must be corrected in the final version.
   - Similarly, eq. (6.4) gives $\|f\|^2_{L^2(\rho)} = \sum |a_j|^2 |q|^{-j(j+1)} w_j$ for $f$ in $L^2_{AH}$, while Theorem 6.3 (and the last display of its proof) states the norm is $\sum |a_j|^2 |q|^{j(j+1)} w_j$ (and in the final line even $\sum |a_j|^2 w_j$). The q-powers cannot both be correct for |q| ≠ 1. Again, please re-check the typesetting/derivation.
   I am flagging these as they appear in the version under review; they may be typographical, but they concern the paper's core formulas.

2. **Much of the content is conditional on an unresolved moment problem.** Sections 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10 all begin "suppose the resolution of the identity (4.1) holds for some measure ρ." The only fully solved example is the classical one (w_n = n!, |q| = 1, Gaussian ρ). So for the genuinely noncommutative cases of interest, the paper establishes a *framework* for which the basic existence of ρ is unknown and (per Remark 4.1) need not be unique. That is honest, but it means the headline applications (Segal–Bargmann space, coherent state quantization, upper symbols) come with an asterisk in every new example. At minimum, one more explicit noncommutative case where ρ is exhibited (or proved not to exist) would materially strengthen the paper.

3. **The "extreme quantum theory" definition (Def. 3.3) is less discriminating than intended.** "Point spectrum of at most one point" is vacuously satisfied by any bounded annihilation operator with *empty* point spectrum. If the conjecture Spec(T_{θ̄}) = cl B_w fails in general, perfectly ordinary examples (e.g., weighted backwards shifts with |q| ≥ 1, w_n = 1) could be classified as "extreme quantum" merely because their spectrum might lie on the boundary circle. The term "extreme" should either be defined in terms of the full spectrum plus the phase-space interior, or the point-spectrum-based definition needs a caveat to the effect that the *generic* boundary-only spectrum case is excluded from being called extreme for substantive reasons.

4. **The left-multiplication convention has costs the authors only note in passing.** Because the symbol is multiplied on the left rather than on the right, T_θ is *not* the adjoint of T_{θ̄} for q ≠ 1 (Remark 9.5 / Section 9 discussion), and the "correct" creation operator for the theory turns out to be (T_{θ̄})^*, which is not a Toeplitz operator at all. This is acknowledged, but it arguably weakens the claim that the construction is a "quantization" in the usual C*-algebraic sense: the creation/annihilation pairs one is used to in physics are no longer Toeplitz operators with holomorphic/anti-holomorphic symbols. A longer, more reflective discussion of why left multiplication is the right choice (beyond "right multiplication gives results that are too easy") would help readers.

5. **Thin physical content.** Planck's constant is deliberately absent (the Manin plane is treated as a noncommutative *geometry* object), and with it go the tools that make coherent states useful: there is no classical limit, no uncertainty principle, and the time evolution is just λ ↦ λe^{-it} under the number operator. Section 5 is mathematically correct but essentially vacuous as dynamics. For a paper in the coherent-states literature, one would at least like a semi-classical discussion or a statement of which weights w_n (if any) give a "physical" theory — the two examples w_n = n! and w_n = 1 are treated, but their physical status is unclear (what is |q|? Is |q| = 1 required? Why?).

6. **Originality is real but incremental.** The machinery — dense Toeplitz quantization, coherent states as eigenvectors of annihilation operators, Segal–Bargmann spaces from coherent state transforms — is all prior work of Sontz ([16]–[19]). This paper is the specialization to the Manin plane plus the "reverse order" (spectrum → phase space) viewpoint and the paragrassmann comparison. Valuable, but an extension of an established program rather than a new method; the series in (3.2) is a familiar q-oscillator-type object.

7. **Minor remarks.** The phrase "direct notation (which technically does not apply...)" for P² shows the projection formalism is used outside the strictly justified regime — fine given the care taken, but worth a sentence on why it is legitimate. The term "dimensionless time" is a fun aside in Section 5, harmless. The exclusion of R_w = 0 (Remark 3.4) is well motivated given current tools.

## Verdict

This is a careful, well-written piece of mathematics within a coherent research program, with one genuinely interesting observation (the paragrassmann/one-point phase space comparison) and several clean normality identities (Theorems 9.1, 9.2, 10.1). Its limitations are: core formulas that need repair (Theorem 2.1's general form, Theorem 6.3's norm formula), a large body of results that are conditional on an unresolved moment problem (with the new noncommutative cases left open), a somewhat ad hoc convention that breaks Toeplitz adjointness, and thin physical interpretation. Overall a sound, honest, mid-level contribution that should see a corrected final version and perhaps one explicit nontrivial example with ρ constructed.

## [1906.07707v2-critique] Critique


# Critique — *"Coherent States for the Manin Plane via Toeplitz Quantization"* (Đurdević & Sontz, arXiv:1906.07707v2)

## What the paper does

The paper builds a coherent-state calculus on the non‑commutative Manin plane $\mathbb{C}Q_q(\vartheta,\overline{\vartheta})$ ($\vartheta\overline{\vartheta}=q\overline{\vartheta}\vartheta$) using densely defined Toeplitz operators. The centerpiece is a deliberate **reversal of the usual quantization logic**: rather than starting with a classical phase space and quantizing it, the authors take the non‑commutative algebra as the "classical" input and **define the phase space as the point spectrum of the Toeplitz annihilation operator** $T_{\overline{\vartheta}}$. They then extract, in sequence: explicit coherent states (3.2) and their radius of convergence $R_w$ (the ball $B_w$ of possible eigenvalues); a resolution of the identity; a time‑evolution flow generated by the number operator; a coherent‑state transform into a *generalized* Segal–Bargmann space with a reproducing kernel (6.1); a *secondary* Toeplitz quantization with ordinary function symbols (Sec. 7); coherent‑state/frame quantization ($Q_{cs}$, Sec. 8); and upper/lower (Berezin) symbols. A final section compares with paraGrassmann algebras.

## Strengths

- **The conceptual inversion is the paper's genuine contribution.** Remark 3.2 makes it explicit: the phase space is a quantum object characterized purely by the properties of the quantum theory. This is a clean, non‑standard framing — for $w_n=n!$, $|q|>1$ the phase space is all of $\mathbb{C}$; for $w_n=1$, $|q|=1$ it is the closed unit disk; and for $R_w=0$ it collapses to the single point $\{0\}$, a "trivial phase space." The observation (Sec. 11) that the paraGrassmann algebra $PG_{1,q}$ for $q\ne1$ is a genuinely non‑commutative theory whose coherent‑state phase space is *one point* (so the coherent states do not span the Hilbert space) is a striking, well‑framed result and a nice application of the "extreme quantum theory" notion.
- **Honest functional analysis.** The domain treatment is careful ($\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathrm{D}(T^\sharp)\subsetneq\mathcal{H}$), the moment equations (4.6) are reduced correctly to a Hausdorff/Stieltjes moment problem, and — commendably — the authors note on p. 9 that the naive Fubini interchange **fails** (4.5 diverges) and restrict to the dense subspace $\mathcal{P}$ to justify it. Good mathematical hygiene for a math‑ph paper.
- **Self‑contained and well written.** Every tool is introduced before use; the calculations are explicit and checkable.

## Weaknesses

- **The entire second half is conditional on an unconstructed object.** The resolution of the identity (4.1) — hence the unitarity of the coherent‑state transform (Thm 6.1), the Segal–Bargmann/Hilbert‑space identification, the secondary quantization (Sec. 7), the frame quantization (Sec. 8), and the key upper‑symbol result (Thm 10.1) — all assume *there exists* a measure $\rho$ on $B_w$ obeying (4.6). That measure is only ever written down explicitly in the one Segal–Bargmann special case ($w_j=j!$, $|q|>1$). So the bulk of the machinery is a conditional program rather than a theorem; the central object, $\rho$, is asserted, not found. This is the paper's most serious limitation: much of its advertised "second quantization" is "suppose $\rho$ exists, then…".
- **A choice-driven inelegance.** Because Toeplitz operators use *left* multiplication of the symbol (Thm 2.1, differing from [16] by $q^{-jn}$), $T_\vartheta$ and $T_{\overline{\vartheta}}$ are **not adjoints** for $q\ne1$. The authors handle this correctly and honestly, but it forces the awkward device of promoting the *non‑Toeplitz* operator $(T_{\overline{\vartheta}})^*$ to the status of "the" creation operator. The consequence — that the *normalized* lower symbol of the annihilation operator is independently of $q$ and the weights (9.5) — is flagged as "remarkable" but then left as **a deliberately informal calculation** ("we decided not to give the technical details… formal calculation"), an actual gap in an otherwise rigorous note.
- **Thin physical content.** The physics flavor is light. The authors themselves concede that the quantum harmonic oscillator is only linked through the number operator, and that "neither the Manin plane nor $T_{\overline{\vartheta}}$ has anything to do with it a priori." This is ultimately a non‑commutative‑geometry / analysis paper wearing a physics coat; readers seeking physical insight (semi‑classical limit, minimal uncertainty, which the conclusion promises for a *forthcoming* paper) get little here.
- **Incremental assembly + redundancy.** Each individual ingredient — coherent‑state transform, reproducing kernel, upper/lower symbols, Toeplitz operators, frame quantization — is standard. The originality lies in the synthesis and the inversion of the phase‑space construction, not in new tools. There is also noticeable padding: repeated dense‑subspace / self‑adjointness arguments could be compressed, and the strict‑vs‑equality inclusion question for $\mathcal{SB}\subsetneq L^2_{\mathring{\mathcal{A}}_H}$ is repeatedly deferred rather than resolved.
- **Minor notational looseness:** $\mathcal{B}$ is both an orthonormal basis and its inner product is written before $\mathcal{H}$ is complete, and the use of "direct notation (which technically doesn't apply…)" on p. 3 acknowledges this but does not clean it up.

A competent, self‑contained, well‑written note in the non‑commutative‑geometry / coherent‑states niche. ## 2. The method, in reconstructable detail

The starting data (Section 2) are exactly **two free choices**:

**(i) Inner product & basis.** Define a sesquilinear form on the polynomial algebra ℚ₀ = ℂ[θ] by ⟨θ^⊖, θ^k⟩ = wᵢ δᵢ,ₖ (antilinear in the *first* entry — an explicit, flagged, non-standard convention). **(ii) Toeplitz operators & creation/annihilation.** A projection P = P²: ℚ→ℚ, P(θ^⊖) = Σₖᵤₖ^{-1}⟨θ^k, θ^⊖⟩ θ^k, and T_g = P(g φ) with **left** multiplication by the symbol g (deliberately different from [16]'s right multiplication — flag for [16] §4.4). The annihilation (symbol ȳ) and creation (symbol θ) operators are then explicit (Theorem 2.1, eqs. 2.2, 2.3, 9.1). These formulae are the engine of everything downstream.

The **phase space** B_w is then *not an input but an output*: the convergence radius R_w = limsup (|q|^{(n+1)} wₙ^{1/n})^{-1/2}, so B_w is the open disk of radius R_w (possibly all of ℂ, e.g. wₙ = n!, |q|=1; degenerate to a point if R_w = 0, which the authors deliberately exclude).

**(iv) Resolution of identity / moment problem (Sec. 4).** Look for a positive Borel measure ρ on B_w with I = ∫ dρ(λ) |φ_λ⟩⟨φ_λ|. Reduce to a radial density ρ(r) and get necessary moment conditions — eq. **(v) Coherent state transform & Segal–Bargmann space (Sec. 6).** With the ROI assumed, Cψ(λ) = ⟨φ_λ, ψ⟩ is a unitary map ℋ → its range 𝒮ℬ ⊂ L²(D_w, ρ), and 𝒮ℬ is the reproducing-kernel Hilbert space with kernel K(μ,λ) = ⟨φ_μ, φ_λ⟩ (eq. 6.1), reproducing the function space of the anti-holomorphic functions. Everything of substance from Section 6 onward — the generalized Segal‑Bargmann space $\mathcal{SB}$, the secondary Toeplitz operators $S_f$ (§7), the coherent‑state quantization $Q_{cs}$ (§8), and the upper‑symbol identifications of §10 — is stated **conditionally** on the standing hypothesis that a Borel measure $\rho$ on $B_w$ exists such that

$$I = \int_{B_w} d\rho(\lambda)\,|\phi_\lambda\rangle\langle\phi_λ| \qquad (4.1)$$

This is not proven. What the paper actually produces from its data is only the set of *necessary* moment conditions (4.3), and (for radial, absolutely‑continuous $\rho$) the single scalar moment problem (4.6):

$$\int_0^{R_w} dr\,\rho(r)\,r^{2j+1} = |q|^{-j(j+1)}\,w_j/2\pi \quad (j\ge 0).$$

Remark 4.1 is explicit that (4.6) is merely a *solvability prerequisite* ("either a Hausdorff moment problem if $0<R_w<\infty$, or a Stieltjes moment problem if $R_w=\infty$") and that, even after solving it, one "still has to check the possibly stronger condition (4.2)." A Hausdorff/Stieltjes moment problem does not, for arbitrary prescribed moments, admit a positive measure — it requires infinitely many Hankel‑matrix positivity conditions — and the paper **never verifies these for the general $(q,w)$ it advertises.** The only place a concrete $\rho$ is exhibited is the degenerate Segal‑Bargmann case ($w_n=n!$, $|q|=1$, $\rho=\pi^{-1}e^{-r^2}$), i.e. the one case that reduces to the ordinary coherent states of the harmonic oscillator.

So the data — "here is a parametrized family of coherent states $\{\phi_\lambda\}_{\lambda\in B_w}$ and here is their phase space" — does **not** entail that these states resolve the identity. A parametrized family forming a (tight/over‑complete) frame is a strictly stronger property than the coherent states themselves, and it is exactly the property the entire downstream construction needs. The authors import it as an assumption rather than deduce it. Every theorem labeled "Suppose there exists a measure $\rho$…" / "Assume the same hypothesis…" is downstream of this unproved step, so the paper's headline conclusion — a full *composition of quantization schemes* for the Manin plane — is only delivered **modulo a standing, never‑verified hypothesis** in the general case.

Two secondary symptoms confirm this is the load‑bearing gap rather than a local slip:

- **Boundedness of $T_{\overline\theta}$** is silently assumed in §10 for the main upper‑symbol result (Thm 10.1, "Suppose that $T_{\overline\theta}$ is a bounded operator"), another condition the general $(q,w)$ data does not supply.
- **§7 assumes $\mathcal{SB}=L^2_{AH}(D_w,\rho)$** to "avoid details" — Thm 6.3 and Remark 6.4 prove only the inclusion $\mathcal{SB}\subseteq L^2_{AH}$ and flag the reverse as an open problem.

(Apart from this, one real but minor notational defect: the printed *unnormalized* lower symbol on p. 19, $(T_{\overline\theta})^{\sharp}(\lambda)=\lambda\sum_{k\ge0}|\lambda|^{2k}|q|^{k(k+1)}w_{k-1}^{-1}$, does not reduce to the claimed normalized value $(T_{\overline\theta})^{\wedge}(\lambda)=\lambda$ unless $w_{k-1}=w_k$; the correctly‑executed calculation gives $\lambda\|\phi_\lambda\|^2=\lambda\sum_{k\ge0}|\lambda|^{2k}|q|^{k(k+1)}w_k^{-1}$. The conclusion of Thm 9.1 is nonetheless correct — the printed intermediate line has an $w_{k\mp1}$ index typo — but it is a typo, not the gap, because the actual computation closes.)

## Bottom line

The conclusion "the Manin plane supports a coherent‑state (frame) quantization with a Segal‑Bargmann realization and matching upper/lower symbols" stops being entailed by the data precisely at the **assertion of the resolution of the identity (4.1)**: the authors derive only the necessary moment conditions, recast them as a Hausdorff/Stieltjes moment problem, and leave its solvability unproven (and unverified on positivity) for the general $q$ and $w$ — a hypothesis under which all of Sections 6–10 are conditionally, rather than unconditionally, established.

**Originality.** The conceptual inversion — treating the non‑commutative Manin plane as the *classical* object and deriving the phase space from the *quantum* annihilation operator's point spectrum — is a neat and non‑trivial reformulation, and the secondary Toeplitz quantization built from the coherent‑state transform is a fresh construction. But the underlying apparatus (Toeplitz operators, coherent states, Berezin symbols, moment theory) is entirely standard and the exposition is largely synthetic, so it is moderately rather than strongly original.

**Overall.** Sections 1–5 are correct and the coherent states are handled carefully, but the paper's central program is built on an unverified standing assumption (the resolution of the identity) plus a few other assumed conditions, and contains a printed inconsistency in an intermediate symbolic calculation. Sound in the places it goes unconditional; conditional to the point of being a research‑program outline rather than a completed result for the general case.

**Impact.** A well‑written niche synthesis at the intersection of non‑commutative geometry and coherent‑state quantization; unlikely to reshape the field or be broadly adopted.

# Critical Review of "Coherent States for the Manin Plane via Toeplitz Quantization" by Ćurdević and Sontz

## Scope and contribution

The paper applies Sontz's general Toeplitz-quantization framework ([16,17]) to the Manin plane $\mathbb C Q_q(\vartheta,\bar\vartheta)$ (defined by $\bar\vartheta\vartheta=q\vartheta\bar\vartheta$) and develops the associated coherent-state machinery: eigenvectors of the annihilation operator $T_{\bar\vartheta}$, resolution of the identity, coherent state transform, a "secondary" Toeplitz quantization on the resulting Segal–Bargmann space, Berezin/Glauber upper and lower symbols, and a finite-dimensional analogue using the paragrassmann algebra.

The conceptual inversion the authors emphasize — that the "phase space" is the point spectrum of the annihilation operator, hence a *quantum* object rather than a classical input — is an honest and interesting framing (made clear in Remark 3.2). The "secondary" quantization ($S:L^\infty(D_w,\rho)\to\mathcal L(\mathcal{SB})$ on the reproducing-kernel space $\mathcal{SB}$) is a genuinely new construction and the main structural novelty of the paper. The paragrassmann-algebra comparison (Section 11) cleanly recovers [5] as a (trivial-phase-space) special case.

## Mathematical soundness — notable issues

1. **The general radius-of-convergence formula is wrong (sign error in $w_n$).** On p. 6 the authors give
$$R_w=\bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}|q|^{(n+1)}w_n^{1/n}\bigr)^{-1/2}=\bigl(\liminf_{n\to\infty}|q|^{-(n+1)}w_n^{1/n}\bigr)^{1/2}.$$
   From (3.3), $\|\phi_\lambda\|^2=\sum_{n\ge0}|\lambda|^{2n}|q|^{n(n+1)}w_n^{-1}$, a power series in $x=|\lambda|^2$ with $u_n=|q|^{n(n+1)}/w_n$. The root test gives $R_x=(\limsup |q|^{n+1}w_n^{-1/n})^{-1}$, hence $R_w=(\limsup |q|^{n+1}w_n^{-1/n})^{-1/2}=\bigl(\liminf |q|^{-(n+1)}w_n^{1/n}\bigr)^{1/2}$. So the *second* expression above is correct but the *first* has the wrong sign on the $w_n$ exponent. The two displayed forms are not equivalent to each other, which is a self-contradiction in the paper.

   Concretely, taking $w_n=n!$, $|q|=1$ (the Segal–Bargmann case), the authors' correct value is $R_w=\infty$, as their own Stirling calculation on p. 7 yields. But plugging into the *first* displayed formula gives $R_w=(\limsup (n!)^{1/n})^{-1/2}=0$, which would assert a trivial phase space for precisely the example that ought to recover Bargmann's $\mathbb C$. The authors evidently did not cross-check the two forms.

2. **Slip in the $w_n=n!$ specialization (p. 5).** The text states "$T_{\overline{\boldsymbol\vartheta}}\phi_n=q^{-n}n!^{1/2}\phi_{n-1}$" for $w_n=n!$. From (2.3), $T_{\overline{\boldsymbol\vartheta}}\phi_n=q^{-n}(w_n/w_{n-1})^{1/2}\phi_{n-1}=q^{-n}\sqrt n\,\phi_{n-1}$ for $w_n=n!$. The "n!^{1/2}" is a typographical/arithmetic slip (should be $\sqrt n$) and would not survive a reader's check.

3. **Inconsistent conventions, flagged but not harmonized.** The authors explicitly note (p. 4 and Rem. 2.1) that they differ from [16] by placing the symbol to the *left*, with the $q^{-jn}$ factor as the only consequence. This is the right call and is documented, but the reader has to track two parallel formulas (their $q^{-jn}$ vs. [16]'s $q^{jn}$) throughout; a one-line translation table at first use would have helped. Likewise, the "direct notation" caveat on p. 3 ("technically does not apply, since we are not in a Hilbert space here") is a fair warning but a recurring source of informality.

4. **Several proofs rest on an unproven existence assumption (4.1)** — the resolution of the identity — that the authors themselves note is "a giant step beyond Toeplitz quantization" and a "strong condition." Sections 4, 6, 8–10 are all conditional on it. The moment-problem reformulation (Theorem 4.1) is useful, and the Segal–Bargmann example $\rho(r)=\pi^{-1}\exp(-(r^2/2))$ with $w_j=j!(|q|=1)$ is correctly identified, but the general existence question remains wide open, and many of the "main" theorems in the paper are really theorems about what follows *if* the moment problem is solved. The paper would be strengthened by a clearer statement of which results are unconditional (e.g. Thms 2.1, 3.1, 9.1, 9.2) and which are conditional.

5. **Unbounded-operator caveat (Section 10).** Theorem 10.1 assumes $T_{\overline{\boldsymbol\vartheta}}$ is bounded, which excludes the Segal–Bargmann case $w_n=n!,|q|=1$ precisely there. This is acknowledged in Remark 10.1 but the main "upper symbol of the annihilation operator is the identity map" result is then most cleanly stated only under a hypothesis that rules out the physically most important example. The connection to the $q$-deformed literature (e.g. $q$-oscillators, $q$-coherent states built from $e_q$) is never drawn, even though the coherent state series in (3.2) — with $w_n=n!$ — is closely related to a $q$-exponential and would have been a natural point of contact.

6. **"Phase space" terminology.** Remark 3.2 is appropriately candid: functionally, $B_w$ is the point spectrum of a densely-defined operator, not a classical phase space. The authors are explicit that nothing classical is being quantized, which is intellectually honest and worth repeating in the abstract, where the term "phase space" is currently used without the caveat.

7. **Minor.** Lemma 6.1's "⇐" argument ("Take $\psi\in\ker C$") is slightly circular in its phrasing — the right logic is: density of $\{\phi_\lambda\}$ in $\mathcal H$ + $C\psi=0$ pointwise on $D_w$ ⇒ $\psi=0$; this is what is intended but is more compressed than necessary. In (10.1) the $n=0$ boundary convention $w_{-1}=1$ is needed and is set in Thm. 2.1, but the proof of (10.1) relies on a "formal" identification of the $T_{\overline{\boldsymbol\vartheta}}$ matrix elements that the reader must follow carefully.

## Originality

The framework is Sontz's own (from [16,17]); this paper instantiates it on a new non-commutative plane and adds a "secondary" Toeplitz quantization (Definition 7.1) on the resulting RKHS. That secondary construction is the most original piece: the natural use of the resolution of identity to give $\mathcal{SB}\subset L^2(D_w,\rho)$ its RKHS structure, and then re-running Toeplitz quantization with *functions* as symbols, is a legitimate and interesting twist. But on the whole the paper is more application-and-development than invention. The Manin-plane specialization itself is fairly standard; the conceptual point (phase space as a quantum object) is suggestive but not new in its underlying mechanism.

## Review: *Coherent States for the Manin Plane via Toeplitz Quantization* (Đurdević & Sontz)

### What the paper does

You develop the "reverse" quantization program (Sontz, refs. [16]–[17], the viewpoint in [1],[3]) on one more example: the non‑commutative Manin plane $\mathbb C Q_q(\theta,\bar\theta)$, $\bar\theta\theta=q\theta\bar\theta$. Starting from a weight sequence $w_n$ on the holomorphic subalgebra, you get a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, a left‑Toeplitz annihilation operator $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}$, and define coherent states as its **eigenvectors** (so the "phase space" $B_w$ is the annihilation operator's point spectrum, a ball of radius $R_w$). You then: (i) postulate a resolution of the identity $I=\int_{B_w}d\rho\,|\phi_\lambda\rangle\langle\phi_\lambda|$ and derive its necessary conditions ($\S4$); (ii) build a coherent‑state transform, showing its range $\mathsf{SB}$ is a reproducing‑kernel (anti‑Segal‑Bargmann) space ($\S6$); (iii) use $K$ to define a *secondary* function‑symbol Toeplitz quantization ($\S7$) and a coherent‑state quantization $Q_{c.s.}$ ($\S8$); (iv) prove the Berezin lower symbol of $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}$ is the identity, and of $(\overline{\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}})^*$ is $\lambda^*$ when $q$ is real ($\S9$–$\S10$); and (v) compare with the paragrassmann algebra ($\S11$).

### What is good

- **The core computation is correct and I checked it.** The recursion (3.1)→(3.2), the radius‑of‑convergence formula (the $(n+1)$ exponent on $|q|$ is right: $|q|^{n(n+1)}$ to the $1/n$ power is $|q|^{n+1}$), and the convergence/domain statement (3.3) / Prop. 3.1 all hold. The $w_n=n!$ reduction to the Segal‑Bargmann $\partial_z$ for $q=1$ is right, and the $w_n=1$ weighted‑backward‑shift boundedness claim is right (consistent with your $q^{-n}$ sign).
- **Theorem 9.1 is a clean, correct result** and Remark 9.5 (normalized symbol independent of $q$ and of the $w_k$) is a genuinely nice unifying observation. The $\S11$ paragrassmann comparison (your coherent states are convergent single vectors, not the formal sums of [5]) is a good touch and shows the framework generalizes.
- **Honest scoping.** You repeatedly flag that the radiality assumption is a "severe restriction," that (8.3) is sufficient but $f\equiv1$ violates it, that the $q$‑real hypothesis is needed for Thm. 9.2, and that the $R_w=0$ case is excluded. That intellectual honesty is a strength.
- **Writing is clear** and the terminology discussion (Manin plane = non‑commutative object, no $\hbar$) is a defensible, well‑motivated stance against the Weyl–Heisenberg comparison.

### Issues I want you to fix

**1. (The main one.) You never exhibit a single *non‑commutative* measure $\rho$ for which the resolution of the identity actually holds.** Everything in $\S4$–$\S10$ is *conditional* on there existing a $\rho$ satisfying (4.1)/(4.2). You derive *necessary* conditions — (4.3), (4.6) — but the paper's central claim that the machinery is "a giant step beyond Toeplitz quantization" rests on a measure whose existence you take as a hypothesis. The only case where it is actually verified is the classical one ($q=1$, $w_n=n!$, Gaussian — the standard Bargmann measure), which is not really *your* contribution.

For $q\ne1$ (or non‑trivial $w_n$) I could not find any $\rho$ being constructed. Note the moment condition (4.3) with the extra $|q|^{n(n+1)}w_n^{-1}$ factor is *not* the standard Laguerre/Gaussian moment sequence, so the Gaussian does **not** extend to $q\ne1$ — you'd need a new measure. Two options:
- Construct one explicit non‑commutative instance where the identity resolves (or prove non‑existence in one natural instance), or
- Honestly re‑scope: state clearly "we prove the *consequences* of a resolution of the identity; its **existence** for $q\ne1$ is open (and conjectured under radiality)," and put the verified $q=1$ case up front as the model. 

As written, a reader finishes not knowing whether the non‑commutative theory you've built has a coherent state measure at all. That is the difference between a complete and a conditional paper.

**2. A $q$‑sign inconsistency with your own stated commutation relation (cosmetic but real).** On p.3 you state $\bar\theta\theta=q\,\theta\bar\theta$. Under *that* relation, $\bar\theta\,\theta^n=q^{+n}\theta^n\bar\theta$, so a direct computation of $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}\varphi_n=P(\bar\theta\,w_n^{-1/2}\theta^n)=w_n^{-1/2}q^{+n}\,P(\theta^n\bar\theta)$ gives
$$\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}\varphi_n=q^{+n}\Bigl(\tfrac{w_n}{w_{n-1}}\Bigr)^{1/2}\varphi_{n-1},$$
the **opposite** $q$‑power from (2.3)/(2.2). I reproduced your weight and index factors exactly ($w_{n+i}/(w_n w_{n+i-j})^{1/2}\,\varphi_{n+i-j}$) — the only mismatch is $q^{+jn}$ vs. $q^{-jn}$. Because everything else in the paper is internally consistent *with* $q^{-n}$ (e.g. the "$w_n=1$ bounded iff $|q|\ge1$" claim), I suspect the line on p.3 should read $\theta\bar\theta=q\,\bar\theta\theta$ (i.e. $\bar\theta\theta=q^{-1}\theta\bar\theta$), or all the $q$‑powers should be flipped. This does **not** affect any conclusion (only $|q|$ ever enters: $\|\phi_\lambda\|^2$, $R_w$, the $q$‑real hypothesis, rotation‑invariance), so it is harmless — but a reader who re‑derives under the stated relation will hit the sign and lose confidence. Pin the convention in one sentence at the top of $\S2$ and make the relation and the results agree.

**3. The left‑ vs. right‑multiplication convention creates downstream friction, and you don't pay the debt on it.** p.3 promises "we will explain later in why we preferred using multiplication on the left"; that explanation never really comes — p.4 just notes [16] used right multiplication. Consequences you inherit and have to work around: $\mathcal T_\theta$ and $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}$ are *not* adjoints for $q\ne1$, so your "creation operator" for symbol purposes is really $(\overline{\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}})^*$ (not a Toeplitz operator), and Thm. 9.2 needs the extra $q$ real hypothesis. That's fine *if* you justify the left choice (e.g. left multiplication is what makes $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}$ the *lower*‑degree / annihilation operator with the clean eigenvalue semantics). If you can't motivate it, say so plainly and present the right‑multiplication version as a corollary. Right now it reads as an unexplained convention that makes the natural theorem messier.

**4. Notation: $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}$ vs. $\overline{\mathcal T_{\overline\theta}}$.** The T‑alone (domain $\mathcal P$) vs. T‑with‑closure‑bar (domain (2.4)) is easy to lose track of, and Def. 3.1 mixes them ("eigenvector of $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}$ … in $\mathrm D(\overline{\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}})$ with $\mathcal T_{\bar\theta}\phi_\lambda=\lambda\phi_\lambda$"). Pick one name for the densely defined annihilation operator you actually diagonalize and use it everywhere.

**5. Minor, but worth a line each:**
- For the natural $w_n=n!$, $R_w=\infty$ for $|q|\le1$ but $R_w=0$ for $|q|>1$ (Stirling: $|q|^{-(n+1)}(n!)^{1/n}\sim|q|^{-(n+1)}(n/e)$). So the harmonic‑oscillator weights give a full phase plane only on $|q|\le1$ and a *trivial* one for $|q|>1$. You only worked the $|q|=1$ example; add the sentence that $R_w$ is sensitive to $|q|$ this way — it's a nice illustration of the framework.
- Thm. 9.1 is essentially *by construction*: since $\phi_\lambda$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\lambda$, $(\mathcal T_{\bar\theta})^\#(\lambda)=\langle\phi_\lambda,\lambda\phi_\lambda\rangle=\lambda\|\phi_\lambda\|^2$ and the normalized symbol is $\lambda$ trivially. Keep it (it's a nice consistency check and the *unnormalized* formula is non‑trivial), but don't let the prose oversell it — the real content is the existence/radius/measure questions, not that a Berezin symbol equals its eigenvalue.
- The "$\S11$ phase space is $\{0\}$" for the paragrassmann algebra: fine, but make explicit *why* (the nilpotency forces the only eigenvalue to be $0$) in the body rather than leaving it to (11.1).

### Bottom line

This is a **correct, competent, well‑written extension of the authors' Toeplitz‑quantization program to the Manin plane**, and the coherent‑state and Berezin‑symbol computations check out. To move from "solid conditional framework" to "complete result," you need (a) at least one fully verified non‑commutative resolution of the identity (or an explicit, honest statement that it is open for $q\ne1$), and (b) the $q$‑sign / commutation‑relation and closure‑notation cleanups. None of these are fatal. It is *not* field‑changing — it adds one well‑doctored instance to an existing program, which is entirely appropriate for a specialist audience in NCG / Toeplitz coherent states, and it makes a useful template for the non‑commutative planes and $n$‑tuples you list as future work.

Verdict as a senior scientist: strong B+ work; publishable with the fixes above, most importantly an explicit honest treatment of the resolution‑of‑the‑identity existence question.
