## [1505.06415v3-general-review] General Review


# Review: "Single-Inclusive Production of Hadrons and Jets in Lepton-Nucleon Scattering at NLO" (Hinderer, Schlegel, Vogelsang; arXiv:1505.06415v3)

## What the paper argues

The paper computes the NLO QCD cross sections for $lN \to hX$ and $lN \to \text{jet}\,X$ with the scattered lepton unobserved, so that the hard scale is the hadron/jet transverse momentum. The physics case is that these processes are the single-inclusive-hadron-production analogues in lepton scattering, the denominator needed to give meaningful NLO meaning to measurements of transverse-spin (Sivers) asymmetries, for which LO-only theory comparisons were already in the literature (HERMES, JLab). The core results: (i) an analytic NLO partonic cross section in three partonic channels, with the collinear lepton singularity handled either via a photon-in-lepton PDF (Weizsäcker–Williams, WW) or via a finite lepton mass, with equivalence of the two approaches checked; (ii) the NLO correction to the spin-averaged cross section is large — 50–70% at HERMES, larger at JLab 12 GeV, up to a factor of ~2 at COMPASS and in EIC kinematics; (iii) adding just the WW term to LO (Eq. 31) recovers a substantial but not complete fraction of the NLO correction, so quasi-real photons do not dominate the correction at moderate $P_{h_\perp}$; (iv) predictions for HERMES, JLab, COMPASS, an EIC with $\sqrt S=100$ GeV, and NLO jet production at the EIC.

This is a well-motivated gap-filling calculation. LO NLO results for $lN \to hX$ had not been presented despite lots of activity on the spin side, and the claim that the denominator needs NLO is exactly the kind of statement the spin-physics community needed.

## Strengths

- **Clean, complete technical structure.** Dimensional regularization, explicit plus-distribution treatment, MS-factorized PDFs and FFs, virtual corrections borrowed consistently from DIS, and the analytic NLO coefficients given in the Appendix make the result reproducible — which matters for a denominator that others will reuse in asymmetry analyses.
- **The two-treatment equivalence check is a real contribution.** Obtaining the same cross section from (a) a massless lepton with dimensionally regularized collinear poles absorbed into an MS photon distribution and (b) a finite lepton mass kept to leading $\log$ in $\alpha_{\rm em}\log(\Lambda/m_l)$ is a nontrivial consistency check, and it gives the WW distribution a more solid footing than it usually had in the HERA literature. Equivalently, it independently fixes the hard coefficient functions.
- **The practical WW-plus-LO recipe (Eq. 31) is useful in practice.** It lets the community approximate the NLO spin-averaged cross section with one less order of complexity, and the paper honestly quantifies where it works (HERMES: ~50–70% of the NLO effect) and where it does not (COMPASS: 30–40%, because $\ln(m_\mu)\supset\ln(m_e)$ is small). The candid notes that the $R$-independence for EIC jets "is essentially fortuitous" and the acknowledgment of the previously noted inconsistencies (d'Alesio) show good scientific hygiene.
- **Forward-looking phenomenology.** EIC predictions for hadron and jet production were timely in 2016 and remain relevant to the EIC spin program.

## Criticisms

**1. Large corrections without uncertainty quantification is the central weakness.** The NLO corrections are 50–70% at HERMES (hard scale 1–2.2 GeV) and up to a factor of ~2 in other setups. In that regime the paper itself concedes ("the corrections … come with increasing powers at each higher order of perturbation theory") that higher orders may matter, yet nowhere does it provide a systematic uncertainty estimate: no scale variation bands on the HERMES/JLab figures (scale dependence is shown only for one COMPASS cut and for EIC jets, where it is described as "fairly sizable" even when it decreases), no PDF or FF error sets, no attempt to bound or resum the missing $\mathcal O(\alpha_s^3)$ (or soft-gluon) contribution. A result claiming "you need NLO before comparing with data" should itself carry an honest statement of how much NLO+NNLO could still shift the number; as written, the reader is given a central value with no error bar in precisely the regime where perturbative convergence is borderline. This is the point I would press hardest: the predictions for HERMES at $P_{h_\perp}\gtrsim1$ GeV are plausibly in a regime where the NLO number is a lower bound of an asymptotic series rather than a converged expectation.

**2. There is no comparison to data, and the paper is somewhat silent on why that matters.** The authors note the experiments did not publish spin-averaged cross sections, but that is an unfortunate fact about the literature, not an excuse in a paper whose entire motivation is the spin-averaged denominator of the asymmetry. At minimum one could have compared shapes/magnitudes against published HERMES $x_F$ and $P_{h_\perp}$ spectra, or derived approximate constraints on the $A_{UT}$ numerator over the NLO denominator. This is arguably the single most practically useful piece of the paper's stated motivation, and it is entirely absent: no single number for what the transverse-spin asymmetry becomes when LO twist-3 numerators are divided by these NLO spin-averaged cross sections. Even a rough statement ("the asymmetry changes by X in region Y") would have connected the calculation to its purpose. The paper stops at the denominator and points to future twist-3 NLO work, which is fair but leaves a motivational gap.

**3. A promised cross-check is never performed.** The Introduction says that the NLO "1-jetness" calculation of Mangano et al. (Ref. [43]) should be recoverable from the present $lN \to \text{jet}\,X$ result by integration over $\tau_1$, and that the full $\hat\sigma^{i\bar\jmath\to\text{jet}}$ was computed there. Not doing this numeric cross-check is a missed opportunity for a strong independent verification of the NLO machinery, particularly for the jet section that is only shown at EIC kinematics.

**4. Details dated relative to the claims.** For the EIC projections, CTEQ6M PDFs and older FFs are used without any uncertainty discussion; for a 100 GeV $e p$ machine the small-$x$ PDF tail and scale dependence matter at exactly the probed $x_{\rm min}\sim U/(S+T)$. Target-mass corrections at HERMES $x\sim0.3$–0.5 with $P_{h_\perp}\sim1$ GeV are not discussed. These are minor relative to point 1, but they compound the lack of error quantification.

**5. Minor.** The equivalence of the WW and finite-mass approaches holds "up to" power-suppressed (but non-logarithmic) $\alpha_{\rm em}$ terms, so the "universality" language is slightly stronger than the proof gives; the $1/t^2$-vs-$1/sv$ discussion of why the WW approximation is skewed forward in $x_F$ is presented in one paragraph when it deserves a sentence about the physical implication for spin asymmetries, which is where the approximation will actually be used.

## Bottom line

A solid, gap-filling NLO calculation, cleanly derived and honestly presented, which identifies a real problem (LO theory is insufficient for the denominator of transverse-spin asymmetries in fixed-target measurements) and gives the community both the exact NLO result and a cheaper practical approximation. Its impact is nonetheless limited by the absence of any uncertainty estimate on a result whose corrections are 50–200%, by the failure to connect the result to the asymmetry it was meant to serve, and by the missing comparison to both data and the prior 1-jetness NLO calculation. For its subfield it will be a standing reference; the caveats above bound its quantitative reliability at the lower end of the probed $P_{h_\perp}$ range.

## [1505.06415v3-expert-collegue-review] Expert Collegue Review


This is a careful and complete NLO perturbative-QCD calculation of single-inclusive hadron and jet production in lepton–nucleon scattering with an unobserved lepton ($\ell N\to hX$ and $\ell N\to \text{jet }X$). I've read it in full, including the appendix with the explicit NLO coefficient functions. Below is my honest assessment.

## What the paper does well

- **It fills a real gap.** This is, to my knowledge, the first full NLO result for $\bar\ell N\to hX$ in this kinematic regime. The process is the lepton-nucleon analog of $pp\to hX$ single-inclusive, so the result is not a curiosity; it is the natural "denominator" that any future twist-3 NLO spin-asymmetry calculation will need. The authors are candid that they compute the spin-averaged (denominator) cross section only, and that the numerator is left to the much harder twist-3 NLO machinery. That is the right scoping decision for a first step.

- **The internal cross-checks are strong and the most valuable part of the physics.** The two independent treatments of the leptonic collinear singularity — (i) keeping a finite lepton mass and dropping the $\log\ni m_\ell$ terms, and (ii) introducing a Weizsäcker–Williams photon distribution in the lepton with $\overline{MS}$-like renormalization in a QED channel — give the same NLO cross section. Explicitly verifying the equivalence and thereby demonstrating the "universality" of the WW distribution (Sec. II.E–II.F) is a genuinely nice robustness feature and a useful tool for others.

- **The quasi-real-photon analysis is a genuinely useful result.** Practitioners have historically used a "WW approximation" (LO hard part + the photon spectrum) as a stand-in for the full NLO correction when estimating higher-order effects on transverse-spin observables. The paper shows this captures the qualitative $x_F$ shift but systematically underestimates the NLO correction (WW gives only ~50–70% of the full NLO increase at HERMES/JLab, and the mismatch is much worse at COMPASS where the muon mass suppresses the $\log\ni m_\ell$ driving the photon spectrum). This is a concrete, actionable warning to the spin-physics community: the WW shortcut is not a reliable NLO denominator.

- **Phenomenology is broad** — HERMES, JLab 12 GeV, COMPASS, and EIC, with both hadronic and jet channels — and the analytic results (Eqs. 25–29 plus Appendix A) are given in closed form, which makes the work auditable and reusable.

## Where it falls short

- **No uncertainty quantification of any kind.** For a paper whose stated purpose is to provide NLO denominators for precision spin observables, the absence of error bands from PDF/FF variation (they use CTEQ6.6 + one FF set, fixed) is the most significant omission. The scale dependence is touched on (it decreases LO→NLO — reassuring), but there is no systematic factor-of-2 scale variation to bracket the NLO residual, and only a single alternate scale is shown for a couple of figures. "NLO predictions without an error estimate" is a real limitation here.

- **No direct confrontation with data.** HERMES cross-section data that would allow a genuine LO-vs-NLO comparison are, the authors note, not yet available; the JLab data are for transverse polarization. So every figure is a prediction, not a test. The phenomenological motivation is forward-looking but the paper does not empirically demonstrate NLO improvement anywhere, which weakens the "phenomenology" label.

- **The resolved / "observed"-photon contribution is deferred to future work.** The authors correctly note it is formally of the same NLO order and only *typically* suppressed in the fixed-target regime, but they do not bound the effect. Since the whole narrative is about how non-negligible photon-initiated contributions are at NLO, leaving this piece open leaves a hole in the claimed completeness of the NLO result.

- **A minor but real error**: the Fig. 7b caption states "upper: $\mu=2P_{h_\perp}$; lower: $\mu=2P_{h_\perp}$" — the two labels are identical, whereas the figure is presumably meant to show an upper/lower scale variation. This should be fixed before it propagates into the spin-analysis literature that will build on these plots.

- **The twist-3 motivation, while stated, remains indirect.** Because only the denominator is computed, the paper does not by itself change a measured spin asymmetry; its spin-physics contribution is to inform the *future* numerator work. That is honest, but it means the impact on the actual spin program is one step removed.

## Verdict

A technically clean, self-consistent, and well-motivated paper. The NLO computation itself, including the double treatment of the leptonic collinear pole, is done correctly to the standard of the field, and the finding that quasi-real photons do *not* dominate the NLO correction is a nontrivial and practically important result. The main deductions are methodological rather than conceptual: no PDF/FF/scale uncertainty estimates, no data-level validation, an open resolved-photon contribution, and a small caption typo. It is a solid "good" subfield paper — precisely the kind of result the community needs — but not a flawless one.

## [1505.06415v3-critique] Critique


## Critique: Hinderer, Schlegel, Vogelsang — *Single-Inclusive Production of Hadrons and Jets in Lepton–Nucleon Scattering at NLO* (arXiv:1505.06415v3)

### What the paper does

The authors compute the spin-averaged hadronic cross sections for ℓN → hX and ℓN → jetX at NLO in QCD (O(αsα²em)), where the scattered lepton is **not** observed so the hard scale is pₜh/pⱼ⊥. They present analytic partonic cross sections, treat the initial-lepton (lepton–photon collinear) singularity by two independent equivalent methods — (i) massless lepton + dimensional regularization + factorization into a Weizsäcker–Williams (WW) photon-in-lepton distribution, and (ii) finite lepton mass with an expansion keeping leading ln(mℓ²/s) + constant terms — and give numerical predictions for HERMES, JLab-12 GeV, COMPASS, a future EIC, and EIC jets via the narrow-jet approximation (NJA).

### Strengths

1. **Genuine gap filled.** The spin-averaged cross section is the denominator of the transverse-single-spin asymmetry recently measured by HERMES and JLab Hall A; no complete NLO result existed, and the earlier LO comparisons (twist-3 in Ref. [7] vs. generalized parton model in Refs. [8–10]) are only meaningful at leading power. This paper is a necessary precursor to any NLO twist-3 analysis.
2. **Technical care.** The dual treatment of the collinear lepton singularity is the standout contribution: the WW photon distribution is derived from an operator matrix element (Eq. 20), renormalized in MS, and the equivalence of the WW and finite-mass approaches is used as a cross-check for all three channels (q→q, q→g, g→q). This explicitly demonstrates the universality of the WW distribution in this context — a point that is more than a notational convenience, since it determines whether a cheap "real-photon NLO" surrogate is legitimate.
3. **An honest and useful negative result.** The headline phenomenological finding is that quasi-real photons do **not** dominate the NLO correction at fixed-target energies (they capture only ~50–70% of the NLO increase at HERMES; even less at JLab and COMPASS, where the muon mass suppresses the log). This undercuts the tempting route to approximate NLO twist-3 results via WW photons, and the paper is commendably candid about it rather than dressing up the approximation.
4. **Physically important punchline.** NLO corrections are large (nearly ×2 at HERMES/JLab), so LO-only extraction of transverse-spin asymmetries from the data — in both the twist-3 and gPMC frameworks — is undermined. Identifying the plus-distribution (soft-gluon) terms as the bulk of the correction and pointing toward resummation sets up subsequent work well.
5. **Reproducibility:** full analytic NLO coefficients in the appendix, standard IR-cancellation structure verified, and the jet section transparently flags its approximations.

### Weaknesses

1. **The most motivating claim is left as an inference.** The paper promises motivation for re-analyzing the HERMES spin data at NLO, but delivers only the denominator: no NLO spin-dependent term, no NLO asymmetry prediction, no comparison to the experimental asymmetries, even numerically approximate ones. The statement "this would likely have ramifications for analyses of spin asymmetry data" is asserted, not shown. A crude NLO correction to the asymmetry (e.g., ratio of NLO/NLO numerator estimates, or LO spin-dependent over NLO spin-averaged) would have cost little and added most of the impact.
2. **Quantification of uncertainties is thin for how central it is.** With K-factors near 2, systematics matter: the PDF is a single set (CTEQ6.6M) with no uncertainty band; fragmentation functions single-set; scale is fixed at µ = pₜh with no systematic variation for the fixed-target plots (only one µ = 2pₜh point for COMPASS/EIC). The size of the residual scale uncertainty at √S = 7.25 GeV is arguably as important as the K-factor itself, and a "resum at least the plus distributions" program (which the authors themselves flag) is not even attempted.
3. **The WW-approximation analysis is slightly circular in its framing.** The "approximate NLO" of Eq. (31) requires choosing an ad hoc virtuality cutoff µ₀ (pₜh or √S/2). The conclusion that LO+WW misses 30–50% of the NLO correction is a statement about the best-fit of that one free scale, and the argument that this "opens the door" (or not) to cheap NLO for the spin case would be more robust if it had quantified how varying µ₀ within any a priori acceptable window changes the result, or if it had compared against the full NLO at several (pₜh, xF) points rather than in the two integrated bins shown.
4. **Marginal kinematics at fixed target, only acknowledged.** At HERMES/JLab the relevant pₜh values (1–2 GeV) sit at the lower edge of the regime where an O(αs pQCD) K-factor of 2 should be trustworthy in the first place; power corrections (both 1/pₜh and the gPQ p⊥-model effects that the community uses to describe the same data) are unquantified. The comparison to the JLab Hall A data is also impossible for pₜ < 1 GeV, leaving HERMES as the only direct experimental anchor — and no direct comparison to HERMES cross sections is made because they were not published.
5. **Jet section relies on an approximation without a benchmark.** NJA neglects O(R²), borderline at Pⱼ⊥ = 10 GeV and R = 0.7, and the paper itself notes the WW–NJA agreement at R = 0.7 is "essentially fortuitous." More importantly, the full-numerical NLO 1-jettiness calculation of Kang–Liu–Mantry [43] is cited, and the authors claim only that "in principle" one could recover their result by τ₁-integration; actually performing that one-point numerical check would have been a cheap, strong validation of the entire jet analysis.
6. **Minor:** no discussion of ³He target isospin treatment, deconvolution of the electron beam, or lepton-flavor dependence for the JLab electron case; the scale choice µ = pₜh is never justified against µ = √(2pₜh⟨z⟩) type alternatives.

### Verdict

A technically sound, careful, and well-motivated NLO calculation that fills a real missing piece for the transverse-spin program in DIS, whose most valuable conclusions (large NLO corrections; absence of WW dominance at fixed-target energies) are stated with commendable honesty. Its limitation is that it stops exactly one step short of the physics case it was built to make: no NLO (or even LO-over-NLO) asymmetry predictions, limited uncertainty estimates, and one approximated (jet) channel left unbenchmarked against the existing fully numerical NLO result. Solid, field-relevant step-stone work rather than a field-advancing result.

## [1505.06415v3-novelty] Novelty of the contribution


## Stated contribution, in my own words

Hinderer, Schlegel, and Vogelsang compute, for the first time, next-to-leading order (NLO) partonic cross sections for **truly single-inclusive** hadron production (ℓN → hX) and jet production (ℓN → jet X) in lepton–nucleon scattering — i.e., inclusive in the final-state lepton, so only the struck-target system is observed. The practical reason for caring (their stated motivation) is that these cross sections are the *denominator* of the large transverse single-spin asymmetry recently measured in ℓN → hX by HERMES and JLab, and getting a reliable NLO denominator and a first estimate of the NLO corrections to that asymmetry requires NLO partonic cross sections that did not exist. They:

- derive the full NLO QCD partonic cross sections (virtual + real-emission, all three partonic channels) in MSbar,
- treat the characteristic collinear singularity of the quasi-real exchange photon in two equivalent ways — finite lepton mass (mℓ expansion) or a Weizsäcker–Williams (WW) photon-in-lepton distribution (a lepton-space "parton distribution" with a QED counterterm),
- show the NLO corrections are sizable (≳50–100% in many kinematics) at HERMES, JLab-12, COMPASS and a proposed EIC,
- quantify how well a much simpler "LO + WW" approximation reproduces the full NLO (works qualitatively, but typically captures only ~50–70%), and
- extend the formalism to single-inclusive jets at the EIC via the Narrow Jet Approximation.

## Novelty versus their cited prior work

**What is new:**

1. **The NLO partonic cross sections for ℓN → hX with the lepton unobserved are genuinely new.** They explicitly note that LO calculations for this process exist (Ref. [16], Metz–Pitonays–Schafer–Schlegel–Vogelsang–Zhou, 2012) — a leading-order spin asymmetry in terms of twist-3 PDFs — but "this calculation [NLO] has not been presented so far." The extension to ℓN → jet X at NLO is likewise a first, done via NLA + the anti-kt algorithm, and no NLA-based NLO jet cross sections for these processes had appeared in the citations.
2. **The two-way treatment of the photon collinear singularity** (finite-mℓ expansion vs. WW distribution in the lepton, with an explicit cross-check of their equivalence — Eq. (24)) is a neat, well-motivated technical contribution; treating the WW photon as a parton and renormalizing a *lepton* "distribution" (Eq. (19)–(22), following the lepton PDF construction in the QED literature, e.g. [43], [6]) is applied here to an NLO hadronic result in a fresh combination.
3. **The phenomenological decomposition** — how much of the NLO is WW-driven vs. genuine virtual/real QCD — is a new quantitative picture across four experimental setups, including the striking finding that the WW term is tiny for muons (COMPASS, ∼30–40% vs much larger NLO) because of the muon mass.

**What is *not* new (i.e., the building blocks are all cited prior work):**

1. **The entire LO framework** (factorization, Mandelstam variables, LO partonic cross section in Eq. (4)) is standard and inherited from Refs. [16], [6], [1].
2. **NLO single-inclusive hadron production in *hadron* collisions** (pp → hX) is mature and cited ([54]–[56]); the real/virtual structure, plus-distributions, splitting functions, and MSbar subtraction are textbook. NLO **semi-inclusive** DIS (the lepton-*observed* case) also exists ([34], Ref. D. de Florian et al. [32] at NLO), and 1-jettiness NLO formalism is available ([42], [43]) though, as they note, the numerical 1-jettiness results were not yet public. So the paper is a *first application* of a well-understood toolkit to the lepton-unobserved channel, not a conceptual advance.
3. **The WW approximation itself** (Refs. [42]–[46] and the HERA experience [43]) is entirely pre-existing; they re-derive/apply it here rather than inventing it. Similarly the narrow-jet approximation ([54], [55]) and anti-kt jet ([60]) are cited off-the-shelf.

So: the conceptual and methodological content is well-trodden; the novelty is that this particular **NLO partonic result + its first phenomenological use for the transverse-spin asymmetry denominator and EIC projections** had not been published, and the lepton-space PDF / finite-mℓ equivalence check is a tidy addition.

## Verdict

This is a clean, complete, first-of-its-kind NLO calculation for a physically motivated process — solid work, well-documented (analytic results in the appendix, multiple independent cross-checks, four experimental setups), but it is *calculation-level* novelty: standard pQCD machinery applied to a channel nobody had computed yet, with a useful (not surprising in hindsight) finding that the quasi-real photon dominates but does not exhaust the NLO corrections. Its value is as an enabling ingredient (the NLO denominator for transverse-spin asymmetries and EIC estimates) rather than a new idea.

# Evaluation: Hinderer, Schlegel, Vogelsang — "Single-Inclusive Production of Hadrons and Jets in Lepton-Nucleon Scattering at NLO" (1505.06415v3)

## Summary
First NLO calculation of the spin-averaged partonic cross sections for ℓN→hX and ℓN→jetX (unobserved scattered lepton → genuinely inclusive). The key insight: because the lepton is not observed, QED radiation off the lepton line (Weizsäcker–Williams, WW, photons) enters at the same order α_em·α_s as QCD real emission, so a proper treatment of nearly-real photons is mandatory.

## Methodology, in reproducible detail

**1. Framework (Sec. II–B, D).** Collinear leading-twist factorization:
E_h dσ^lN→hX/d³P_h = (1/S) Σ_ij ∫dx/μ ∫dz/z² f^{ij/N}·D^{h/j}·σ̂^{ij}, Eq.(2). Expand σ̂ = σ̂_LO + (α_s/π)σ̂_NLO + O(α_s²). New variables v=1−v⁺=… (Eq. 5) with v⁺=1−v, w=−u/(s+t).

**2. Virtual diagrams (II–B).** Since only the quark line matters, borrow the DIS virtual result directly (Eq. 8): σ̂_NLO^vir ∝ (C_F α_s/2π)·(4πη²/−t)·(−2/ε² − 3/2/ε − 8)·σ̂_Born. Channels q→g and g→q are infrared finite at NLO. Regulate in d = 4−2ε dimensions; plus-distributions for the double-pole handling (Eq. 11–12).

**3. Real emission (II–C).** Phase-space integration in d dimensions of the 2→3 diagrams (Figs. 3a–c); result Eq. (10): σ̂_q→q^real = σ̂_A + B_hat·δ(1−w) + [σ̂̂_B/(1−w)^{1+2ε}]. All three parton channels q→q, q→g, g→q are computed.

**4. Hadronic collinear poles (II–D).** The usual 1/ε poles are absorbed into MS-renormalized PDFs/FFs with the standard splitting functions P_qq=C_F[1+y²]/(1−y)+3/2δ(1−y), P_qg=T_R[y²+(1−y)²], P_gq=C_F[1+(1−y)²]/y (Eqs. 15–17).

**5. Leptonic collinear singularity — two equivalent treatments (II–E, F):**
- *(a) m_ℓ=0:* regularize in MS; absorb the pole into a QED photon-in-lepton distribution f_ℓem^γ(y,μ)= (α_em/2π)P_γ(y)[ln(μ²/(ymax·m²))−1], i.e. the WW spectrum (Eq. 22).
- *(b) m_ℓ≠0:* keep the mass, expand phase-space integrals (Eq. 51 of [51] cited), drop O(m_ℓ) terms; the leading log α_em ln(m²/μ²) reproduces the WW result.
Both give Eq.(24): σ̂_NLO = σ̂_bog + σ̂_0·ln(m_ℓ²/s) + σ̂₀(0) + O(m_ℓ²). WW factorized contribution in the final cross section (Eq. 23, 25).

**6. Final NLO partonic cross section (II–G).** Eqs. (25)–(29): σ̂^{γγ̂} contains σ̂_LO(v,w)+[α_s/π]σ̂_NLO with the coefficients A_i^q→q, B_i^q→q, C_i^q→q given in Appendix A (Eq. 26 for q→q; simpler for q→g and g→q, Eqs. 27–28).

**7. Jet channel (II–H).** Single-inclusive jet cross section E_j d³σ^lN→jetX/d³P_j expressed in terms of the narrow-jet approximation (NJA) mapping two-parton final states to a jet of size R (Eqs. 30–31 for the WW part). NJA corresponds formally to R→0 but is accurate for 0.4≲R≤0.7. Anti-k_t algorithm (Cacciari et al.). Fragmenting functions for jets are δ(1−z).

**8. Central experiment (Sec. III).** NLO spin-averaged pion cross section as a function of x_F and P_h⊥ for:
- **HERMES:** √s=7.25 GeV, 1 GeV<P_h⊥<2.2 GeV, 0.3<x_F<0.55 (Fig. 5) — the *only* P_h⊥ bin used by HERMES for the spin asymmetry; the cross section itself was not published by HERMES.
- **JLab (JLabA after CEBAR, 12 GeV):** P_h⊥=1.5 GeV fixed; −0.4<x_F<0.4 (Fig. 6); no 6 GeV results because the available energy is too small to reach the hard-scattering regime.
- **COMPASS:** 160 GeV muons, √s=17.4 GeV; hadron pseudorapidity η as Feynman-x with positive η counted toward the *forward* muon direction (Eq. 34); −0.1≤η<2.38; P_h⊥=2 GeV fixed (Fig. 7).
- **EIC (ep, √s=100 GeV):** single-inclusive pion (Fig. 8) and inclusive-jet production (Fig. 9) at P_j⊥=10 GeV; R=0.7 and R=0.2 shown.

**Inputs.** CTEQ6.6 PDFs [57]; fragmentation functions of [58] (DSS, Kniehl et al. 93). Renormalization and factorization scales μ=μ_F=P_h⊥ for hadrons. For the WW approximation the logarithm upper limit in Eq. (22) is fixed by the kinematic constraint m_ℓ²/(1−y)=ymax, yielding μ_0 ~ P_h⊥ or μ_0 ~ √S/2, as benchmark values.

## Assumptions, approximations, and limitations the paper papers over or under-discusses

**A. Factorization is assumed valid at NLO.** The collinear factorization of inclusive lepton-nucleon scattering was established at LO (the generalized parton model [30–32]); there is **no NLO-level factorization proof** for the transverse-spin case, and the paper simply assumes the LO factorized form survives at NLO. (The generalized parton model is acknowledged to be invalid at finite P_h⊥, but the collinear twist-2 approach they now use has no proof at this order either.)

**B. Hard scale is P_h⊥, with no discussion where perturbation theory breaks down.** At P_h⊥=1 GeV (HERMES minimum) the perturbative expansion is marginal. The paper does not discuss this, nor give an estimate of the missing O(α_s²) terms despite noting NLO corrections are 30–70 % (which is large enough to hint the series converges slowly). They note in the conclusion that hard gluon resummation ("similar to photohadroproduction [61]") could be worthwhile, but do it nowhere.

**C. WW approximation is a crude proxy.**
- The choice of the virtuality upper limit μ_0 (Eq. 22) is **ad hoc**; they try μ_0=P_h⊥ and μ_0=√S/2 as two benchmark values and report no error band.
- The approximation inherits the **1/t² (or 1/s v w) behaviour from LO**, so it only describes the forward (x_F>0) region well. It *fails at negative x_F* (Fig. 5a) because the true NLO partonic cross section has extra pieces (plus-distributions, especially at negative x_F) that the WW approximation misses completely.
- At COMPASS (muon, m_μ≈200 m_e) the WW approximation recovers only **30–40 %** of the NLO correction, so it would give quantitatively wrong spin-asymmetry denominators there.
- "Partonic structure" of real photons (resolved-photon exchange, [62, 111]) is **ignored** entirely; it is argued to be a higher power in P_h⊥ and hence subleading, but this is only a handwave.

**D. Photon-in-lepton PDF computed at leading order in α_em.** Since this distribution enters at order α_em α_s = same order as NLO, the *photon-PDF itself* would need an NLO (α_em²) treatment for consistency. It is not done.

**E. m_ℓ is then set to zero** (main result) after establishing equivalence of the two approaches. Power-suppressed O(m_ℓ²/μ²) terms are dropped, which is standard but never quantified.

**F. Spin asymmetry is not computed.** The point of the paper is the *spin-averaged cross section* (denominator of the asymmetry). The NLO corrections to the numerator (transverse-spin, twist-3) are unknown (only LO exists, [7]), so the actual NLO asymmetry cannot be obtained from this result alone; comparing the LO numerator to an NLO denominator would break perturbative order. The paper is honest about this, but it means the "central experimental target" (HERMES/JLab spin asymmetries) is not actually computed.

**G. PDF/FF inputs.** CTEQ6.6 is a 2002 DJS fit; the DSS fragmentation functions are from 1993. **No PDF or FF error bands** are propagated. The choice μ=μ_F=P_h⊥ has no sensitivity study (only the WW-approximation μ_0 is varied).

**H. Jet production.** (i) Narrow-jet approximation (NJA) assumed to be accurate for R∈[0.4,0.7]; the first-order WW jet result happens to agree with the full R=0.7 NLO — explicitly noted as "fortuitous", so it gives no reliable R-dependence at NLO. (ii) Fragmentation is set to δ(1−z); for jet observables the *jet* itself is the fragmentation object, but a proper NLO treatment of jet fragmentation (e.g. DGLAP-evolved jet FF, or JIM/anti-k_t evolution) is not done. (iii) The EIC jet results are for 20–40 GeV transverse momenta with a 20-year-old PDF; no systematic uncertainty.

**I. No comparison to data.** Because HERMES never published the unpolarized cross section needed here, and no other experiment has a corresponding measurement in this kinematics, **none of the NLO results can be checked against experiment in this paper.** This is explicitly stated but represents the main gap.

**J. Kinematic conventions differ across experiments** (Feynman-x sign, pseudorapidity, etc.) and are set case-by-case (Eq. 32 vs. 34), which complicates direct comparison between the different setups; these conventions should be re-derived carefully by a reproducer.

**K. EIC predictions are for a hypothetical facility** (√s=100 GeV proposed EIC [59]), not a real dataset.

## Score rationale
- **Originality 4.** First NLO spin-averaged cross section for ℓN→hX and ℓN→jetX; the realization that the *photon-in-lepton* contribution must be included at NLO (not just as a correction to the DIS-like process) is the genuinely new piece. Builds on well-known ingredients (DIS virtual correction, WW distribution, MS-subtracted factorization) but the assembly and the lepton-line treatment is novel. Not 5 because each component is textbook.
- **Overall 3.** The calculation is clearly correct at the level presented: the two equivalent treatments of the collinear lepton singularity are explicitly cross-checked, the IR cancellation between real and virtual is standard, the partonic functions are given in closed form in the Appendix, and the NLO corrections are physically motivated. Minus points: no NLO factorization proof, no data comparison, no uncertainty bands, the "WW approximation" is a heuristic, not an approximation under control.
- **Impact 4.** The result is the only NLO prediction that will enter any future spin-averaged lepton-hadron inclusive cross-section comparison, and will be needed as the denominator in any NLO twist-3 spin-asymmetry analysis. It motivates future EIC physics. But because the central spin-asymmetry target is not itself computed (only the denominator), the immediate impact is as an enabling theoretical ingredient rather than a direct phenomenological result. Not 5 because of that, and because the paper's own conclusion notes the result is "preliminary" until full NLO twist-3 is done.

## [1505.06415v3-logical-fallacies] Logical fallacies


# Evaluation: *Single-Inclusive Production of Hadrons and Jets in Lepton-Nucleon Scattering at NLO* (Hinderer, Schleigel, Vogelsang, arXiv:1505.06415v3)

## My understanding of the logical argument

**Motivation and setup.** The paper targets the processes ℓ N → h X and ℓ N → jet X, in which a lepton scatters inelastically off a nucleon and produces a high-p_T hadron (or jet) while the outgoing lepton is *not* detected. These are the lepton-nucleon analogs of single-inclusive pp production and are the natural laboratory for extracting twist-3 transverse-spin parton correlations, because the single-transverse spin asymmetry A_N^⊥ is simpler than the pp case. Data from HERMES and Jefferson Lab Hall A first released the cross section and the asymmetry A_N^⊥. LO predictions in the twist-3 framework failed to describe the HERMES asymmetry, while the generalized-parton-model (GPM) seemed to work better. The authors' core claim is that **it is premature to draw conclusions from a twist-3 calculation at LO**: order α_em α_s (NLO) corrections must be included first.

**The calculation.** Because the exchanged photon propagator gives a 1/t̂² term, there is a would-be collinear singularity when the lepton radiates a collinear photon that participates in the hard scatter — a singularity that is a pure *artifact* of neglecting the lepton mass m_ℓ (the spin-dependent part lives only on the quark line, so no genuine infrared collinear divergence arises there). The authors handle this two ways and prove equivalence:
1. Keep m_ℓ finite, generating a well-defined α_em log(Λ/m_ℓ) correction, and take m_ℓ → 0.
2. Massless lepton in dimensional regularization; subtract the resulting lepton collinear pole and absorb it, analogously to PDF/FF renormalization, into a *photon-in-lepton* (Weizsäcker–Williams) distribution f_ℓ^γ, whose bare QED form is given in Eq. (21).

With this, the full NLO hadron-production cross section (Eq. 25) becomes the standard spin-averaged factorized result — virtual (borrowed from DIS, Eq. 8), real emission (Eqs. 10, 26–28 for the q→q, q→g, g→q channels, absorbed into renormalized PDFs/FFs and MS counterterms) plus the WW photon–nucleon sub-process Eqs. (29). The jet cross section follows by the NIA/anti-k_t conversion (Eq. 30). The finite-mass and WW results agreeing is used as a cross-check of the "universality" of the WW distribution.

**Phenomenology.** Using CTEQ6.1 PDFs and the FFs of ref [58], the authors compute the NLO (and LO) cross sections for HERMES, JLab-12, COMPASS, an EIC at √S = 100 GeV, and EIC jet production. Findings: NLO corrections are sizable across the board; the WW photon contribution accounts for a large but incomplete fraction of the NLO shift (50–70% at HERMES, smaller for the muon-initiated COMPASS since the larger m_μ suppresses the photon spectrum log), and it works better at large √S and small P_h⊥, in the forward-region (positive x_F / large |η|) kinematics.

## Feedback — where the argument holds and where it breaks

**1. The headline question is set up but never answered.** The entire motivation is the *transverse-spin asymmetry* A_N^⊥, and the stated purpose of NLO is to decide whether the LO twist-3 failure to fit HERMES is an artifact of truncation. But the paper computes only the **spin-averaged denominator**. The NLO spin-dependent numerator (twist-3, three-parton correlations) is *explicitly not done* — the authors concede that no full NLO twist-3 calculation exists for these observables (only Drell–Yan has been reached). So the deliverable is one ingredient of the quantity of interest, and the conclusion that "the spin cross section likely also has large NLO corrections, so LO analyses may need refinement" is an *analogy*, not a result. This is the paper's central logical gap: a compelling question, then only the denominator delivered, presented as "a first step." It cannot say whether the HERMES/LO-twist-3 discrepancy is fixed at NLO.

**2. Genuine, non-obvious technical insight, but bounded originality.** The cleanest idea — that the lepton-line collinear singularity is an artifact to be absorbed into a WW photon-in-lepton distribution, verified against an independent finite-mass calculation — is a real and useful contribution, and to the authors' credit this spin-averaged NLO for ℓ N → h X/jet X "has not been presented so far." The rest (factorized NLO in x, ȳ, ẑ; PDF/FF renormalization; the WW photon–nucleon partonic cross sections; NIA) is a competent extension of standard DIS NLO machinery rather than something new in spirit.

**3. No data comparison, so "phenomenology" is really prediction.** Because the NLO asymmetry is absent, the cross-section plots are predictions, not fits; there is no demonstration that NLO improves the description of the HERMES/JLab measurements. The phenomenological section is therefore interesting (scale choices, the surprising x_F shift of the WW approximation, the COMPASS-vs-electron muon-mass effect) but does not move the actual data question forward.

**4. Minor weaknesses.** (a) The NIA treatment of jets is acknowledged to be unreliable at small R (the R = 0.2 vs 0.7 comparison shows the cross section is not flat in R, and the WW term's accidental agreement with R = 0.7 is flagged as "essentially fortuitous") — fine, but it tempers the EIC jet statements. (b) Formally the same order as the WW term are real-photon contributions from high-energy real photons carrying partonic structure ("resolved photons," ref [61]), which are neglected; these can matter for the spin asymmetry and are deferred to future work. (c) The observation that the dominant γq real-photon piece (∝ 1/t̂²) "favors x_F > 0," i.e. the *backward*-scattering region, is presented a little tersely and is somewhat counterintuitive without more justification.

**5. Technical soundness.** The regularization/renormalization bookkeeping (dimensional regularization, MS, virtual + real infrared cancellation, UV/UV-collinear counterterms) is standard and internally consistent, and the finite-mass ⇔ WW equivalence is a strong internal check. I see no obvious logical fallacy in the derivation; the concerns above are about *scope* rather than *correctness*.

## Bottom line

A technically solid, well-motivated, well-presented NLO calculation that fills a real gap — the NLO spin-averaged cross sections for lepton-nucleon single-inclusive hadron and jet production, with a clean WW treatment of the lepton-line singularity. Its limitation is that it stops at the denominator of exactly the asymmetry it cares about, so it motivates rather than resolves the physical question it opens. As a "first step" it is exactly what a first step should be, but readers expecting it to constrain A_N^⊥ will not get that.

> scores: originality_score=3, overall_paper_score=3, impact_score=2

## [1505.06415v3-quantitative-claims] Audit of quantitative claims


I've read the full paper. This is "Single-Inclusive Production of Hadrons and Jets in Lepton-Nucleon Scattering at NLO" (Hinderer, Schlegel, Vogelsang; TUbingen, 2016/2018). It presents the first NLO pQCD calculation of the spin-averaged cross sections for ℓN→hX and ℓN→jet X with the scattered lepton unobserved (so the hard scale is the hadron/jet transverse momentum), and investigates how strongly the process is dominated by quasi-real (Weizsäcker-Williams) photons.

## Strongest quantitative claim and its audit

**Claim.** The centerpiece is Fig. 5 and the Section III.H.1 text: *"We find the NLO corrections to be large. The NLO cross section is almost twice as large as the LO one."* Corollary claims: the quasi-real (WW) photon contribution accounts for 50–70% of the NLO correction at HERMES, and full NLO corrections persist for P_h⊥ > 1 GeV (i.e., the process is *not* an automatically quasi-real-photon photo-production process).

**Is the comparison fair?** Mixed. The LO-vs-NLO ratio is the more prominent comparison, but it is an *order-in-α_s* comparison, not a method competition: NLO is strictly more complete (adds virtual and real-emission diagrams and the α_em·α_s WW term), so a large ratio is partly an artifact of what LO provably lacks. The large factor-of-two is physically explained (the WW contribution dominates and is missed by LO), so it is defensible — but it is not a "fair" test of NLO *against an independent standard*. The one comparison that *is* fair is full-NLO-vs-WW-approximation (both approximations to the same NLO quantity), and there the honest finding is that the WW approximation undershoots by 30–50%, with an explicitly "fortuitous" agreement at some kinematics. The authors are self-critical about this, which is good.

**Are baselines current?** Weak here. The predictions use CTEQ6.6 (a 2007 PDF set) and an older FF parameterization. By the 2016–2018 time of the paper, MMHT/NNPDF would have been current, and—more importantly—the paper reports **no PDF/FF uncertainty variation at all**, so even the age of the baseline isn't quantified.

**Error bars / statistical significance?** This is the decisive gap. **None are reported.**
- No scale-variation band is drawn on the headline NLO curves; the only scale studies shown are two discrete scales for the *WW approximation* (μ0 = P_h⊥ vs √S/2).
- No PDF/FF error propagation.
- **Crucially, there is no comparison of the NLO cross section to any measured cross section.** The paper itself states (Sec. III) that the HERMES/JLab experiments "publish[ed] only the spin asymmetries; corresponding cross section data were not presented." So the NLO prediction is *not experimentally validated anywhere*—the figures are pure predictions. The only "check" is an internal consistency check (massless lepton vs finite lepton mass give equivalent NLO results), which tests universality of the WW distribution, not agreement with data.
- No statistical significance is discussed anywhere, appropriate for a theory paper but meaning the headline "almost twice as large" rests entirely on the perturbative argument, not on data.

## Verdict

The perturbative calculation is carried out carefully (standard NLO factorization, internal consistency cross-checked, full coefficient set in the appendix) and it is genuinely the first NLO result for this observable, with an honest and useful physical insight (quasi-real photons matter but do not dominate for P⊥ > 1 GeV). But the central quantitative claims are **untried against data and unaccompanied by any uncertainty quantification**—a real weakness for a paper whose stated purpose is to give "estimates for possible future experiments." The CTEQ6.6 baseline is also somewhat dated for 2016–2018.

Net: a solid, technically sound "first-of-its-kind" fixed-target NLO calculation, but of narrow scope and with its key numbers neither error-banded nor data-anchored.

## [1505.06415v3-weak-point] Weakest link in the argument


# Review: Hinderer, Schlegel, Vogelsang — NLO $p$QCD cross sections for $\ell N \to hX$ and $\ell N \to$ jet $X$

## What the paper does

This is the first NLO computation of the **spin‑averaged** partonic cross sections for single‑inclusive hadron and jet production in lepton–nucleon scattering with the scattered lepton unobserved. The calculation is the non‑trivial one in two respects: (i) the hard scale is set by $P_{h\perp}$ rather than photon virtuality, so the photon is quasi‑real and the usual DIS collinear cancellation is replaced by a **Weizsäcker–Williams (WW) photon‑in‑lepton distribution**, an extra QED two‑flavour DGLAP evolution on top of the QCD one; and (ii) both a massless‑lepton (dimensional‑regularisation of the collinear pole) and finite‑mass (lepton‑mass expansion) treatment are carried out and shown to agree (Eq. 24), a nice internal check. The virtual corrections are the standard QED/DIS result (Eq. 8), the real‑emission channels are worked out (Eqs. 26–28), and collinear singularities are absorbed into PDFs/FFs in $\overline{\text{MS}}$. It is a careful, technically sound computation, and it is the first of its kind for this process.

## The single weakest link

The paper's stated purpose (abstract; §I) is to build the denominator needed to interpret the **transverse‑spin asymmetry** in $\ell^{\uparrow}N \to hX$ and to argue that NLO is now the right starting point for that observable. The conclusion therefore runs as a chain:

> compute NLO spin‑averaged section → "NLO corrections are sizable" → "hence the spin asymmetry needs NLO / is well described by these tools."

The step where the conclusion stops being entailed is the last arrow: **the inference from the NLO spin‑averaged (denominator) cross section to the transverse‑spin asymmetry itself.** The authors compute only $\hat\sigma^{\ell N\to hX}$; the spin‑dependent twist‑3 numerator — the piece that actually carries $A_T$ — is explicitly out of reach and is *not* calculated (conceded in §I and §IV). But $A_T$ is a **ratio** of that numerator to the section they compute. A large (30–90%) NLO correction to the denominator does *not* entail that the ratio is materially NLO‑sensitive: the twist‑3 NLO numerator could be perturbatively small, or could partially compensate the denominator shift, in which case the NLO denominator correction largely cancels in $A_T$. The paper's motivating claim — "we present estimates for a possible future programme / the spin‑averaged NLO section is the denominator of the asymmetry" — is therefore asserted, not derived: no data in the paper constrain the numerator/denominator interplay. This is the structural gap.

It is compounded by a second, empirical weakness that removes any external anchor: **Section III contains zero experimental validation.** The authors themselves note that the corresponding cross‑section data are *not* available in HERMES or JLab (the existing data are the *asymmetries* in $pp^{\uparrow}\to hX$, a different process), and that earlier LO work (refs. [7]) "fail to describe the HERMES data." Every figure (5–8) is thus a pure prediction whose magnitude and shapes rest entirely on un‑tested modelling choices — default $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scale $\mu=P_{h\perp}$, CTEQ6.6 PDFs, default FFs — in exactly the moderate‑$P_{h\perp}$, $x_F\sim 0$, large‑$\sqrt S$ regime where those LO tools already struggled. Consequently the headline phenomenological statements (WW supplies only ~50–70% of the NLO correction; "sizable" corrections; $e^- \neq \mu^-$ because $\log\Lambda/m_\mu$ is small; WW works better for smaller $P_{h\perp}$) are read off the model curves, not entailed by any measurement, and inherit all of that uncontrolled scale/FDF/PDF sensitivity. The internal consistency of the computation (massless vs. massive equivalence, WW pole structure, LO→NLO continuity) is solid; what is missing is the bridge from "denominator computed" to "the spin observable — and its sensitivity to it — is now under control," and, independently, any data contact that would make the magnitudes rather than just their shapes meaningful.

## Summary judgement

A technically competent, genuinely first‑of‑its‑kind NLO $p$QCD calculation with two independent treatments of the lepton collinear singularity and a clean WW factorisation. It is the right piece of infrastructure for the proposed single‑inclusive lepton‑scattering spin programme. Its limitation is scope, not arithmetic: it computes the denominator but not the twist‑3 numerator, so the conclusion it exists to serve (NLO interpretation of $A_T$) is a motivated extrapolation rather than an entailed result, in the same kinematic regime where the calculation itself is currently unconstrained by any cross‑section data and where prior LO work was already failing. The work is sound as far as it claims the cross section goes, and its impact is real but gated on the still‑missing numerator and on forthcoming data.

## [1505.06415v3-writing-framing] Writing and framing critique


I read the full 14-page paper (Hinderer, Schlegel, Vogelsang, "Single-Inclusive Production of Hadrons and Jets in Lepton-Nucleon Scattering at NLO", arXiv:1505.06415). Below is the critique of its writing and framing.

   - "Direct analogs" is asserted, not demonstrated. The factorization structure is materially different from the hadronic case: there is no beam-recoil parton, the lepton side enters through a Weizsäcker–Williams photon distribution (an extra α_em flux and a lepton PDF to be computed), and there is a single hard scale, P_h⊥. The "promising tools for transverse spin" clause is the sharpest overclaim: the paper computes only the *spin-averaged* cross section (the denominator of the asymmetry), never an NLO twist-3 numerator, and never a NLO asymmetry at all. The inference "big NLO corrections to the average → big NLO corrections to the asymmetry" is stated as a suspicion in the conclusion ("This would likely have ramifications..."), so the abstract is selling a conclusion the body only hedges.

2. **Abstract:** *"We find that the NLO corrections are sizable for the spin-averaged cross section."*
   - True at HERMES (50–70% increase, Fig. 5), but at JLab-12-GeV the paper itself calls the corrections "modest" (Sec. III.2) and at COMPASS "significant but not so large... roughly 30–40%" (Sec. III.3). A bare "sizable" generalization flattens a 30% vs. 70% spread across the three fixed-target regimes, and is then implicitly extended to the spin-dependent observables that motivated it, which were never computed.

3. **Sec. I:** *"We stress that the twist-3 formalism... Given the kinematics (and the precision) of the present data, one may expect higher-order QCD corrections to be important for a meaningful comparison of the data to theory. At least next-to-leading order (NLO) corrections should be included."*
   - Stated as necessity, but the paper never makes a single data comparison. The HERMES/JLab measurements cited are spin asymmetries; no absolute cross-section data are available to validate these NLO cross sections (the authors acknowledge this in Sec. III.1), so the entire quantitative output — HERMES, JLab, COMPASS, EIC — is untested prediction, and the "meaningful comparison" is postulated rather than performed.

4. **Sec. III:** *"This constitutes an attempt to obtain an approximation to the full NLO correction by assuming that the 1/q²-behavior of the hard cross section is valid over most of the kinematic regime."*
   - "Valid over most of the kinematic regime" has no support beyond a side-by-side plot with two arbitrary μ₀ choices. The consequence is visible: at fixed P_h⊥ the two μ₀ choices separate by a factor of several on the linear-axis plots (Figs. 5a, 6a), i.e. the "NLO approximation (31)" they recommend is itself scale-sensitive by an amount that is never quantified or banded.

5. **Sec. III.1:** *"The result with μ₀ = √S/2 provides a slightly better description of the full NLO..."*
   - "Slightly better" is a visual verdict on log-scale plots; no metric (χ², integral deviation) is given, and the statement is then carried into the conclusion as a general recommendation ("it may be worthwhile to use the WW... which is much simpler") for the future NLO asymmetry work.

6. **Sec. III / Conclusions:** *"... contributions by quasi-real photons to the cross sections of the single-inclusive processes ℓN → hX and ℓN → jetX are not the dominant contributions, at least for large transverse hadron momenta P_h⊥ ≳ 1 GeV."*
   - At HERMES the WW piece *is* the 50–70% of the NLO cross section on top of LO, and at small P_h⊥ the WW approximation actually works best — the "not dominant" verdict is essentially equivalent to "the full NLO partonic term is comparable to LO," which is a weaker and more honest way to put it. The ≳1 GeV qualifier also silently covers the only kinematics the paper can access, so the caveat does the work of an untested extrapolation to smaller P_h⊥.

7. **Sec. I:** *"We note, however, that this [ℓN → hX with unobserved lepton] is not... [a DIS-like process], which one might naively think is equivalent..."* / *"The process ℓN → jetX has been extensively studied in terms of the concept of '1-jettiness'."*
   - Minor: "extensively studied" cites two papers [42,43]; and the paper's own jet results (Sec. II H, Fig. 9) rest on the NIA plus an anti-k_t prescription, with R-dependence called "essentially fortuitous" to match at R = 0.7 — so the jet channel is a plausibility check, not a robust result, yet it is listed in the title and abstract on equal standing with the hadron channel.

8. **Framing of the whole paper:** "we take a first step toward an NLO calculation of the transverse-spin asymmetry" (Sec. I) is a fair self-description, but the abstract and conclusion frame the deliverable ("numerical estimates for present-day fixed-target experiments and a possible EIC") as if it were already *the* NLO description of ℓN → hX/jetX. It is the NLO spin-averaged piece plus an *approximation (31)* that the authors themselves show is inadequate at JLab and COMPASS ("clearly insufficient to match the NLO result", Sec. III.2).

## What an adversarial reviewer would put in the first paragraph

> "The paper's stated motivation is to make the transverse-spin asymmetry in ℓN → hX comparable to HERMES and JLab data, which for that purpose requires the spin-averaged cross section at NLO. What the paper actually delivers is (a) that average, computed in a standard MS-scheme collinear-factorization framework, together with (b) a Weizsäcker–Williams (WW) approximation to it. Three points limit the claim. First, no number in the paper is compared to any data: the published HERMES/JLab observables are asymmetries, and no absolute cross section exists to validate the 50–70% NLO corrections that carry the paper's central phenomenological message; 'numerical estimates for present-day experiments' therefore means 'untested predictions.' Second, the WW approximation — which the authors recommend as the practical tool for the promised transverse-spin NLO work — is shown to be poor at JLab (Fig. 6) and COMPASS (Fig. 7) and to retain a μ₀ sensitivity of a factor of several at the only place where it works (HERMES, Fig. 5), yet no error estimate or uncertainty band accompanies any recommendation. Third, because neither an NLO twist-3 numerator nor an asymmetry is computed, the title promise and the abstract's 'promising tools for exploring transverse spin phenomena' rest entirely on the unverified inference that denominator corrections translate to asymmetry corrections; this should be stated as an open question, not as framing. The fixed-target results are consequently of limited immediate impact, and the EIC projections, while useful, inherit all of the above."

## Bottom line

The physics is sound and the two lepton-mass treatments agreeing is a good internal check, but the framing systematically outruns the content: the spin-physics motivation is never closed, no data validation is performed, and the WW-approximation recommendation is stronger than the supporting comparisons justify.

# Review of "Single-Inclusive Production of Hadrons and Jets in Lepton-Nucleon Scattering at NLO"

## Overview
The paper by Hinderer, Schlegel, and Vogelsang presents first NLO perturbative-QCD calculations of the $\ell N \to hX$ and $\ell N \to \text{jet}X$ cross sections, with the scattered lepton unobserved. This is a meaningful gap-filling calculation: the leading-order versions exist, but a NLO treatment for transverse spin observables in lepton-nucleon scattering was not available. Since the final-state lepton is missing, the process is genuinely one-hadron (or one-jet) inclusive, and the natural hard scale is the produced hadron's transverse momentum.

## Summary of the approach
- **Factorization:** Standard collinear leading-twist QCD factorization, but with a twist: no observed lepton means the incoming lepton acts as a source of quasi-real photons, described by the Weizsäcker–Williams (WW) distribution. The authors carefully separate the "standard" lepton-initiated piece from a WW-like contribution (Eqs. 13–24, 25).
- **Two treatments of the lepton mass:** They work keeping the lepton mass $m_\ell$ finite (to regularize the collinear singularity from lepton-photon splitting) and also present a massless variant with dimensional regularization. They verify both give equivalent results — a good consistency check.
- **Channels:** $q\to q$, $q\to g$, $g\to q$ at NLO with the appropriate partonic cross sections, plus a WW term. The jet cross section follows using the Narrow Jet Approximation and the anti-$k_T$ algorithm.
- **Numerics:** Predictions for HERMES, JLab 12 GeV, COMPASS, and a future EIC, using CTEQ6.6 PDFs.

## Strengths
1. **Genuine novelty and importance.** A NLO computation of the spin-asymmetry denominator for $\ell^N \to hX$ was absent from the literature, and the authors are candid about this. Given recent HERMES/JLab spin data, the calculation is directly relevant.
2. **Theoretical care with the collinear lepton-photon singularity.** The key subtlety — that with a massless lepton there is a new collinear singularity not present in DIS (because there is no observed lepton to fix the lepton's collinear direction) — is handled in two independent ways (finite $m_\ell$, and dimensional regularization + factorization into a WW-like photon-in-lepton distribution). The agreement of the two methods is a strong internal check (Sec. II.F).
3. **A useful, clearly-motivated approximation (Eq. 31).** The observation that the dominant LO/QCD term inherits the $1/t^2$-like behavior of the LO process while the WW term carries the $1/q^2$ dependence justifies a simpler approximation where one simply drops the full NLO partonic term and adds the WW piece. This is pragmatic and transparently presented, and the authors honestly show where it works (large $P_{h\perp}$, forward $x_F$) and where it fails (COMPASS, small c.m.s. energy).
4. **Physical insight.** They correctly note that "positive $x_F$ hadrons imply on average backward scattering of the lepton, whereas the WW approximation should work better if the lepton is scattered forward," which explains the shift of the WW approximation. Good phenomenological discussion.
5. **Jet production at the EIC** is a timely and well-motivated extension.

## Weaknesses / Concerns
1. **The WW-approximation result is only approximate and can fail badly.** At COMPASS the WW approximation is off by a factor of ~2 (Sec. III.3), and the authors attribute this to the muon mass being 200× larger than the electron mass, shrinking the log. This is a significant caveat for a tool the paper partly positions on, and it limits how much one can trust the simpler prescription across facilities. It would be worth stating more prominently in the abstract/figures that the "LO + WW" curves should be taken as illustrative, not precision results.
2. **Fixed PDF set and modest kinematics.** The use of CTEQ6.6 PDFs (now dated) and the modest c.m.s. energies at HERMES/JLab mean that, as the authors themselves note, "the scale dependence slightly decreases" and the hard-scattering regime is hard to reach at 6 GeV. The numbers are predictions, but the precision is limited by PDFs and the boundary of the pQCD domain. Updating to a modern small-$x$-adapted PDF set (or at least a more contemporary one) and discussing PDF uncertainty would strengthen the phenomenology substantially.
3. **No comparison to data.** The authors explicitly state no spin asymmetry data exist yet for these kinematics, so all figures are theoretical curves. While understandable, this leaves the whole calculation unvalidated against any measurement, including the un-observed-lepton inclusive cross sections themselves being a new quantity. It would be valuable even to compare the total (unpolarized) $\ell N\to hX$ to any existing inclusive data as a sanity check; it is not clear this is done.
4. **Missing-lepton "one-hadron inclusive" vs. the more familiar DIS.** A reader coming from DIS may be confused about why the lepton being unobserved makes the process a source of quasi-real photons and changes the power counting; the paper explains this but the conceptual leap (this is really $\gamma^*N$-like physics, closer to $pp$ single-inclusive production) deserves more emphasis up front in the intro, which is somewhat dense.
5. **Presentation/length.** The paper is technical and the notation (Mandelstam variables for a lepton-hadron system, $v,w$ rescaling, the "hat" superscripts, $S_\varepsilon$, plus distributions, etc.) is introduced rapidly. The appendix carries all coefficient functions, so the reader cannot easily verify any cross-section numbers. The two-equivalence check (massive vs. massless lepton) is asserted ("we have checked explicitly") without a worked example or numerical demonstration.
6. **Minor:** The jet cross section in the NJA at NLO drops the WW term (order $\alpha_{em}^2\alpha_s$) and this is stated, but the size of the dropped term and whether it could matter for the EIC jet program is not quantified.

## Verdict
This is a solid, above-average technical paper that fills a real gap in the literature. The core contribution — a careful, doubly-check-verified NLO computation of $\ell N\to hX$/$\ell N\to \text{jet}X$ with an unobserved lepton, plus a useful and honest WW approximation — is novel and important for the transverse-spin program. The main weaknesses are phenomenological (no data comparison, dated PDFs, an approximation that can fail by a factor of two) and in expository pacing rather than technical soundness. I would rate it as a good, publishable work with room for stronger validation and clarity.

Scores:
- **Originality (4):** First NLO of its kind; the WW/two-method treatment is genuinely novel, though it is a natural extension of known techniques.
- **Overall (4):** Technically sound and well-executed, with an honest handling of its approximation; docked for lack of data validation, dated PDFs, and pacing.
- **Impact (4):** Directly relevant to the HERMES/JLab/COMPASS transverse-spin programs and the EIC; it provides the missing denominator for asymmetry extraction, but impact is contingent on comparisons to data that do not yet exist.
